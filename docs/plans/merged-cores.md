# Merged junction cores laid member by member (#854)

Part of #854 (the old junction layout's share brought to zero, then deleted). The user's choice (Oct 19 2026): keep
going category by category. The largest category left is merged multi-node junctions (#818 phase 5c): groups of nodes
whose cores overlap, merged into one synthetic junction with every member's outer arms. `CorePlanner` then lays that
junction as if all its arms met at one node, and on elongated or parallel-armed groups it breaks: connectors looping
round, kerbs paving whole blocks (a 38,000 m2 core at Geneva 2495250,1118173), mouths at 0.5 m. Those fail their checks
and stay on the old layout (most of the 210 "a check failed" on the 168 real tiles).

## Approach: the members as single-node cores, walked round the tree

A merge group's inner streets form a tree (a loop of them is refused: it encloses a real island). So:

1. **Each member laid alone**: an ordinary single-node core with all its arms, those toward the other members (its inner
   arms) included, their mouths not held at half the street (they may overlap the neighbour's core).
2. **The outline walked round the tree**: from each outer arm's mouth, the kerb goes round its member's corner to the
   next arm of that member; where that next arm is an inner one, along that inner street's side to the other member,
   round that member's corners, and so on until the next outer arm. Each inner street is walked twice, once each side.
   Where the two members' kerbs overlap (the street shorter than both mouths), they meet where they cross (as on
   `RoundaboutCore`). The result is one simple outline, no general polygon union needed.
3. **Connectors composed**: a move from an outer arm of member A to an outer arm of member B is A's connector onto the
   inner street's lane, along it, and B's connector off it; where the two members' mouths overlap, cut where they meet
   along the inner street's axis.
4. **The rest as today**: the merged junction keeps its outer arms, transitions, exterior, the writer and `JPTH`.

## Steps

1. Members re-planned unheld on their inner arms inside `PlanAndMerge`, kept beside the merged plan.
2. `MergedCore` builds the outline by walking the tree; the checks (simple outline, connectors inside, kerbs tangent)
   apply to the result.
3. Composed connectors; `--signal-check` (path ends on their lanes) on the result.
4. The test region's merged cases (the 8 m pair, the staggered T's) and the real tiles' failing groups: renders to the
   user against SWISSIMAGE before it becomes the default for merged junctions.
