# Stadium concert: Stade de Genève, a DMX light rig driven by the music, pyro, smoke and confetti

Tracking: #885. Status: **phase 1 (the stadium, its interior, seats) in PR #886; phases 2-7 (random events, stages, DMX rig, show control in Art-Net/sACN/OSC, the music driving the desk, effects, the desk screen, the crowd) on `feat/885-stage-lights`, PR #897**; Art-Net/sACN *output* to real fixtures and bots attending remain (Oct 2026).

Asked along the way (Oct 2026): **random artist events**, each with its own stage layout (end, side, thrust, in the round) and screens (none, centre, sides, a cube), not one fixed show (`concert/concerts`).

A real stadium, built to its real shape where the world already stands, hosting a concert: a stage on
the pitch, a light rig patched like a real one (fixtures on DMX universes), a virtual light desk that
runs cues and effects from the music, and the special effects of a stadium show (pyro, CO2/smoke,
confetti). The same rig can be driven **from outside by a real console** over Art-Net or sACN, and
can send its own output out, so the game works as a show visualiser.

## The venue: Stade de Genève (Stade de la Praille)

Why this one: it is the biggest concert-capable stadium in the Geneva area, it is in our tiles, and it
has hosted stadium concerts (Johnny Hallyday 2018, Mylène Farmer 2023). Arena Genève (Palexpo, about
9,500 places, indoor) is the fall-back if we later want an indoor venue.

### Concert facts that shape the game

- **The real roof bans big pyro.** After the 2018 Hallyday shows, organisers named a flammable roof
  that forbids large pyrotechnic effects, few and difficult access points, contested acoustics in the
  bowl, and a PA heard for kilometres (Le Temps). In the game, **pyro stays on and in front of the
  stage**, low (flames, gerbs, a few airburst shells over the open middle, never under the roof).
  That is both true to the venue and what a real show would get licensed.
- **The stage stands on the pitch, at one end**; the floor in front is standing. The stage takes about
  ten days to build, and the crowd wrecks the turf (Grand Conseil, Farmer 2023). The game builds it
  instantly, but the pitch can show the floor cover.
- **Capacity**: 30,000 is the legal limit (the Hallyday organiser was prosecuted for letting in 8,000
  to 13,000 more per night). Seats behind the stage are closed in a concert set-up.

## What gets built

### 1. The stadium (landmark)

- A `Landmark` entry for the stadium (like the IKEA): the data building at the stadium's place is
  replaced by a procedural `StadiumBuilder` sized from its footprint and the table above: the bowl
  (raked concrete tiers, seat rows as vertex colour, vomitories), the 48 pillars, the truss roof with
  its translucent inner ring, the pitch. Low-poly, both styles (PS1 and Cartoon), `[Showcase("Landmarks")]`.
- Walkable: tiers and stairs have collision; the floor and the stage too. No interiors under the stands
  in this plan (concourses later).

### 2. The stage and the rig

- A stage at the south end of the pitch (side to confirm against the real concerts' photos): deck,
  a roof truss grid, two side towers, a delay tower or two on the pitch, LED screens.
- **Fixtures**, as data: each has a type with a real channel layout and a DMX address.
  - Moving head spot / beam / wash: pan, pan fine, tilt, tilt fine, dimmer, shutter-strobe, R, G, B,
    W, colour wheel, gobo, zoom, focus (16-bit pan/tilt like the real ones).
  - Strobe / blinder: dimmer, strobe rate, duration.
  - LED bar / pixel batten: RGB per cell.
  - Hazer and smoke machine, CO2 jet, confetti cannon, flame unit: DMX channels too (in real shows
    these are also on DMX, armed by a safety key).
- A **patch**: universes of 512 channels, fixtures addressed into them. A JSON file per rig, so a
  real show file's patch can be copied in.
- Rendering: few real `SpotLight3D` (Godot's forward renderer only lights so many), the rest are
  volumetric beam cones (additive, a MultiMesh: the church disco's cones, `ChurchStage.Disco.cs`, at
  stadium scale) plus a lit lens. Haze makes beams visible: beam alpha follows the hazer's output.
  No allocations per frame (`general/perf-no-per-frame-allocations`).

### 3. The virtual light desk

The desk is the single place DMX values come from. Every frame it fills the universes, then the rig
reads them, wherever they came from:

1. **Internal show** (default): cues and effects driven by the music.
2. **Art-Net in / sACN in**: a real console (grandMA3, ETC Eos, QLC+, Chamsys...) on the LAN drives
   the rig; the game is a visualiser.
3. **Art-Net out / sACN out**: the internal show also drives real fixtures or a real visualiser.

Protocols (all plain UDP, `System.Net.Sockets`, no dependency):
- **Art-Net 4**: UDP 6454; packet ID `Art-Net\0`, opcode little-endian (`ArtDmx` 0x5000, `ArtPoll`
  0x2000, `ArtPollReply` 0x2100), protocol version 14; one universe of up to 512 channels per ArtDmx;
  discovery by ArtPoll / ArtPollReply.
- **sACN (ANSI E1.31)**: UDP 5568, multicast 239.255.{universe hi}.{universe lo}, receivers join the
  group (IGMP); priority and sequence numbers per source.
- Both are **client-local and opt-in** (a setting, off by default, LAN only): they never cross the
  game's ENet link and the server never opens these sockets.

**Sound to light** uses what the CD pipeline already computes (`Audio/Cd/CdAnalysis.cs`,
`BeatAnalyzer`): the beat grid and downbeat (bars), a 20 Hz loudness and kick envelope, and sections
tagged Calm / Groove / Peak / Chorus. Played back from the shared clock, every client derives the same
look at the same moment, and **nothing crosses the network per frame** (the CD and the show's start
time do, once). The desk maps them the way a lighting designer would:
- the **section** picks the cue (Calm: warm washes, slow pan; Groove: colour chases on the beat;
  Chorus: full rig, wide beams, blinders; Peak: strobes and the effects below);
- the **beat and bar** clock the effects (chases, phase-spread fans, ballyhoo), like a console's BPM
  master; the **kick** pulses dimmers; **loudness** is the master fader's ride;
- **timecode**: the song position on the shared clock plays the role of SMPTE/LTC on a real show, so
  a hand-made cue list can be programmed against song time instead of the auto mapping.

A desk screen lets a player program it like a simple console: fixture groups, a few palettes, cue
list with GO, effect engine (sine, saw, step over a group, speed in beats), BPM tap, faders, and the
special-effects arming key. Only the stage's owner (the host, or whoever the server gives the desk)
can change it; their changes go to the server as show events, everyone plays them on the shared clock.

### 4. Pyro, smoke, confetti

All `CpuParticles3D`, like the existing fire and smoke (`Build/GadgetMeshes.cs`, `BrCrates.cs`), and
all DMX channels of the rig, so the internal show fires them on cues (Chorus and Peak starts, the last
downbeat) and an external console can fire them too.
- **Pyro**: gerbs (fountains on the stage lip), flame projectors (columns of fire with heat light),
  mines and comets from the front, a few airbursts over the open middle of the bowl. Synthesised bangs
  and whooshes in `SfxBank` (so they show in the sound player).
- **CO2 jets** (white plumes) and **smoke / haze** (slow, low-lying; haze fills the bowl and makes beams
  visible).
- **Confetti**: cannons and blowers, paper that tumbles, lights up in the beams and settles on the
  floor for a while.
- Effects are deterministic from the show time, so all clients see them together; the damage of a
  flame unit to a player standing on it is the server's (like fire in `Build`).

### 5. The crowd

Out of scope for the first phases: empty seats, or simple instanced silhouettes waving to the beat
(MultiMesh, shader motion, no nodes per person). Bots attending as spectators come later
(`bots/bot-capability-map` row: "attend a concert = walk to a seat/floor spot").

## Devices and bots

- **Keyboard / pad / VR**: entering the stadium is walking. The desk is a screen opened at the desk on
  the front-of-house riser (interact key / pad A / VR: grip the desk), with `InputHints` shown; its
  faders in VR are gripped. Rows go to `xr/vr-action-map`.
- **Bots**: a bot can attend (walk to a place); running the desk is a server method (start show, GO a
  cue), so a server-run show needs no player.
