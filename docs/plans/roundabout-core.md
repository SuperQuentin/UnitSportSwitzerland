# Roundabouts in the junction planner (#854)

Part of #854 (the old junction layout's share brought to zero, then deleted). Today a roundabout's ring nodes are
left to the old layout: their cores overlap (the ring's links between entries are short) and the merge refuses a
roundabout. The user's decision (Oct 18 2026): **tight roundabouts get their own core builder; large ones are laid as
T's on a one-way ring.**

## Which is which

- A ring is what `RoundaboutShaper` rounds (#122): flagged ring lines on one circle, centreline radius 4-60 m.
- **Tight**: the ring is too small for each entry to be a T of its own, so it is laid whole. The cut is set from the
  real rings' radii and entry spacing (Swiss mini and compact roundabouts: outside diameter up to about 40 m); the
  Sion and Riddes rings (centreline radius 4.7-8.4 m) are all tight.
- **Large**: every other ring. Each ring node is a T on a one-way main road (the ring, #121 already makes it the main
  road), never merged with its neighbours; the entry gives way.

## The tight builder (`RoundaboutCore`)

One junction for the whole roundabout: the ring nodes are its members (as a merged core, #818 phase 5c), the ring's
links its inner streets, every other link an arm. Its core is laid by its own planner instead of `CorePlanner`, and
produces the same `JunctionCore` (mouths, outline, triangles, connectors, lines), so transitions, the exterior, the
writer, `JPTH` and the game take it as any core.

- **Ring**: the circle `RoundaboutShaper` fitted, its carriageway width the ring line's; the central island stays its
  island (raised and grassed, or a flush disc for a mini roundabout).
- **Mouths**: each arm's mouth a little out from the ring's outer edge along its axis, where the entry's kerbs leave
  the ring.
- **Outline**: between two arms, the kerb runs from one arm's mouth corner into the ring's outer circle on a fillet
  (the entry radius), along the circle, and out on a fillet to the next arm's mouth corner.
- **Splitter islands**: a two-way arm's mouth section gets an island between its arriving and departing lanes, raised
  (an exterior island area prop, as refuges are), tapering out along the arm; a mini roundabout's is painted (a
  hatch).
- **Connectors**: every arriving lane to every departing arm (no U-turn unless the data allows it), running in to the
  ring lane, counter-clockwise round it, and out to the exit lane: the traffic paths.
- **Exterior**: the give-way row across the arriving lanes at the ring's edge, the crosswalk set back about one car
  length behind it (not at the mouth, as at other junctions).

## Steps

1. The ring statistics on the real tiles (radius, entries, distance between entries): the tight cut.
2. Large rings: their nodes kept out of the merge groups, each planned as a T on the one-way ring; fix what its core
   checks trip on (curved one-way main arms, short half-streets).
3. Tight rings: one junction with the ring nodes as members; `RoundaboutCore` lays the core.
4. Splitter islands in the mouth sections and the exterior; the give-way row and the set-back crosswalk.
5. Checks: the test region gets a mini, a compact and a large roundabout; `--signal-check` (paths on their lanes),
