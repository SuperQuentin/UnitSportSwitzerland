# Urban streets: sidewalks with raised kerbs out to the facades (#119)

Status: **done** for this round (branch `feat/119-urban-streets`, PR into `main` carrying the #114
integration branch). Built in rounds driven by in-game review (below, round 3 onwards). Not done:
door steps/ramps where a sidewalk still lies off a door sill, sidewalks on bridges, smoother ramp
wall tops. Notes: `tools/urban-streets`, `terrain/sidewalks-tunnels-runtime`.

## Goal

Roads lined by buildings become streets: carriageway, a ~12 cm raised kerb, and a sidewalk out to
the facades (1.5..5 m), in collision. Country roads between villages stay as they are. A v1/v2
region renders as today.

## What exists

- `.road` v3 (`road-format-v3`): ATTR `Urban` flag and per side `sidewalkDm`, `kerbCm`,
  `vergeDm` (unused so far); `APRP` area props with `AreaPropType.Sidewalk` and a `Height`
  (reserved, never written or drawn).
- `RoadEmbankment.EdgeOffset` already adds the sidewalk and verge: the road blend levels the
  ground out to the sidewalk's edge, and the slopes and walls (#125) start there.
- `PaintEmitter` already reads `Urban` (3 m/3 m Leitlinie, 5.5 m centre-line minimum).
- The OSM overlay sets `Urban` + a 1.5 m sidewalk where OSM maps one (a placeholder "until #119
  measures facades").

## Design

### Build: `StreetPlanner` (RoadGen network stage)

Runs in `TileRewriter` on a tile's final segments, after the paint (the paint must not be split
with the segments) and **before** `EmbankmentPlanner` (walls stand outside the sidewalk).

1. **Facades**: building footprints of the tile and its 8 neighbours (`.bldg`, triangle soups),
   rasterised in plan into a 0.5 m occupancy grid (every triangle, so eaves count; a sidewalk
   under an eave is rare and harmless).
2. **Candidates**: at-grade `Major`, `Road`, `Minor`, `Lane` and `Square`, paved, not divided
   motorway-like (`Motorway`, `Expressway`, `Ramp` never), no ford or stairs.
3. **Stations** every 2 m. Per side a ray from the carriageway edge out to `UrbanReach` (15 m):
   distance to the first occupied facade cell, and to the first other at-grade line's paved edge
   (another road, rail, stream). Free width = the nearer of the two.
4. **Urban runs** per side: a station is urban when a facade is within `UrbanReach`; a sliding
   majority over ±15 m, gaps < 10 m closed, runs < 20 m dropped. Also urban: OSM `sidewalk`
   (it already writes 1.5 m) where present.
5. **Width** per station = facade distance − 0.2 m clearance, clamped to [`MinSidewalk` 1.5,
   `MaxSidewalk` 5.0]; where the facade or another line is nearer than `MinSidewalk` the sidewalk
   narrows to what is free (down to 0.8 m), and below that there is none. Piecewise constant:
   0.5 m steps, a min filter over ±1 station, pieces < 10 m merged into the narrower neighbour.
   Width = the minimum of the piece, so it never covers a footprint (at 2 m sampling).
6. **Split** the segment where the side state (none / width) changes; attributes per piece:
   `Urban`, `SidewalkDm`, `KerbCm` 12 (0 on a `Square`: paved flush to the facades). Points are
   cut, never moved; dashes keep their phase because paint was emitted on the whole segment.
7. **Junction corners** (`APRP` Sidewalk): between two consecutive arms of a junction whose
   facing sides both have a sidewalk, the patch from one arm's sidewalk end, around the junction
   polygon's kerb curve, to the other's start; triangulated with `EarClip`. Rejected (and
   counted) if it would cover a footprint or another road.
8. **Stats**: urban km per class, sidewalk km per width bucket, splits added, corners
   built/rejected, stations limited by a facade vs by another road, ms/tile, bytes/tile. SVG plan
   view coloured by sidewalk width (RoadGen's SVG writer).

### Runtime

- **Mesh** (`RoadMeshBuilder` → new `RoadStreetBuilder`): per side with a sidewalk, a slab
  offset like the ribbon edges (per-vertex bisector): top at road + kerb, a vertical kerb face
  (kerbstone colour) from the ribbon edge, an outer skirt 0.5 m down (meets any lattice), end
  faces. Paving colour, `MarkingStyle.None`. Corner patches: top + kerb face along the edge
  that borders the junction.
- **Ground** (`ComputeRoadBlend`): cells under a sidewalk more than one lattice spacing past the
  kerb line take road + kerb height, and that side's slopes start there; the one-cell ramp from
  road to sidewalk height lies inside the slab (the walls' free-line trick), never under the
  carriageway.
- **Collision**: top and kerb face of every slab and corner into the two-sided bridge-deck
  body (as `RoadWallBuilder.BuildCollisionFaces`). The kerb face is **chamfered 45° in
  collision** (12 cm up over 12 cm): a foot capsule (r = 0.32 m) meets a vertical 12 cm step at
  51°, at the edge of its 52° `FloorMaxAngle`; a car capsule (0.85 m) at 31°, a motorbike
  (0.42 m) at 41°. Visual face stays vertical.
- **Trees** (`CoverStage.BuildRoadMask`): mask radius from `EdgeOffset` (sidewalk included).
- **Sound**: `Surfaces.At` reports paving on a sidewalk (if a surface fits; else asphalt).

### Checks

- `RoadGen --format-check`: a split street round-trips; a corner patch round-trips.
- `RoadGen --street-check` (new, synthetic): a straight street between two building rows gets
  the expected widths, no sidewalk on a country road, none through a footprint, split count.
- `BlendCheck --roads`: sidewalk cells at road + kerb; no cell under the carriageway raised.
- `--roadcheck --sidewalks` (headless): a body rests on a sidewalk within 5 cm, on the
  carriageway next to the kerb within 5 cm; a car driven at the kerb (`--garagecheck drive
  --drive-end`) climbs it.
- **Windowed, batched at the end (asking first)**: screenshots of Riddes centre (`--shot-queue`),
  and the multiplayer check: dedicated server + client, remote car mounts a kerb, remote player
  walks on the sidewalk (`--netsmooth` floor gap).

## Where the build departs from the design above

- **Urban is decided per street, not per side**: a facade within 15 m on either side at 40 % of
  the stations within ±25 m. Judged per side and per house, sidewalks came and went every 20 m in
  detached-house quarters. An urban street gets a sidewalk on both sides.
- **Width**: to the facade only where it stands within 5 m; behind a front yard, or with no facade,
  an ordinary 2 m sidewalk (paving 12 m to a facade read as a square: 40 % of stations hit the 5 m
  cap). 3 m lanes get no sidewalk but where a wall lines them, and that paving is flush (no kerb),
  as on squares.
- **Sidewalks stop short of another street's sidewalk zone** (its edge + 1.5 m), and the corner
  patch joins them; also bare bends (two street ends turning > 30 deg, no cap).
- **Corner collision is vertical-edged** (top + 12 cm faces), the kerb along segments chamfered.
- The road blend's round line ends are only the carriageway's width, and the ground under a corner
  patch is its base: both found by `--roadcheck --sidewalks` (a slab 6 cm under the ground).

## Measured (6-tile test region)

- Network stage: 16.6 of 41.1 km of candidate roads urban; 18.9 km of sidewalk (one side each),
  17.2 km of it 1.5-2.5 m; 631 candidate segments -> 1,274 pieces; 119 corners built (110
  squared), 376 one-sided, 5 rejected; planning 69 ms/tile; byte-identical on rerun.
- Size: 82.8 -> 93.0 KB/tile raw (+12.3 %, over the +10 % budget; corners ~4 KB of it),
  57.6 -> 62.1 KB/tile deflated (+7.8 %).
- Blend (`BlendCheck --roads`): 5.6 -> 7.7 ms/tile mean. More, shorter pieces each get round
  discs at both ends; skipping the disc where a piece carries on is the obvious saving.
- `--roadcheck --sidewalks` on 4 tiles: all pass (bodies within +-1.4 cm of slab tops and the
  carriageway by the kerb, corner floors exact, ground past the sidewalk = blend). `--roadcheck`
  and `--roadcheck --embankments` unchanged; `--format-check`, `--plan-check`, `--street-check` ok.
- **Ground at the facade** where a sidewalk runs to it: median 0.46 m **below** the sidewalk top,
  36 % of stations more than 0.5 m below. Roads are draped 0.35 m above the terrain
  (`RoadExtractor.DrapeOffset`) and the kerb adds 0.12 m; so the fill slope from a sidewalk that
  reaches a facade raises the ground against the house by up to ~0.35 m (house looks sunk, door
  sill under the outside ground). See phase 4.

## Phase 4: road heights (agreed 2026-10-01)

Streets in town are lowered so the sidewalk top lies on the ground (road 12 cm below it, as in
Cities: Skylines 2 and real streets); outside towns roads lie at the ground or a little above it
(drainage) instead of the extractor's 0.35 m drape; gentle transitions between the two.

- **`UrbanField`** (RoadGen): a position-only value from the building walls (`.bldg`), on a 2 m
  grid per tile: `near` = a wall within 18 m; density = the share of `near` within +-25 m. Depends
  on nothing but the position and the walls around it, so both sides of a tile seam, every arm of
  a junction and every piece of a split line get the same value at the same point. The street
  planner's urban vote is replaced by it (urban = density >= 0.4), so a sidewalk only stands
  where the road is fully lowered.
- **Height offset**, applied in the network stage to the raw lines before anything else reads
  them (junction caps, rails #124, paint, walls #125, corners): `y += mix(rural, urban, s)` with
  `rural = 0.08 - 0.35` (paved roads land 8 cm + class lift over the ground; class-independent, so
  ends that met still meet), `urban = -(0.35 + classLift + kerb)` (the road at ground - 0.12), `s`
  a smoothstep of the density from 0.15 to 0.4. The density changes by at most ~0.025 per metre,
  so the transition grade stays under ~2 %. Motorways and expressways never take the urban part.
  Bridges, tunnels and 30 m round their ends keep their heights (smoothstep on the distance to the
  nearest structure end). Rails and watercourses keep theirs (a level crossing blends the rail to
  the road over 8 m, #124).
- **Blend**: the visual clearance (0.35 m) stays under the road and its sidewalks but fades out
  over 1.5 m on the slopes, or a road at ground level would sit in a 0.35 m trench.
- **Far ring**: roads are drawn up to 4 tiles out (Medium), the terrain is blended only within 3.
  The road shader pulls the road toward the eye with distance (like the paint bias), so a road at
  or under the coarse ground still shows.
- **Then**: re-measure the facade step (expected median near 0), and door steps/ramps at runtime
  where the sidewalk still lies off the sill; raising doors only if that is still needed.

## Round 3: after the first in-game test (z-fighting, missing sidewalks)

- **Z-fighting**: the town height had dropped the extractor's class lift (1.2 cm per class step),
  so overlapping ribbons of different classes became coplanar; the offset is now the same for every
  class at a place (lift kept everywhere). Rails take the same offset as roads (they stood 0.4 m
  over a lowered town street, or fought it where they blended down). The road shader pulls the
  whole road mesh 0.04 % of its distance toward the eye (decimated stride-2/4 terrain lies in a
  ground-level road's plane). Every ribbon runs 15 cm past its ends, 1 cm lower: the hairline
  crack between a junction cap and an arm's end (the ground showed through as a green line; in
  the 114 baseline too) is closed. Junction caps are road surface in the blend. Trams on a road
  bridge are embedded in its deck (#124 only embedded at ground level; the ballast fought the
  deck).
- **Missing sidewalks**: (1) the urban field gets a city scale: the share of near-a-wall nodes
  within +-150 m (urban at 0.45), besides the local +-25 m one; a quay, a park side or a station
  square is in town although no wall stands at hand (Bahnhofquai was "rural"). (2) 3 m lanes get
  kerbed sidewalks like other streets. (3) Another line within a sidewalk's width stops it rather
  than narrowing it (narrowed stations were then smoothed away, 10 m+ before every junction);
  paths and tracks running alongside (within 30 deg) do not stop it. (4) Corners look 30 m along
  each arm for the sidewalk, and the default corner is a band that follows the kerb round the
  corner at the sidewalks' width, narrowing to stop 0.2 m short of a wall, emitted as a strip of
  quads each checked on its own (no simple outline needed); squared corners only where compact.
- **Zürich now**: 285.6 of 300 km urban, 487 km of sidewalk, 5,256 corners (3,236 squared, 2,006
  along the kerb), 140 rejected (was ~1,200). Size 342 -> 433 KB/tile raw (+27 %), 236 -> 284 KB
  deflated (+20 %): more pieces and corners. Blend 12.4 -> 19.8 ms/tile (Release, quiet machine).
- **Not done**: sidewalks on bridges (needs a wider deck and the parapet moved out).

## Round 5: tunnels keep their cover

- `TunnelCarver` punches the hole at the mouth only: within 8 m (plan) of a portal, an end of the
  tunnel line that meets a road or a rail, or nothing (`RoadExtractor.Portals`). Along a shallow
  tunnel the ground stays over it (it used to be carved wherever the ground lay inside the bore's
  vertical span: a whole cut-and-cover tunnel opened as a trench, and the A9 median over a Riddes
  underpass had a hole). An underpass's approach trench is the at-grade approach road's own cut
  (road blend, #125). `--probe` still enters the Riddes underpass.
- Cover: a shallow tunnel's roof (ground < 2.5 m over the bore, open ground or town paving) is
  `CoverClass.TunnelRoof`, light concrete.

## Round 6: tunnel mouths and ramp walls

- Mouth: the bore runs 1.5 m (was 5 m) past the tunnel's end, aimed along its first 3 m (was its
  first 5 cm chord: the mouth stood off the road); the hole is a strip within 3 m of the portal
  along the axis (discs reached a bore width further in and opened the ground over the tube).
  Zürich: 3,266 carved quads (was ~16,000 before round 5).
- Ramp walls: within 60 m of a tunnel line's end, a wall wherever the ground 3 m past the edge
  lies more than 1.2 m above or below the road (the terrain data holds the trench already, so the
  slope rule never fired); their solid is 1.5 m, not 3 m. No wall stands on another street's
  sidewalk. Applied town-wide first: 1,876 walls (39 km) in Zürich, many wrong; ramps only: 170.

## Round 7: tunnels you can drive through

- **Floor and walls**: tunnels had no collision of their own (bridges had a deck body, bores
  nothing): past the hole a body fell, and the safety net (`FootPlayer.RescueFromVoid`, more than
  2 m under the terrain) put it on the ground above: "teleported to the surface". Bores now get
  floor (bore width + 0.6 m, run out through the mouth) and wall collision in the road body;
  `ChunkManager.InTunnel` (bores kept per tile with its collision) exempts them from the player's
  and the parked vehicles' safety nets and keeps a vehicle exit inside the bore. Checked: a car
  driven 80 m through the Bahnhofbrücke underpass at 76 km/h, out and back in at road level, no
  rescue.
- **Size**: `RoadTunnels.ClearHeight` = the class height (4.8 m for a Minor road), lowered only
  under a road crossing over it (0.6 m of deck), at least 3.2 m. It was lowered to the raw terrain
  cover, down to 2.4 m, which over a city underpass is the open trench in the elevation model.
- **Roof**: the road blend lifts the ground over every bore to its crown + 0.3 m (roads crossing
  over are left alone; not past the line's ends), so a mouth always has ground over it. The hole is
  a fixed punch: quads within half a quad in front of the portal to 1.5 m behind it, bore width +
  0.3 m each side; the bore and its headwall stand 1 m out, and the headwall has a 3 m concrete
  top over the punched strip. The bore mesh has a floor wall to wall.
- `--probe` counts a carved cell open when its ray meets nothing or the road body (the floor).

## Round 8: a clear way into the mouth

- No faces across the bore inside a tunnel: a portal face was built at every end of every tunnel
  piece (tile seams, trims), its outline sized to the ground above, standing across the road a few
  metres in. Only an end that does not carry on into another piece is a mouth now.
- No flat walls lining the punched hole's edges (left from the trench carve; one stood across the
  bore just inside the mouth). The portal block alone closes the mouth.
- Approach roads get the bore's width: an at-grade piece running out of a mouth along its axis
  (within 30 deg, 1.5 m of the axis, 60 m) gets flush paved shoulders out to the bore's half width,
  so the ground is levelled across and the ramp walls stand in line with the bore's walls (on the
  2.2 m divided carriageways into a 6 m bore they stood inside the mouth).
- A wall's face (and half a metre in front of it) may not stand on another line either.
- Safety nets (player and parked vehicles) compare against the raw terrain, which on a ramp the
  road blend cut 4 m down is street level: walking the east ramp rescued the player to the street.
  They now also need no collision floor within 4 m under the body (`ChunkManager.FloorBelow`).
- A tunnel end is a mouth only where the bore meets the surface (ground < 3 m over its crown,
  `RoadTunnels.AtSurface`) and, at runtime, not on a tile seam: the rail lines under Zürich HB are
  cut by TLM at every station hall and junction, 15 m under the street, and every cut opened as an
  entrance. Station tile: 78 hole cells (the 4 underpass mouths and 3 real rail mouths).
