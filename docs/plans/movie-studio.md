## Decisions taken with the user (Oct 2026)
- **Recording:** a ReplayMod-style **Record / Stop** that writes the whole session to disk, **and** the
  always-on "last N minutes" buffer as a backup. Both record **everything this client knows about**, not only
  players. In multiplayer that is what the local game sees: no server change, no extra network traffic.
- **Shots ("plans")** are set up on their own **Shots screen**, apart from the timeline, and grouped in named
  folders. The library is a compact list: a small image per row and a bigger live preview on hover.
- **Rigs:** *World* (fixed in the world, optionally looking at a target), and three rigs relative to a player:
  *Lead* (a camera car in front of the player, moving with them), *Chase* (behind or beside, smoothed) and
  *Mount* (rigid on the player or the vehicle: bumper, wing, cockpit, helmet).
- **Setting up a rig:** pick a player and a moment, fly the view where the camera should be, press
  *Frame from view*: the offset in the target's frame is saved. Sliders fine-tune it afterwards.
- **A shot is a placement, and may also carry its own keyframed move** (dolly, crane), which plays over the
  shot's time in a sequence.
- **Shake:** per shot, a style preset plus strength, speed, turn/move mix and roll.
- **Beat links:** one or more camera parameters of a shot follow the beat, in three shapes: *smooth pulse*,
  *step / alternate* and *ramp up*.
- **Sequences:** named sequences, each with its own timeline. The final movie chains them. Shots are put in by
  **dragging shot cards** onto the sequence's shot track; an **auto-director** can lay out a first pass.
- **Transitions:** a small library: hard cut, camera blend, crossfade, fade to black, plus a few effects
  (wipe, whip pan, zoom punch).
- **Filming time, then editing time** (decided after trying the overlay): the editor is **full screen**, the
  scene in a 16:9 **preview player** with the transport under it, the camera tools beside it and the timeline
  below (a draggable divider). F5 (keyboard) or Pause > Movie studio (pad, VR) opens it; Shift+F5 or Pause > Record is Record / Stop; "Back to game" (or Esc) resumes
  exactly where the game was. **Offline the game is paused** while editing; online it goes on and the player is
  left as it was (stopping somewhere safe first is the player's call). Shots are framed inside the player;
  *Enlarge* (H) gives it the whole editor while framing.
- **Buttons:** an icon **plus one word**, smaller than today; the full description and shortcut show on hover.
  The 11 px shortcut strip goes.

## R1 Full recording
**Why things are missing today:** only `FootPlayer` is recorded. Unridden vehicles, traffic, birds, dropped and
placed items, doors and lifts, structures, gadgets, farming, combat effects, battle royale objects, time of day,
weather and the sea are not.

**A recording contract every world system implements**, so a new system cannot be forgotten silently:
- `IReplaySource` (registered in a `ReplayRegistry` by the system's manager, like the existing network
  registration) offers three kinds of data:
  - **Entity streams:** an id, a kind, spawn data (model, colour, variant) and a sampled transform plus a few
    floats (wheel spin, rotor, door angle), with spawn and despawn times. Vehicles, traffic, birds, animals,
    lifts, hall cars, dropped items in flight, projectiles. Rates per kind: 30 Hz for vehicles, 10 Hz for
    birds and traffic far from the player.
  - **World events:** time-stamped changes (door opened, item dropped or picked up, block placed or broken,
    crop stage changed, explosion, crate opened). Each event carries the system's own small payload.
  - **Global state:** sampled at 1 Hz and interpolated: time of day, weather and occasion state, sea state.
- `IReplayView` is the playback side: it shows the recorded state at time `t` with **visual-only** nodes (no
  physics, no network, no gameplay), built from the same mesh builders the live system uses. Seeking is done
  from the nearest **snapshot** (each source writes a full state every 10 s), then by replaying the events
  after it, so scrubbing backwards stays cheap.
- What the live world does in the studio: the live systems are paused and hidden near the stage, exactly as the
  live player is frozen today; the views replace them.

**Two recorders over the same sources:**
- *Buffer* (exists, extended): the same ring idea for every source, sized by `GameSettings.ReplayMinutes`.
- *Session* (new): **Record / Stop** streams to `user://movies/recordings/<name>.usreplay`. Fixed-size chunks
  (snapshot + events + streams for 10 s) are written by a background thread; the main thread only copies
  into preallocated chunk buffers (`perf-no-per-frame-allocations`). Shown while recording: elapsed time, size,
  object count. A recording opens in the studio like a grabbed buffer; long recordings stream chunks from disk
  instead of loading whole.
- Audio: the game-sound capture is written into the same chunks.

**Order of sources** (each one a commit with its view): vehicles (parked and driven) → traffic → doors, lifts,
hall vehicles → dropped and placed items → birds and animals → structures and gadgets → farming → combat and
battle royale effects → time of day, weather, sea.

## R2 Shot model and sequences
Replaces `CameraTrack` + `CameraCut`. `.usmovie` v5 loads v4: every old camera becomes a World shot with a move
in a group "Imported", and the old cut list becomes a sequence "Main".

- `Shot`: name, group, **rig**, **target** (actor lane + part: the body, the head, or the ride; stored by lane
  id, never by node path), offset, lens, look-at (none, a target, a world point), smoothing, optional **move
  keys**, **shake**, **beat links**.
  - *World*: an LV95 position + rotation; with a look-at it pans and tilts like a tripod.
  - *Lead* / *Chase*: the offset lives in the target's **heading frame** (yaw only, so the camera does not roll
    with every bump), followed through a critically damped spring of time constant `smoothing`. Lead has the
    camera ahead looking back; Chase behind or beside. The spring is evaluated **as a pure function of time**
    (integrated from the shot's start at a fixed step, cached per shot and invalidated on edit), so scrubbing
    and export give the same frames.
  - *Mount*: rigid in the full frame of the target or of its ride (`Rideable` visual), with no smoothing.
  - *Move keys*: keys relative to the rig's frame (for World, in the world), with the existing ease modes. A
    shot without keys is a pure placement.
- **Shake:** presets Handheld, Walking, Engine, Impact; strength, speed (Hz), turn/move mix, roll. Made of a few
  octaves of seeded smooth noise on time, so the same shot always shakes the same way.
- **Beat links:** each link has a parameter (lens, shake strength, distance, height, roll, orbit angle, fade),
  a shape, an amount, a rate (every beat, every 2, every bar of 4, or markers) and a phase.
  - *Smooth pulse*: a raised cosine locked to the beat period.
  - *Step / alternate*: steps through a list of values on each beat (24 mm, 50 mm, 24 mm …).
  - *Ramp up*: rises over the bar and drops back on the downbeat.
  - **Beats come from the sequence**, not the shot: its music lane, its game-sound grid, or its markers. The same
    shot reused in another sequence follows that sequence's rhythm.
- `ShotGroup`: a named folder, ordered.
- `Sequence`: a name, a span of recording time (in and out), the actor and sound lanes it shows, and a
  **shot track**: blocks (shot id, start, end) and a **transition** between neighbours (kind + duration).
  The movie is the sequences in order.
- **Evaluation:** `ShotEval.Pose(shot, t, out Transform3D, out float fov)` = rig → move keys → beat links →
  shake. The sequence picks the block under `t` and blends with the next one during a transition.

## R4 Sequences screen, transitions and auto-director
- A tab per sequence along the top (+ to add; rename and delete on right-click).
- Top: the program preview. Right: the shot library as a narrow strip to drag from.
- Lanes: **Shots** (blocks in the shot's colour with its name; drag to move, drag an edge to move the switch
  point, Del to remove), the actors, a single **World** lane (the recorded world objects, for orientation),
  and the music and game sound with beat markers.
- A transition is a small circle between two blocks; clicking it opens the transition library: Cut, Camera
  blend (pose and lens interpolated, with ease), Crossfade, Fade to black, Wipe, Whip pan (a fast pan with
  motion blur between the two shots), Zoom punch. Crossfade and wipe render the two shots into two viewports
  and mix them in a full-screen shader; a cut costs nothing.
- **Auto-direct:** on the chosen shots, it reuses the GPX cinema director's scoring (`src/Gpx/Cinema/Director`,
  `TrackAnalyser`): shot length from the music's tempo, cuts on beats, a player rig when the target moves fast,
  a World shot on turns and stops, no shot twice in a row. The result is ordinary blocks to edit.
