## Context
The user wants to make films inside the game. They would record scenes (for example a player flying a plane),
then scrub the timeline forwards and backwards. They would act a character with a VR headset plus cheap
full-body tracking from a webcam or smartphone cameras, then film the result with virtual cameras: several
camera tracks with cuts, a live view and a video file. Decisions taken: solo/offline first, with takes layered
one after another. Projects are saved to disk.

What already exists and gets reused:
- **Seekable clock, scrubber and export:** `src/Gpx/RacePlayback.cs` (`Seek`, `StepTo`, `Duration`),
  `src/Gpx/PlaybackHud.cs` (HSlider timeline) and `src/Gpx/VideoExporter.cs` (frame-exact ffmpeg export,
  waits on `ChunkManager.SettledNear`). The exporter is coupled to the GPX code in only a few places:
  `_race.{Duration,Speed,Playing,StepTo,SetProcess,Focused}` and `_camera.{Step,GlobalPosition,Current,SetProcess}`.
- **Complete visual state of an actor:** the `FootPlayer` `MultiplayerSynchronizer` property set (`NetE/N/Alt`,
  `NetVel`, `NetYaw`, `NetPose`, `Anim`, `RideKindId`, …). `src/Player/SyncProbe.cs` `Replicate()` (L201)
  already copies it into a puppet "mirror" `FootPlayer` with foreign authority. Rideables animate from it
  through `Rideable.AnimateRemote`, so planes, helicopters and cars come for free.
- **VR:** `src/XR/XrRig.cs` provides the head and both controllers and writes `player.VrHands`. `XrUi` is the
  world-space panel, and `XrWristMenu` and `XrPad` exist.
- **Avatar:** procedural mesh, `HumanMeshBuilder` with a private `Rig` of joint positions and two-bone IK
  `Limb.Solve`. `ApplyVr` already overrides the wrists. There is no skeleton.
- **Cameras:** `SpectatorCamera` (free fly), `PlaybackCamera` modes, `Items/PhotoCapture` (SubViewport viewfinder).
- **Rules:** store positions as `GlobalPos`/LV95 (`core/floating-origin`). No per-frame allocations.
  Every action is designed for keyboard, gamepad and VR together.

## Core workflow: always-on replay buffer → timeline clips
The user plays as usual, for example flying the plane. The game **always records the last N minutes**
(a ring buffer, like a console's "save the last minutes" feature).

**Buffer**
- Default 5 min, set in Settings; 0 turns it off.
- It records every `FootPlayer` in `PlayerSnapshot`: the local player plus any remote players, whose
  replicated properties are already arriving.
- Cost: about 100 floats × 30 Hz × 300 s ≈ 3.6 MB per actor. It is preallocated, so nothing is allocated per frame.

**Grab**
- From the pause menu → **Movie studio**, or with a "Save clip" hotkey, the buffer is copied into the open
  movie project as one **clip per actor**.

**Studio (opens over the current world)**
- The live player is frozen and hidden; puppets replay the clips at their recorded `GlobalPos`.
- Edits: drag a clip along its lane, trim its in/out handles, split it at the playhead, delete,
  duplicate, scrub forward and backward, play in reverse.
- Further grabs and acted takes are added as more clips or lanes.

## Cheap full-body tracking choice
No webcam pose estimation gets written in-game. The game **receives the VMC protocol** (OSC over UDP, port 39539),
the de-facto standard for webcam mocap apps:
- **XR Animator** (free, MediaPipe, runs from a webcam). A smartphone works as a remote camera through
  DroidCam or Iriun, or through apps that send VMC directly.
- Any other VMC sender can be swapped in later, including multi-camera tools, without touching the game.

When the headset is on, the **headset owns the head and hands**, and the webcam supplies the hips, spine,
knees and feet. A short T-pose calibration aligns the two.

## Architecture (new `src/Movie/`, own `CLAUDE.md` index + `docs/notes/movie/` notes)
- `MovieProject`: actor tracks, camera tracks, cut list and a world anchor (`GlobalPos`).
  - Saved in a versioned binary file at `user://movies/<name>.usmovie`.
- `ActorTrack`:
  - **Continuous channels at 30 Hz:** position (`GlobalPos`), velocity, yaw, `NetPose` floats, `Anim`,
    head rotation and optional mocap joints.
  - **Discrete channels:** the change-only properties (`RideKindId`, `SeatIndex`, `HeldItemId`, `DoorsOpen`, …),
    stored as (time, value) events.
  - **Sampling is a pure function of time:** lerp, and slerp for the quaternion inside `NetPose`. That makes
    reverse play and scrubbing free.
- `TakeRecorder`: copies the local player's replicated properties every tick, the way `SyncProbe.Replicate` does.
  It also records local-only extras that never touch the wire, so the network protocol is unchanged.
- `ActorPuppet`: a `FootPlayer` with foreign authority, like the SyncProbe mirror.
  - It needs a new `FootPlayer` **direct-drive** hook, `SetPlaybackState(...)`, which bypasses
    `RemoteInterpolator`, because that interpolator cannot jump back in time. `Clear()` runs on a seek.
- `MovieClock`: the same API shape as `RacePlayback` (`Time`, `Speed` with negative values for reverse,
  `Playing`, `Seek`, `StepTo`, `Duration`).
  - It implements a new small interface, `IExportTimeline`. `RacePlayback` implements it too.
- `CameraTrack`: GlobalPos, rotation and FOV at 30 Hz.
  - Recorded from a free camera (`SpectatorCamera` with keyboard, mouse or pad), or from a **VR handheld
    camera**: a camera body gripped in the hand with a SubViewport viewfinder screen, reusing the
    `PhotoCapture` pattern.
  - The FOV is the zoom.
- **Camera lock (`CameraRig`)**: a camera can lock onto a **target**. A target is a player (a puppet or the
  live player), an **object** such as the plane, helicopter or car that a puppet rides (its `Rideable` visual),
  or a parked `VehicleBody` / dormant vehicle. Targets are stored by clip id + part, never by node path, so
  they survive a reload.
  - **Lock modes**, keyed on the timeline:
    - *Look-at* (tripod: the camera stays put and pans and tilts to follow);
    - *Follow* (fixed offset in the target's frame: chase cam, wing or bumper mount, cockpit);
    - *Orbit* (circles the target at a set radius and speed);
    - *Free* (the recorded hand or free-cam path).
  - Damping and offset sliders soften the lock, and a smooth blend runs when the lock target changes.
  - The focus can follow the same lock target automatically.
  - Locked shots are computed from the replayed target at every frame, not baked, so they stay correct
    after a clip is trimmed or moved. The export steps them frame-exactly like everything else.
- **Lenses and focus (`CameraLens`)**, on each camera, driven by Godot's `CameraAttributesPhysical`
  (focal length, focus distance and aperture produce real depth-of-field blur).
  - **Lens kit:** 14 / 24 / 35 / 50 / 85 / 135 mm primes on a 36×24 sensor, plus a 24–200 mm zoom.
    The focal length sets the FOV, the f-stop (f/1.4 – f/16) sets the blur.
  - **Focus points:** focus is a keyframed channel on the camera track. Each key is either a fixed distance or a
    **target**: an actor's head, any clip puppet, or a point picked in the world by click or VR pointer.
  - **Rack focus:** between two keys the focus eases from one point to the next over the key's duration.
    Autofocus mode follows the target continuously.
  - A **lens change** is itself a timeline key, so one camera can cut from a 24 mm wide to an 85 mm close-up.
  - The viewfinder and preview show the blur live, with an optional focus peaking overlay.
  - Depth of field needs the Forward+ or Mobile renderer, not Compatibility. The PS1 post-process runs after the DoF.
- `CutList`: (time → camera track) entries. `MovieCamera` follows the active cut and implements `IExportCamera`.
  `VideoExporter` is changed to depend on `IExportTimeline` + `IExportCamera` instead of `RacePlayback` + `PlaybackCamera`.
- `MocapReceiver`: a UDP OSC listener on a background thread with a tiny OSC parser and no new dependency.
  - It reads `/VMC/Ext/Root/Pos` and `/VMC/Ext/Bone/Pos` (Unity humanoid bone local rotations).
  - It hands a lock-free latest frame to the main thread.
- `MocapRetarget`: forward kinematics over the bone rotations, using this avatar's segment lengths, giving
  joint positions in the body frame. It then does the headset fusion and calibration.
- `HumanMeshBuilder`: a public `MocapPose` override. It fills `Rig` joints directly and skips the gait, like
  `ApplyVr` does for the wrists.
  - While mocap is live the mesh rebuilds once per frame. The pose key is quantized so stills still cache.
- `MovieStudioUi`: a `Ui/Screen` page in `UiTheme` glass style.
  - Contents: timeline with track lanes, play/pause/reverse, frame step, speed, record, take list,
    camera list, cut markers and export.
  - Mirrored on an `XrUi` panel in VR.
- **Entry:** a "Movie studio" button in `PauseScreen` (the buffer is grabbed on open) and a "Save clip" action.
  - The studio is a local overlay, so it also works during a multiplayer session: the puppets exist only locally.

## Verification
- `tools/test.sh quick` on every PR. No net tier is needed because the feature is offline and the wire protocol is unchanged.
- New headless check `--moviecheck` (`--world fixture`, output in `test_output/`):
  - drive a scripted actor (SyncProbe-style holds) through foot → plane, with the replay buffer running;
  - grab the buffer and check that the buffer wraps correctly past its length;
  - split and trim a clip;
  - seek forward, backward and to random times, and assert that the puppet transform and `RideKindId` match
    the recorded samples within ε;
  - save → load → identical samples.
- Lock check: a camera in Follow mode on a puppet's plane keeps the same offset in the plane's frame at
  random seek times, forward and backward. A Look-at camera keeps its position while pointing at the target.
- Lens check: per lens, the camera FOV matches the focal length and sensor. A rack-focus key between two
  actors moves the `FrustumFocusDistance` from one actor's distance to the other's.
- Export check: a 3-second `--moviecheck --export` produces an mp4 with the expected frame count.
  The GPX export check still passes.
- Mocap check: replay a recorded VMC UDP fixture file into `MocapReceiver` and assert the knee and ankle
  joint positions of the posed rig.
- Manual: screenshots of the timeline, the puppet replay and the VR viewfinder sent along the way.
  On-headset and webcam trials are done by the user.
