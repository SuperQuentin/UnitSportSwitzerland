# Floating origin

Status: **phases 1-3 done** (offline floating origin, #215; positions on the wire and the server
in LV95, in one PR). **Phase 4 is waiting on the Realistic styles** (#212): only the `--origin` pin
question could be settled now (see Phase 4). The working rules are in
`docs/notes/core/floating-origin.md` and `docs/notes/net/positions-on-the-wire.md`. Issue: #185.

## Why

Today the world has one fixed origin per process: `Core/WorldOrigin`, set once from
`manifest.json`'s `suggestedOriginLv95` (the centre of all tiles). All terrain math stays in double
LV95, and coordinates become `float` only in `WorldOrigin.ToWorld`. That is correct as far as it
goes, but everything Godot sees (nodes, physics, shaders, the network) is float32 world space
measured from that one point.

| Distance from origin | float32 step | Where that is |
|---|---|---|
| 4 km | 0.5 mm | what a floating origin keeps us under |
| 100 km | 7.8 mm | a large region |
| 175 km | 15.6 mm | edge of Switzerland, origin centred |
| 1,000 km | 62.5 mm | a multi-country world |
| 2,000 km | 125 mm | a continent-scale world |

The world is an open world that will grow to several countries (France is already merging in via
`FranceStage`). Things that will break, roughly in the order they appear:

- **Shading**: `ps1_terrain` and `ps1_tree` compute flat normals from `dFdx/dFdy(world_pos)`, so the
  normals get noisy far from the origin. Surface patterns (`fract(world_pos.x / vine_row_spacing)`,
  parking bays, mowing stripes) and water waves and sparkle all read raw world XZ.
- **Vertex jitter** on close geometry and on the camera. The PS1 snap hides some of it, but the
  planned Cartoon and Realistic styles (`docs/plans/visual-styles.md`) will not.
- **Physics**: Jolt contacts, wheel raycasts and character controllers at centimetre resolution.
  Vehicles at speed jitter first.
- **Network**: `FootPlayer.NetPos` and vehicle positions are float world space, and the server
  relays them. At 1,000 km a remote player's position is quantised to 6 cm on the wire, whatever
  the receiver does.

## Decisions

| Question | Decision |
|---|---|
| Floating origin or a double-precision Godot build? | **Floating origin.** See "Alternative considered". |
| What is the global coordinate? | **Double LV95 metres** (`E`, `N`, altitude), as the terrain already uses. Choosing a better projection for a multi-country world is a separate question (see "Out of scope"). |
| Which axes shift? | **Only horizontal (X/Z).** Altitude stays absolute: at most ~5 km on the ground and ~10 km for aircraft, which is under 1 mm per step. |
| Shift granularity | **Whole 1 km tiles.** Every delta is a multiple of 1000, which float represents exactly, so tile meshes placed at tile corners stay bit-exact and nothing drifts over many shifts. |
| When to shift | When the **active camera** is more than **3 km** horizontally from the origin (to be tuned). The new origin is the corner of the tile the camera is in. Teleports move the origin to the destination first. |
| Who has an origin? | **Every peer has its own**, client and server. Origins are never compared across the network. |
| What goes on the wire? | **Global positions only** (doubles, or tile + offset). A `Vector3` world position never crosses the network. Velocities and directions do not depend on the origin and stay `Vector3`. |

## Alternative considered: a double-precision Godot build

Godot can be compiled with `precision=double` (large world coordinates), which makes `Vector3` and
transforms 64-bit and renders relative to the camera. Rejected as the main approach because:

- It needs a custom engine, a custom editor, custom export templates for every platform
  (Windows, macOS, Linux and Android, see `docs/plans/android-client.md`) and a double build of the
  C# glue. That cost lands on every contributor and on `tools/release.sh` from then on.
- The GPU stays float32. Godot's camera-relative rendering fixes model and view matrices, but any
  shader maths on world positions still loses precision. All our world shaders do that (normals
  from derivatives, patterns, portal clipping, the horizon cover mask). We would need an
  origin-relative shader path anyway.
- It doubles the size of every `Vector3` in memory and on the wire, and does nothing about the
  network format. The network format has to change either way.

A floating origin gets the precision with stock Godot. A double build can still be added later on
top of it if a reason appears.

## Design

### 1. `WorldOrigin` becomes movable at runtime

`WorldOrigin` already exists and is injected into ~60 classes. It gains:

- `Shift(TileId newCorner)`: moves the anchor by whole tiles and raises one event,
  `Shifted(Vector3 delta)`, where `delta` is the world-space amount every position must move by
  (`old − new`, integer kilometres in X/Z, 0 in Y).
- `Rebase` (the existing "adopt the server's origin before anything is placed" path) goes away. A
  live shift does the same job without `ChunkManager.ResetAll` and without
  `ClientWorld.RespawnAfterRebase`.

**Make the shift a rigid transform from day one.** `Shifted` carries a `Transform3D` (identity
rotation for now), and subscribers apply the whole transform, not just `+ delta`. The follow-up
`docs/plans/spherical-world.md` adds a small rotation to each shift (the local "up" follows the
Earth's surface). Doing it this way now means the cached-position audit is done once, not twice.

A new **`GlobalPos`** value type (`double E, N, Alt`) is the origin-independent position, with
`WorldOrigin.ToWorld(GlobalPos)` and `ToGlobal(Vector3)`. Anything that **stores** a position
across frames should store a `GlobalPos`, or store a `Vector3` and subscribe to `Shifted`.

### 2. One shift, at one point in the frame

A new `Core/OriginShifter` node runs first in the frame (lowest process priority, before physics).
It checks the active camera, and when a shift is due it does everything **synchronously in one
call**, so no other code ever sees half the world moved:

1. Computes the new tile corner and calls `WorldOrigin.Shift`.
2. Moves every **top-level world node** by `delta`. These are the children of the world containers:
   tile roots, horizon blocks, players, vehicles, items, loot, birds, traffic, occasion decor and
   anything else spawned into world space. Nested nodes follow their parents for free. Nodes that
   must not move (UI, the `WorldEnvironment`, managers without a transform) are skipped by type or
   by group.
3. Raises `Shifted(delta)` for code that **caches** world positions in fields.
4. Updates the shader globals that depend on the origin (below).

Physics bodies are moved with their velocities preserved. For `RigidBody3D` and `VehicleBody` that
means writing the body state through `PhysicsServer3D` (or inside `_IntegrateForces`), not a plain
`Position =` that Jolt treats as a teleport and that would reset contacts. Phase 0 verifies this on a
car at 80 m/s.

### 3. Shaders

- New global uniform **`world_origin_offset`** (`vec2`): the origin modulo a fixed period
  `P` (for example 10 km, chosen so every pattern period divides it). Wherever a shader reads world
  XZ for a **pattern** (vineyards, bays, stripes, water waves, sparkle, noise), it uses
  `world_pos.xz + world_origin_offset` through one helper in a shared include. Patterns then stay
  put across shifts, and the sum stays small enough to be precise.
- Normals from `dFdx(world_pos)`, portal clipping and door lights stay in local world space. That
  is the point: near the camera, local values are small and precise.
- The **horizon coverage texture** (`HorizonLayer.SetCovered`, sampled by world XZ) and the terrain
  `cover_origin` must take the origin into account. Each one gets its uniform rewritten on
  `Shifted`.
- Per-frame globals already recomputed from current positions (`portal_clip_eye_*`,
  `portal_clip_plane_*`, `world_door_light_*`) need nothing beyond running after the shift.

### 4. Network: global positions on the wire

- `FootPlayer`'s replicated state becomes origin-independent. Two options to settle in Phase 2:
  - **Doubles**: `NetE`, `NetN` (a Godot `float` Variant is 64-bit) plus `NetAlt`. This is the
    simplest option. Cost: about 8 more bytes per state.
  - **Tile + offset**: `NetTile` (`Vector2I`) plus `NetPos` relative to that tile's corner. This
    keeps `Vector3` and its bandwidth, but both must arrive as one state. `NetTime` is already
    replicated last and its setter consumes the whole state, which allows this.
- Vehicles, items, loot, races (checkpoints) and NPCs: same rule for their replicated positions.
- RPCs that carry **positions** (`CombatManager.SendShot`'s muzzle, item drops, placements) switch to
  global. RPCs that carry velocities or directions are unchanged. The audit separates the two
  (~100 `Vector3` RPC parameters and synchronized properties today).
- `RemoteInterpolator` keeps its ring of states in `GlobalPos` and converts on output. A shift then
  needs no special case and cannot trigger its 25 m teleport snap.
- **Client and server no longer need the same origin.** The origin mismatch refusal in
  `ClientTerrainSync.Adopt` and the adopt-on-connect path disappear. The manifest's
  `suggestedOriginLv95` becomes only the starting origin.
- There is no protocol version check today. This is a breaking wire change, so either add a
  version handshake first or ship client and server together and say so in the release notes.

### 5. Server

The dedicated server does almost no physics: proxies have no collision, and parked vehicles sleep
where they are placed. Its players, however, can be thousands of kilometres apart, so it has no
"good" single origin. Rules:

- The server **relays global positions unchanged**, so it adds no precision loss.
- Server logic that measures or places things uses `GlobalPos` (double): `InterestService` ranges
  and line of sight over `horizon.bin`, `RaceNpcs` zones (600 and 900 m), `VehicleManager.Place`,
  loot spawning, and combat checks.
- The server's own Node3D positions for proxies may stay approximate, as long as nothing
  precision-sensitive reads them. The audit lists every server-side `GlobalPosition` read.
- Server terrain rings (coarse grids for height queries) are per tile and already
  origin-independent internally. The query API takes `GlobalPos`.

### 6. The cached-position audit

This is the bulk of the work. Today: 73 `ToWorld` callers, 29 `ToLv95` callers, ~520
`GlobalPosition` uses and ~60 classes holding a `WorldOrigin`. Each place that keeps a world
position **across frames** is a bug after a shift: it either stores `GlobalPos` or handles
`Shifted`. Known cases to start from:

- `Interiors/DoorIndex.SetTile` (tile corners in world space), door portals, interiors.
- `RemoteInterpolator` rings, camera smoothing and follow state, spectator camera.
- Traffic and trains (paths), race checkpoints, race NPC targets, GPX tracks and `TrackRibbon`.
- Birds (`BirdLife` flocks and targets), occasion creatures and decor.
- Particles in world space (`Explosion`, `FlagFx`, `PlayerFeel`): live particles would stay
  behind. Use local coordinates where possible, or accept a pop of the few that are alive.
- Audio: `EngineSynth` uses `DopplerTracking.PhysicsStep`, so a 3 km jump in one step is a
  doppler spike. Doppler must skip the frame of a shift.
- `ToLv95(Vector3)` on a vector saved **before** a shift returns the wrong place. This is the
  bug class to hunt.

A transient use (computed and used in the same frame) needs no change.

### 7. Tooling

- **`--originstress <metres>`**: shift at a tiny threshold (for example 50 m), so the origin moves
  every few seconds in normal play and every cached-position bug shows within a minute. Every
  phase is tested with it on.
- **`--origincheck`** headless self-check, in the style of `--interestcheck`: drive or teleport
  across many shifts and assert that global positions are continuous (< 1 mm jump), that
  velocities are kept, and that nothing is left behind.
- Performance overlay (F3, Detailed): current origin, camera distance from it, float step there,
  and shift count. Session perf log records shifts so hitches can be attributed.

## Phases

**Phase 0: measure and spike (throwaway).**
- Screenshots and a `--perflog` run at the edge of the current world (Geneva, Graubünden) against the
  centre, to show today's jitter and noisy normals.
- Spike: move a car at 80 m/s and a walking player across a forced shift with Jolt. Confirm that
  velocity, contacts and wheel state survive and the frame time stays within budget.
- Output: shift mechanism confirmed, threshold chosen, list of what pops.

**Phase 1: offline floating origin (client only).**
- `GlobalPos`, `WorldOrigin.Shift` and `Shifted`, `OriginShifter`, world containers, shader
  `world_origin_offset`, horizon and cover uniforms.
- The cached-position audit for everything that runs offline: player, vehicles, camera, terrain,
  horizon, trees, interiors, birds, traffic, occasions, GPX replay, particles and audio.
- `--originstress` and `--origincheck`.
- Done when: offline play with `--originstress 50` shows no visible pop, jump or doppler spike, on
  foot, driving, flying and in an interior.

**Phase 2: global positions on the wire.** Done.
- Replicated positions and position RPCs become global, and the interpolator works in `GlobalPos`.
  Remove the origin mismatch refusal and the adopt-on-connect rebase.
- Done when: the multiplayer test below passes.
- As built: positions are **doubles** (`GlobalPos`; lists as an anchor plus float offsets), bodies
  carry a `Net/NetPlace`, and connecting starts with a **version handshake** (`Net/Handshake`,
  protocol 2). Race courses carry their frame and `Follow` the receiver's shifts.

**Phase 3: server in global coordinates.** Done.
- Interest, NPC handoff zones, vehicle placement, loot and combat checks in `GlobalPos`. Audit
  server-side `GlobalPosition` reads.
- As built: the server measures with what players publish (`FootPlayer.Global`); interest judges
  each viewer's pairs in a frame anchored at the viewer; each race is built and checked in a frame
  at its host. The audit left only metre-scale checks between nearby bodies on the server's own
  world floats (door and seat reach, despawn ranges): 6 cm at 1,000 km does not matter to them.
  Server-simulated birds still fly in the server's world space, so far from its origin they move
  on a coarser float grid (6 cm at 1,000 km).
- Verified with the server's own origin 1,000 km away (`--server --origin E,N`, new) rather than
  players 1,000 km apart: the generated fill does **not** cover any LV95 coordinate, only 40 tiles
  round the spawn and the real set, so a server has no ground (birds, buildings, roads) for a player
  1,000 km off. Growing the fill round players is a terrain change (#27), not this one.
- Done when: two players 1,000+ km apart (the generated fill covers any LV95 coordinate) each see
  correct terrain and their neighbours, and the server log has no precision-related warnings.

**Phase 4: visual styles.** Waiting on the Realistic styles.
- Check the new styles against shifts: SDFGI cascades and reflection probes in Realistic+ (a shift
  may force them to relight; measure it), decals, texture UVs taken from world space (use
  `world_origin_offset`).
- This phase depends on `docs/plans/visual-styles.md`. Whichever lands second adapts to the other.
- Settled: `--origin E,N` (#212's pin for screenshot runs) pins the **starting** origin and the
  shifter runs from there; `ShotRunner` maps queued shots from that first frame, and
  `--originshift 1000000` keeps the origin still when a run needs it.

## Multiplayer test (required by the workflow)

Dedicated server plus two clients on loopback, all with `--originstress 50`:

1. Client A drives a car in a straight line across many shifts while client B watches from 200 m.
   On B, A moves smoothly with no pop, no 25 m snap and no stutter in the engine sound.
2. Both clients far from the manifest origin (for example 150 km), near each other: they see each
   other at the right place, and shots and hits land.
3. One client in a race with NPCs while shifting: checkpoints count, and NPC handoff works.
4. Client A teleports 300 km. B loses it from view, and A's world rebuilds around the new origin
   with no respawn hack.

## Risks

- **Missed cached positions**: shows up as things left behind or jumping by a kilometre multiple.
  The stress mode is the mitigation: `--origincheck` runs with the other headless self-checks,
  and the stress flag is on for every multiplayer test, not only for this feature.
- **Physics hitch** on shift: moving many bodies in one frame. Jolt handles this well in principle.
  Phase 0 measures it.
- **Concurrent features**: open PRs (#180 PvP combat, #169 walking in a bus, #148 steering wheel)
  add new positions on the wire and new cached positions. Land Phase 1 and 2 as early as possible,
  and add a note so new code uses `GlobalPos` from the start.
- **Wire break**: old clients cannot talk to new servers. See the protocol version point above.

## Out of scope

- **The global projection.** LV95 stretched over several countries distorts distances (about 1 %
  at 1,000 km from Bern). That is about which map projection the preprocessor and tile lattice use,
  not about float precision. Worth its own issue before the world grows past the neighbouring
  countries. `GlobalPos` is named so that the frame behind it can change.
- A double-precision Godot build (see above).
