# Road bike realism: the owner's Scott Addict RC 15 (2018) (#1124)

A road bike that rides like the real one in the **Sim** profile: the right speed for the watts,
gears, brakes that can pitch you over the bars, and steering that countersteers and wobbles at
walking pace. The first bike is the owner's own. The **Game** profile stays arcade.

## The bike and rider (decided with the owner, Oct 22 2026)

- **Scott Addict RC 15 (2018)**, HMX carbon frame, size **S**, black.
- **Shimano Ultegra Di2**, hydraulic **disc** brakes, **52/36** chainrings, **11-28** 11-speed
  cassette (11-12-13-14-15-17-19-21-23-25-28).
- About 8.0 kg (listings say 7.6-8.0).
- **Rider 120 kg.** Rider mass becomes a setting, not a constant.
- **To measure on the owner's bike**: wheelbase, head angle, fork offset, saddle height and
  setback, bar reach and drop, tyre size and pressure. Online listings mix model years, and the
  2018 size S geometry table was not found.
- **Assumed until measured**: 700×28c tyres (the disc RC 15's stock Schwalbe One), circumference
  2.136 m, 172.5 mm cranks.

## What is there

`Bicycle` (`src/Player/Bicycle.cs`) runs the power equation on `RiderWatts`. The trainer writes
into it (#901 / PR #969).

What is fixed today:
- 82 kg total mass, one CdA (0.32), one Crr (0.005), constant air density (1.20);
- no gears: cadence comes from a notional 6.2 m development, capped at 112 rpm;
- constant 5.2 m/s² braking;
- `Rideable.SteerByLean`: the input sets a bank, which eases in, and yaw = g·tanφ/v.
  There is no bar angle, no countersteer and no grip limit;
- the slipstream only works for cars and motorbikes, and only above 10 m/s.

`BikeMeshBuilder` is one generic 56 cm frame, recoloured per rider. `Motorbike` already has the
friction circle, the wheelie and stoppie limits, and a spec catalogue to copy.

## Phases

Status: 1 done (PR #1129), 2 done (PR #1130), 3 done (PR #1133), 4 done (PR #1134), each stacked on the one before.
2 differs from the plan: the position is chosen automatically, so there is no new key yet, and the
figure keeps the hoods pose. 3 differs too: the shifting is automatic only. Manual shift buttons
need their keyboard, pad and VR ways decided first. 4 is Sim only (Game keeps its arcade brakes),
has no wet roads (no rain in the game), and has one lever for both brakes (a rear-only brake would
be a new action).


1. **Bike catalogue.**
   - `BicycleSpec` + `BicycleCatalog`, as `MotorbikeSpec`: geometry, bike mass, wheel size and
     inertia, chainrings and cassette, brake type, CdA per position, look.
   - `BikeMeshBuilder` builds from the spec, and the rider is solved from its contact points.
   - A `[Showcase]` entry in the model viewer.
   - The Addict RC 15 is entry 0.
   - Rider mass in Settings → Movement, also shown in the training app.
2. **Longitudinal.**
   - `m = rider + bike + 1.5 kg kit`.
   - Wheel inertia as effective mass `I/r²` (~1 kg for both wheels).
   - Drivetrain efficiency 0.975.
   - Crr by `RideGround.Surface`: asphalt 0.0045, rough 0.008, cobbles 0.015, gravel 0.012-0.02,
     wet × 1.1.
   - CdA by position, scaled for a large rider: hoods 0.38, drops 0.34, tuck 0.30, out of the
     saddle 0.45. These are calibrated in validation.
   - ρ from altitude and temperature: `ρ = p/(R·T)`, with `p` from the barometric formula on the
     LV95 height.
   - Bike-to-bike draft: about 0.35 at 0.5 m, falling to 0 at 10 m, from 4 m/s.
   - Wind later.
3. **Drivetrain.**
   - The real 22 gears; cadence = v / (circumference × ring / sprocket).
   - Di2 shifting: automatic by default (keep 85-95 rpm), manual up/down as an option.
   - Keys and pad: power depends on cadence (a peak near 90 rpm, falling to 60% at 50 and 130 rpm).
   - With a trainer, the watts and cadence are the measured ones, and the legs turn at the
     sensor's cadence.
4. **Braking.**
   - Front/rear split, each limited by its tyre (μ: dry 0.9, wet 0.6, gravel 0.5).
   - The front is capped at the pitch-over limit `g·b/h` (about 0.56 g for this rider).
   - The rear locks and skids.
   - Disc brakes: no loss in the wet.
   - Braking takes lean away through the friction circle, as on the motorbike.
5. **Steering (largest change in feel).**
   - The linearised Whipple benchmark (Meijaard et al. 2007), fed with the Addict's geometry and
     masses: roll + steer, 4 states, stepped at the physics tick.
   - A rider controller turns the input (target lean or heading) into handlebar torque. That
     gives countersteer, wobble below the self-stable band (about 4-6 m/s) and stability at speed.
   - Lean is capped by grip: `tanφ ≤ √(μ² − (a/g)²)`.
   - The bar angle is drawn and added to the replicated pose.
   - `AutoPilot` (races, training app) and server bots drive it through the same controller.
6. **Rider physiology.** CP/W' (Skiba) for keys and pad, so a sprint drains you. Heart rate from
   power when there is no strap. Out of the saddle on ramps over 8%, which raises CdA and sways
   the bike.

## Validation

- **`--bikecheck`** (flat or fixture world, quick tier): the table above, stopping distance from
  50 km/h (dry and wet), never past the pitch-over limit, the gear chosen at 90 rpm, the lean
  limit in the wet.
- **The owner's own ride.** Take a recorded power-meter ride (FIT or GPX with power), replay its
  power on the same snapped roads, and compare speed per km and total time. CdA and Crr are
  calibrated here, not guessed.
- Phase 5: a steer-step response with countersteer visible; capsize and weave speeds against the
  benchmark's eigenvalues.

## Three devices and bots

- **Shift up/down and riding position** are new actions. Each needs keyboard, pad and VR decided
  in its phase (`xr/vr-action-map`).
- **Bots** ride through `RideControls`. Automatic shifting and the rider controller mean a bot
