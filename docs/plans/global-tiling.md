# Global tiling: one coordinate system for the whole Earth

Issue: #1443. Follows #187 (Earth curvature, drawn in the shaders) and comes before the Space phases of
`docs/plans/ellipsoid-handover.md` (#1430), whose section 10 outlined it.

## Why

The world is one flat LV95 plane: game X is east, −Z is north, Y is altitude, and terrain comes in
1 km LV95 tiles. That was right for Switzerland. Now the neighbours are being imported (France
#1399, plus German and Austrian heights), and the whole Earth is meant to follow. **Importing more
of it must be data work only: the coordinate system changes once, now.**

Measured across France with the rigorous LV95 chain (`tools/TerrainPreprocessor/DemProjection.cs`):

| | Paris | Marseille | Lille | Bordeaux | Brest | Ajaccio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| distance from Bern | 435 km | 438 km | 521 km | 665 km | 909 km | 569 km |
| LV95 scale error | 0.06 % | 0.20 % | 0.21 % | 0.05 % | 0.06 % | 0.39 % |
| grid north vs true north | 3.7° | 1.5° | 3.2° | 5.8° | 8.7° | 0.9° |
| roads and buildings off the terrain (`SwissProjection`'s polynomials) | 9.6 m | 11 m | 27 m | 70 m | 276 m | 23 m |

LV95 would stay usable to about 1,000 km once every conversion is rigorous, but not to Tokyo, and
not over the poles. The last row is a bug today; it is fixed by this plan's preprocessor (decided:
no interim LV95 fix, France is rebuilt once, in the new format).

## Decisions

| Question | Decision |
|---|---|
| The goal | Whole-Earth geodata is data work only. Switzerland and France are the first two regions. |
| Tiles hold | **True Earth positions.** The preprocessor converts every source sample to ECEF in doubles and stores the tile relative to its own anchor: the east-north-up (ENU) frame at the tile's centre on the ellipsoid, floats in metres. Neighbouring tiles share their edge points exactly. |
| Addressing | **A cube sphere** (decided with the user over a lat/lon quadtree): six faces, each a quadtree, with the **equal-angle** mapping (cells within ~1.4x in area everywhere). No singular point: a pole is an ordinary point of a face; at the 8 corners 3 cells meet at 120°, no pinching. The cube is **oriented with a face centred on Switzerland**, turned about that axis to put as many corners as possible in oceans (G1 computes it). Rejected: a lat/lon quadtree (poles pinch, as in Google Earth and MSFS) and lat/lon plus polar grids (a stitched ring seam). |
| Leaf size | **Level 14** (decided with the user on G1's numbers, below): ~0.6 km a side at a face centre, 512 cells of ~1.2 m. Against level 13 it costs the same to preprocess and store per area, and in a streaming flight it had a third of the hitches, a quarter of the worst commit (31 against 123 ms) and half the memory; it costs 4x the keys (~400 M land tiles for the Earth, bundled). Detail inside a tile (1 m in CH and FR, 30 m from Copernicus) is independent of its size. Parent levels serve the far view (they replace `horizon.bin`). |
| Collision | **Trimesh patches round bodies** (decided with the user on G1's numbers): the mesh's own triangles, ~128 x 128 cells (~150 m) a patch, built on workers round players, vehicles and bots. A tangent-plane `HeightMapShape3D` misses the 2 cm target on 1 m terrain at either level; a whole-tile trimesh is too heavy. |
| Far view outlines | Where no DEM exists, the coarse levels take land and sea from **Natural Earth** (public domain), as today's coarse Swiss geometry does for the horizon (the user, in G1); G2. |
| The game's frame | **Still one flat Cartesian frame, Y up**: #1430's anchor frame, used on the ground too. It is the ENU frame at an anchor near the camera; travelling re-anchors it with a small rotation (0.027° per 3 km). Physics, builders and gameplay keep their flat, Y-up world: within a few km of the anchor "up" tilts at most ~0.03° and the ground falls d²/2R below the plane (8 cm at 1 km, 70 cm at 3 km). |
| Curvature | **Geometry**: tiles are round because their points are true. #187's shader drop becomes the refraction part only: `world_curvature` = 1/2R_eff − 1/2R = −k/2R (a small lift), through the same `CURVED_CLIP` sites. The server's sight lines see the real bulge in the geometry. |
| Position type | `GlobalPos` (LV95 doubles) becomes an **Earth position in ECEF doubles** (H2's `EcefPos`, #1435), with geodetic and LV95 views. LV95 stays an input and display notation for Swiss data and UI. |
| Altitude | One helper, `AltitudeOf(world)`: height above the geoid (sea level) from the ECEF position and H2's geoid grid; near the anchor a fast path (Y plus the drop). Gameplay keeps metres above sea level. |
| North | Headings and yaws are stored and sent **relative to true north at the object** (its local ENU), not grid north. The sun comes from latitude and longitude (#1430's H6). |
| The wire | Single positions as ECEF doubles (three doubles, as LV95 today); orientations and velocities in the body's local ENU, so every peer's own anchor is irrelevant. Anchored point lists carry the anchor's full frame. One protocol bump. |
| Saves | A schema version and a one-time rigorous LV95 → ECEF migration of the 14 stores that hold LV95. |
| Migration | **Incremental on `main`** (decided with the user): seams first, each a small PR with no behaviour change, checked by #1429's regression net; the switch to cube tiles is a short last step. No long-lived branch against parallel work. |
| France now | Fixed by G1's preprocessor, not before: one French rebuild. |

## Findings while starting G0 (2026-11-03)

- **The anchor frame is the exact ENU frame at the anchor**, so near it +X is east, −Z true north,
  +Y up and Y the altitude, to within the tilt and the ground's fall (0.03° and 70 cm at 3 km).
  `SunPath`, the compass, the snow line, air density, the altimeter and gravity keep working as
  they are: they need no helper. What breaks is long distances, stored data and the wire.
- **`GlobalPos` is already a good seam**: its distances (`DistanceTo` in 15 files,
  `HorizontalDistanceTo` in 20) become Earth chord and geodesic distances inside it with no caller
  change; only its two operators (5 files) need a frame for their axes. The work is the code that
  bypasses it: raw `e, n` arithmetic (66 hand-written `de*de + dn*dn`, offsets like `e + dx`) and
  the 61 records holding LV95.
- **LV95 as a notation survives far out**: the rigorous projection is exact wherever it is
  defined (Brest has exact LV95 coordinates), and only treating `e, n` as Euclidean metres breaks.
  But it diverges 90° from Bern's central line, so it cannot hold the whole Earth: storage moves to
  the Earth position type all the same.
- **The server** (not in the audit) keeps one world frame that never shifts, because its players
  can be anywhere, and builds collision round its bots within 300 m (`bots/server-collision`).
  With tiles in true positions, one flat frame cannot serve players 500 km apart: far tiles would
  sit tilted in it and floats lose centimetres. **G2 needs physics in several local frames on the
  server**, one per region with players or bots in it (Godot physics spaces per region), and
  measuring done there in Earth positions. A G2 prerequisite of its own (G2a below).
- **The tile makers ask the key too** (G0c, `src/Terrain`, PRs #1482 and the makers' PR): the
  generated world, the fixture courses and the fill keep sampling their LV95 source (noise, relief,
  network) but ask each tile where its points are (`Local` / `Global`) and which LV95 box it covers
  (`Lv95Box`, `Touches`), so they can fill a cube tile in G2 without a rewrite. Grid-shaped and left
  for G2: the generator's lattice windows start at the box's south-west corner, the fill's domain and
  the horizon's coverage texture are `TileRect`s of LV95 keys, and the generator's road network
  (`ProceduralWorld.Network.cs`, a ratchet seam) is LV95 throughout.
- **LV95 has a seam on Bern's antimeridian** (found by #1430 H5, #1480, 2026-11-04): along a polar 400 km
  orbit from Bern, 100,001 of 200,000 samples came back from LV95 → ECEF more than 1 cm off, worst 31 km,
  all past the pole. Two causes in `SwissGeodesy.FromBessel`'s `Alpha·(lon − Λ0)`:
  - the angle was not wrapped: from 172.6° W to 180° it read −187° to −180°, so that whole 7.4° band was
    wrong (the orbit flew down its edge). **Fixed in #1480** (`FromCentre`), bit-identical inside ±π;
  - the Gauss conformal sphere's longitude is α ≈ 1.00072 times the ellipsoid's, so it covers 2πα: within
    ~0.13° either side of 172.56° W (~15 km wide at the equator) two places share one LV95 point. This is the
    projection itself. `SwissGeodesy.InDomain` names the sliver, and `FromEcef` reports a point in it
    (`OutsideDomain`, logged by the game) instead of landing it 31 km off quietly.
  - **Consequence for G2**: `GlobalPos` cannot stay LV95 inside, for the whole Earth. It must become ECEF
    or cube-keyed; the wire, interest and the region planner with it. Until then a body crossing the
    sliver is misplaced for its peers for a second or two (an orbit), and the log says so.

## G1 results (2026-11-03, #1452, PR #1455)

Code and commands: `docs/notes/tools/cube-tiles.md`. Measured on the local region (825 km² of
swissALTI3D, Geneva to Valais) plus RGE ALTI samples (63 km² on the Geneva border, 9 km² in Paris,
9 km² in Brest, fetched from the Géoplateforme while the NAS was out of reach).

- **Orientation**: face 0 on 46.95108° N 7.43863° E, twisted 28.5°: all eight corners in oceans,
  the nearest 151 km from a 1:110m coast. The North Pole is an ordinary point of face 0, the South
  Pole of face 2. Pinned by `CubeSphereTests`.
- **Mapping**: the cube direction is the geodetic normal itself (no iteration for lat/lon; a height
  moves the point along it). Round trips under 0.1 mm everywhere, poles and edges included.
- **Exact edges**: shared lattice points are bit-identical from every face (tested to level 23);
  in the built regions every pair of built neighbours holds identical edge heights (1.19 M edge
  vertices at level 13, 2.38 M at 14, 0 differ). The tiler computes edge vertices rigorously and
  only the interior from control points.
- **Accuracy**: the 17 × 17 control-point resampling is within 0.28 mm of the exact one; mesh
  vertices are within 5.3 mm of their rigorous ground point (target 1 cm), the 1 cm height quantum
  being most of it.
- **Height encoding**: 1 cm quanta, planar prediction, varints, Brotli: 111 KB a level-14 tile,
  290 MB for the region (today's LV95 `.terr`: 1.6 GB at 7 cm). A per-tile offset and scale over
  16 bits would miss 1 cm on any tile spanning over 1,310 m.
- **Heights' datum**: ellipsoidal (WGS84) from LHN95 through `SwissGeodesy`; French IGN69 heights
  plus dz are taken as LHN95 (as the LV95 build does), so the border is continuous but absolute
  heights far from Switzerland are off by the Swiss geoid's edge value against the real one
  (metres). G3 needs a geoid for each source (RAF20 for France, EGM2008 elsewhere).
- **France fixed**: BD TOPO placed from its own lat/lon on the vertex normal: on the terrain by
  construction (median 1 cm from the 1.2 m mesh below). `SwissProjection` had them 9.7 m off in
  Paris and 276 m in Brest (0.2 m by Geneva).
- **Level 13 against 14** (heights only, warm cache, 16 threads):

  | | Level 13 | Level 14 |
  |---|---|---|
  | tiles in the region | 709 | 2,547 |
  | heights a tile / in all | 391 KB / 284 MB | 111 KB / 290 MB |
  | preprocessing | 113 s (2.5 CPU-s a tile) | 119 s (0.75 CPU-s a tile) |
  | flight 95 km at 200 m/s, 4 km radius: hitches > 33 ms | 22 | 7 |
  | worst main-thread commit / worker build p95 | 123 / 313 ms | 31 / 86 ms |
  | draw calls max / working set | 17 / 1.2 GB | 51 / 0.6 GB |

  Features (swissTLM3D roads and footprints, BD TOPO) add ~1 s a tile today, mostly GeoPackage
  and WFS reads; peak memory ~11 GB with a 300-tile source cache.
- **Collision** (`--cubeharness`, Jolt, rays against the mesh): a tangent-plane height field
  (1024² at 1.2 m) misses: median 3-22 mm, p99 0.1-1.3 m, 11-53 % of rays over 2 cm (rough 1 m
  terrain sampled twice at 1.2 m disagrees). The mesh's own triangles match (median under 2 mm;
  the rare misses are cliffs where the projected mesh folds), at 6 s and 185 MB a level-13 tile,
  1.1 s and 25 MB a level-14 tile, 50-60 ms and 1-3 MB a 128-cell patch. Hence the patches.
- **Natural Earth**: the 1:110m land, used to choose the twist, also gives the far view its
  outlines where no DEM exists (G2).

## Design

### 1. The cube sphere

- **Mapping**: equal-angle gnomonic. A face point (u, v) in [−1, 1]² gives the direction
  `normalize(face_axis + tan(u·π/4)·right + tan(v·π/4)·up)` in the cube's frame, turned by the
  orientation into ECEF; the point on the ellipsoid along the geodetic normal comes from H2's
  `Geodetic`. A quadtree cell at level L is a square of side 2/2^L in (u, v).
- **Orientation**: one fixed rotation, chosen once and never changed (it is part of every tile's
  address): a face centred on Bern, turned about that axis to put the corners in oceans. Stored as
  constants in `TerrainFormat` with a unit test that pins it.
- **Tile keys**: (face, level, x, y), printed as `F<face>/L<level>/<x>_<y>` with the latitude and
  longitude of its centre in every tool's output. Neighbour lookup across a face edge turns the
  axes by 90°; at a corner a cell has three neighbours round it. Written once, unit-tested
  exhaustively (every edge and corner at several levels).
- **Edges and corners are not seams**: two faces subdivide their shared edge identically, so edge
  vertices coincide exactly (the same ECEF point from either face).

### 2. The tile format

- **Heights** on the cell's (u, v) grid, as ellipsoidal heights (the geometry). The geoid (H2's
  grid) is applied for display and gameplay altitudes only. The encoding replaces today's ushort
  band (−50..4900 m): G1 measures a per-tile offset plus scale against the 1 cm target.
- **Positions in the file** relative to the tile anchor's ENU frame, floats: within 1 km of the
  anchor the float step is under 0.1 mm.
- **Everything per tile in its own frame**: roads, buildings, trees, water, cover, photos,
  traffic endpoints. Builders mesh in the tile frame and keep a local up, so they change only in
  where their frame comes from.
- **Rendering mesh**: vertices at the cube grid's true points, so neighbours meet exactly.
- **Collision**: trimesh patches of the mesh's own triangles round bodies (~128 × 128 cells each,
  built on workers). G1 measured the tangent-plane `HeightMapShape3D` this bullet first proposed:
  it misses 2 cm on 1 m terrain (G1 results above).
- **Patterns** (`pattern_xz`, waves, cover noise) stop coming from `world_origin_offset`: under a
  rotating frame they would jump at every re-anchor (0.027° is 5 cm at 100 m). They come from
  per-vertex coordinates baked in the tiles (the face's (u, v) in metres, continuous within a
  face), with a 3D-noise or triplanar blend across face edges. G2 checks a re-anchor shows no jump
  in the reference shots.
- **Packing**: the whole Earth's land is ~100 M tiles at level 13 and ~400 M at level 14, so
  tiles are packed into bundle files indexed by key (one bundle per parent cell at a fixed level),
  not one file per kind per tile as today.

### 3. The game's frame on the ground

- `WorldOrigin` / `OriginFrame` gain #1430's anchor frame: an ECEF origin and a basis. The ground
  frame is the anchor frame; `ToWorld` is R⁻¹·(p − O) in doubles, then cast.
- `OriginShifter` re-anchors past a distance from the anchor (3 km today; G2 measures 3 against
  10 km: fewer shifts, a larger tilt at the edge, 0.09° at 10 km). The shift is a rigid
  `Transform3D` with a small rotation, through the existing `IOriginShiftAware` handlers.
  `Apply(Aabb)` gets a correct rotated form.
- Physics: gravity is −Y of the anchor frame (≤ 0.03° off at 3 km, felt by nothing).
  CharacterBodies keep `up_direction` +Y. Long downward rays far from the anchor (birds, aircraft,
  probes) use the local up at their point.
- A tile node's transform is one rigid transform: tile ENU → ECEF → anchor, composed in doubles,
  set when the tile loads and on every re-anchor (a re-anchor moves every node, as a shift does
  today).

### 4. Streaming and the far view

- `ChunkManager` rings become quadtree rings: leaves near the camera, parent levels farther out,
  chosen by on-screen size (#1434's `world_lod_scale` already picks detail that way).
- `horizon.bin` and `HorizonLayer`'s 10 km blocks are replaced by the coarse quadtree levels: one
  pyramid from the leaves to the whole globe, which is also #1430's H4 globe. Where no DEM exists
  yet, Natural Earth's land and sea outlines shape it (decided in G1). Decided: H4 builds its
  coarse levels and their renderer first, in G1's format and by G1's tiler, and G2 extends the
  same renderer down to the leaves.
- Data-source borders (CH/FR today, Copernicus elsewhere) keep the edge masks and blends the
  French import already has, now on the cube grid.

### 5. Positions, the wire, saves

- `GlobalPos` keeps its name at first and changes its inside to ECEF doubles, with `.Lv95`,
  `.Geodetic` and `AltitudeOf`; its `+`/`−` go through the current anchor frame. The 254 files of
  raw `e, n` move to it area by area (G0).
- The wire: as in the Decisions table. `Handshake.Protocol` bumps once, at the switch.
- Saves: each of the 14 LV95 stores gets a version; loading an old one converts it rigorously.

### 6. North, the sun, maps

- Stored headings (givers, traffic endpoints, berths, placed objects, sleepers) are converted to
  true north by the meridian convergence at their point (0.9° at Geneva, 0 at Bern).
- `SunPath` from latitude and longitude: #1430's H6 (the real sun, already decided there).
- Maps keep their own projection: LV95 for the Swiss map, a local conformal one elsewhere, never
  the world frame.

### 7. The preprocessor

- One cube-sphere tiler fed by every source through the rigorous geodesy module: swissALTI3D and
  swissTLM3D (LV95 → ETRS89 → ECEF), RGE ALTI and BD TOPO (Lambert-93 / geographic), Copernicus
  GLO-30 and OSM elsewhere.
- Resampling per sample: exact projection at ~17 × 17 control points per tile, interpolated in
  between (errors under 1 mm, ~10 ns a sample). France is already resampled today (Lambert-93 to
  LV95, ~3.3 s of CPU per 1 m tile); the cube adds nothing in kind.
- Sizing: the land at 30 m is ~1,100 samples per tile against a million for 1 m data; the height
  pass for every continent is about 150 CPU-hours, about as long as reading Copernicus (~650 GB).
- Licences: Copernicus DEM free with attribution; OSM ODbL (attribution, share-alike on the
  database); IGN open licence. The in-game credits list them.

## Tests

- **Unit**: the cube mapping round trip (< 0.1 mm), neighbour lookup at every edge and corner and
  across levels, the orientation constant, geodesy against swisstopo and IGN reference points,
  the save migrations.
- **Seams**: a check that loads tiles on both sides of a face edge, a corner, and a data border,
  and compares shared vertices (exact) and collision heights (2 cm).
- **The regression net (#1429)** after every G0 step (no change) and at G2 (global against LV95).
- **Re-anchoring**: `--originstress` with rotation (every handler sees a non-identity basis), and
  reference shots on both sides of a re-anchor (no pattern jump).
- **Net** (`tools/test.sh net`): two clients with different anchors see each other, vehicles
  and items at the right place and orientation across a re-anchor.
- **Far places**: a Brest flight, a pole flight on a coarse test world, a drive over a face edge
  in a fixture placed on one.

## Risks

- **Size**: ~250 files of `e, n` and ~340 of `TileId`. Incremental seams keep it reviewable, but
  it is months, not weeks; areas must not be half-moved at the G2 switch.
- **Parallel work**: new code written against LV95 during G0 adds to the migration. Once G0b/G0c
  land, new code uses the seams (a note and a check that flags new raw `TileId` arithmetic).
- **Collision patches**: G1 found the separate height field misses 2 cm, so G2 builds trimesh
  patches round bodies (50-60 ms, 1-3 MB each): the server's bots need them too (G2a).
- **Every `OriginShift` handler meets a rotation for the first time** (as in #1430).
- **Data volume and licences** for the whole Earth: packing, a CDN or download-on-demand
  (`docs/plans/in-game-map-downloader.md`), attribution.

## Out of scope

- The Space regime, orbits, the atmosphere from above: #1430's H4-H7.
- Content for new regions (buildings detail, POIs, traffic data beyond OSM).
- A double-precision Godot build (rejected in `docs/plans/floating-origin.md`).
