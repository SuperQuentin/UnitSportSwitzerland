# Three clocks: environment time, simulation speed, real time

Status: **phases 1-4 built** (PR #594). Tracking issue: #579.

| Phase | State | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 `RealClock` | done | An unscaled wall clock and frame delta, so the real-time bucket survives a time scale |
| 2 Server-owned sim clock | done | `SimClock`: the server owns `(sim0, epoch, scale)`; every peer flips `Engine.TimeScale` at the same server instant |
| 3 Env time onto sim time | done | `WorldClock` re-keyed from `ServerNow` to `SimNow`, with a monotonic persisted `EnvNow` |
| 4 Move the misplaced timers | done | The ~10 simulation timers currently on the wall clock move to sim or env time |
| 5 Guards | not done | A lint check for wall-clock use in gameplay, and two-peer probes for sim speed and env scaling |

## Goal

Every timed system in the game reads one of exactly three clocks, and the two that describe the
world are owned by the server so that every peer agrees.

- **Environment time** — the world's own pace: time of day, traffic and railway signals, plant and
  build growth, campfire burn, craft stations, resource regrowth, fishing odds, the dawn chorus,
  clocks on walls.
- **Simulation speed** — everything that follows physics: vehicles, player movement, projectiles,
  weapon rate of fire, AI and NPCs, ragdolls, damage windows.
- **Real time** — UI, audio presentation, network plumbing, persistence stamps, force feedback,
  waiting on worker threads. Per-process, never synced, never scaled.

Out of scope: per-player time scales, rewind or replay.

## The two decisions, and the maths they imply

**Environment time follows simulation speed.** At 0.25× the cars crawl *and* the sun, the traffic
lights and the crops crawl with them. The world stays internally consistent: signal-following AI
keeps agreeing with the signals it follows, which it would not if the lights cycled at full rate
past a crawling car. The cost is that environment time is no longer a function of elapsed real
time, so it cannot be derived from `ClockSync.ServerNow` the way it is today — it needs a shared
simulated clock underneath it, which is why phase 2 now comes before phase 3.

**Simulation speed is applied as `Engine.TimeScale` on every peer.** `GameClock.Now` is
`Engine.GetPhysicsFrames() / Engine.PhysicsTicksPerSecond`, so it already reads simulated seconds
under a time scale, and every `delta`-driven gameplay timer already follows. Gameplay churn for
that phase is approximately zero. The price is collateral damage on the real-time bucket, which
phase 1 pays off first.

### Layered, not parallel

The two world clocks stack, each the same epoch-based shape `WorldClock` already uses:

```
SimNow(serverNow) = sim0 + (serverNow - simEpoch) * scale        // rebased on each /speed
EnvNow(simNow)    = env0 + (simNow - envEpoch) * dayFactor       // rebased on each /time speed
Hour              = Wrap(EnvNow / 3600)
dayFactor         = 86400 / (minutesPerDay * 60) = 1440 / minutesPerDay
```

Keying env to `SimNow` rather than to `ServerNow` is what makes this cheap: **a simulation-speed
change needs no environment rebase at all.** Env time rides whatever sim time does, automatically,
and only a day-length change (`/time speed`) rebases the env layer. Collapsing the two into one
clock at an effective rate of `scale * dayFactor` would be slightly simpler to implement but loses
`SimNow` as a thing peers can compare, which phase 4 needs for replicated timers.

Both layers keep the #452 properties: nothing is summed frame by frame, so nothing drifts; a late
joiner handed both tuples computes the same hour as everyone else; and the trip over the wire does
not move either clock.

Sanity check at the defaults (`DayLengthMinutes = 24`, so `dayFactor = 60`): at 1× env advances 60
env-seconds per real second and a day takes 24 real minutes, as today. At 0.25× it advances 15, and
a day takes 96 real minutes.

### `SimNow` versus `GameClock.Now`

Two simulated-seconds clocks that must not be confused:

- `SimNow` is authoritative-by-formula and comparable across peers. Anything whose timing crosses
  the wire uses it.
- `GameClock.Now` is this peer's *actual* physics progress. A machine that drops physics frames
  falls behind `SimNow`. Purely local gameplay timers use it, because it matches what the physics
  actually did.

They differ by a constant while a peer keeps up, and they diverge when it does not. Phase 2 should
print the gap on the server's stats line so the divergence is visible rather than mysterious.

## Where we start

- `src/Core/GameClock.cs` (#461) is the local simulation clock already, and already respects both
  `Engine.TimeScale` and the quick tier's `--fixed-fps`.
- `src/World/WorldClock.cs` (#452) is the environment clock and has the right *shape* — server owns
  `(hour0, epoch, minutesPerDay)`, admin-gated `/time`, sent on join and on change, persisted across
  a dedicated server's restart — but it is keyed to `ClockSync.ServerNow`, which is real time. Under
  this decision its derivation changes; the epoch machinery and the wire format largely survive.
- `ClockSync.LocalNow` / `ServerNow` / `ServerUnixNow` is the real clock, plus `Time.GetTicksMsec`
  directly in many places.

Missing: a server-owned simulation speed of any kind (`DebugMenu.SetTimeScale` is local and refuses
outright when online), and about ten simulation timers that read the wall clock instead of either
world clock.

## Phase 1 — `RealClock`, and fixing the real-time bucket

`Engine.TimeScale` scales `_Process` and `_PhysicsProcess` delta for the whole process, and Godot
offers no unscaled per-frame delta. So before a time scale can be switched on in anger, anything
that must stay real needs a source a scale cannot touch.

- Add `src/Core/RealClock.cs`: `Now` (monotonic seconds, `Time.GetTicksUsec`) and `Delta`, the
  unscaled frame delta, measured once a frame from `Time.GetTicksUsec` deltas rather than taken
  from the engine.
- Fix `ClockSync._Process`: it accumulates its ping period from scaled `delta`, so at 0.1× pings
  would drop from every 2 s to every 20 s and the clock sync would degrade exactly when both world
  clocks depend on it. It moves to `RealClock.Delta`.
- Audit the other `delta`-driven network and UI cadences for the same bug. The ones that already
  read `Time.GetTicksMsec` directly (`Handshake`, `InterestService`, `ServerStats`, `Swarm`, the
  chat rate limit) are safe and stay as they are.
- Decided while building: **UI chrome ignores the scale** (`SetIgnoreTimeScale(true)` on the menu
  and HUD tweens). Most HUD animation here is absolute wall-clock phase
  (`Mathf.Sin(Time.GetTicksMsec() / 160f)` for the BR ping bob, the zone pulse, camera shake, cloth
  sway), which a scale never touched and which needed no change at all; the only real exposure was
  Godot `Tween`s, which `Engine.TimeScale` does scale. A sluggish menu is not a useful signal that
  slow motion is on. That is also why phase 1's `RealClock` needs no `Delta`.

No behaviour change at scale 1. Quick tier.

## Phase 2 — Server-owned simulation speed

- A `SimClock` owning `(sim0, simEpoch, scale)`, broadcast as a tuple plus an `applyAtServerNow`.
  Applying on receipt instead would let peers flip at different instants and rubber-band the
  replicated bodies for the width of the RPC spread; scheduling the flip at a future `ServerNow`
  makes every peer change at the same server instant.
- Peers set `Engine.TimeScale` when `ServerNow` reaches `applyAt`, and rebase `sim0` there so
  `SimNow` stays continuous across the change.
- Sent on join as well as on change, like `WorldTime` in `ChatManager.Handshake`.
- A `/speed` chat command, admin-gated, mirroring `/time speed`: `/speed 0.25`, `/speed 1`,
  bounded the way `TimeCommand` bounds day length.
- `DebugMenu`'s existing Time ×0.1–×4 picker routes through the same path instead of setting
  `Engine.TimeScale` directly, and stops being offline-only. Offline, "the server" is this process,
  so it applies immediately.
- Not persisted: a restarted server comes back at 1×.
- `--fixed-fps` must not combine with a scale ≠ 1; the quick tier runs at 1× and should assert it.
- **Scale 0 is not a pause.** It would freeze env time, growth and the day, and it is a different
  feature with different requirements (input, UI, net keepalive). Bound the scale away from 0;
  stopping the day stays `/time speed 0`, which zeroes `dayFactor` and leaves the simulation running.

Replicated state, so **tier 2** (`tools/test.sh net`), checking the scale on the **remote** peer.

## Phase 3 — Environment time onto simulation time

- `WorldClock` re-keyed: `EnvNow(SimNow)` per the formula above, with `Hour` derived from `EnvNow`
  instead of being the stored quantity. `TimeCommand.HourAt` gains the sim layer; `Advance` and
  `Wrap` are unchanged and stay unit-tested.
- `EnvNow` is monotonic and unwrapped, so growth and burn get a counter that does not reset at
  24 h. `Hour` stays for the sky.
- Persist `EnvNow` in `user://world_clock.cfg` next to `minutes_per_day`, replacing the saved
  wrapped `hour`, which loses the day count. On load, `env0 = saved` and `envEpoch = SimNow(now)`.
- Built differently from the plan: `/time set` and `/time add` move a **`HourShift`**, they do not
  rebase the counter. The plan would have let `/time set 3` at 22:00 wind `EnvNow` back nineteen
  hours, which is fine for a sky and wrong for everything phase 4 is about to hang off it — a
  campfire lit ten env-minutes ago would have become one lit in the future. Splitting the monotonic
  counter from the admin-movable hour was cheap to do now, with no consumers to break, and the trap
  would have been expensive to find in phase 4. `/time speed` still rebases the env layer; `/speed`
  rebases only the sim layer.
- The wire semantics changed, so `Handshake.Protocol` goes 17 -> 18.
- A `docs/notes/core/three-clocks.md` note with the bucket rule, the layering, and the table of
  which system is in which — replacing the looser wording in `docs/notes/general/fast-checks.md`.

**The unit trap, worth stating loudly:** at the default day length environment time runs 60× sim
time. A duration moved from the wall clock into env time must be re-expressed in env units, never
copied. `CampfireClock.BurnSeconds` is `20 * 60` *real* seconds today; read as env seconds at the
default day length that is a 20-second fire. Each constant moved in phase 4 is re-tuned as a
fraction of a day, not renamed.

Tier 2: the hour is replicated state, and the point of the phase is that two peers agree.

## Phase 5 — Guards

- A lint-style unit check: no file under the gameplay directories reads `Time.GetTicksMsec`,
  `Time.GetTicksUsec` or `Time.GetUnixTimeFromSystem` unless it is on an explicit allow-list with a
  reason. This is the only thing that stops the three buckets rotting back into one.
- A two-peer probe that the simulation scale matches on both peers after a `/speed`, and that a
  replicated body's position does not jump across the flip.
- A two-peer probe that the **hour advances at `scale * dayFactor`**: the whole point of the flipped
  decision, and the thing a future refactor is most likely to break silently.
- A probe that `EnvNow` survives a server restart and that nothing mid-growth resets.

## Risks

- **`Engine.TimeScale` is a blunt instrument.** It scales audio pitch, particle systems and
  animation players too. Phase 1 can only fix what reads a delta; anything the engine scales
  internally will slow whether we want it to or not. Phase 2 should be tried at 0.25× and 4× with a
  careful look at audio and particles before it is called done.
- **Errors now compound through the layers.** A peer with a stale sim tuple gets the wrong hour as
  well as the wrong speed, where before those were independent. Both tuples go out reliably on join
  and on change; the probe in phase 5 is what proves it.
- **Scale ≠ 1 online is new ground for replication.** `FootPlayer` net timestamps are real time
  while bodies move in simulated time. With a uniform scale on every peer this stays consistent;
  the transition window is the risk, which is why the flip is scheduled rather than immediate.
- **Re-tuning env durations is a gameplay change, not a refactor.** Phase 4's env moves will make
  fires and regrowth feel different. Worth a separate commit from the mechanical ones so it can be
  reverted on its own.
