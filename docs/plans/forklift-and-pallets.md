# A drivable forklift, and pallets you can pick up

Status: **planned.** Issue #583, the last bullet of #496's original request and part of the #580
tracking issue. Deferred out of #496 on purpose, because the forklift is the easy half: *making
pallets stop being furniture* is the feature.

A `FurnitureType.Forklift` already stands in every warehouse and works hall (#497). It is a box.
`FurnitureType.Pallet` stands beside it, also a box, and both are welded into the one merged mesh
the interior is drawn from. The target is a player who walks into a warehouse, gets into the
forklift that is standing there, drives it at a pallet, raises the forks, drives out through the
loading bay (#528) with the pallet on the forks, and puts it down in the yard — with a second
player watching all of it happen.

## Where we start from

These seven facts decide every trade-off below. All were read out of the code, not assumed.

1. **`Airstairs` (RideKind 126) is a forklift with a different top half.** A slow truck whose
   entire extra state is `Height` / `TargetHeight`, eased by `Lift(dt)` at a fixed rate; the
   height rides in `WritePose().X` for remote copies and in `VehicleState.Flags` (centimetres) for
   a parked one, and `PackFlags` / `UnpackFlags` are six lines each. It is not a `HeavyCatalog`
   entry — it needs none of the heavy driveline — but it *is* drawn as a `HeavyRig`, so the
   steering wheel in the driver's hands, the wheels, the dials, first person, the mirrors and the
   driver seen from outside all came free. A mast is that same machinery pointed up.
2. **Wiring a new mount in is four lines.** `Rideable.For(kind)`, `VehicleState.CreateRide`,
   a `RideUi` card, and the `RideKind` value itself. `RideKind` 193 is the next free number
   (120 trailer, 121-123 boats, 124 pigeon, 125-128 aircraft, 129-192 the second motorbike range).
3. **Interiors live 3 km under their building** (`InteriorManager.InteriorBaseY`), reached through
   a `DoorLink`'s rigid map, and a vehicle drives through a loading bay and across that portal
   already — `tools/baycheck.sh` (#531) does exactly that on the **generated world**, so a check
   can reach a site without the 100 GB tile download. Interior space is still *world* space, so one
   LV95-plus-altitude record identifies a spot inside a hall as well as one in the yard (#185).
4. **The interior is one merged mesh**, furniture included (`InteriorMeshBuilder.Build` walks
   `l.Furniture` into the same `Scratch`). But there is already a precedent for carving one
   furniture index out of it and giving it its own node: **`InteriorManager.AddLockDoors`** builds a
   `Node3D`/`MeshInstance3D` per locked container so its door can swing. A liftable pallet is the
   same shape of exception, and it needs **no change to the plan format and no plan-version bump**.
5. **Loose pallets are few.** `Aisles` puts up to two pallets or drum stacks in each aisle lane,
   `Line` a handful of stock pallets at the ends of its conveyor runs: order 6-20 per hall, not the
   108 racking bays. Per-pallet nodes are affordable; instancing is not needed.
6. **`SiteYards.For(tile, roads, grid)`** already gives, per industrial site, the yard rect in front
   of the wall the door is on, its heading and its ground height, as a pure function of the tile's
   bytes — and `DormantSlots.ForSite` already fills it with a fleet. A deterministic pallet
   population is a second consumer of the same rect, and a forklift standing in the yard is one
   more entry in the fleet's mix once it is a `RideKind`.
7. **`PlacedObjects`** (`World/Placed`) is the nearest existing home for a world object: server-owned,
   LV95-positioned, payload, per-kind visual factory, reach and ownership checks, snapshot on join.
   It is **not** the right home here — see "Why pallets get their own service".

## The decisions, taken up front

| Question the issue asks | Decision |
|---|---|
| Does a lifted pallet keep its load? | **Yes.** `InteriorMeshBuilder.Goods` already draws boxes, drums or sacks from a hash; that hash becomes a **load byte** carried with the pallet. A pallet of drums stays a pallet of drums. One byte, and the alternative — every lifted pallet turning into a bare pine deck — looks like a bug. |
| Are the pallets already in a plan convertible, or must forkliftable ones be placed separately? | **Both, and they are convertible.** The loose floor pallets the hall already plans become their own nodes (fact 4) and are liftable; *and* a deterministic population is seeded in the yard (fact 6), because a forklift that can only work indoors is half a forklift. Pallets **on racking** stay drawn inside the `PalletRack` piece and are not liftable. |
| Who owns a pallet in flight? | **Nobody — it is part of the forklift.** While carried, a pallet is a child of the forklift's visual and its load byte rides in the forklift's own replicated state (`WritePose`, and `Flags` when parked). The driver already has authority over the vehicle, so there is no second owner to contest, nothing new on the wire per frame, and a pallet cannot be half-carried by two peers. |
| How long does a moved pallet stay moved? | **The session.** This is the dormant-vehicle answer (#499: "once woken it stays a vehicle for the session"): the server remembers which deterministic pallets have left their birthplace and where the loose ones are, and forgets it all on restart. No disk format, no plan-version interaction, no per-furniture persisted record. |

## Why pallets get their own service and not `PlacedObjects`

`PlacedObjects` is close, and reusing it was the first instinct. It is wrong here on four counts,
each of which would mean bending it for one kind:

- It **persists everything** to `user://placed/server.json` on every change. We decided session-only;
  a kind that opts out of saving is a new concept in a node whose whole point is that things stay.
- It has **no carried state**. A pallet on the forks is neither placed nor gone.
- Its **owner is the chat display name**, with a 200-per-owner cap and `AnyoneMayRemove` as the
  escape hatch. A pallet in a warehouse has no owner; shuffling a hall would spend the cap.
- It has **no notion of a deterministic original**. The thing the server must remember is mostly
  *absence*: "furniture index 37 of building 2585_1114_52 is no longer there".

So: **`PalletService`**, `World/Pallets`, a sibling of `World/Loot` and `World/Placed`, built by
`ServerWorld` and `ClientWorld`, with the offline client playing the server's part through the same
methods — the house pattern. Server-owned, session-only:

- `HashSet<string> Taken` — the ids of deterministic pallets that have left their birthplace. An id
  is `"<buildingKey>:f<furnitureIndex>"` for a hall pallet, `"<buildingKey>:y<slot>"` for a yard one.
- `List<LoosePallet>(long Id, double E, double N, double Altitude, float Yaw, byte Load)` — pallets
  that have been set down, in LV95 plus altitude so they survive an origin rebase and mean the same
  spot inside a hall 3 km down as out in the yard (fact 3).
- Two requests, both server-checked: `RequestTake(string id)` — the asker must be driving a forklift
  with empty forks, with its forks at that pallet; `RequestDrop(byte load, GlobalPos at, float yaw)`.
- `Snapshot` on join, `Took` / `Dropped` broadcasts, `Taken`/`Loose` readable, `Changed` observable.

That is one small node and no disk format.

## Phase 1 — the forklift drives, and the mast works

Deliberately no pallets in this phase: a mast that moves, seen from a second peer, is the thing
every later phase stands on, and it is checkable on `--world flat`.

- **`src/Player/Forklift.cs`**, `RideKind.Forklift = 193`, modelled line for line on `Airstairs`:
  `Height` / `TargetHeight` / `Lift(dt)`, `PackFlags` / `UnpackFlags`, `WritePose` / `AnimateRemote`,
  `Step` with a signed speed and a bicycle yaw rate. Numbers: 18 km/h forward and in reverse (a
  forklift is symmetric and is driven backwards half the time), **rear-wheel steer** with a short
  wheelbase and a big lock, so it turns in its own length; mast 0.05-3.2 m at 0.5 m/s.
- **`src/Avatar/ForkliftMeshBuilder.cs`** + `ForkliftLayout`, as `AirstairsMeshBuilder`/`AirstairsLayout`:
  one set of numbers shared by the mesh, the hull boxes and the fork rectangle. A counterbalance
  body, the overhead guard, the mast channels, a carriage and two forks that move with `Height`.
  Drawn as a `HeavyRig` (fact 1) with a minimal cab frame: wheel, seat, the mast lever, and the
  small dial reading the fork height the way the airstairs' reads its platform.
- **The mast's controls, all three devices, decided here and not later** (the project's rule):
  **reuse `ShiftUp` / `ShiftDown`**. A forklift has no gearbox, so its shift paddles are free, and
  they are already bound on every device — keyboard **Shift / Ctrl**, pad **RB / LB**, and in VR
  `XrPad` maps RB/LB to the **right / left hand grip** when mounted. So the mast is reachable on all
  three the day it ships, with no new binding and no R5 gap. The `xr/vr-action-map` row's *target* is
  R1 proper: grip the mast lever itself. `Blurb` uses `{shift_up}` / `{shift_down}` placeholders so
  `InputHints` names each device's own control; a row goes in `Core/ControlsHelp`.
- Wiring: `Rideable.For`, `VehicleState.CreateRide` (`UnpackFlags`), a `RideUi` card.
- **Check `--forkliftcheck --world flat`** (tier 0.5, `src/Player/ForkliftCheck.cs`): driven a set
  distance and turned, the mast run to both stops and the rate measured, `PackFlags`/`UnpackFlags`
  round-tripped, and the fork rectangle where the layout says. `ForkliftTests` (tier 0) pin the
  layout arithmetic and the flag packing.

## Phase 2 — a pallet is a thing, and the forks pick it up

- **`src/Items/Pallets.cs`** (tier 0, no Godot): the load byte and what it draws, the id format, and
  the **fork geometry rule** — a pallet is forked when the carriage is below `ForkEntry` (0.35 m),
  the pallet's centre is inside the fork rectangle in the forklift's own frame, and the forklift is
  under 2 m/s. Set down when the carriage comes below `SetDown` (0.12 m) over the ground. No button:
  driving the forks in and raising them *is* the interaction, which is also why it needs no new
  action beyond phase 1's.
- **`src/Items/PalletService.cs`** as above.
- **Interior pallets become nodes.** `InteriorMeshBuilder.Build` skips a loose floor pallet
  (`FurnitureType.Pallet`, `Lift == 0`); a new `InteriorMeshBuilder.PalletPiece(p)` returns its mesh
  on its own; `InteriorManager.AddPallets`, beside `AddLockDoors`, builds one node per index with a
  body, and hides it when `PalletService` says it is taken. No plan change, no version bump.
- **Carrying.** The pallet's node is reparented to the forklift's carriage while carried, so it
  crosses the bay's portal with the forklift for free and needs no per-frame network. `Forklift.Carried`
  (0 = empty, else `1 + load`) rides `WritePose().Z` — the channel `Airstairs` spends on `AtDoor` —
  and `PackFlags` keeps it for a parked one. A carried pallet has no collider.
- **Loot.** `FurnitureType.Pallet` is a loot container (`LootTables`, 0.25). A pallet that is still
  where the plan put it keeps its loot exactly as now; a pallet that has been **moved stops being a
  container** — it has been shuffled, and `LootService`'s records key on the furniture index at a
  fixed spot. Stated here because it is a real, if small, loss and should not be discovered later.
- **Checks**: `--palletcheck` (tier 1, a hand-made hall as `--sitecheck` does) — a pallet forked,
  carried, set down, and the hall's node hidden and a loose pallet in its place;
  **`tools/palletnetcheck.sh` (tier 2)** — B watches A fork a pallet in the hall, drive out through
  the bay and set it down in the yard, and must see the pallet on A's forks and then on the ground,
  which it cannot derive from its own guess. That is the remote-peer rule.

## Phase 3 — pallets in the yard, and a forklift standing in one

- **`SitePallets.ForSite(yard, siteType, key)`** (tier 0, pure): stacks on the apron, by trade — a
  warehouse's rows by the dock, a works' raw stock at one end, a dealership none.
- A small per-tile drawer hooked to `ChunkManager.TileEntered`, freed on unload, as
  `DormantVehicles` does; a taken yard pallet is simply not drawn.
- **`DormantSlots.ForSite` gains a forklift** in a warehouse's and a works' yard, which is what
  #583 means by "the dormant-vehicle layer can park them once they are a `RideKind`".
- Check: `--sitecheck` asserts the yard pallets per trade; the drive is phase 2's net check extended.

## Not in this feature, and why

- **Pallets on racking.** They are drawn inside the `PalletRack` piece, one of the most expensive
  pieces in the game; making each shelf slot an object would multiply the node count by ten for a
  thing you can only reach with the mast at 4 m. Worth a follow-up once the mast exists.
- **Surviving a restart.** The session decision above. The follow-up, if it is ever wanted, is a
  per-building removed-furniture record on the model of `LootService`'s take mask.
- **A pallet as a physics body** — tipping, crushing, falling off the forks on a slope. It is posed,
  not simulated, like every deck walker and every pallet's worth of cargo in the game.
- **Stacking a pallet on a pallet.** The single most satisfying thing a forklift does, and the most
  likely to be asked for next; it needs a height query against the loose pallets rather than the
  ground, and nothing else. Left out of phase 2 only to keep it checkable.
- **A pallet truck or a reach truck**, pallet jacks, and the weighbridge (#593), which is its own issue.
