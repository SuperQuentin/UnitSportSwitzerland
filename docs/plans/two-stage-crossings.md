# Two-stage pedestrian crossings at the lights (#1030)

Decisions with the user before coding (Oct 21 2026, also on the issue):

- **Which arms**: a lit arm whose crosswalk crosses the centre island (the refuge, or the left repeater's island the
  crosswalk splits, #1000), the island spanning the whole zebra and at least **2.0 m** wide across the walking direction
  at it. Narrower ones stay a refuge with one kerb-to-kerb group.
- **Groups**: two pedestrian groups for such an arm. The **arriving half** (kerb to island over the arriving lanes, on
  the arriving driver's right) conflicts only with the approach's own movements; the **departing half** (island to the
  other kerb) with the movements into the arm (turning in: permissive, by the flasher, as today). Each half's clearance
  and flasher walk cover its own half. Each half is green wherever its conflicts allow (`Expand`), so more often than
  the kerb-to-kerb group.
- **New constraint, the island wait**: the first walker of each half's green (either direction) steps off as it turns
  green, reaches the island at 1.2 m/s and waits there at most **a third of the cycle** for the other half's next green.
- **Where it fails: stretch**: the phase in which the far half turns green for those walkers gets longer (the cycle
  grows, up to 120 s) until the wait fits a third of it. Past 120 s it stays two-stage and `--signal-check` reports it.
  A walker never reaches the far kerb later than with one crossing: each half is green at least whenever the
  kerb-to-kerb group would be.
- **Heads**: kerb poles show their own half (the right kerb the arriving half, the left kerb the departing one); on the
  island two heads back to back, on the left repeater's pole where there is one, else on a pedestrian pole of its own by
  the crosswalk. Straight crosswalk, no stagger, no special visors.
