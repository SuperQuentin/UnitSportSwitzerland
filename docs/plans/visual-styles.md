# Visual styles: PS1, Cartoon, Realistic− and Realistic+

Status: **phase 1 in progress** (issue #212: part 1 merged in #224, part 2 next; the issue's
"Status and how to resume" section has the current steps). Phase 0 is done (issue #181): the
prototype's results, at the end of this file, decided to build the framework and to do Cartoon
first.

## Goal

Let the player choose how the world looks, from more than the current PS1 look:

- **PS1**: today's look (vertex snapping, stepped sun, dither, posterize).
- **Cartoon**: Breath-of-the-Wild-like. Flat facets kept, a cel terminator with real sun
  shadows, cool blue shade, olive-gold pastel greens, a soft rim, and strong aerial haze. Not
  ink outlines.
- **Realistic−**: realistic textures and detailed models on the **Mobile** renderer. No GI or
  screen-space effects. Must stay viable on Android.
- **Realistic+**: Realistic− plus **Forward+** lighting (SDFGI or other GI, good shadows,
  volumetric fog, SSR, SSAO). Desktop only.

## Decisions (agreed)

| Question | Decision |
|---|---|
| Name | **Visual style**: a `VisualStyle` enum and "Visual style" in the settings menu. |
| Depth | **All the way to authored assets**: shaders, lighting, geometry detail, textures and imported models. |
| Player choice | **One preset per style.** No mix-and-match knobs. |
| Renderer | Realistic+ uses Forward+. Realistic− and the others work on Mobile. |
| Switching | Live switching is ideal. Trade-offs below; Realistic+ needs a restart either way. |
| PS1's status | **Cartoon may replace it** as the main look. Partly an experiment. |
| Asset sources | CC0 libraries, Swiss open data, AI-generated. No commissioned art for now. |
| First step | **Vertical prototype** before any framework. |
| Scope | **Client-only.** The server never renders, and gameplay must not depend on the style. |
| Performance | **Realistic+ must run reasonably on a gaming laptop; the other styles must be easy to run.** Every change is measured (see Prototype results). |
| Trees | **Far trees are billboards in every style**, PS1 included. Near trees are 3D, at the style's detail level. |
| Fallback | **Styles fall back on each other**, per material role and per model, down to a complete base style. A new feature is built once, in the base style, and other styles inherit it until they get their own version. |

## What exists today

The PS1 look is not one switch. It is spread across three layers.

**1. Shading.** There are nine unshaded `shaders/ps1_*.gdshader` files: terrain, road, building,
tree, water, prop, interior, path and snowfall. They do their **own lighting** from global
uniforms in `project.godot` (`world_sun_dir`, `world_tint`, `world_sky`, `world_night`,
`world_snow`, `world_mist_*` and others), which `World/DayNight` pushes every frame. The scene has
no real `DirectionalLight3D` and no shadows. `ClientWorld` sets up a `WorldEnvironment` whose
background is just a flat colour. Every world shader also includes behaviour that the game
**depends on**, not just looks:

- `portal_clip.gdshaderinc`: clipping through door portals. Interiors break without it.
- `door_light.gdshaderinc`: light spilling from doorways.
- `world_occasion.gdshaderinc`: snow cover and mist for the seasonal occasions.
- Shader-side content: surface patterns in `COLOR.a` (parking bays, vineyard rows, mowing
  stripes), road markings, building windows from `CUSTOM0` frames, the far-horizon cover mask.

**2. Geometry.** About 40 procedural mesh builders, with no imported model or texture anywhere in
the repo:

- Terrain is vertices plus colours only: no normals and no UVs. Flat shading comes from
  `dFdx`/`dFdy`. LOD strides go up to 20–40 m.
- Roads and buildings already carry `UV`/`UV2` (for markings and windows).
- Avatars are built from tubes and boxes (`MeshScratch`), one surface each. Every pose is solved
  in code (`Limb.Solve` two-bone IK, a gait derived from the no-slip constraint, a dance layer),
  and there is **no `Skeleton3D`** anywhere.
- Cars, trucks, buses, bikes, birds and occasion props each have their own builder.
  `CarCatalog` holds real vehicle specs.
- Trees and occasion decor are `MultiMesh`.

**3. Platform.** `renderer/rendering_method="mobile"`, `scaling_3d/scale=0.75`, D3D12 on
Windows. `docs/plans/android-client.md` assumes the cheap low-poly look.

**Where materials are created:**

- `ClientWorld` creates the five shared world materials (terrain, road, building, tree, water)
  and passes them to `ChunkManager`, which reuses the terrain shader for the horizon layer.
- About 15 other places load a `ps1_*` shader themselves: `InteriorManager`, `TrackRibbon`,
  `OccasionDecor`, `OccasionCreatures`, `PortalDemo` and others.
- About 35 places create a `StandardMaterial3D`, mostly items, FX and vehicles. Avatars use
  `HumanMeshBuilder.Material()` (vertex colour, per-pixel shading, specular off).

**Existing pieces that help:**

- `GameSettings` already offers a style-like choice: `EngineVoice` picks a PS1 or NES sound chip.
- `FogUniforms` shows the pattern for re-pushing a setting to every world material.
- `--shot-queue` (#174) takes many screenshots in a single launch, which is the basis for
  comparing styles side by side.

## The four styles, layer by layer

| Layer | PS1 | Cartoon | Realistic− (Mobile) | Realistic+ (Forward+) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vertex treatment | snap to 640×480 | none | none | none |
| Lighting model | hand-rolled, stepped sun, unshaded | lit, toon ramp in `light()` or 2–3 hard bands, rim light | lit PBR, `DirectionalLight3D`, one shadow cascade | PBR, 3–4 cascades, SDFGI or other GI, SSAO, SSR |
| Colour | posterize + Bayer dither | clean flat palette, saturated, slight gradients | textures (albedo, normal, roughness) | same as Realistic− + detail maps |
| Terrain | flat facets, vertex palette | smooth normals or soft facets, palette from cover | triplanar or splat CC0 materials driven by cover class, optional SWISSIMAGE far away | same, plus parallax and wetness |
| Sky / environment | flat colour, distance fog | gradient sky, stylised clouds | `ProceduralSky` or physical sky, tonemapping | physical sky, volumetric fog, volumetric clouds (shader) |
| Outlines | — | inverted hull or a post-process edge pass | — | — |
| Post-FX | — | optional light bloom | tonemap (ACES or AgX), bloom | tonemap, bloom, auto exposure, DOF in cinema mode |
| Geometry | today's builders | builders at a higher **detail level** (rounder, more segments, overhangs) | imported glTF models where they exist, otherwise high-detail builders | same as Realistic− + LOD-heavy higher-poly variants |
| Avatars | `MeshScratch` figure | smoother `MeshScratch` figure (more sides, hands, a face) | rigged glTF human driven by the existing IK | same |

## Architecture

### `VisualStyle` setting

- `GameSettings.VisualStyle { Ps1, Cartoon, RealisticLow, RealisticHigh }`, persisted like
  `EngineVoice`, with a `--style <name>` flag for runs and screenshots.
- The style is **client-local and never replicated**. The server neither knows nor cares.

### A style kit instead of scattered shader loads

A `VisualStyles/StyleKit` (one per style) answers "give me the material for role X":

- **Roles:** `Terrain`, `Road`, `Building`, `Tree`, `Water`, `Prop`, `Interior`, `Path`,
  `Precip`, `Avatar`, `Vehicle`, `Glass`, `Lamp` and others. The roles are exactly what
  `ClientWorld` and the ~15 scattered `GD.Load<Shader>("res://shaders/ps1_*")` calls create today.
- **Environment and lights** come from the kit too: `Environment`, whether a sun light exists and
  its shadow settings, and the tonemapper. `DayNight` keeps owning time of day. It drives the
  kit's sun light and sky as well as the global uniforms, and every style reads the same globals.
- **Shader layout:** `shaders/<style>/terrain.gdshader` and so on, with the **functional
  includes shared** in `shaders/common/` (`portal_clip`, `door_light`, `world_occasion`, global
  uniforms, surface patterns and markings as functions that return a colour or mask). Each style
  only decides how to light and finish that colour.
- **Uniform contract:** every style's shader for a role accepts the same uniforms (fog, cover
  mask, building window frames and so on). Then the code that pushes uniforms, like `FogUniforms`
  and `ChunkManager`'s building uniforms, does not care which style is active.

### Fallback chain

Without a fallback, every new visual would have to be built in four styles before it could ship.
Instead, each style names a **parent**, and anything it does not define resolves through the
chain:

```
Realistic+ → Realistic− → Cartoon → PS1 (base)
```

- **Order:** each step goes to the nearest look. Lit styles fall back to lit styles before
  reaching the unshaded base.
- **The base style is the only complete one.** Every role, model id and feature exists there. It
  is PS1 today; if Cartoon becomes the default (phase 5), the chain is reordered and Cartoon
  becomes the base.
- **Resolution is per item, not per style.** `StyleKit.Material(role)`, the environment pieces,
  `ModelCatalog.Find(id)` and a builder's `MeshDetail` each walk the chain on their own. Realistic−
  can have its own car models and terrain but still borrow Cartoon's water and PS1's snowfall.
- **A fallback must keep working, even if it does not match.** The functional includes
  (portal clipping, door light, occasions) are shared, so a borrowed material is always correct.
  It can still look out of place, for example an unshaded PS1 material in a lit scene. To limit
  that, the base shaders get **neutral switches** for their retro treatment: snapping, dither and
  posterize off, and a lit or unlit choice. They are driven by uniforms, so the kit turns them off
  whenever a PS1 material is borrowed by another style.
- **Gaps are visible.** `--style-report` logs, for each style, every role and model id it resolves
  through a fallback, and from which style. That list is the to-do list for that style, and a PR
  can show it got shorter.

### Geometry: detail levels and model overrides

- **Detail level.** Mesh builders take a `MeshDetail` parameter (Low = today, High = cartoon and
  realistic fallback). This covers terrain normals, tube sides, bevels, roof overhangs and tree
  shapes. Tile meshes are built on worker threads, so it goes into the build request.
- **Model overrides.** A `ModelCatalog` maps a semantic id to a glTF scene per style, for example
  `car:<CarCatalog id>`, `human`, `tree:<species>`, `prop:jack-o-lantern`. A missing entry
  resolves through the fallback chain: a parent style's model, then the parent's builder at its
  detail level. So a style can ship with a single model.
- **Gameplay never reads the visual mesh.** Collision, hitboxes, seat and hand anchors, and wheel
  positions keep coming from the canonical procedural dimensions (`CarCatalog`, the
  `hitboxes-measured-never-typed` note). An imported model is **fitted** to those dimensions
  (scale and offset baked at import), never the other way round. Otherwise two players with
  different styles would disagree about where a car ends.
- **Avatars are the hardest part.** Nothing uses skeletons: the figure is posed by solving joint
  positions in code, then re-meshed. A rigged glTF human needs an adapter that writes the solved
  joints (pelvis, spine, head, the two-bone limbs, the dance channels) into `Skeleton3D` bone
  poses. The IK stays the source of truth. Item arm poses, cockpit seating and cycling all go
  through the same joints, so once the adapter works they come along for free.

### Trees: LOD in every style

Forest tiles hold up to 60k trees, so trees decide the frame time more than anything else.

- **Far: billboards in every style.** A quad per tree, from the same instance buffer. PS1
  **ray-traces** its unit meshes on the quad (both crowns are convex bipyramids, the trunk a
  prism: 17 plane tests a pixel), so the silhouette and facets match the 3D tree from any angle;
  a camera-facing picture of a cone shrinks to a sliver seen from above, and the forest floor
  shows through (found in phase 1, `docs/notes/styles/tree-lod.md`). Cartoon's puffy blobs and
  tiered cones can be ray-traced the same way (ellipsoids, cones). The realistic styles sample an
  **impostor baked at startup** from the 3D tree, so near and far match.
- **Near: 3D trees**, crossfaded per tree with a dither, the billboard taking exactly the pixels
  the 3D tree gives up. The distance is measured from the player's camera (`world_cam_pos`),
  not the rendering camera, so every view (and in the lit styles the shadow pass) agrees.
- **Cheap trees (PS1 20 triangles, Cartoon ~120) can stay per tile**: the shader collapses the
  far ones, and a tile-level visibility range drops whole tiles.
- **Expensive trees (realistic, ~2.5–3.5k triangles) need CPU culling per tree** (`NearTrees`):
  one MultiMesh per kind holding only the trees within range, refilled from the tiles' instance
  buffers when the camera moves. Collapsing them in the shader left 360M primitives a frame,
  because the GPU still processes every vertex.
- **Realistic tree source: [EZ-Tree](https://github.com/dgreenheck/ez-tree)** (MIT, bark
  textures CC0). It runs headless in Node (`assets/proto/trees/gen.mjs`) with presets for pine,
  ash, oak and aspen plus bushes, and game-budget options. The presets are 7–20k triangles; the
  game cuts are 2.5–3.5k. CC0 scanned trees (Poly Haven) are 0.3–7.8M polygons, unusable at this
  scale.

### Terrain texturing (realistic)

Two candidate sources, which can be combined:

- **Splat or triplanar from cover classes (recommended for the near field).** `CoverPalette`
  already classifies every vertex (forest, meadow, rock, scree, glacier, settlement, vineyard and
  others) and writes it as a vertex colour. A realistic terrain shader maps the class to a CC0 PBR
  set (ambientCG or Poly Haven). Triplanar handles cliffs, so no UVs are needed, and the shader
  blends by slope and altitude. It costs no extra data and looks right at ground level.
- **SWISSIMAGE aerial photos (good for the mid and far field and flying).** swisstopo publishes
  them as open data (10 cm, and 2 m downsampled per 1 km tile). At 2 m, a 1 km tile is a 500×500
  texture, about 125 KB as BC1. All of Switzerland at 2 m is roughly 5 GB compressed. Baked
  shadows and parked cars show, and up close it is a smeared photo. It fits the
  `TerrainPreprocessor` pipeline as a new per-tile layer and needs a GDAL step
  (`docs/notes/tools/gdal-setup.md`).
- **Suggested:** splat near, photo blended in with distance and altitude, so pilots see the real
  patchwork of fields and walkers see grass.

Terrain **normals** are needed by every lit style. Computing them per vertex in
`TerrainMeshBuilder` costs 12 bytes per vertex, or 4 bytes octahedral-packed. Check this against
the `stutter-main-thread-commit-problem` budget, since a million-vertex tile already costs 30 ms
to pack.

### Assets

- **Location:** `assets/<style>/...`. Godot has to import them, so no `.gdignore`. Each asset has
  a row in `assets/ASSETS.md` (source, author, licence, URL, date, and an "AI-generated" flag).
  CC0 and Swiss OGD both allow redistribution. For AI-generated assets, check each tool's terms
  before committing anything.
- **Repo size:** textures and models will grow the repo quickly. Options: Git LFS; a separate
  asset repo or download (like terrain tiles, via `TerrainPaths`-style lookup); or keep only
  compressed `.ctex`/`.scn` imports in releases. Decide after the prototype shows the real volume.
- **Budget for Realistic−:** textures at 1K maximum, BC or ASTC compressed. Models under about 5k
  triangles for cars and about 3k for people, with LODs (Godot's automatic mesh LOD works on
  imported glTF).

## Switching: live versus restart

| What changes | Live? | How | Cost / risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shaders (PS1 ↔ Cartoon ↔ Realistic− shading) | **Yes, cheap** | The world materials are **shared**: set `ShaderMaterial.Shader` on the kit's instances and every tile updates at once. Needs the uniform contract above. | A shader-compile stutter on first use. Mitigated by warming up at load and by the Godot 4.4+ ubershader / pipeline cache. |
| Environment, sun light, post-FX | **Yes, cheap** | Swap the `Environment` and toggle the light. | none |
| Geometry detail level | **Yes, slow** | `ChunkManager` drops the built meshes and rebuilds them around the player, as on a ring-count change. Avatars and vehicles rebuild their rigs. | Tens of seconds of pop-in. Fine behind a "Rebuilding world…" overlay. |
| Imported models | **Yes, slow** | Load on demand, swap the visual node under the rig; collision is unchanged. | Load hitches unless loaded in the background (`ResourceLoader.LoadThreadedRequest`). |
| Renderer (Mobile ↔ Forward+) | **No** | Godot reads `rendering_method` at startup. Restart with `--rendering-method forward_plus`, or write `override.cfg`. | One restart. The settings menu says "Applies after restart" for Realistic+ only. |

**Recommendation:** live switching for everything except crossing the Realistic+ boundary. Build
it as "rebuild the world in place" rather than true hot-swapping: tear down and recreate world
visuals with the new kit, keeping the network session, the player and physics untouched. That is
one code path instead of a swap handler per system, and it is also how the game starts.

Thanks to the fallback chain, **a new visual feature only has to be built in the base style.**
Other styles inherit it, and porting it to them is separate, later work. To keep that cheap:

- Features go into `shaders/common/` as colour or mask functions, so a style's own shader can
  pick them up with one call.
- New mesh builders take `MeshDetail` from day one, even if they only implement Low.
- `--style-report` lists what each style still borrows.
- A **style screenshot check** runs `--shot-queue` over a fixed set of viewpoints for each style,
  for reviewers. A PR that adds a visual says which styles it checked; "borrowed, looks
  acceptable" is a valid answer.

## Open questions and risks

- **Per-style scope of special looks:** cinema mode, photo/polaroid, the zoom bubble, speed lines
  and pixel icons are PS1-flavoured too. Do they follow the style or stay retro? Proposal: the HUD
  and UI stay as they are; 3D FX follow the style.
- **Audio:** should the visual style also set `EngineVoice` (PS1 chip versus realistic synth), or
  stay independent? Proposal: independent, with a suggested default per style.
- **Distance:** a realistic style at the 60 km horizon. Flat-coloured far terrain can look wrong
  next to textured near terrain, so a SWISSIMAGE far field may be needed sooner than phase 4.
- **Interiors:** `ps1_interior` rooms are never lit by the environment. Realistic interiors need
  their own lights and probes, or a baked-ambient look.
- **Night and occasions:** lit styles need real light sources (street lamps, glowing jack-o'-
  lanterns, headlights) where PS1 fakes them with `max(col, glow)`. With many lights this costs
  Mobile's per-object light limit.
- **AI-generated assets:** quality and licence vary by tool; we need a rule for what is acceptable
  in the repo.
- **Repo and release size** once textures and models land (see Assets).

### What was built

- **Role bodies + style wrappers.** The five tile shaders (terrain, road, building, tree, water)
  moved to `shaders/body/<role>.gdshaderinc`. `ps1_`, `cartoon_` and `real_<role>.gdshader` are
  3-line wrappers (`render_mode` + `STYLE_*` defines). PS1 is **unchanged**: a pixel diff against
  `main` averages 0.01–0.02/255, and the remaining differences come from streaming timing.
- **`VisualStyleKit`**: `--style ps1|cartoon|real-|real+`, the fallback chain for shaders
  (interiors, props, paths and snowfall stay PS1 in every style and still work), the sun, sky,
  fog and tonemap per style, driven by `DayNight`.
- **Cartoon (BotW-like):** a cel `light()` (one soft terminator, shadows as a band, rim), cool
  blue ambient, olive-gold grading, painted noise, blue aerial haze, puffy and tiered 3D trees,
  and toon avatars and vehicles.
- **Realistic:** ambientCG CC0 textures tinted by the land-cover vertex colour (grass, forest
  floor, gravel, triplanar rock, asphalt, plaster, roof tiles), a SWISSIMAGE 2 m drape blended in
  beyond ~150 m, EZ-Tree trees (pine, ash), ACES. Realistic+ adds Forward+, 4-split shadows, SSAO,
  SSR and SDFGI (each switchable with `--nofx`).
- **Tree LOD in every style:** billboards far (PS1 included), 3D near, per-tree crossfade;
  `NearTrees` CPU culling for the lit styles' 3D trees; impostors baked at startup for the
  realistic billboards.

| Realistic+ (Forward+), SDFGI, SSAO, SSR | 60–75 | 29–36M |
| Realistic+, no SDFGI | 47–56 | 25–30M |
| Realistic+, shadows only (no SDFGI/SSR/SSAO) | 45–59 | 25–30M |
| Realistic+, no SDFGI, `--detail low` | 27–40 (one run at a far origin; not reproduced) | 10–13M |

### Findings

1. **Terrain triangles are the shared budget.** The near rings are a 1 m grid, which puts
   25M+ primitives in every frame of every style. `--detail low` (2 m near) cuts that 4–8× and
   Realistic− from ~25 to 11–20 ms; past that point the lighting and shadows dominate. With
   textures carrying the surface detail, the textured styles can use coarser terrain than PS1
   needs. **Per-style LOD rings are the biggest geometry lever.**
2. **Trees are the second.** Forest tiles hold up to 60k trees. Billboards far plus per-tree CPU
   culling near (`NearTrees`) took Cartoon from 66–90 to 22–25 ms and made PS1 faster than `main`.
   Shader-side collapsing does not scale to heavy meshes (360M primitives).
3. **Lit shading costs ~5–10 ms over PS1's unshaded path, and sun shadows 3–12 ms more.** Terrain,
   roads and water must not cast shadows: the full-resolution tiles under the cascades doubled the
   primitive count. Buildings, trees and props cast.
4. **Forward+ costs ~2× Mobile on the M1 Pro**, before any effect. SDFGI adds ~15–20 ms; SSR and
   SSAO are minor. A sun with an angular size (`LightAngularDistance` 0.6°, PCSS soft shadows,
   Forward+ only) **erased the sun shadows entirely**; it is 0 now. Apple GPUs favour the Mobile renderer, so **this machine understates
   Realistic+ on a gaming laptop** (discrete NVIDIA/AMD). Measure there before deciding on
   SDFGI; plan Realistic+ as "shadows + SSAO + SSR + glow, coarser terrain, SDFGI optional".
5. **The SWISSIMAGE drape is free at runtime** (2 m: ~190 KB per km², ~8 GB for Switzerland
   delivered as JPEG GeoTIFF; 10 cm is 65 MB per km², unusable). It reads well from the air;
   on the ground the cover textures are better, hence the distance blend.
6. **Realistic trees:** CC0 scans are 0.3–7.8M polygons, unusable. **EZ-Tree** (MIT, headless in
   Node, CC0 bark) gives believable trees at 2.5–3.5k triangles with leaf cards, and impostors
   baked from it keep near and far consistent. Other candidates found: the Sketchfab "Pine trees
   pack (game ready, LODs)" (CC-BY), itch.io "Spruce Forest – game ready with LODs" ($8.99, with
   billboard LOD), Meshy community models (CC0, AI-generated), and the GodotFest 2025 talk
   "Plants, Polygons and Pixels" on large-scale vegetation in Godot (EZ-Tree + Blender pipeline).
7. **A single vertex-coloured material for avatars and vehicles is a compromise** in the lit
   styles: car paint, cloth and skin want different roughness. Phase 3 splits it.
8. **Test-tool gotchas.** (a) Intermittently, a game window stops presenting on macOS:
   `--shot-queue` then saves stale frames and reports a flat ~6.9 ms with repeated primitive
   counts. `--always-on-top` made it rarer but did not stop it, and the cause is not known (the
   display was awake). Such runs are discarded and every published picture was checked by eye.
   (b) Forward+ needs a warm-up shot (first-launch pipeline compilation). (c) The terrain folder is
   shared between worktrees: another session regenerated it mid-run and moved the manifest's
   origin 70 km away, which silently changed every shot's place and precision. Prototype runs
   pin it with `--origin E,N`; phase 1's timing check should do the same.

### Go / no-go

- **Cartoon: go.** It reads as intended, costs ~1.3–1.5× PS1, and gets cheaper still with
  per-style terrain LOD. It is the natural candidate for the default (phase 5).
- **Realistic−: go**, with coarser terrain as part of the style (11–20 ms here, the cheapest lit
  configuration measured).
- **Realistic+: go, pending a measurement on a real gaming laptop.** On the M1 Pro it is 47–56 ms
  without SDFGI on today's terrain grid; coarse terrain should roughly halve the geometry, but
  that run was not reproduced. SDFGI stays an option, not part of the preset, until measured on discrete GPUs.
- **PS1: keep**, and take the billboard trees regardless of the rest: a pure performance win.
- **Order:** phase 1 framework (with tree LOD and per-style terrain LOD in it), then Cartoon,
  then Realistic−, then Realistic+.
