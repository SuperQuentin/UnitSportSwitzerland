# Ellipsoid handover: from the flat world to a real WGS84 Earth at altitude

Status: **H2 built (PR #1435); H6's ground sky built (PR #1436); C1 the telescope built (PR #1441); C1b the solar system and satellites in the sky built (PR #1445); H1 the curvature at altitude built (PR #1458)**; #187's curvature merged (PR #1432); the rest planned, H3 next.
Issue: #1430.
Prerequisites: the floating origin (#185, `docs/plans/floating-origin.md`, done), the round world
near the ground (#187, `docs/plans/spherical-world.md`) and its regression net (#1429).

## Why

#187 was re-planned so that, near the ground, the Earth's curvature is only a **render-space drop**:
every world shader lowers a vertex by d² / (2·R_eff) around the camera (`world_cam_pos`, pushed by
`src/Styles/CameraGlobal.cs`), with refraction k = 0.13, so R_eff = R / 0.87 ≈ 7,332 km. CPU
positions, physics, the wire and the tiles stay flat LV95. That is the right model for walking,
driving and flying a plane. It is wrong once the camera is high enough to see the planet as a
planet, and it cannot work at all in space:

- **The drop is a parabola, not a sphere.** It never turns verticals or compresses distances. At
  20 km of altitude the error is about half a km at the 505 km horizon (invisible); at 100 km the
  horizon is 1,134 km away, mountains there lean 10° away from the viewer and the true ground is
  ~6 km nearer than the parabola puts it.
- **The flat world has no "beyond".** Positions are LV95, which is only defined near Switzerland
  (`SwissProjection` uses swisstopo's approximate polynomials, accurate to under a metre inside the
  country and unusable a few hundred km out). There is no point in space, no other side of the
  Earth, no orbit.
- **"Down" is -Y everywhere.** A craft at orbital speed in a flat frame with constant gravity never
  orbits: it falls, or flies off along a tangent.
- **The sky is fixed.** `src/World/SunPath.cs` is one Swiss summer day (sunrise 6 h, sunset 18 h,
  noon elevation 62°, grid north = -Z) with no latitude, longitude or date. From orbit the
  terminator, the stars and the moon must be where they really are.

Nothing caps altitude today: the spectator camera flies up to 3,000 m/s × 6 boost = 18 km/s
(`src/Player/SpectatorCamera.cs`), the UFO has no gravity (`src/Player/Ufo.cs`), and the airliner's
ISA density (`AirlinerFlight.Density`) is the troposphere formula clamped at 0.05, i.e. frozen
above ~42 km at 3.6·10⁻⁶ kg/m³. So a player can already climb out of the model.

The goal: a **handover to a real WGS84 ellipsoid** when the camera climbs high enough, with no
visible pop either way, built so that a future space and multiplanetary travel feature sits on it
unchanged. The ground game does not pay for any of it.

## Prior art

Microsoft Flight Simulator 2020 (Asobo, GDC 2022, summarised in `docs/plans/spherical-world.md`):
64-bit positions on the CPU, and on the GPU an **anchor space** whose origin is the camera, whose Y
is the Earth's normal there and whose X/Z are carried over from the previous frame (no pole
singularity). We take the anchor, but as a *regime* reached at altitude rather than the model for
everything: #187 already showed that rigid tile frames on the ellipsoid crack by h·1000 / R
(~30 cm at 2,000 m) with heightmap collision, so the ground stays flat.

Kerbal Space Program is the reference for the multiplanetary part: patched conics with spheres of
influence, a floating origin, and a "scaled space" layer that draws distant bodies small and near
so they fit the depth range.

### The three regimes

| Regime | Camera altitude | Curvature model | Position type that rules | Anchor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ground** | 0 – 20 km | #187's vertical drop d²/(2·R_eff); k fades 0.13 → 0 between 5 and 20 km (`world_curvature`) | `GlobalPos` (LV95) | flat LV95 frame, whole-km corners, altitude absolute (today) |
| **Transition** | 20 – 50 km (blend), held up to 60 km | `curved_view` blends from the drop to the **exact sphere wrap** around the camera (`world_curve_blend` 0 → 1) | `GlobalPos` | still the flat LV95 frame |
| **Space** | above 60 km climbing; left below 50 km descending | real geometry on the ellipsoid (globe), no shader trick | `EcefPos` | tangent plane at the camera, rotating 3D shifts |

The blend is complete (1.0) at 50 km, below both handover altitudes, so the image is already the
exact sphere whenever a handover happens. The 10 km hysteresis band keeps a craft hovering at
55 km from flipping back and forth.

## Design

### 1. Ground regime: the refraction fades out (H1)

- New `world_curvature` global (part of #187's `Curvature.cs`): the k used by `curved_view`. It
  follows the camera's altitude, 0.13 below 5 km and 0 above 20 km (smoothstep). Refraction bends
  near-horizontal rays in dense low air; from the stratosphere the planet must look geometric,
  and the Space regime has no refraction at all.
- `InterestService`'s line of sight keeps its own fixed k: it is about the ground, not the camera.
- Distance fog (`Core/FogUniforms`) fades with altitude the same way, or the planet sits in a
  white box seen from 40 km.

### 2. Transition regime: from a parabola to the exact sphere (H1)

In `curved_view`, with the camera C, a vertex v (flat world, y = altitude), d = v.xz − C.xz,
s = |d|, u = d / s and θ = s / R:

- **drop** (today): v' = (v.x, v.y − s² / (2·R_eff), v.z)
- **exact wrap** (flat x/z read as arc length on the sphere through the camera's nadir):
  horizontal C.xz + u·(R + v.y)·sin θ, height v.y·cos θ − R·(1 − cos θ)
- output = mix(drop, exact, `world_curve_blend`), with `world_curve_blend` 0 at 20 km and 1 at 50 km.

Precision rule: **never form R + y or R·cos θ in float** (the float step at 6.4·10⁶ m is 0.5 m).
Write 1 − cos θ as 2·sin²(θ/2) and keep everything relative to the camera. Normals rotate by θ
about u × up in the same function, or the lit styles shade the curved ground as if it were flat.

What the blend changes (R ≈ 6,378.8 km, as #187 uses at Bern):

| Altitude | Geometric horizon √(2Rh + h²) | Horizon compression (s − R sin θ) | Verticals at the horizon lean |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 km | 253 km | 0.07 km | 2.3° |
| 20 km | 505 km | 0.5 km | 4.5° |
| 60 km | 877 km | 2.7 km | 7.9° |
| 100 km | 1,134 km | 6.0 km | 10.2° |

The exact wrap with R_eff equals the drop to second order, so H1 may simply use it at every
altitude (one formula, `world_curvature` only) if it costs nothing measurable. H1 measures and
picks.

**The view grows with altitude**: `GameSettings.CameraFar` is today max(20 km, (`HorizonKm` + 10) km),
with `HorizonKm` 60 by default and 150 at most, whatever the altitude. In Transition and Space the
far plane becomes max(that, √(2Rh + h²) + 200 km): ~1,100 km at 60 km, ~2,500 km at 400 km.
Godot ≥ 4.3 already renders with a reversed-Z depth buffer; H4 checks that the Mobile renderer
(`renderer/rendering_method="mobile"`) gets a 32-bit float depth format, which is what makes a far
plane of thousands of km harmless.

**Culling**: a vertex lowered by tens of km leaves its mesh's AABB. Horizon blocks and anything
far get an `extra_cull_margin` from the drop at their distance, or they vanish at the edges of the
view from altitude.

**Beyond the data**: `horizon.bin` covers only the preprocessed area, so from 20 km the edge of the
data is in view. Until the globe (H4) the fog hides it; with H4 the globe is drawn under and around
the Swiss tiles already in Transition (below).

### 3. `EcefPos` and the conversions (H2, built: PR #1435)

As built (`docs/notes/core/swiss-geodesy.md`):

- `EcefPos`, `Geodetic` and `Ellipsoid` (`Wgs84`, `Bessel1841`; geodetic ↔ ECEF by iterated
  Bowring, exact from below ground out past the moon) live in `tools/TerrainFormat`, beside the
  projection, so the preprocessor has them for global data too.
- `SwissGeodesy`: the rigorous LV95 ↔ CH1903+ cylinder and the CH1903+ → CHTRS95 translation.
  Against swisstopo's REFRAME it matches to < 2 mm in Switzerland, France and Bavaria; LV95 → ECEF
  → LV95 comes back within 0.1 mm from −3 km to 400 km up and 1,400 km out. LHN95 ↔ ellipsoidal
  heights through `BesselGeoid`, a 20 km CHGeo2004 grid (within 0.25 m of REFRAME between nodes).
- `Core/Planet` (Godot-free): the shape, GM, ω, `Gravity(r)`, `CircularSpeed(r)`; Earth only until H7.
  Radii of curvature and `Up` come with their first caller (H3).
- `GlobalPos.ToEcef()` / `GlobalPos.FromEcef`; the `GlobalPos + Vector3` operator's comment already
  marks itself as "the place to change" for a rotating frame.
- Pure maths, linked into the unit tests, no game change.

### 4. The rotating 3D anchor (H3)

`WorldOrigin` gains a second kind of frame. `OriginFrame` (immutable, taken by workers today)
records which one it is:

| | Ground frame (today) | Anchor frame (Space) |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | whole-km LV95 tile corner, altitude 0 | the camera's `EcefPos` at the last shift |
| Axes | grid east, up, grid south (+X, +Y, +Z) | Y = ellipsoid normal; X/Z carried by minimal rotation |
| `ToWorld` | `(E − E₀, Alt, −(N − N₀))` | R⁻¹ · (p − O) in doubles, then cast |
| Shift | horizontal, km multiples, identity rotation | any direction, ≥ 20 km, a small rotation |

- **Climbing (Ground → Space, at 60 km)**: build the anchor from the current ground frame: its
  origin moves to the camera and its axes are the ground frame's grid axes expressed in ECEF
  (local up of the LV95 frame at the tile corner, grid north including meridian convergence, up to
  ~1° at the edges of Switzerland). The shift that goes with it is rigid and applied to everything,
  the camera included, so **the image cannot change** by construction. All that could differ is
  the curvature model: the Transition shader's sphere of radius R against the real ellipsoid. Over
  the 877 km horizon at 60 km, the ellipsoid's two radii of curvature differ by ~0.3 %, i.e.
  ~180 m at the horizon, 0.012° seen from there: about 0.2 px at 1080p. Then the shader's
  `world_curve_blend` switches off and the globe takes over in the same frame.
- **In Space**: `OriginShifter` shifts when the camera is 20 km from the anchor. The new up is the
  ellipsoid normal under the camera; X/Z follow by the minimal rotation from the old up to the new
  one. The shift is a `Transform3D` with rotation and translation, delivered through the existing
  `IOriginShiftAware` handlers (`shift.Point`, `shift.Direction`, `shift.Apply`). This is the first
  time those see a non-identity basis: every handler is re-checked under `--originstress` with
  rotation (Tests).
- **Descending (Space → Ground, at 50 km)**: the new frame is the whole-km LV95 corner below the
  camera, axes grid-aligned, altitude 0. Again one rigid shift for everything: a rotation about the
  vertical (the anchor's X/Z have drifted from grid north while in Space) plus a translation of
  ~50 km down. The tiles then stream in as after a teleport (`ChunkManager`), from the horizon
  blocks first, which were already drawn in Transition.
- **Where `Position.Y` stops being altitude**: only in Space. The audit is limited to what runs
  there: the flight model and its ISA density, `CockpitInstruments`, the camera rigs, HUD, wind
  audio, `DayNight`. They read altitude from `Geodetic.h` of the camera's or body's `EcefPos`.
- Physics bodies are rotated by `OriginShifter.Teleport` like any shift (kinematic bodies made
  static, set, made kinematic again); velocities and angular velocities go through
  `shift.Direction`.

### 5. Physics at altitude and in orbit (H5)

- **Atmosphere**: replace the clamped troposphere formula (`AirlinerFlight.Density`,
  `CockpitInstruments.Sigma`) with the layered **US Standard Atmosphere 1976** to 86 km
  (tropopause at 11 km, isothermal to 20 km, then +1, +2.8, 0, −2.8, −2 K/km), an exponential above,
  and zero above 150 km. Today's formula is 60× too thin at 40 km (6.2·10⁻⁵ instead of
  4.0·10⁻³ kg/m³) and stays at 3.6·10⁻⁶ forever above 42 km, where the real air reaches 5.6·10⁻⁷
  at 100 km.
- **Gravity**: `Planet.Gravity(r)` = GM/r² toward the centre, in the current anchor frame.
  Ground gameplay keeps 9.81. An aircraft gets the true value from the Transition regime up
  (−0.6 % at 20 km: nobody feels the switch).
- **Orbit**: a craft above the atmosphere owns a double state (position, velocity) in ECI, advanced
  by velocity Verlet each physics step (symplectic: energy does not drift). ECEF = ECI rotated by
  the Earth rotation angle at sim time (`SimClock`, so time scale works). At the Space boundary a
  craft's velocity gains ω × r (317 m/s at Bern's latitude). Jolt holds the craft in the anchor
  frame, set from the double state each step; it is only dynamic for contacts (docking, landing
  on the globe). 400 km circular orbit as the reference: 7,669 m/s, 92.4 min.
- Point-mass gravity only. J2, drag decay, the moon's and sun's pull: later, if a feature needs them.

### 6. Rendering the planet (H4, H6)

- **Globe**: a cube-sphere quadtree (six faces, patches of 33 × 33 vertices), each patch meshed
  around its own ECEF centre in float and placed by anchor⁻¹ · patch, computed in doubles. Built on
  workers, cached by patch key, never rebuilt per frame (`no-mesh-rebuild-per-frame`); a shift only
  rewrites patch transforms. In the model viewer as a `[Showcase]` set.
- **Data (a task of its own)**: a coarse global DEM (Copernicus GLO-90, or ETOPO 2022 at 1′ for the
  sea floor) and an albedo (NASA Blue Marble, 500 m), converted by `tools/TerrainPreprocessor` into
  a LOD pyramid on the same cube-sphere keys. The coarse levels (~5 km, a few tens of MB) ship with
  the game; finer ones stream through the planned map downloader
  (`docs/plans/in-game-map-downloader.md`). Licences and attribution are checked first.
- **Swiss tiles into the globe**: in Transition the Swiss horizon blocks stay and the globe is drawn
  under and around them; the globe patches under the data are masked the way `HorizonLayer`'s
  cover mask hides horizon blocks under loaded tiles. Above ~150 km the Swiss blocks fade out and
  the globe alone shows Switzerland, with an albedo derived from our own tile colours there so the
  hue does not jump.
- **Style**: built in **Cartoon first** (`styles/style-kit`), then PS1 and Realistic.
- **Atmosphere**: a limb and scattering shell around the globe (single scattering, Rayleigh + Mie,
  in the spirit of O'Neil's GPU Gems 2 chapter or Bruneton's precomputed tables for Realistic), and
  the `WorldEnvironment` sky fading from today's sky to black as the camera leaves the air.
- **Stars**: the Yale Bright Star Catalogue (~9,100 stars, public domain) on the celestial sphere in
  ECI, turned into the anchor frame by sidereal time; also at night on the ground.
- **Sun and moon** (H6, **built for the ground**: PR #1436): `Core/Ephemeris`, Meeus (the sun's
  low-precision theory, the moon's full chapter 47 series, nutation, apparent sidereal time),
  topocentric through ECEF, so it serves a place in orbit as well. Against JPL Horizons at four
  Swiss towns: sun 0.008°, moon 0.0013° (its distance to 40 km); Bern's June noon sun 66.49°.
  `SunPath.Toward(body, date, hour, e, n)` turns it from true north to grid north at the camera's
  LV95 place; `DayNight` shades the night from the real moon. `Ephemeris.MoonPhase` gives the lit
  fraction for C1. Still to come with H3/H4: the moon drawn as a disc, stars, the frame in Space.
- **The real sun on the ground** (decided): the game hour stays local civil time (CET/CEST,
  `Europe/Zurich`), converted to UTC for the ephemeris; in Space the HUD shows UTC. The callers of
  `SunPath.Direction` today: `DayNight` (which also takes an occasion's own sunrise, sunset and noon
  elevation, a hand-made short winter day that the real date now gives by itself: those overrides
  go), `AirLift` (thermals follow the season) and `FunBackdrop` (a fixed dusk in a world that is not
  on Earth: stays fixed). `GameSettings.StartHour` keeps meaning a clock time. As built: `Core/SwissTime` (CET/CEST by the
  EU rule, no `TimeZoneInfo`); the occasions' own sunrise, sunset and noon stay for the fixed day
  only; `--sun fixed` is also the default of every check, fixture world, screenshot run and
  trailer, so their results do not move with the date.
- **North**: the compass, map, minimap and GPX overlays keep **grid north** on the ground (the map
  is LV95). In Space the HUD heading uses true north computed from the anchor frame (the ECEF Z
  axis projected on the tangent plane).
- **Very far**: the far plane covers the whole visible globe up to ~8,000 km altitude with
  ~13,000 km. Beyond that, and for other bodies (H7), a **scaled-space** pass draws distant bodies
  shrunk and brought closer with the same angular size.

Over Switzerland and wherever leaves exist, the coarse levels come from the leaves themselves
(heights averaged by the tiler, colour from our own tile colours), so the hue and the relief do not
jump when the globe takes over from the Swiss blocks; ETOPO and Blue Marble fill the rest, blended
across the data border as the French import's edge masks do. The two geoids (CHGeo2004 inside,
EGM2008 out) differ by metres, under one coarse cell.

**The format at coarse levels: two rules proposed to G1** (#1455):

- **A lattice cap.** G1's lattice is 2^(23 − level) cells a side (~1.2 m at every level), which no
  level above the leaves can hold (level 6 would be 131,072 a side). Every level keeps **at most 512
  cells a side**: bits = min(23 − level, 9), so the level-14 leaves are unchanged, a level-0 tile is
  19.6 km a cell, level 2 4.9 km, level 4 1.2 km (ETOPO's own spacing). One function,
  `CubeKey.LatticeBits(level)`, which every reader uses. Edges stay exact: a coarse lattice point
  is a leaf lattice point, and a renderer may draw every 2nd, 4th or 8th vertex as G1's harness
  does.
- **A colour payload.** A tile carries an optional albedo: 12 monthly images (WebP, 256² on the
  shipped levels, 512² on the streamed ones), in bundles
  of their own beside the height bundles (`F<f>_L<l>_x_y.cuba`, same index), so leaves without a
  global colour carry none and G2 can give the leaves their colour another way.

**Which levels, and where they live** (estimates, the tiler measures):

- Levels 0-2 (6 + 24 + 96 = 126 tiles) **ship with the game**: the whole Earth from far out and
  the showcase's last seconds. Heights a few MB; colour ~126 × 12 × 12 KB ≈ 18 MB.
- Levels 3-4 (384 + 1,536 tiles) stream through the map downloader
  (`docs/plans/in-game-map-downloader.md`): the view from low orbit (~0.3 km a pixel at nadir from
  400 km; level 4 is ETOPO's own 1.2 km).
- Over Switzerland, levels 5-13 from the leaves, by G1's tiler.

**The renderer** (`World/Globe`, client only):

- One node per drawn tile, its mesh built once on a worker from `CubeGeometry` (the patch around
  its own ENU anchor, floats) and placed by one rigid transform composed in doubles (the frame's
  `TileTransform`, H3 step 1). A shift only rewrites transforms (`no-mesh-rebuild-per-frame`).
- Levels chosen by size on screen through `world_lod_scale` (C0): a tile splits when its cell would
  cover more than a set number of pixels. G2 carries the same rule to the leaves.
- Its own shader: real geometry, so **no `curve_offset`**. Until G2, in the flat ground frame, the
  globe is placed by the frame's embedding (the ground frame as the tangent frame at its origin,
  grid axes: `OriginFrame.Embedding`), drawn under and around the flat tiles and `HorizonLayer`,
  masked under them as `HorizonLayer`'s cover mask hides blocks under loaded tiles, and faded in
  with altitude; above ~150 km the Swiss blocks fade out.
- **The seam with H1**: above 20 km H1 bends the flat tiles onto a sphere of `CURVE_EARTH_R`; the
  globe is the ellipsoid. A check measures the gap between the two where they meet (the edge of the
  data, at 20, 60 and 120 km) and picks the sphere's radius as the local Gaussian radius at the
  origin if the gap is over a pixel.
- In the model viewer as a `[Showcase]` set (a few patches and the whole globe).

### 7. Network

- Ground and Transition: nothing changes, positions stay LV95 `GlobalPos`
  (`docs/notes/net/positions-on-the-wire.md`).
- **A body in Space replicates `EcefPos`**: its `NetPlace` and `FootPlayer`'s `NetGlobal` carry a
  frame flag plus three doubles, either LV95 or ECEF; the receiver converts into whatever frame
  it is in. A ground client watching a craft at 100 km converts ECEF → LV95 and lets the
  curvature shader lower it (or `Curvature.Drop` on the CPU if its materials lack `curved_view`).
  `Handshake.Protocol` is bumped.
- `RemoteInterpolator`'s 25 m teleport snap scales with speed: at 7.7 km/s two 20 Hz samples are
  385 m apart. Linear interpolation in ECEF is fine: the chord between them sags 3 mm.
- **Interest in Space is distance only** (ECEF chord), plus an Earth-occlusion test (segment
  against the ellipsoid) instead of the line of sight over `horizon.bin`. `ReportView` already
  reports the camera's far distance, which now grows with altitude.
- The server still never shifts; it measures Space players with their published `EcefPos`.
- Bots stay on the ground for now; a bot in Space would need the same server-side double state
  (row added to `bots/bot-capability-map` when a Space feature adds an action).
- No new key or interaction in this plan: regimes switch on their own, the same for keyboard,
  gamepad and VR (in VR the head camera's altitude decides).

### 8. Multiplanetary (H7)

The same pattern one level up:

- **`Planet` registry**: Earth, Moon, Mars first; each with a, f, GM, rotation (IAU pole and prime
  meridian), atmosphere model and data sources.
- **Frame tree**: heliocentric inertial (ICRF / J2000) → body-centred inertial → body-fixed. An
  anchor always lives in one body-fixed frame; `EcefPos` generalises to a position in a named
  body frame.
- **Sphere-of-influence handovers**: patched conics; crossing r_SOI = a·(m/M)^(2/5) re-expresses
  the craft's double state in the new body's frame and re-anchors, exactly like Ground ↔ Space.
  Moon in Earth: ~66,000 km; Earth in the Sun: ~925,000 km.
- Doubles are enough: at 4.5·10¹² m (Neptune) their step is 1 mm. Planet positions from JPL's
  approximate Keplerian elements.
- Each body's ground regime uses the global tiling of #1443 with that body's ellipsoid.

### 10. Global tiling: the ground anywhere on Earth

Its own plan now: `docs/plans/global-tiling.md` (#1443), the next step after #187 and ahead of the
Space phases here. In short: tiles hold true Earth positions on a cube sphere, the game keeps one
flat Y-up frame (this plan's H3 anchor, used on the ground too), and #187's drop shrinks to
refraction. H2 is its first phase's foundation; H3's anchor is built once for both.

### 9. Features that ride on it (C0-C3)

Small features that make the frame playable early, each a separate issue when it starts.

**A telescope** (**C1 built**, #1440, PR #1441: `docs/notes/items/telescope.md` has it as built; the
moon is the only target until H7, the zoom stops at 8,000 mm with the 4k moon map, and below 1° the
camera is a frustum, `Core/CameraLens`). A telescope on a tripod for looking at the moon and the planets from the ground (or
at the Earth from the moon), and at where other players are out there.

- **Set up, then look through (decided: a tripod)**: carried as an item, set down with Use where a
  ghost shows it would stand (the planted flag's pattern, `items/flag-plant`: one raycast decides
  set down, refuse or pick up), and picked up again the same way. A set-up telescope is a
  replicated object (`NetPlace`) anyone can look through, one at a time, with the interact key, like
  taking a seat; the same key leaves. It is steady at any zoom because it does not move with you.
- **Zoom**: the camera's focal-length zoom (`items/camera-zoom`: FOV = 2·atan(12 / f)), with a much
  longer range. The moon is 0.52° wide, so it fills the view at ~2,300 mm; Jupiter (~45″) needs a
  0.1° view, ~14,000 mm. At that zoom one pixel on the moon is ~0.6 km, so lunar data at ~0.5-1 km
  covers the telescope: NASA's LROC mosaic and LOLA elevation, down-sampled (~60 Mpx at 1 km;
  coarser levels ship, finer ones stream like the Earth's globe data).
- **Tracking**: the sky turns 15″ per second, so at 0.1° a planet crosses the view in 24 s. Locking
  on the body under the crosshair keeps it centred (a go-to mount).
- **Markers**: a 2 m player on the moon is invisible from Earth, so the telescope labels them. The
  server sends every client a small **far-marker list** (players off the Earth or in orbit, their
  position in the body's frame, ~1 Hz), outside the normal interest range. From the moon, the Earth
  is 1.9° wide and Switzerland fills half of a 0.1° view, so the same list shows players at home.
- **Devices**: looking through it, the mouse turns the tube (slowed with the zoom, the camera's
  `LookScale = fov / 76`), the wheel zooms and the left button locks; on the pad the right stick
  turns it, the D-pad zooms (as the camera) and RT locks; in VR you grip the tube to swing it, the
  view is drawn on the eyepiece (a `SubViewport` on the lens), the stick zooms through `XrPad` and
  the trigger locks. Rows in `InputHints` and `xr/vr-action-map`.
- **Bots**: no gameplay effect, so bots do not look through it (a row saying so in
  `bots/bot-capability-map`); a set-up telescope is a small static prop in the server's collision,
  like a planted flag.
- **Detail on the target**: see "Detail by size on screen" below. Without it, a ~400× zoom shows
  the far mountains at the detail picked for their true distance.
- **Needs**: H6 only for the moon (a textured sphere at its ephemeris place in the sky, no globe or
  anchor needed); H7 for the planets and the markers.

**Detail by size on screen.** Today every level-of-detail choice is by distance only: 41
`VisibilityRangeEnd`s on props and signs, the tree shaders' 3D/billboard handover (`tree_near`
measured from `world_cam_pos`, `shaders/body/tree.gdshaderinc`) and the terrain rings
(`RenderDistanceRings`). Zooming breaks that: the camera's 200 mm is already a 5.7× zoom over its
35 mm (a house 300 m away looks 50 m away but is drawn for 300 m), and the telescope zooms ~400×.
What should decide detail is how big a thing is **on screen**, in pixels:
size · H / (2 · d · tan(fov / 2)), with H the viewport's height.

- **One zoom factor for everything that picks detail by distance**: `CameraGlobal` already pushes
  `world_cam_pos` each frame before drawing; it also pushes a `world_lod_scale` global, the active
  camera's tan(fov_ref / 2) / tan(fov / 2) (1 at the normal FOV, ~5.7 at 200 mm, ~400 in the
  telescope), and keeps it in C# for the managers. Each distance LOD compares d / scale instead of
  d: the tree shaders directly; the terrain rings and horizon blocks prefer the view cone, where
  the extra detail goes (a narrow FOV sees a thin cone, so the cost per pixel stays about the same);
  Godot's visibility ranges, which have no FOV term, get their ranges rescaled while zoomed, only
  for what is in the cone.
- **The globe and the moon are built on it from the start**: their cube-sphere patches split when
  their geometric error exceeds ~1 px on screen (the usual screen-space-error rule), so the
  telescope gets lunar detail from the same code that draws the globe from orbit.
- **What else benefits**: the camera item's zoom, the movie studio's cameras, the GPX zoom inset,
  scope-like views in combat if they come. The retrofit of today's distance LODs does not need
  any of this plan and can land on its own first.

**A spaceplane.** A craft that takes off from a runway like a plane, flies to space and moves at
deliberately unrealistic speeds, for easy exploration rather than real rocketry (decided: no top
speed).

- **In the air it is an aircraft**: a new `AirlinerSpec` in `AirlinerCatalog`, flown by
  `AirlinerFlight` with the same controls, gear and runway, plus a rocket thrust that does not lapse
  with altitude. Back in the air it glides home and lands on a runway like the airliners
  (the US Standard Atmosphere of H5 gives it lift up high).
- **In space, a cruise drive** that can be switched on above 60 km (the Space regime). Its speed
  grows with the distance to the nearest surface: v = throttle · d / τ, with τ ≈ 10 s. It slows
  down by itself near any body, which also gives the globe time to stream in (from ten radii out
  down to 100 km above the surface takes about a minute), and the trip time grows only with the
  logarithm of the distance:

  | Trip | Time with τ = 10 s | Top speed on the way |
  |---|---|---|
  | 400 km orbit → lunar orbit | ~2 min | ~19,000 km/s |
  | Earth → Mars (closest, 55 M km) | ~4 min | ~9 times light |
  | Earth → Jupiter (closest, ~590 M km) | ~5 min | ~100 times light |

  Switching the drive off leaves the craft in a circular orbit where it is (an arcade convenience),
  and the orbital physics of H5 take over from there.
- **Precision at those speeds**: while the drive is on, the anchor re-centres on the craft every
  frame (the MSFS way) instead of every 20 km, which is cheap in space: no tiles, only the craft
  and the globe patches, whose transforms are recomputed from doubles. The craft is kinematic,
  integrated in doubles; Jolt plays no part until the drive is off.
- **Network**: its position goes on the wire in the frame of its body (or the heliocentric one) as
  doubles; receivers extrapolate with its velocity rather than interpolate. Out of interest range
  it is still on the far-marker list.
- **Devices**: the airliner's controls, plus a drive switch: a key, a pad button, and in VR a
  switch in the cockpit to grip. Rows in `InputHints` and `xr/vr-action-map`.
- **Bots**: `AirlinerFlight.Controls` is already the intent, so the drive switch is a field on it
  and a bot pilot is possible later; in the first version bots only ride as passengers.
- **Rules that apply**: its own procedural model in the model viewer (`[Showcase]`), its rocket and
  drive sounds in the sound player, Cartoon first.
- **Landing elsewhere**: the moon has no air and no runway, so landing there needs vertical thrust
  and the moon's ground regime (the global tiling question). The first version stays in Earth orbit
  and lands only on Earth's runways.

## Tests

- **Climb and descend with reference screenshots** (with #1429's reference-shot set): a
  `--shot-queue` that flies the spectator camera straight up from Bern to 100 km and back down,
  and the same flight with the UFO. Shots every few km and **the frames either side of each
  handover** (5 km and 20 km where k fades, 50 km where the blend completes, 60 km up, 50 km
  down). A pop shows as a diff spike between neighbouring frames against the frames around it.
- **`--originstress` with rotation**: in Space, a 50 m threshold, so the anchor rotates many times
  a second; `--origincheck` extended to ECEF continuity (< 1 mm per shift over 10,000 shifts) and
  velocities turned with the frame. Each `IOriginShiftAware` handler gets its first non-identity
  basis here.
- **Landing 300 km from take-off**: take off at Geneva, climb to 100 km, cross to Val Müstair
  (~330 km), land. The landing point matches the commanded LV95 to < 1 m, tiles stream in as after
  a teleport, gravity points down and the heading reads right all the way.
- **Orbit**: one period of a 400 km circular orbit at `--fixed-fps` with a time scale; the craft
  returns within 10 m and the energy drift stays under 10⁻⁶.
- **Unit tests**: conversions (H2), the atmosphere table against USSA 1976, the ephemeris against
  NOAA's solar calculator, the shader maths re-implemented in C# against a double reference
  (the exact wrap within 1 cm at 1,000 km).
- **Network** (`tools/test.sh net`): server and two clients; A climbs to 100 km in the UFO and
  travels while B watches from the ground, then B climbs too. On B, A is at the right place in
  every regime pair with no snap; with `--originstress` on both clients.
- The quick tier on every change; the coords check set from #1429 confirms the ground did not move.

## Risks

- **Every `OriginShift` handler meets a rotation for the first time.** The `Transform3D` was chosen
  for this in #185, but nothing has exercised a basis yet. `--originstress` with rotation is the net.
- **Materials outside our shaders** (particles, standard materials) do not follow `curved_view`:
  invisible near the player, wrong for far things seen from altitude. Far objects need the CPU
  drop or a shader of ours.
- **The globe's data**: size, licences (Copernicus DEM and Blue Marble allow free use with
  attribution; check Sentinel-2 cloudless before using it), and a preprocessor path of its own.
  It must not block H1-H3.
- **Shader precision**: the exact wrap in float is safe only written relative to the camera with
  the 2·sin²(θ/2) form; anything else shakes by half a metre.
- **Two height systems**: a wrong sign on the geoid offset puts the globe 100 m off the Swiss tiles
  where they meet. One function, one grid, tested both ways.
- **Gameplay change from the real sun**: thermals by season, and evening events that assume
  darkness. In June the sun sets at Bern around 21:30 CEST, so a concert at 20:30
  (`ConcertEvent.StartHour`) and fireworks at 21:30 (`FireworkShow.StartHour`) start in daylight.
  Trailer renders run on their film's date (`TrailerDirector.FilmDate`), so their light changes
  with that date: re-check each film. Reference screenshots pin `--sun fixed` or a `--date`.

## Out of scope

- Global tiling for the ground anywhere: #1443.
- Weather, clouds and their shadows seen from orbit; oceans with waves; tides.
- Re-entry heating, realistic rocketry, docking and landing on other bodies: this plan is the frame
  they stand on (the spaceplane and telescope above are the only content it carries).
- Perturbations (J2, drag decay, third bodies), n-body physics, time warp beyond the existing
  time scale.
- A double-precision Godot build (rejected in `docs/plans/floating-origin.md`).
