# Industrial sites: warehouses, works, depots and garages

Status: **phase 1 built (#497), phases 2-4 planned.** Today `BuildingKind.Industrial` is the dullest kind in the
game: whatever the solid is — a 4,000 m² distribution hall, a sawmill, a haulier's depot, a
dealership — it becomes one `RoomType.Workshop` with a workbench, two racks and three crates, and
nothing at all outside. This document turns the industrial stock into recognisable **sites**, and
says what each phase costs.

The target is the same one #213 hit for houses: a player who walks into two industrial buildings
should be able to tell, without being told, that one is a warehouse and the other a body shop.

## Where we start from

These facts decide most of the trade-offs below.

1. **`BuildingTypes.Detect(tile)` is a pure function of the `.bldg` tile**, cached per tile object
   and never sent: server and clients agree on it from the same bytes. It knows two types,
   `Church` and `Bank`. Anything derived from the tile alone — footprint box, height, kind,
   neighbouring solids, a stable hash of the key — can join it for free.
2. **A `BuildingGroup` already gives one door per member solid, all opening into one interior.**
   That is how a church's nave and tower work: `InteriorManager.PlanKey` maps any member's door to
   the primary's plan, and `InteriorLayout.Entrances` has an entry per door. A works hall plus its
   office annex plus its silos is the same shape of problem.
3. **A single solid could carry only one door** — `DoorIndex.Entry` was keyed by `BuildingKey` and
   `Find(key)` returned the first match. **Fixed in #498 (PR #500)**: see Phase 2.
4. **The roll-up door exists and is gated on the kind.** `DoorLeaf.RollsUp(kind)` is
   `kind == Garage`; the shut slats are baked in `BuildingMeshBuilder.AppendDoor`, the live leaf is
   `DoorLeaf.CreateRollUp`, and `InteriorManager.OpenForVehicle` already opens a vehicle door for a
   vehicle heading at it from 16 m. Nothing about it is garage-specific but the gate.
5. **`RoomType`, `FurnitureType` and `BuildingType` are append-only**, with stored plans
   regenerated by bumping `InteriorLayout.CurrentVersion`. Furniture is boxes with one collision
   box per piece (`InteriorMeshBuilder.Furniture`), so new props are cheap to author.
6. **Loot keys on `(FurnitureType, RoomType)`** (`LootTables.RoomContainers`) and the room is
   derived from the plan, never stored. New rooms and pieces plug straight in.
7. **`BuildingKey` text is persisted.** `LootService` writes `{TileE}_{TileN}.json` and keys
   records by `BuildingKey.ToString()`. A door identity must therefore stay *separable* from the
   building identity, or every stored loot record breaks.
8. **Industrial facades have no window grid.** `BuildingMeshBuilder.Storeys` returns `(0, 0)` for
   `Industrial`, so the facade is flat sheet metal with one door box on it.
9. **Only an admin conjures vehicles online.** `VehicleManager.MayPark` gates *client* parks; a
   server-initiated `Place` is not gated (`AfricaTwinEgg` relies on this).
10. **#73 (truck delivery mode, open) wants docks.** It says "place docks on the side of the
    building that faces a road". The dock geometry belongs here, and #73 consumes it rather than
    building its own.

## The taxonomy

Six industrial site types, detected from the tile alone. The hash is of the building's own key, so
the type is stable, agreed by every peer, and never sent. The "Detected from" column is this plan's
first sketch; the rules as built are the table in `docs/notes/terrain/industrial-sites.md`.

| Type | Detected from | Inside | Outside |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Warehouse` | big footprint (≥ 600 m²), low-ish and boxy (height ≤ 12 m, one storey), long wall toward a road | aisles of pallet racking, pallets with barrels/boxes/sacks, a goods office in a corner | dock platform, bay doors, trailers backed in, pallet stacks, a skip |
| `Factory` | big footprint, taller (8–20 m), or a silo/stack solid in the group | production hall: conveyor runs, presses and machinery, overhead gantry, control room, locker room, canteen | bay doors, gas tanks, a transformer hut, pipe runs, a yard crane |
| `Depot` (haulier) | medium hall (200–900 m²), **tall door-height facade**, beside a wide road or a paved/parking cover area | truck garage: two to four deep bays, a pit or a lift, big tool chests, a parts mezzanine, a dispatch office | a parked fleet: tractors, trailers, swap bodies; a diesel pump, a wash bay |
| `Mechanic` | small-to-medium (80–400 m²), `Garage` or `Industrial`, near a street | workshop: car lifts, pits, tool chests, tyre stacks, oil drums, a waiting room with a counter | customer cars, wheels, a scrap pile |
| `Dealership` | `Commercial` or `Industrial` with a *large* street-facing wall and a shallow plan | showroom: polished floor, cars on plinths, a sales desk, banners; a workshop wing if the plan allows | stock rows of cars under a flag line |
| `Yard` (no building) | an `Industrial`/`Quarry`/`Landfill` cover area with no solid on it | — | containers, aggregate piles, a weighbridge, a site hut |

`Works` sites group solids the way a church groups nave and tower: the hall is the primary, an
attached or nearby smaller solid becomes a `BuildingPart.Office`, a tall slender one a
`BuildingPart.Silo`, a flat-roofed low one a `BuildingPart.Shed`. Every member keeps its own door
and they all open into one interior, exactly as §2 allows.

## Phase 1 — interiors (#497, **built**)

The whole of the inside, with no change to the facade and no exterior props. This is the #213 move
applied to industry, and it needs nothing new in the networking. What shipped, and where it differs
from this plan as first written — the full detail is `docs/notes/terrain/industrial-sites.md`:

- **`BuildingTypes.SiteFor`**: the five building types above, as a pure function of key, kind,
  footprint and wall height. **No grouping.** A site needs no `BuildingGroup` — `layout.Type` is set
  directly, the way `IsBank` does it — so `BuildingTypes.Detect` is untouched and churches cannot
  regress. Grouping a hall with its office annex and its silos (and `BuildingPart.Office`/`Silo`/
  `Shed`) moves to phase 2, where it belongs with the multi-door work it shares machinery with.
- **`InteriorGenerator.Industrial.cs`**: one hall rising through the building's full height, plus a
  **low service block** of small rooms built against the back wall — a real room with its own 2.6 m
  ceiling standing inside a 9 m hall (`RoomPlan.Clear`), which is how these are really built and
  costs no stairs. That replaced the planned mezzanine and cored office annex, both of which need a
  stair the hall does not have.
- **New rooms** (11, not 14): `WarehouseHall`, `ProductionHall`, `TruckBay`, `ServiceBay`,
  `Showroom`, `ControlRoom`, `LockerRoom`, `BreakRoom`, `PartsStore`, `Dispatch`, `PaintBooth`.
  `Mezzanine`, `WashBay` and `Weighbridge` wait for the stair and the exterior.
- **New furniture** (25): `PalletRack`, `Pallet`, `BarrelStack`, `SackStack`, `Conveyor`, `Machine`,
  `Gantry`, `ToolChest`, `CarLift`, `TyreStack`, `OilDrum`, `Compressor`, `JerryCan`, `SafetySign`,
  `HardHatRack`, `FireExtinguisher`, `Locker`, `Bench`, `Forklift` (static), `ShowroomPlinth`,
  `TruckProp`, `DeskCounter`, `Whiteboard`, `TimeClock`, `Banner`, `FloorMarking`. `VendingMachine`
  already existed (#273) and keeps its per-building dice.
- **The hall lays itself out**, rather than being filled by the generic wall-hugging placer: aisles
  of racking, conveyor lines with machinery and a crane beam, workshop bays with a vehicle up on its
  lift, showroom plinths with a car on each. Car paint varies by hash.
- **Lighting and surfaces**: concrete floors, painted aisle and bay markings in hazard yellow, and a
  **grid of high-bay lamps** in any room over ~11 m a side (`RoomLights`), where one lamp with a
  30 m reach had left a shed's corners black. The industrial hum in `BuildingSounds` was dropped
  from this PR: it needs a new synth bank, which is its own change.
- **Loot**: 14 new containers and the new `(piece, room)` overrides, and `Abundance` becomes a flat
  rate per container for a site (`SiteAbundance`) instead of one building's budget spread over
  however many racking bays its hall fits.
- **Plan version 13**; stored plans regenerate.
- **Checks**: `--sitecheck --systems ui` (`SiteProbe`) plus `IndustrialSiteTests` in tier 0.
  `--lootstats` was the plan, but **it needs real tiles**, and neither the generated world nor the
  fixture world has an industrial building in it — so the probe builds its own, the way `PortalDemo`
  and `ChurchStageProbe` do.

Nothing in phase 1 touches the network, so tier 0.5 is the right tier.

## Phase 2 — loading bays, as real portals

Agreed: the bays are doors you can back a trailer through, not painted-on scenery.

**The door layer is built and pushed — #498, PR #500.** What landed, which supersedes this
section's first sketch:

- **Door identity**: `DoorKey(int TileE, int TileN, int Index, int Slot = 0)` in
  `src/Interiors/DoorKey.cs`, with `.Building` giving the `BuildingKey`. Slot 0's text is
  **byte-identical** to `BuildingKey.ToString()` and extras are `"E_N_I_<slot>"` — an underscore,
  not the `#` this plan first proposed, so one `Split('_')` still parses it and it matches
  `BuildingKey`'s own form. §7's guarantee holds either way: `BuildingKey` is still the building,
  the plan key and the loot key, and `InteriorManager.PlanKey` strips the slot.
- **The leaf is a property of the door, not of the kind**: `DoorHang { Inward, OutwardPair, RollUp }`
  plus `bool Vehicle` on `DoorSpot`/`EntrancePlan`/`DoorLink`/`DoorIndex.Entry`, defaulted from the
  kind by `DoorBudget.HangFor`/`VehicleFor`. A loading bay is simply `Hang = RollUp, Vehicle = true`
  on an Industrial door — `DoorLeaf`, `OpenForVehicle`, `VehicleDoorAhead` and the server's reach
  check all read the door, and driving out of an interior no longer gates on the kind at all. This
  is better than this plan's "`DoorLeaf.RollsUp` becomes a garage's, or an industrial bay's", and
  replaces it.
- **Where doors come from**: `BuildingFootprint.ComputeDoors` returns a flat `DoorSpot[]` with
  several per building; the rules are pure and unit-tested in `src/Interiors/DoorBudget.cs`.
  `InteriorGenerator.FitEntry(l, ground, side, want, width, out room, out center)` picks the
  ground-floor room an extra door's doorway is cut in, clear of other cuts, slab holes and the
  stair flight.

Still to do, in this epic:

- **Bay placement**: `DoorBudget.AlongRun` spaces doors 22 m apart, which is not a loading bay.
  Bays want a per-site-type run — on the longest wall facing the yard, N at 4.5 m centres, each a
  4 m roll-up door (a depot's 4.5 m at 5.0 m centres, a body shop's 3.2 m at 4.0 m, a showroom
  none) — as an override `DoorBudget` consults before its generic rule, keyed on
  `BuildingTypes.SiteFor`. Shape: `readonly record struct DoorRun(int Count, float Spacing,
  float Width, float Height, DoorHang Hang, bool Vehicle)`, returning null to fall through.
- **The dock**: a raised 1.2 m platform inside the bay wall, with bumpers, a canopy and a leveller.
  This is a change to the hall's floor, not just to the door, which is why no dock height was added
  to `DoorSpot` in #498.
- **Facade**: industrial walls get their own dressing — a painted base band, a ridge vent, a
  company sign over the main door (the `SignFlag` machinery the garage sign already uses),
  downpipes, and bay numbers.
- **Testing**: this is authority and replicated state, so **tier 2 (`tools/test.sh net`)**, checked
  on the *remote* peer: a truck backed into bay 3 on one client is in bay 3 on the other, with the
  right leaf up.

### Where the two branches meet

`#498` sizes an extra door's opening as `top = min(door height, StoreyHeight − Slab)` — the
*storey's* headroom. `#497` introduced rooms with a ceiling of their own inside a tall hall
(`RoomPlan.Clear`), so a side door landing on a service-block room's outer wall could be cut
taller than that room. Whichever branch rebases onto the other clamps `top` to `l.ClearOf(room)`.
`InteriorValidator` now **rejects any opening taller than its room**, so this cannot land quietly:
it fails `--sitecheck` the moment the two trees meet. 264 ordinary plans (houses, flats, barns,
garages, shops, churches) pass the new rule unchanged.

## Phase 3 — dormant vehicles in the yards

**The shared layer is #499's, not this epic's, and it is built.** Parking areas need parked cars for
exactly the same reason yards need a fleet, so rather than invent the mechanism twice, #499
("Parking areas as real lots") built `src/Vehicles/VehicleSlot.cs` and
`src/Vehicles/DormantVehicles.cs` generalised over **slot providers**, with parking bays as the
first. The design sketch below is what it was built from, and is kept because this plan is where it
was worked out. **Phase 3 of this epic is then one thing: `DormantSlots.ForSite(...)` as a second
provider** — the fleet mix per site type and the yard positions. No draw, no wake path, no RPC and
no consistency test left to design.

What phase 3 inherits, and the three things it still needs from #499:

- `VehicleSlot(Owner, Ordinal, E, N, Height, Yaw, KindId, Paint, Van)` — pure data, no Godot, linked
  into tier 0. `KindId` is a `RideKind` as an int so the file never reaches into `src/Player`.
- **The node name is the wake-once key**, `veh_slot_<owner>_<ordinal>`, and
  `DormantVehicles.SlotOf()` is the single place that parses it. It first assumed a **tile** owner
  (exactly five underscore parts), which a car park has and a yard does not: a yard's owner is a
  building, `2593_1120_7`, so its name has six and the parse failed — the dormant copy was never
  dropped when the real vehicle appeared, the same double-draw #499's tier-2 check caught for late
  joiners but permanent and invisible to the parking check. Fixed in #499 by taking the **last**
  segment as the ordinal and the rest as the owner, along with two more in the same blast radius:
  `TileOf` made the same assumption and would have refused to wake a yard vehicle at all, and the
  awake-key was built from the tile rather than the owner, so two yards in one tile would have
  woken each other's cars. Pinned by a theory over both owner shapes.
- **A trailer is not a `KindId`.** `RideKind.Trailer` (120) says only "a trailer"; *which* one, and
  how loaded, is `TrailerCatalog` plus a code `(index + 1) | load% << 8` — what
  `FootPlayer.TrailerCode` replicates, and what makes a timber trailer's logs and a tanker's slosh.
  One `ushort TrailerCode` on the record (0 = none) covers the lot: with a trailer `KindId` it is a
  trailer standing on its legs; with a tractor `KindId` it is a **coupled train**, woken as one
  `VehicleState.Train` with straight articulation. A parked artic must never be two adjacent slots —
  the pin angles are part of the parked state and would drift.
- **Re-sleeping** stays off, as first planned, but the wake trigger is what decides whether it is
  ever needed: wake on *intent* (a `VehicleReach` aim, a real impact, a shot), never on proximity,
  or a player walking through a forty-car showroom leaves forty live `VehicleBody` nodes behind.
  Measure the live count after a busy session first. If re-sleep does become necessary, the
  condition has to be strict — still within ~0.3 m and ~5° of its slot pose, undamaged, unclaimed,
  nobody within 200 m — because a vehicle that re-sleeps anywhere else teleports, and one that
  re-sleeps damaged silently repairs itself.

The yards need a *fleet*: a haulier with twelve tractors and twenty trailers, a dealership with
forty cars. Spawning forty replicated `VehicleBody` nodes per site is not affordable, and making
them scenery means you cannot drive the dealership stock away. So: **dormant vehicles**, promoted
to real ones only when someone actually touches them.

- **Placement is a pure function**, like `BuildingTypes`: `SiteVehicles.For(tile)` yields
  `(DoorId site, byte slot, VehicleSlot)` where the slot carries LV95 position, yaw, `RideKind`,
  catalogue index and colour, all hashed from the site key and slot ordinal. Server and every
  client compute the same fleet from the same bytes; nothing is sent.
- **Dormant draw**: the shared cached meshes `Traffic` already uses for cars at up to 600 m, as one
  MultiMesh per tile per kind. Collision from a pool of static bodies for the nearby ones, the way
  `TreeColliders` pools tree collision rather than building it per tile.
- **Waking**: anything that would move a dormant vehicle — `VehicleReach` aiming at it, a collision
  with it, a shot — makes the client ask the server `WakeSiteVehicle(site, slot)`. The server
  promotes it exactly once through `VehicleManager.Place` under the deterministic node name
  `veh_site_<E>_<N>_<I>_<slot>`, and every peer that sees that name stops drawing the dormant copy
  (the check `AfricaTwinEgg` already does for its bike). A server-initiated `Place` is not subject
  to `MayPark` (§9).
- **Sleeping again** is the open question: simplest is "once woken, it stays a vehicle for the
  session"; nicer is "a woken vehicle left in its own slot, with nobody within 200 m, goes back to
  dormant". Start with the first, measure, then decide.
- **Cost to measure before merging**: dormant draw per tile, the wake round-trip, and the count of
  live vehicles after a busy session.
- **A trailer is not a car slot.** `RideKind.Trailer` (120) is a lone trailer in the world, never
  mounted, and what it *is* lives in a second index: `TrailerCatalog` plus a **code**
  `(index + 1) | load% << 8`, which is what `FootPlayer.TrailerCode` replicates. A slot that can be
  a trailer therefore needs that code, not just a catalogue index. A coupled train is worse: a
  tractor and its trailer park as **one** `VehicleState.Train` with `Angles`, `Flags` and `Load`,
  and the server counts it as two vehicles for `MayPark` — so a parked artic is one slot holding a
  train, never two slots that happen to be adjacent. See `docs/notes/player/trucks-buses.md`.

## Other ideas worth having

Beyond the agreed list, in rough order of value for effort:

1. **Shift and time of day.** A works at 03:00 is dark with a few security lamps; at 10:00 the
   high bays are on, the yard gate is open and the staff car park is full. `DayNight` already
   drives window light; the yard fleet and the bay doors can read the same clock. Cheap, and it is
   the single thing that most makes a site feel inhabited.
2. **Site-specific industry, from the real data.** TLM already maps `Quarry`, `Landfill` and
   `Industrial` usage areas, and the cover raster is loaded per tile. A works on a quarry area is a
   crushing plant with aggregate piles and a weighbridge; on a landfill, a recycling hall with
   baled waste; next to a watercourse with a weir, a small hydro station. This costs one cover
   lookup and buys real regional variety.
3. **Sawmills along the forest edge.** An `Industrial` hall with `Forest` cover within 100 m gets
   log decks outside, a debarker and a band saw inside, and timber stacks. Switzerland has
   hundreds.
4. **Cold stores and silos.** `GKLAS` 1252 is explicitly "tanks, silos and warehouses". A tall
   slender industrial solid should be a silo — no floors, a ladder and a filling pipe — not a room.
   Today it gets a door on a round wall and a 16 m-tall empty box.
5. **Safety as decoration with meaning.** Hard hats on a rack by the door, high-vis on pegs, a
   first-aid station, eye-wash, fire points, floor-marked walkways, "Hörschutz tragen" signs in the
   three languages by canton. The clothing already exists (`Garments`, `HumanMeshBuilder.Clothing`),
   so a hard hat and a high-vis vest can be *wearable loot* found where they should be.
6. **Rail sidings.** `tools/` already builds railways. A warehouse whose tile has a siding ending
   at it gets a rail dock, a buffer stop and a wagon. This is the detail that reads as "real
   Switzerland" from a passing train.
7. **Compound fences and gates.** A site boundary traced from the cover area: palisade or chain
   link, a sliding gate on the road side, a gatehouse. It makes a yard a *place* rather than props
   on open ground, and gives BR and combat real cover.
8. **Wear, not uniformity.** Rust streaks, a patched roof panel, graffiti on the base band, weeds
   at the fence line, an abandoned site with boarded bays (which #434's "messy and abandoned" wants
   for houses anyway — share the mechanism).
9. **Noise and smell.** A hum and a compressor cycle near a works, a reversing beeper in a yard, a
   sawmill's whine, extractor fans on the roof. `OccasionAmbience` and `BuildingSounds` already
   have the hooks.
10. **A weighbridge that weighs you.** Drive a truck onto it and a sign shows the mass. Trivial to
    build on top of the vehicle mass that already exists, and it is exactly the kind of toy that
    #73's delivery career wants.

## Risks

- **The plan-version bump regenerates every stored interior.** Expected, and #213 did the same, but
  it means old loot take-masks stop lining up for one restock period.
- **Big halls are expensive.** A 4,000 m² warehouse full of racking is far more geometry than a
  house. The bay grid must thin by distance from the door, or cap the piece count by area, and
  `--perflog` must be read before merging.
- **The door-id change in phase 2 is broad and touches the server's entry checks.** It is the one
  part of this that can break ordinary houses, so it stays its own PR with tier-2 tests.
- **Dormant vehicles are a new consistency surface.** If a client's fleet disagrees with the
  server's, a player sees a truck that is not there. The placement function must be a pure function
  of the tile bytes, with a test that pins its output.
