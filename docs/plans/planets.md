## The goal

The user's words: "planet terrain, aka earth is not the only planet anymore. this probably means adding an
interplanetary coordinates system to allow seamless travel between different reference systems", and then: "my main
goal is that you handle all planets (including earth) with the same systems: data based, with fallbacks, generation".

So the Earth stops being special. It becomes **body 0 of a registry**, and every body (the Moon, Mars, Mercury, Venus,
the big moons, the giants and the Sun) is described by the same record and drawn, streamed, generated, flown round
and landed on by the same code. A body differs only by its **data** (what exists at which resolution) and its
**profile** (the rules that generate what the data lacks). Where real data exists it is used; where it stops, a
coarser level stands in; where nothing exists, the body is generated from its profile. The Earth already works this
way (Swiss leaves, the ETOPO and Blue Marble pyramid, the shipped levels 0-2, the fill and its biomes); H7 makes that
chain take a body.

## 2. Time: the sky clock and the physics clock

The crux. The game's day lasts 24 real minutes (`GameSettings.DayLengthMinutes`): the sky (`DayNight.Sky`, H6's
ephemeris) turns 60 times faster than the real one. H5's orbits integrate on sim time with the Earth's real spin
(`Orbit.RotationAngle` = ω·t), or the ground would race under an orbit at 28 km/s. Both are right for what they do; H7
needs a rule for where the other bodies are.

- **Where bodies are and how they are turned: the sky clock**, one moment for every peer (`SkyMoment`, two parts,
  both from server-owned clocks): **how the Earth is turned** follows the game hour (the 24-minute day, as the sky does
  today), and **when the bodies are** (each one's place on its orbit, the Moon's and the planets' spins) follows the
  shared sim clock from the calendar date. The Moon in the sky, the Moon in the telescope and the Moon you land on are
  the same Moon: C3's test (B sees A's marker) depends on it. Seen from the Earth, the Moon rises about every 25 minutes
  and its phase follows the real calendar, exactly as today; on the Moon, a lunar day lasts its real 29.5 days.
- **Physics inside a sphere of influence: sim time, the body's real spin.** Every body gets H5's integration frame:
  its body-fixed axes at sim time 0, turned by `Spin`·t. A lunar orbit, a landing, a walk on the Moon run in the Moon's
  frame exactly as an Earth orbit runs in the Earth's today. `Orbit.RotationAngle` takes the body; nothing else in H5
  changes.
- **Across a sphere-of-influence boundary: patched conics with the body's true velocity.** The position is re-expressed
  exactly. The velocity loses the entered body's **true orbital velocity** (its real motion about its parent: the
  ephemeris' derivative per real second, ~1.02 km/s for the Moon, turned into the integration frame), and gains it back on
  the way out. It never takes the sky clock's swing: at the 24-minute day the Moon sweeps through the Earth's integration
  frame at ~1,650 km/s (the sky's turn at its distance), and subtracting that would fling a coasting craft at the Moon at
  that speed. So arrival speeds are the real ones (a coast from the Earth reaches the Moon at about 1 km/s relative to
  it) whatever the day length; only the Moon's place moves on faster.
- **`/time speed real` makes it exact.** The command already exists (a game day a real day, `World/TimeCommand`): the
  sky clock then ticks with sim time, the Earth's turn in the sky equals its turn under an orbit, the Moon goes round in
  27.3 days, there is no swing, and the rule above is plain patched conics, KSP's model. What stays arcade even then is
  by choice: the cruise drive's and the spectator's speeds, patched conics instead of n-body, the ground game's tuned
  9.81 m/s².
- **The anchor** stays body-fixed (today's rotating ECEF anchor, generalised). Bodies other than the anchor's are
  placed every frame from the frame tree at the sky moment. In the Earth's anchor the Moon moves at the sky's pace; in
  the Moon's, the Earth hangs still on the near side and spins once a game day.
- **Checks** pin it: planet checks run `--sun real --date 2027-07-15 --time <h>` (the frame tree needs a real date;
  `--sun fixed` keeps its fixed light for everything else and puts the bodies at the fixed date's noon).
- **Found building H7a (#1847, 2026-11-14): the sky's date does not advance with game days.** The sky's moment is
  today's calendar date plus the game hour (`SunPath.Today`, `DayNight`), so at game midnight the bodies jump back a
  day (the Moon by ~13° of its orbit, ~87,000 km). Nobody sees it on the ground; a frame tree on it would. Three ways
  out: (a) the bodies at the sim clock's pace from the calendar date; (b) the bodies at the game's pace (the date
  advancing each game day: a lunar day of ~12 real hours, but the Sun's season drifting away from the calendar 60 times
  as fast, and with it the thermals, the snow line, the occasions' light); (c) today's, with the jump. **Decided: (a)**
  (logged below): continuous, nothing changes on the ground, the seasons stay the calendar's, and with `/time speed
  real` it is the real sky to the second. The price is the real lunar day: two weeks of sun, two of night, so the night
  gets its ambient floor and checks and shots pick landing sites in daylight. Wired in #1850.

### A worked example: from Geneva to the Moon and back

- **On the ground in Geneva**, as today: the sky clock runs 60 game seconds a real second. The Moon rises, crosses and
  sets in about 25 real minutes, and its phase follows the real calendar. Physics is real time.
- **Climbing by spectator**: the position is in the Earth's coordinates. The Moon is where the sky clock says, 384,000 km
  away, and it crosses the sky at the sky's usual pace (a quarter of a degree a real second). Measured in Earth
  coordinates, that is ~1,650 km/s. The spectator's speed grows with the distance to the nearest surface (tens of
  thousands of km/s halfway), so it catches up, and the target cue leads it.
- **66,000 km from the Moon** (its sphere of influence): on that frame the position is re-expressed in the Moon's
  coordinates. Same place, same view, nothing moves on screen. From now on the Moon stands still under you, and the
  Earth hangs in its sky, turning once a game day.
- **Landing and walking**: physics in the Moon's frame, real time, gravity 1.62 m/s². The Moon's own spin is slow (one
  turn in 27.3 days), so the ground under a walker effectively does not turn. The sun crosses the lunar sky in two
  real weeks, then two weeks of night (with an ambient floor): a landing site is in daylight or not for days.
- **A craft coasting across the boundary** (C3a, later): it loses the Moon's true ~1 km/s, not the sky clock's
  ~1,650 km/s swing, so it arrives as a real transfer would. The spectator and the cruise drive never meet it: their
  speed is always relative to the body they are near.
- **Going home**: the same in reverse; at 66,000 km the position goes back to Earth coordinates, invisibly.

What it says for us: we can't shrink the Earth (KSP, Outer Wilds), and we keep the 24-minute day (Elite has a real-time
one). So we do what Space Engineers' orbit mod and Star Citizen do: **the sky is a clock-driven picture, and physics
runs in each body's own frame**, with a rule at the boundary. Star Citizen's trouble is a boundary between frames that
turn differently. Our sphere-of-influence handover avoids it: the anchor is re-expressed, not moved, and the velocity
changes only by the body's true orbital velocity. KSP's rotating frame low and inertial frame high is what H5 already
does (body-fixed anchor, inertial integration). And `/time speed real` gives one clock, as KSP and Elite have.

## 3. Frames and positions

### The frame tree (`Core/Frames`, plain C#)

- Nodes: the Sun's inertial frame (ICRF axes, J2000 equator, heliocentric: the root), and for each body an inertial frame
  (its centre, ICRF axes) and a body-fixed one (its centre, `Orientation(t)`).
- Body centres: planets from `Ephemeris`' Keplerian elements (made public: `Heliocentric(i, t)`, today private), the
  Earth from the barycentre less the Moon's share, the Moon geocentric (Meeus 47), Mars' moons and the big moons from
  their circles (`Ephemeris.Satellites`; Phobos and Deimos added). Velocities by central difference over ±60 s of sky
  time. Light time and aberration stay in the sky's drawing only: the frame tree is geometric.
- `Frames.Transform(BodyFrame from, BodyFrame to, SkyTime t)` → a rigid transform in doubles (`DTransform`: a 3×3 and
  a `DVec3`) and the frame's velocity. Cached per sky moment (one evaluation per frame, ~50 µs a body measured for the
  sky today).
- **Spheres of influence**: Laplace's r = a·(m/M)^(2/5) of the parent; a position belongs to the deepest body whose
  sphere holds it. Entered at r, left at 1.05·r (no flicker at the edge).

### `GlobalPos` gets a body

- `GlobalPos` (three ECEF doubles today, `Core/GlobalPos.cs:35`) gains `Body` (a byte, `default` = Earth). Its doubles
  are that body's body-fixed frame. `Alt`, `Geodetic`, `Enu`, `Moved`, `WithAlt` and the midpoint ask the body's shape
  and datum instead of `Ellipsoid.Wgs84` and `SwissGeodesy.Lhn95Height`. Earth's results are bit for bit today's (the
  Earth datum *is* today's code).
- Arithmetic between two positions of different bodies goes through `Frames` at the current sky moment
  (`a.In(body, t)`); same-body arithmetic is unchanged. A debug assert catches mixed-body arithmetic that skips it.
- `EcefPos` stays the plain triple (the tools' type); `GlobalPos.Body` says whose frame it is in.
- The LV95 helpers (`FromLv95`, `Lv95`) assert `Body == Earth`; D3 deletes them anyway.
- **Precision**: body-fixed doubles of the SOI body. Worst case inside a planet's sphere: 925,000 km → 0.2 µm. In the
  Sun's frame at Neptune: 1 mm. A float never holds a global position: world space is the anchor's, within 20 km.

### The anchor across bodies

- `FrameAxes` (origin and axes, ECEF today) gains the body. `FrameAxes.Tangent`, `Enu` and `OriginFrame.UpAt`/`EnuAt`
  ask the body's shape.
- **A sphere-of-influence crossing is a relabelling**: the anchor's pose (origin, axes) at that moment is re-expressed in
  the new body's frame through `Frames.Transform`. World space is untouched, so no handler sees a shift. Positions read
  afterwards come out in the new body. `OriginShifter` emits an `AnchorBodyChanged` event for the few systems that keep a
  body (the globe set, the sky's observer, gravity, the HUD).
- Anchor shifts keep their rules per body (20 km in Space, 3 km on the ground). While the drive is on, the anchor
  re-centres every frame (the parent plan's rule).

## 4. One surface chain for every body

What the Earth does today, made per body. A body's terrain is one `BodySurface` (per body, created when the body is
near or in view): its pyramid, its leaves, its fill, its datum and its sea. The client holds the anchor's body near
and the others far; the server holds every body that has a player on or near it.

### The chain, from best to fallback

1. **Leaves from fine data** (`CubeKey` at the body's leaf level, `.cubl` sections): the Earth's regions (Switzerland,

4. **Generated**: anything finer than the best level the chain has is generated by the body's profile (section 5) on top

|---|---|---|---|---|
| `EarthProfile` | Earth | today's: the generator through `Lv95Source`, #1678's pyramid relief | #1809's biomes (photo guess, snow and tree lines, sea) | trees, farms, villages (today's fill) |
| `CrateredProfile` | Moon, Mercury, Callisto, Phobos, Deimos | a crater field by the lunar size-frequency law (a crater of diameter D per area ∝ D⁻², from the data's resolution down to 2 m): bowls with rims and ejecta, fresh ones sharp, old ones softened; a low fractal | regolith (grey, the data's albedo carried down), bright ejecta rays on fresh craters | boulders near fresh rims (static props with collision), dust |
| `MarsProfile` | Mars | craters (softened by wind), dune fields in low basins, layered mesas on steep ground | ochre dust, dark basalt sand, polar ice caps by latitude and season | rocks by a thermal-inertia guess |
| `IcyProfile` | Europa, Ganymede, Titan's highlands | ridges and cracks (Europa), grooved terrain (Ganymede), few craters | ice, the mosaic's albedo | ice blocks |
| `VolcanicProfile` | Io, Venus | lava plains, shields, flows | sulphur colours (Io), basalt (Venus) | |

The relief of a profile is band-limited to below the finest data level, so a leaf never contradicts its pyramid tile (the
same rule the Earth's fill follows). Where a body has no heights at all, its albedo map gives a low relief (a coarse,
honest guess) and the profile does the rest.

## 6. Rendering: one renderer from a dot to the ground

`World/BodyView`, one per body, picks its stage by the body's size on screen (C0's `world_lod_scale`):

1. **A point** by magnitude (`SkyBodies` today; also by day for Venus).
2. **The textured sphere** (`SkyBodies`' sphere and `MoonDisc`: their 2k and 4k maps, their axes, lit from the body's
   own sun direction), drawn just inside the far plane at its true angular size: this is scaled space already.
3. **The globe** (H4's renderer, made per body): the body's pyramid, in **scaled space** while the body lies past the far
   plane (the whole globe shrunk about the camera by 0.9·far / distance: same angular size, same occlusion), at true
   scale once inside it. Non-anchor bodies hang under one root node each, its transform recomputed every frame from the
   frame tree (patch floats relative to the body's centre: 0.5 m at the Earth's radius, invisible from the Moon).
4. **The leaves**, near the anchor's body only (`EarthWorld`, made per body).

Stages 2 → 3 cross-fade over a factor of two in screen size; the sphere's map and the globe's level 0 come from the same
source so the hue does not jump. The **atmosphere** (`Globe/Atmosphere`, the full-screen quad) takes the body's air: the
Earth's Rayleigh and Mie, Mars' dust (butterscotch by day, blue round the sun at dusk), none on the Moon. It draws for
the anchor's body and for any body in view that has air (the Earth's limb seen from the Moon).

**The sky from another body** (with H6's space sky, now being built in its own session): the observer is a `GlobalPos`
on any body; the sun's direction, the stars' frame and every other body come from the frame tree. On the Moon: a black
sky at noon, the stars by day, the Earth hanging still in the near side's sky with its phase, earthshine on the night side.
The lunar night lasts two real weeks: it gets an ambient floor (earthshine on the near side, a
starlight floor on the far side) so it stays playable.

## 7. Physics on and around any body

- **Gravity on the ground**: the body's `GroundGravity` (the Earth keeps its tuned 9.81). Set on the physics space for
  the anchor's body (client) and per server region (each region knows its body). A walker jumps six times as high on
  the Moon; a car drives with a sixth of its grip from weight.
- **Orbits**: `SpaceOrbit` and `Orbit` take the body (they already take a `Planet`): GM/r², the body's integration
  frame (section 2), drag from the body's air (none on the Moon). `Orbit.Circular`, `Apsides`, `Period` per body.
  `/orbit <km> [body]` (admin online, free offline): over where you are, or round a named body.
- **Regimes per body**: Space in/out at the body's thresholds (section 1). An airless body is in the "Space" physics
  regime down to 8 km: below it a craft flies the ordinary flight model with zero air (attitude by jets, no lift).
- **Sphere-of-influence handovers in `SpaceOrbit`**: the double state re-expressed in the new body's frame (section 2's
  velocity rule); the HUD's `ORBIT` line names the body (`ORBIT Moon 1.63 km/s, 100 km, Pe, Ap`).

## 8. Travel

### First: the spectator (H7d)

The spectator camera is the first way between bodies (the user's order: online play before the shuttle).

- **Its speed follows the nearest surface of any body**: `SpectatorSpeed.AltitudeCap` (today 0.5 m/s per metre of
  altitude, at least 3 km/s, the wheel up to 100 km/s) takes `Bodies.NearestSurface(pos)` instead of the Earth's
  altitude, and the wheel's top grows past the Earth's sphere so the cap rules (the ground slides by at the same angular
  rate everywhere, as a map zooms). From 400 km over the Earth to 100 km over the Moon at full wheel: about a minute.
  The same keys, pad buttons and VR way as today.
- **Aiming**: the target list (the telescope's: the bodies, later the far markers) as a HUD cue while spectating, with
  the distance and the time at the current speed. Choosing a target is the one new action (section 11).
- **Crossing a sphere of influence** is the anchor's relabelling (section 3): nothing moves on screen.
- **Stepping in** (`StepInFromSpectator`) works on any body: the walker lands under the camera with that body's ground
  gravity. Offline at once; online the server agrees and the body is published with its body (C3b).
- `/goto <body> <lat> <lon>` stays as the admin's and the checks' shortcut.

### Then: craft (C3a, after online play)

- **The cruise drive between bodies**: v = lever · d / τ (τ = 10 s), **d the distance to the nearest surface of any
  body** (`Bodies.NearestSurface(pos)`: min over the bodies of the distance to the centre less the datum radius; the
  giants' 1-bar level, the Sun's photosphere plus 5 radii). It slows by itself near anything and streams the target's
  globe in on the way. Its speed is relative to the body the craft is in.

  | Trip | Time (τ = 10 s) | Top speed |
  |---|---|---|
  | 400 km Earth orbit → 100 km lunar orbit | ~2 min | ~19,000 km/s |
  | Earth → Mars (at 55 M km) | ~4 min | ~9 c |
  | Earth → Jupiter (at 590 M km) | ~5 min | ~100 c |

- **Aiming**: a **target** (the body, or a player from the far-marker list) chosen with a new action, shown as a cue on
  the HUD and on the windscreen (direction, distance, time at the current speed), and an **align** hold that turns the
  nose onto it with lead for its motion (the drive's turn rate, calm as #1528 asked). The same target list as the
  telescope's (`TelescopeTargets`).
- **Arrival**: the drive slows on its own; switched off, the craft is in a circular orbit of the body it is in, at that
  height (`ShuttleFlight.DriveOff` with the body). From orbit: a retro burn as today.
- **Landing where there is no air or runway**: below the body's Space exit on an airless body (Moon: 8 km), the levers
  become **belly jets** (vertical thrust along the craft's up, up to 0.6 g of the Earth: 3.6 times the shuttle's lunar
  weight), the jets hold the attitude level, and the shuttle comes down on its gear anywhere flat enough (a slope limit
  read from the leaf; the HUD's radar height, vertical speed and a slope warning). No new key: the levers do it.
  Take-off is the same backwards: belly jets to 8 km, then the rockets and the drive. On Mars the thin air gives little
  lift: the same belly jets land it (the heat of entry still glows).
- **The UFO** flies there as well: its hover is already a vertical lander (gravity per body), its space drive and
  `/orbit` work per body. It gets the cruise drive's target selector and drive in C3a too (same `ShuttleDrive` seam), so
  two craft can go.
- **On foot on the Moon**: walking, jumping and the camera under the body's gravity; no suit, no oxygen (arcade, as the
  shuttle's unlimited fuel).

## 9. The far-marker list and the telescope (C3b)

- **Server → every client, 1 Hz**: every player `(peer id, body, body-fixed position as 3 doubles, vehicle kind)`, about
  30 bytes a player: 2 KB/s at 64 players. Players in a client's normal interest are skipped (it already has them).
- **The telescope** labels any marker in its view: the name, the body, the distance (km or AU). A marker is placed by the
  frame tree from the observer's position at the sky moment, so a shuttle in lunar orbit is seen at the right place on
  the Moon's disc from Geneva. The target menu lists players on other bodies after the bodies; the lock follows them.
- **The HUD**: the shuttle's target cue can be a player from the list.
- From the Moon, the Earth is 1.9° wide and Switzerland fills half of a 0.1° view: the same list labels players at home.

## 10. The network (C3b)

  remote body that changes `NetBody` is snapped once, re-expressed through `Frames` (it happens far from anyone).
- **Interest off the Earth**: two players on different bodies are never in each other's interest (the far-marker list
  covers them). On one body: the chord distance, and the body's sphere as the occluder (the Earth keeps `EarthSight`

- **Server regions per body**: a region carries its body; its frame, its gravity and its collision (the body's leaves,

- **Every client RPC carrying a position** carries `GlobalPos` (so the body); the D2 audit's list of RPCs with LV95 is

## 14. Risks

- **The Earth regresses while being generalised** (H7b touches the globe, the fill, the anchor). Bar: identical reference
  shots and numbers; the Earth's code paths become the general ones by moving constants, not by rewriting logic.
- **The two clocks** confuse whoever reads physics and sky together: one note (`world/planets`) and one function per
  clock (`SkyClock`, `SimClock`), never a mix.
- **Scaled space and the depth buffer**: a scaled globe past the far plane meets the same depth floor the stars met
  (#1518); it reuses `sky_depth.gdshaderinc`'s rule.
- **The atmosphere quad for a body that is not the anchor's** (the Earth's limb from the Moon): it is drawn from the
  depth and one sphere today; two spheres need a second pass or the scaled globe's own limb.
- **Data size**: the Moon's pyramid ~1.5 GB, Mars ~0.5 GB, on E: and the server; only levels 0-2 ship.
- **Licences**: HRSC (share-alike) and Kaguya are out unless the user decides otherwise; everything else is public
  domain or CC0 with credits added to the credits screen.
- **D2 and parallel sessions**: H7a and H7b touch `GlobalPos`, `WorldOrigin`, `Globe`, `DayNight`. D2 (#1727) and H6's
  space sky are open: H7a branches after D2 merges where it can, touches D2's files only for the body field, and H7b
  waits for H6's space sky.

## Decided without the user (review)

- **The Earth is body 0**, so `default(GlobalPos)` and every key, file and record of today stay valid without a migration.
- **`CubeKey` keeps no body**; the body travels with the store and the request. No file name changes.
- **Leaf level per body** (Earth 14, Moon 12, Mars 13) so leaves stay ~0.6 km and every leaf radius holds.
- **Cube orientation per body**: the Earth keeps Bern and its twist; others put face 0 on 0° N 0° E with no twist.
- **The anchor stays body-fixed**; inertial frames are only for integration.
- **SOI by Laplace's formula**, left at 1.05 r; Phobos and Deimos get a 20 km floor.
- **Ground gravity scaled from the Earth's tuned 9.81** by the true ratio.
- **The bodies run on the sim clock from the calendar date** (section 2, option (a)): the sky's date jumped back a
  day at every game midnight; this keeps the ground's sky as it is and the seasons the calendar's. The lunar day is its
  real 29.5 days.
- **The lunar night gets an ambient floor** so a two-week night stays playable.
- **Space regime thresholds per body** (Moon 10/8 km, Mars 40/30 km).
- **The Moon's pyramid stops at level 5** (167 m cells, from LOLA's 118 m); the rest is generated.
- **Belly jets on the existing levers**, switched on by the regime: no new key for landing.
- **The far-marker list carries every player** at 1 Hz (simple, ~2 KB/s at 64).
- **No suit, no oxygen** on other bodies (arcade, like the shuttle's unlimited fuel).

## Out of scope

- Real rocketry, delta-v budgets, n-body physics, Lagrange points, time warp beyond the time scale.
- Bases, buildings or roads on other bodies; resources and crafting there.
- Phobos and Deimos as anything more than small landable rocks; rings as places.
- Weather and dust storms on Mars, Titan's lakes and rain.
- A bot pilot (#1498 first).
