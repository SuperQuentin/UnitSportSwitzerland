# Connecting interiors and exteriors visually

Status: **exploration, nothing decided.** Written ahead of battle royale (BR) mode, where "I can see
you through that window" stops being a nice touch and becomes a rule of the game.

Today a building is two unrelated things: a closed shell in the street and a room floating in the
dark 3 km underground. From the street you can't see anyone inside, and from inside you can't see
out. This document lists the ways to join the two, with what each costs and what it buys.

## Where we start from

These facts decide most of the trade-offs below.

1. **Interior placement is a pure vertical shift.** `InteriorManager.PlacementFor` puts each
   interior under its building's own centre, turned to the building's own yaw, at
   `InteriorBaseY = -3000`. Going from outside to inside is therefore one translation
   `Δ = (0, InteriorBaseY − groundY, 0)` per building, with no rotation and no scale. Any "portal"
   maths reduces to adding or subtracting Δ.
2. **Visibility is by space, not by sight.** Players in different spaces (outdoors, or a given
   interior) are hidden, their collision is disabled, and their synchroniser stops sending to each
   other (`FootPlayer`: `SameSpaceAsLocal` visibility filter). This is also our interest
   management for 100 players.
3. **Exterior buildings are closed solids, merged per tile.** One mesh and one
   `ConcavePolygonShape3D` per tile, built from swissBUILDINGS3D TINs. They have no wall
   thickness and no openings. Doors are boxes stuck on the facade.
4. **Exterior windows are a shader pattern, not geometry.** `ps1_building` draws a grid every 3.2 m
   along each facade, one row per storey (`BuildingMeshBuilder.Storeys`). Interior windows are
   planned per room (`InteriorGenerator.AddWindows`) and drawn as flat unlit "daylight". **The two
   don't line up.** Neither do storey heights in general.
5. **The interior plan is a rectangle, not the real outline.** The min-area oriented box
   (`PlanBox`) is used for plans. An L-shaped house gets a rectangle that pokes out past its real
   walls, and a church is a nave plus towers snapped together.
6. **Renderer is `mobile`.** Each extra viewport is a full scene render, and there's little
   headroom for per-pixel tricks.
7. **Useful building blocks already exist.** The building and tree shaders have a sightline
   dissolve (`sightline_keep`) that discards fragments between the camera and the player. Reverb
   zones and footstep surfaces already know whether the player is indoors.

## Prerequisite for everything: one window truth

Any real view in or out (options A, B, C, E) looks wrong until an exterior window and an interior
window are the same hole. Two ways to get there:

- **Exterior follows the plan.** Bake each building's planned openings into mesh attributes, or a
  small per-building texture, so the facade shader draws windows only where the plan put them.
  - Pro: the plan is already the authority for doors, loot and collision.
  - Con: every building needs a plan before it can be drawn, even the thousands nobody enters.
    Plans are deterministic, so clients could compute them locally, but that's CPU on the tile
    worker. Storey heights change the facade look project-wide.
- **Plan follows the facade.** Make `AddWindows` place windows on the exterior grid: same storey
  height, same 3.2 m spacing, same phase along each wall (the shader's `u` is metres along the
  facade, so the phase is computable).
  - Pro: no change to how 100k buildings are drawn, and no plan needed to draw one.
  - Con: rooms must accept windows where the grid falls: a wall between two rooms may split a
    window, and bathrooms get big windows.

**Leaning: plan follows facade.** It's cheaper and local to interiors. Wall positions snap to
fall between grid windows. Also needed for A/B/C: plans that follow the **real outline** instead
of the min-area box, at least for walls with openings.

## Options

### A. Portal views, both ways

Render what's on the other side of each opening into a texture and show it in the opening.

- **How:**
  - Inside looking out: a second camera at `mainCamera + Δ⁻¹` (up by 3 km), rendering into a
    `SubViewport` shown in the window's glass.
  - Outside looking in: the same idea, shifted down by Δ.
  - Because of fact 1, the portal camera is a copy of the main camera with a different Y: no
    oblique maths, no rotation.
- **Clipping:** the portal camera looking out starts *inside* the building's closed shell, so the
  shell's own walls block the view. Everything between the portal camera and the window plane has
  to be discarded. The sightline dissolve is nearly that already: a global "portal clip plane"
  uniform, honoured by the building, terrain, prop and tree shaders.
- **Gameplay:**
  - Players seen through a portal must be replicated, so interest management becomes "same space,
    or visible through an opening of a building near me".
  - Hitscan that crosses a window rectangle is re-cast in the other space by adding Δ.
  - A thrown object crossing the plane is teleported by Δ. Same maths, server-side.
- **Pros:**
  - No change to how the world or the interiors are built.
  - Works for every building, at any distance, with the same code.
  - Low resolution is on-style (PS1): a portal viewport at a quarter resolution looks right.
  - Symmetric by construction, which is good for BR fairness.
- **Cons:**
  - One extra scene render per active portal. On `mobile` that means a hard cap: the 1–3 openings
    nearest the camera, others fall back to option D/E.
  - Every system that reasons about sight or space must become portal-aware: AI sight lines,
    audio occlusion, projectiles, network relevance, loot "who is in this building". That's a
    permanent tax on every feature.
  - Seams at the opening edge (lighting and fog differ between the two cameras), and shadows don't
    cross.
  - Needs the one-window-truth work.
- **Effort:** medium to build, high to make every system portal-aware.

### B. Render interiors in place (hollow buildings)

Drop `InteriorBaseY`: build the interior inside the real building, and make the building hollow
around it.

- **How:**
  - Generate a hollow shell for the building: outer skin from the TIN, an inner skin offset by a
    wall thickness, and openings cut where the plan's doors and windows are.
  - Swap that building's triangles out of the tile's merged mesh and collision.
  - Build the interior at the building's real position.
- **Pros:**
  - True continuity: see in, see out, shoot through, throw through, hear through. Nothing else
    needs to know about portals.
  - Interest management goes back to plain distance, which BR needs anyway.
  - The camera, the sightline dissolve and lighting all just work.
- **Cons:**
  - **Slopes:** the ground floor is flat, the terrain isn't. On a hillside the terrain pokes up
    through the uphill rooms. It needs a flattened pad under each hollow building, and every
    system that "must meet the terrain" (roads, trees) respects that pad.
  - **Outline:** the plan has to fit the real TIN outline, not the min-area box, or rooms poke out
    of the walls (fact 5).
  - **Shell generation from TINs** is the risky part. Wall offset on arbitrary LoD2 solids,
    openings cut into triangle soups, roofs and gables: a new class of geometry bugs, 100k times
    over.
  - **Streaming:** interiors for every building in view is too much. Only a radius around each
    player can be hollow, which makes this effectively option C.
  - Interiors can no longer be planned lazily on entry: clients need plans for everything within
    the radius. They're deterministic, so compute locally, but stored and versioned plans must be
    reproducible on clients.
  - Buildings stop being one draw call per tile near the player.
- **Effort:** high, and the largest rewrite of the terrain and building pipeline.

### C. Hybrid: hollow on demand, within a radius

Option B, but only for buildings within ~60–100 m of a player (or inside the BR zone). Everything
further away stays a closed shell, optionally with option D on its windows.

- **Pros:**
  - Bounds B's streaming and memory cost.
  - A BR map is bounded, so "inside the zone" is a fixed, known set of buildings that can be
    hollowed at match start, even precomputed.
  - Beyond the radius nobody can tell the difference.
- **Cons:**
  - Everything in B except streaming, plus a swap at the radius edge (a building popping from
    closed to hollow).
  - Two code paths for a building.
- **Effort:** high, but it can start with one building and one terrain pad.

### D. Interior mapping shader (fake rooms behind the facade)

The facade shader ray-casts into a virtual box behind each window, the technique used for city
games. From outside you see a floor, walls, a ceiling and a back wall with parallax. The room type
and colours can come from the plan: a kitchen behind the kitchen window.

- **Pros:**
  - A few ALU per window pixel and no extra render.
  - Works for every building at every distance, and upgrades the whole city's look.
  - No gameplay implications at all.
- **Cons:**
  - Outside to in only.
  - Shows no players, so it's decoration, not BR information.
  - Driven by the plan, it needs plan data per building (see the prerequisite).
  - On `mobile`, check the cost on the far LOD.
- **Effort:** low to medium.

### E. Panorama inside-out (a captured view)

On entering, render a low-resolution cubemap of the outside from the building's position (camera
at window height, up by Δ⁻¹). The interior glass samples it by view direction instead of drawing
flat daylight. Refresh it every few seconds, or never.

- **Pros:**
  - One capture per entry instead of a render per frame.
  - Very convincing for static scenery: street, mountains, weather, time of day at capture.
- **Cons:**
  - Static: no moving players or vehicles, so it tells you nothing tactically.
  - No parallax, which you notice near the window.
  - Must hide the building's own shell during capture (same clip-plane need as A).
- **Effort:** low. A good first step that reuses A's clip plane.

### F. Door-only portal

Option A restricted to the door the player is standing at, while it's "open" (an open/close door
state). You see the street through the door from inside, and the hall from outside.

- **Pros:**
  - One portal, and only near a player, so the cost is bounded.
  - Proves A's camera, clip plane and projectile crossing on the simplest case.
- **Cons:**
  - Windows stay blind.
  - Still needs portal-aware replication for anyone seen through the door.
- **Effort:** low to medium. The natural prototype for A.

### G. Gameplay cues instead of views

Tell players what they'd need to see, without rendering it:
- lit windows or a moving silhouette in the windows of an occupied building
- muzzle flashes on the facade
- gunfire and footsteps heard through walls, with the reverb zones' occlusion
- a "noise" marker on the minimap

- **Pros:**
  - Nearly free.
  - Works at any range and on any building.
  - Keeps the current, cheap space model.
  - Fair in both directions by design.
- **Cons:**
  - No real line of sight: you can't aim through a window.
  - Can feel gamey.
- **Effort:** low. Worth doing whatever else is chosen (audio especially).

### Rejected: a separate `World3D` per interior

A separate world gives each interior its own physics and rendering, composited back. It has every
cost of A (extra render, clip, portal-aware systems) and loses the one shared physics space for 100
players, which is the reason interiors live in the main world at all.

## What BR changes

- **Fairness beats looks.** Anything asymmetric (see out, can't be seen) is a camping exploit. E and
  D alone are fine for the open world, **not** for BR combat.
- **Shooting through windows is the question.** If BR needs it, only A/F (re-cast across Δ) or B/C
  (native) qualify. If BR only needs "someone is in there", G is enough.
- **The BR map is bounded.** This is what makes C affordable: hollow the zone's buildings once at
  match start. On the open world, C would have to stream.
- **Authority.** Hit checks already run on the server. For A they need the opening rectangles
  server-side (they come from the plan, which the server has). For C the server needs the hollow
  collision for every zone building.
- **Interest management** must move from "same space" to "can plausibly see": distance-based for C,
  or through-opening for A.

## Recommendation

1. **Now, cheap and useful everywhere:**
   - G (audio through walls, occupied-window hints)
   - D on the far city
   - Plan-follows-facade window alignment, as groundwork.
2. **Prototype before choosing the BR path (one sprint, one building, measured on target
   hardware):**
   - F: door portal with the clip plane and a hitscan crossing Δ
   - C: one hollow building with a terrain pad.
   
   Compare frame time, geometry failure rate over a village's worth of TINs, and how much code had
   to become portal-aware.
3. **Then:**
   - If shells from TINs hold up, **C for BR**: bounded zone, native gameplay, no permanent portal
     tax.
   - Otherwise **A capped to the nearest openings**, with E/D beyond the cap.

## Open questions

- Must BR players be able to shoot through windows, or only see and hear who's inside?
- BR map size and player count? This decides whether C fits in memory for a whole zone.
- Will BR run on real swissBUILDINGS3D data, the generated world, or both? Generated buildings are
  clean boxes, so C is far easier there.
- Is the `mobile` renderer a hard target (actual phones or tablets), or just the current choice?
  A's cost depends on it.
- Do we want doors that open and close? F and any real portal need a door state anyway.
