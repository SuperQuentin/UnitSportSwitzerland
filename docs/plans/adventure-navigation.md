# Adventure navigation: compass, pins, quest marker, guiding wind, travel network

Status: **built** (PR #1236). Tracking issue: #1226 (overview: #1228).

One of five Adventure plans (phases 1 and 2), written together: `adventure-objectives` (progress
store, quests, tracker), `adventure-places` (POI catalogue, discovery, fog, Swiss Atlas),
`adventure-stamina`, `adventure-rewards` and this one. This plan draws where things are and moves
players between the places they have reached; it never decides *what* is tracked (objectives) nor
*what* a place is (places).

**Shared contract used here** (from `adventure-objectives`): namespace `UnitSport.Adventure`, code in
`src/Adventure/`; `AdventureService` at `World/Adventure` (server and every client, `Instance?.`),
everything gated by `AdventureService.Instance?.Active(peer)` (off per player in a BR round, off on
test worlds unless a probe names `adventure`); per-player `PlayerProgress` in the pure `AdventureBook`
with sections declared as `SectionKey<T>` in the owner's file, read with `progress.Get(key)`, marked
with `Touch(key)`, synced to the owner by the generic `SectionSync` RPC (`SectionChanged(id)` on the
client); server events through `AdventureService.Events.Raise(...)`; the tracked target is
`AdventureService.TrackedTarget` + `TrackedChanged`; screens are tabs of `Ui/AdventureLog`
(`RegisterTab`). Offline the client plays the server's part (peer 1, key `local`).

| Part | State | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Compass strip | built | `BrCompass` generalised into `Ui/CompassStrip`; free roam shows cardinals, nearby discovered POIs, pins, the tracked target and party members |
| 2 Map pins | built | Up to 30 player pins with 6 icons on the adventure map, optionally shared with the party |
| 3 Target marker | built | The tracked objective drawn in the world, an off-screen arrow, distance and climb readout |
| 4 Guiding wind | built | On-demand Föhn gust: leaves and streaks toward the target, a spatial gust, an accessible arrow fallback |
| 5 Travel network | built | SBB stations, PostBus stops, ship landings, cable-car stations unlock on a visit; departure board and fade; server setting `free | network | off` |
| 6 Checks and notes | built | Unit tests, `--navcheck` / `--travelcheck` screenshots, a two-client net check, notes and index lines |

## Goal

A player who never opens a menu still knows where north is, where the thing they chose to go to
lies, how far and how high it is, and where their friends are. A player who walked to a station
can come back to it by train, bus or cable car, from anywhere, in a way that looks like Switzerland
(a departure board, a chime) rather than magic. Servers choose whether players travel freely (today)
or through the network they unlocked.

Out of scope: a turn-by-turn route along roads or trails (the wind and the marker point as the crow
flies; a route planner is a later issue), timetables or real departures (the board is flavour),
riding a train in real time between stations (`traffic-trains` stays cosmetic and local), the VR
table map (#535, it gets pins and travel when it lands), the portable travel flag item itself
(rewards plan: a hook only, section 5.6).

## 1. Compass strip

Today `src/BattleRoyale/BrCompass.cs` draws the strip (560 px, ±80°, ticks 5°/15°/45°, N in red,
markers pinned to the edge with a turn arrow, label rows) for BR only, reading `BrManager`
directly. It also builds strings, runs LINQ and calls `QueueRedraw` every frame, which
`perf-no-per-frame-allocations` forbids.

- **`src/Ui/CompassStrip.cs`** (namespace `UnitSport.Ui`): the band, ticks, cardinal labels, centre
  caret and bearing, and a generic marker list. No knowledge of BR or Adventure.
  - `public static float Bearing(Vector2 eastNorth)` moves here from `BrCompass.Bearing` (one
    function; #772's F3 "facing NE 47°" line should call it too: coordinate on that PR, #774).
  - `struct CompassMarker { int Key; Vector2 Toward; Color Color; MarkerShape Shape; byte Row; string Label; float Dist; sbyte Priority }`.
    Shapes: `Diamond`, `Down` (triangle), `Dot`, `Icon` (an `Ui/Icons` glyph id: pin icons, a peak,
    a station). A marker source fills a reused `CompassMarker[]` and a count; no list per frame.
  - **Labels on change only**: `Label` is rebuilt by the source when the rounded distance changes
    (10 m steps under 1 km, 0.1 km above; `NavFormat.Distance`, invariant culture). Tick numbers 0..345
    come from a static `string[24]`. The degree readout under the caret is cached per integer degree.
  - **Redraw on change only**: `_Process` computes the heading; `QueueRedraw` runs when it moved by
    more than 0.2°, or the source bumped its `Version`. Standing still costs nothing.
  - Declutter: at most 8 markers drawn; when two land within 10 px, the lower `Priority` drops its
    label (the icon stays). Labels of pinned (behind-you) markers keep their rows, as today.
- **`BrCompass`** becomes a thin `ICompassSource` over `BrManager` (zone, drop, mates, horns, pings,
  Postauto stop, waypoint) feeding the strip, same colours from `BrMapDraw`. `tools/brcheck.sh`
  screenshots must look the same (the refactor's acceptance).
- **`src/Adventure/AdventureCompass.cs`**: the free-roam source. Shown when `Systems.On(Systems.Adventure)`
  and `AdventureService.Instance?.Active(me)`, not `Permissions.InMatch` (BR draws its own), on foot, mounted, driving or flying, and the
  setting `Settings → Adventure → Compass` (on by default; `GameSettings`). Markers, in priority order:
  1. the **tracked target** (amber diamond, the one primary thing on the strip; label `1.2 km ▲340 m`,
     the climb only above ±30 m);
  2. **party members** (`BrMapDraw.Mate` green, name and distance) from `Parties.Members` and their
     replicated `GlobalPos`;
  3. **pins** (their icon and colour, distance only);
  4. **discovered POIs within 1.5 km** (faint icon of their kind, name only within ±12° of the
     caret), at most 5, nearest first. The query (`PoiCatalogue.Near`, places plan) runs at 2 Hz,
     not per frame.
- **Layout**: free roam has no BR phase line under the strip, so it sits at `BrCompass.Top`;
  the top-centre slot is the compass's everywhere (`ux-guidelines`: one owner per screen edge).
  It fades to 30% alpha after 6 s of no change in markers and steady heading (style guide: nothing
  on screen unless in use) and comes back on a turn of more than 20° or a new marker.
- **VR**: the HUD strip is not drawn in the headset (a flat band at the top of a lens is
  uncomfortable). Instead `XR/XrWatch` grows a **bezel**: the dial's ring turns so its N points
  north, with the tracked target as an amber notch and its distance in the existing `Label3D`
  (set only on change). Look at the watch to read it, as people do with a real one.

## 2. Map pins

The full free-roam map is the places plan's (adventure map / Swiss Atlas with fog). Navigation adds
two overlay layers to it through an overlay hook (`IMapOverlay { void Draw(MapView view); bool Click(Vector2 lv95, MouseButton b); }`,
the places plan's to define): **pins** and **travel points** (section 5). The BR map keeps its own
waypoint.

- **`PinBook`** (`src/Adventure/PinBook.cs`, plain C#, unit-tested): `Pin { int Id; double E, N; byte Icon; string Note; bool Shared; long Created }`.
  30 pins a player, note ≤ 24 characters (trimmed, control chars removed), ids increase and are never
  reused within a book. Stored as a progress section declared in `PinBook.cs`:
  `public static readonly SectionKey<PinBook> Section = new("pins", 1, Migrate)` (a new id beside the
  reserved ones; the objectives plan's list should add `pins`), `progress.Get(PinBook.Section)`,
  `Touch` after each server-validated change.
- **Icons** (6, code-drawn in `Ui/Icons`, each with its colour): Flag, Star, Edelweiss (a flower:
  "pretty spot"), House, Camera (photo spot), Warning. No PNGs.
- **Place**: on the map, right click (as the BR map), or pad X with the map's centre reticle, or in
  VR the ray on the map and the map's "Pin here" button. A small popup (`UiTheme.GlassPanel(0.97, 8, 4)`)
  picks the icon (1-6, D-pad, ray), an optional note, "Share with party" toggle, and Track.
  Right click on a pin opens the same popup with Remove.
- **Shown**: on the map, on the compass strip, as in-world markers when tracked (section 3), and in
  `XR/XrMap` (the hand map) as small coloured dots.
- **Party sharing**: a pin with `Shared` is sent by the server to every member of the owner's party
  (`Parties`), drawn with the owner's name and a thin outline. Leaving the party drops them on both
  sides. Shared pins are never written into another player's book.

## 3. Tracked target: in-world marker, off-screen arrow, distance

The objectives plan owns the tracker (one tracked target per player). Navigation reads
`AdventureService.TrackedTarget` (client): `(string Label, double E, double N, float RadiusM)?`, and
re-reads it on `AdventureService.TrackedChanged` (never polled per frame). A `RadiusM > 0` is a
search area (Genshin's circle): the marker points at the area's centre and changes to "in the area"
inside it.

Pins, POIs and party members are tracked from the map. Navigation keeps that choice locally in
`NavTracking` (client), and the newest choice wins. Picking a pin sets
`NavTracking.Local = (label, E, N, 0)`. A later `TrackedChanged` from a quest replaces it, and
"Track" on a pin replaces the quest target until the quest is tracked again from the log. This
needs nothing new from objectives. If objectives later adds a client
`AdventureService.TrackPoint(label, E, N, radius)`, `NavTracking` collapses into it. Every consumer
(compass, marker, wind) reads `NavTracking.Current`, which is the local choice or `TrackedTarget`.

- **`src/Adventure/TargetMarker.cs`** (a `Control` on the HUD `CanvasLayer`):
  - Altitude: the POI's altitude when the target is a catalogue POI (a peak's summit, looked up by
    position in `PoiCatalogue.Near` at 5 m), else `ChunkManager.TryGetHeight` when the tile is
    loaded, else the camera's own; the marker stands 2.5 m over it.
  - **On screen**: `Camera3D.UnprojectPosition`, the amber diamond, the label `Piz Palü · 3.4 km ▲1,210 m`
    under it. Its size eases from 28 px (far) to 18 px (near).
  - **Off screen or behind** (`IsPositionBehind`): clamped to an inset ellipse round the screen edge
    (`NavMath.EdgePoint(screenSize, dir)`, pure), an arrow pointing out, distance only.
  - **Arrival**: within `max(Radius, 15 m)` the marker pulses once and the objectives plan is told
    nothing by us (it detects its own completion); a Poi or Pin target clears itself on arrival.
  - Text and colour set only on change; position is a transform per frame, no draw call churn.
- **VR**: a world-space billboard (`Sprite3D` with a code-built `ImageTexture` + `Label3D`,
  `FixedSize`, no depth test) at the target; the off-screen direction is the watch bezel's notch
  (section 1). A haptic tick on the left hand when the target first comes within 100 m.
- **Distance format** (`NavFormat`, pure): `850 m`, `3.4 km`, `27 km`; climb `▲1,210 m` / `▼80 m`;
  invariant culture (`invariant-culture-floats`).

## 4. Guiding wind (Föhn trail)

Ghost of Tsushima's wind, as the warm Alpine Föhn: a gust from behind the player that blows toward
the tracked target for a few seconds, carrying leaves (larch needles and snow dust above the snow
line), with a sound that comes from the way to go.

- **`src/Adventure/GuidingWind.cs`**, a node on the local player only (cosmetic, never replicated).
- **Summon**: the `guide` action (section Devices) starts a 6 s gust; setting
  `Settings → Adventure → Guiding wind`: **On demand** (default), **Always** (a soft stream while a
  target is tracked), **Arrow only**, **Off**.
- **Particles**: one `GpuParticles3D` (amount 96, lifetime 2.2 s, local coords off) placed 3 m ahead
  of the body; one `ParticleProcessMaterial` whose `Direction` is set only when the bearing to the
  target changes by more than 2°. Particles are quads; the leaf, needle and streak shapes come from
  the fragment shader (`shaders/guide_wind.gdshader`, a cartoon outline in Cartoon, flat colours in
  PS1, soft alpha in Realistic: `styles/style-kit`). No mesh is built after `_Ready`
  (`no-mesh-rebuild-per-frame`). The direction follows the terrain: the emitter tilts by the slope
  along the bearing so the gust climbs a hill instead of entering it.
- **Contrast**: below the snow line warm leaves (orange, olive); above 2,000 m or on `Snow` ground the
  leaves turn **dark** (slate and red streaks), never white on white. The shader takes one `palette`
  uniform set on change. Grass bending along the gust is a follow-up (needs the vegetation shader's
  wind uniform, `FigureWind` style).
- **Sound**: a synthesised gust (`GuideSounds.Gust`, a static `AudioStreamWav` built once, so it
  shows in the sound player by itself; `--sounds,check`) from an `AudioStreamPlayer3D` placed 8 m
  toward the target, moved per frame (a transform, not a new player). A soft chime when the gust
  starts. Audio bus: Ambience.
- **Arrow fallback** (setting *Arrow only*, or the accessibility toggle *High-contrast guide*):
  a chevron of three arrows on the ground 4 m ahead, one mesh built once (`GuideArrowMeshBuilder`,
  `[Showcase("Ui")]`), turned per frame; amber fill with a 2 px-equivalent dark outline so it reads on
  snow, grass and asphalt. It hides beyond 30° off the camera's forward when the target is behind
  (the HUD arrow already says "turn round").
- **Mounted and flying**: the gust rides with the vehicle (emitter on the camera's pivot, 8 m ahead);
  over 25 m/s it shows as streaks only, no leaves.
- **VR**: the gust is summoned by **holding the open left hand up, palm up, for 0.5 s** (feel the
  wind, R4), or the wrist ring's new **Guide** wedge (R5). A light buzz in the left hand on start.

## 5. Unlockable travel network

### 5.1 Travel points

From the places catalogue: kinds `RailStation`, `BusStop` (PostBus and others), `ShipLanding`,
`CableCarStation` (top and bottom of a line) and, optionally, `PassHospice`. Only a subset is a
travel point: every rail station, **every bus stop** (decided: all ~23 000 are kept; the map and the
board cluster them by locality when zoomed out, so the map is not a cloud of thousands), every ship
landing on the big lakes, both ends of every cable car and gondola (not chairlifts or T-bars). The cable-car
POIs should carry the `RopewayService` line `Key` (`world/ski-lifts`), so the station sign stands
where the in-game gondola actually leaves.

- **Unlock radius** per kind: station 60 m, bus stop 30 m, landing 40 m, cable car 60 m, hospice 50 m.
- **`TravelNetwork`** (`src/Adventure/TravelNetwork.cs`, plain C#, unit-tested): rules only.
  `Unlocked(book, poiId)`, `CheckUnlock(book, pos, nearPois) -> poiId?`, `MayTravel(state, from, to, mode) -> Refusal?`.
- **`TravelBook`**, the reserved section `travel`, declared in `TravelBook.cs`:
  `public static readonly SectionKey<TravelBook> Section = new("travel", 1, Migrate)`;
  `HashSet<string> Unlocked` (POI ids), `PortableFlag? Flag` (5.6), `long LastTravel`.

### 5.2 Unlocking (server)

`AdventureTravel` (`src/Adventure/AdventureTravel.cs`, a child node of `AdventureService` at
`World/Adventure/Travel`, so it exists exactly where the service does). At 1 Hz on the server
(`ServerStats.Ran("travel", ...)`), for each player with `AdventureService.Instance?.Active(peer)` who
is on foot or slower than 15 m/s, `PoiCatalogue.Near(GlobalPos, 80 m)` is filtered to travel kinds,
then `TravelNetwork.CheckUnlock` runs. Two consecutive hits unlock, so a jump-cut through a station
does not. The order follows the contract:
1. write `progress.Get(TravelBook.Section).Unlocked`;
2. `AdventureService.Touch(peer, TravelBook.Section)`; the owner gets it through `SectionSync`, and
   its `SectionChanged("travel")` refreshes the map, the signs and the board, and shows a "Travel
   point unlocked" toast;
3. then `AdventureService.Events.Raise(new TravelUnlocked(peer, key, PointId, Kind))`, the shared
   record. Objectives and rewards listen to it, for example "unlock 10 PostBus stops".

Unlocks happen in every travel mode, so a server switched to `network` later keeps what players
reached.

**Reward handler**: `AdventureService.RegisterReward("travel", (peer, progress, arg) => ...)`, where
`arg` is a POI id (`Unlock("travel", "busstop:andermatt")` in a quest's rewards). It unlocks that
point as above, without raising `TravelUnlocked` again for a point already held. It returns null on
success, or an error text for an unknown id, which the objectives catalog test fails on.

In the world, the **nearest 6 travel points within 400 m** get a sign (`TravelSignMeshBuilder`,
`[Showcase("World")]`): the SBB clock-and-board pole, the yellow PostBus stop sign with its horn
glyph, the BLS/CGN landing board, a ropeway station board. One mesh per kind, built once, placed as
`MultiMesh` instances when the set changes. An unlocked sign has a lit board; a locked one is dark.

### 5.3 Travelling

- **Where from**: the departure board opens (a) at a sign, `interact` within 4 m; (b) the
  **Travel tab of `Ui/AdventureLog`** (`AdventureLog.RegisterTab("travel", "Travel", 40, DepartureBoard.BuildTab)`):
  the same board, every unlocked point by canton and kind, with Travel and Track; (c) the adventure
  map: select an unlocked point → **Travel**; (d) the phone's Map app, which in `network`
  mode lists unlocked points (nearest first, search by name) instead of every place.
- **Decided: from a sign by default; from anywhere with the GA card.** Without the item, a player
  travels only from an unlocked sign (a). The **GA card** (`ItemId.GaCard` = 420, the Swiss
  *Generalabonnement*; 400-419 belong to the rewards plan) unlocks (b)-(d) from anywhere. It works
  while held anywhere in the inventory, is never lost on a knock-out (a key item like the phone), and is
  earned once:
  - by the quest "Grand tour" (unlock 50 travel points in at least 5 cantons), or
  - bought at an SBB ticket office (a station `interact`, charged to the bank account with
    `Bank.Charge`) at the real price, CHF 3,995 (2nd class adult; 4,095 from 13 December 2026).

  The server checks it in `MayTravel`. The inventory is the client's, so the server trusts
  `TravelBook.HasGa`, written when the server grants or sells the card.
- **The board** (`src/Adventure/DepartureBoard.cs`, one `Control` used both as the AdventureLog tab
  and, at a sign, alone in a screen panel in `UiTheme` glass, the SBB look
  in its own colours: dark blue rows, white text, amber for the selected row): destination, the
  line glyph by kind (`IR`, `PostAuto 451`, `Schiff`, `Luftseilbahn`), canton, and a "departs in 1'"
  flavour column. One row a destination, unlocked only, filtered by a search line, nearest first.
- **Presentation**: on confirm, a **kind-specific fade** through `Teleporter.Curtain` (the phone
  already uses it): the SBB three-note chime / the PostBus three-tone horn / a ship's bell / a ropeway
  bell, 0.8 s to black, a card "Arriving at Zermatt · 1,608 m" for 1.2 s, fade in. Phone out: the
  phone's own "Travelling to X" screen is the curtain, as today. The sounds are static `SfxBank`
  entries (sound player).
- **Arrival point**: the POI's position nudged to the nearest road or path point within 40 m (the
  sign's foot), facing away from the sign. A cable-car top station arrives at the line's top end.
- **Refused** (the board greys the row with the reason, the server re-checks): not unlocked; in a BR
  match (`BrManager.Playing`); downed or in a fist fight; in a race; driving a vehicle (dismount
  first; a horse or bike you ride is left, equipment comes along); within 10 s of the last travel.

### 5.7 Fares (decided: based on SBB prices)

Every trip costs a ticket, paid from the bank account (`Bank.Charge`, no overdraft; the board shows
the price on each row and greys rows the player cannot afford). Prices are real Swiss prices in
in-game francs, kept as data in `TravelRules` (`res://assets/adventure/fares.json`) so they can be
updated with each December timetable change:

- **Rail and bus**: the SBB distance tariff. The distance is the straight line between the two
  points times a 1.25 detour factor (there is no rail graph to route on), looked up in the tariff's
  kilometre bands, 2nd class, with the minimum fare for short hops.
- **Ships**: the lake operator's tariff by distance (CGN, SGV, BLS, URh...), same method.
- **Cable cars**: the line's own one-way price, from its operator, per line `Key`. A small default
  covers lines without a known price.
- **Discounts**:
  - The **half-fare travelcard** (`ItemId.HalfFare` = 421, CHF 190; 195 from 13 December 2026) halves
    every fare, cable cars included, as in reality. It is the "All aboard" quest's reward.
  - The **GA card** makes rail, bus and ship trips free, halves cable cars, and allows travel from
    anywhere (5.3).
- **Server trust**: the server prices and charges the trip in `MayTravel`; the client's numbers are
  display only. Ownership of both cards is held in `TravelBook` (`HasGa`, `HasHalfFare`).
- **Economy**: real prices are large next to today's quest rewards (60-150 francs), on purpose: the
  half-fare card is reachable in an evening, the GA is a long-term goal. The objectives plan's reward
  amounts and `econcheck` get balanced against this table in the navigation PR.
- **Free modes**: `TravelMode.Free` (the default sandbox) charges nothing; admins never pay.

### 5.4 Travel modes: how `network` coexists with free travel

Today: `Arrival.Rules` carries `FreeTeleport` (`--no-teleport` turns it off; `Permissions.CanTeleport`
greys the place search, the phone map, the VR hand map; the server refuses `/city`). This plan
widens the bool into **`TravelMode { Free, Network, Off }`**:

| Mode | Server flag | Place search (M), `/city`, phone free search, VR hand map, fly camera return | Adventure travel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free (default) | none | as today | allowed too |
| Network | `--adventure-travel network` | refused for non-admins | to unlocked points, from a sign, or from anywhere with the GA card |
| Off | `--no-teleport` (kept as the alias) | refused | refused |

- `Permissions.CanTeleport` stays (free jumps); a new `Permissions.Travel` exposes the mode. Admins
  always jump freely. `/tp`, `/tpa`, party "go to" and "bring here" are invites, kept in every mode
  except Off (a friend is a valid destination, as in Genshin co-op).
- Offline: `Settings → Adventure → Travel: Free / Network` (default Free; Network for players who
  want the adventure way). The Host dialog gets the same choice (`HostedServer` adds the flag).
- **Battle Royale**: off. `AdventureService.Active(peer)` is false while `BrManager.Playing(peer)`,
  so the server refuses, and the client hides the signs, the board, the compass and the wind; the BR
  map and compass stay.

### 5.5 Admin and test commands

`/travel` (list unlocked, with ids), `/travel to <id>` (a player's travel, same rules),
admin: `/travel unlock <id|all> [player]`, `/travel lock <id|all> [player]`; `/pin add|list|clear`.
In `ChatCompleter`. The server-side teleport goes through a new internal

### 5.6 Portable travel flag (rewards plan hook)

`TravelBook.Flag { double E, N; float Alt; long Placed }`: at most one per player.
`AdventureTravel.SetPortable(peer, e, n, alt)` / `ClearPortable(peer)` are server methods the rewards
plan's item calls; the flag then appears as a travel point "Your flag" on the board and the map. No
item code here.

## Bots

Server-run bots (#837) have no HUD: the compass, pins, marker and wind are n/a. They can travel:

- `AdventureTravel.TravelBot(Bot bot, string poiId)`: the same `TravelNetwork.MayTravel` rules, then
  the body is placed with `FootPlayer.DebugLaunch` as `BotSkills` already does (no RPC: the server
  owns a bot's body). A bot's `TravelBook` is a `PlayerProgress` the server keeps in memory and never
  saves (an objectives-plan concern: a bot has no `PlayerKey`).
- A bot unlocks by walking past signs like a player (the same 1 Hz check covers bots).
- Senses: a `WanderBrain` "go somewhere far" step may pick a travel point (`BotSenses` memory of
  unlocked points), walk to the nearest sign and travel; a follow-up, not phase 1.
- Rows in `bots/bot-capability-map`: travel network (seam none, server path `TravelBot`, sense
  memory, skill wander-travel, status gap until the brain step); pins / compass / wind / marker (n/a:
  UI over a target a bot reads directly).

## Network

- **Authority**: the server decides unlocks and travel; the client asks. Transforms are
  client-authoritative (`net/arrival`): travel is enforced like `--no-teleport`, a rule an honest
  client follows plus a server refusal of every request.
- **RPCs** on `World/Adventure/Travel` (`AdventureTravel`), all `Reliable`:
  - client → server: `RequestTravel(string toId, string fromSignId)` (empty = from the map),
    `PinAdd(double e, double n, byte icon, string note, bool shared)`, `PinEdit(int id, byte icon, string note, bool shared)`,
    `PinRemove(int id)`.
  - server → owner: the owner's own `travel` and `pins` sections arrive through the objectives
    plan's generic `SectionSync(id, version, json)` (at most 4 a second), with no RPC of ours. The
    rest:
    - `Refused(byte reason)`;
    - the jump itself through `ChatManager.ForceTeleport`, as every server teleport today;
    - the travel mode rides `Arrival.Rules`.
  - server → party: `PartyPins(long owner, ...)` same packing, sent on a shared pin's change, a party
    join, and empty on leave. Positions as LV95 doubles (`positions-on-the-wire`).
- **Rules**: the travel mode rides `Arrival.Rules` (one byte replacing the bool).
- **Protocol**: this plan bumps `Handshake.Protocol` for its own RPCs and the `Arrival.Rules` byte,
  on top of the objectives plan's bump from 49. The number is whatever `main` holds + 1 at merge time.
- Rate limits: 10 pin edits a second, one travel request a second, per peer.

## Persistence

- Online: the objectives plan's `AdventureBook`, one `PlayerProgress` per player in
  `user://adventure/players/<PlayerKey fingerprint>.json` (`JsonStore.SaveAsync`, at most once per
  5 s and on leave). Ours are the sections `travel` (reserved) and `pins` (new); each carries a
  `SectionKey` version and its `Migrate`. Offline, the client plays the server's part (peer 1, key
  `local`, `user://adventure/local.json`), so the same code path runs.
- Shared pins are not stored for the receivers. The tracked target is the objectives plan's.
- Client settings (compass on, wind mode, high-contrast guide, offline travel mode): `GameSettings`.

## Performance

- Compass: redraw only on a heading change > 0.2° or new markers; labels on change; POI query at
  2 Hz; no LINQ, strings or lists per frame (static `StringName`s for the actions). The BR refactor
  fixes `BrCompass`'s per-frame allocations at the same time.
- Target marker: a transform per frame; text on change.
- Wind: one `GpuParticles3D` (96 particles) and one 3D audio player, idle when no gust; nothing per
  frame beyond a transform and a uniform on change. The arrow: one mesh, turned.
- Signs: one `MultiMesh` per kind, rebuilt when the nearest set changes (on a 1 Hz check).
- Server: the unlock tick is one spatial query per player per second, under `ServerStats.Ran`;
  measured with `--serverstats` at 32 swarm bots (`load-testing`).
- Target budget: < 0.15 ms client frame for all of it (measured on the fixture with `--perf`).

## Built differently (#1226)

- **The travel mode does not ride `Arrival.Rules`**: to stay clear of the stamina PR's change to that RPC, the server
  sends it with `AdventureTravel.TellMode` on load, and a Network server sets `Arrival.FreeTeleport` false (so `/city`,
  the place search, the phone and the VR hand map refuse as with `--no-teleport`). `Permissions.Travel` was not needed;
  offline, `Permissions.OfflineTravelNetwork` greys the free jumps. The Host dialog has no new control: a hosted server
  follows the host's own `Settings > Gameplay > Travel`.
- **`NavTracking` was not needed**: places (#1224) added `AdventureService.TrackPlace`, which pins, travel points and
  places use; `NavTarget` adds the altitude.
- **A trip's jump is `AdventureTravel.Depart`**, not `ChatManager.ForceTeleport`: the owner needs the kind for the
  curtain's chime and card; it calls the same `Teleporter.TeleportTo` the forced teleport does. No
  `ChatManager.TeleportPeer`.
- **Arrival**: 5 m east of the point, not snapped to the nearest road or path (the fixture has none to test it on); a
  cable-car top lands at its POI.
- **Fares**: the whole trip is rounded up to whole francs (the bank keeps whole francs); a GA or half-fare card is the
  `travel` section's flag, the items are its picture and are worth 0 (no shop buys them back).
- **Quests**: "All aboard" and "Grand tour" start by themselves on the first unlock (`QuestStart.OnEvent`); Grand tour is
  one `NetworkStep(50, 5)` reading the totals `TravelUnlocked` now carries (`Canton`, `Points`, `Cantons`), so points
  unlocked before it started count.
- **Pins are placed** by right click, pad X at the centre cross, or the side panel's "Pin the centre" (the VR way: no
  separate popup, the card sits in the map's side panel).
- **Snow contrast** follows altitude (above 2 000 m) only, not the ground's `Snow` material.
- **Not done here**: the phone's Map app listing network points (it greys free travel as before), pins on the VR hand
  map (`XrMap`), grass bending with the gust, `--serverstats` at 32 swarm bots and the `--perf` budget, the econcheck
  rebalance of quest rewards against fares, a bot brain step that travels.
