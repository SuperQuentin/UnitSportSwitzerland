# One scene format: options

**Status:** decided 2026-10-19 (see "Decisions" below); phase 1 built and measured (#924, see
"Measurement" below): the pins are in, takes are needed for vehicle shots. Nicolas asked for "a unified format for rendering
scenes, defining locations, actions, objects, etc., in order for it to be reproducible without
being huge". This compares the ways to get there, from what the repo already has.

## What exists today

Five systems each describe or record a scene in their own way:

| System | What it stores | Format | Size | Reproducible? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trailer director (`src/Trailer/`) | **Intents**: places (LV95), hour, style, occasion, actors and how they drive, camera keys, bubbles, props, UFOs, cows | Compiled C# (`*Script.cs`); about 30 lambdas a film (inputs as functions of time) | ~1 KB of C# a shot, ~60 KB a 4-min film | Re-simulated every render: close, not exact |
| Movie studio (`src/Movie/`, `.usmovie` v6) | **State**: every player's replicated state at 30 Hz, world objects (poses when they move), cameras, cuts, audio refs | Binary, plus `user://movies/looks/*.scn` and `audio/*` files beside it | ~274 KB/min/actor raw; sound 2.65 MB/min | Exact replay of what was recorded; cannot be re-simulated or changed |
| Playtest recorder (`src/Playtest/`) | Reduced state at 10 Hz within 400 m, events, notes | `trace.bin` + JSONL | ~1 MB/min | Inspection only, wall-clock time |
| GPX replay (`src/Gpx/`) | A track per runner; draped, optionally snapped | GPX (XML) | ~4.5 KB/min | Paths yes; the world and cinema seed no |
| `--shot` / `--shot-queue` / probes | A camera pose and chat commands | Command line, text | Bytes | Settles by time only |

Each film render also writes `version.txt` (commit, branch, dirty flag) and `render.log`, which
records the Godot build, GPU and the user's settings line. Nothing records the terrain data version.

## What makes a render differ between runs

Found in a survey of the code on 2026-10-19:

- **Physics and timing**: Jolt is not known to be deterministic, and the trailer's stage/place timeouts
  run on the wall clock. Routes build on a worker thread, and tiles arrive in an order that depends on load.
- **Unseeded randomness**: birds (`new Random()`), occasion creatures (`GD.Randf`), impact effects, vehicle
  ids (`Random.Shared`), a camera's default seed.
- **Global counters**: staged herds and sightings take a run-global index, and the traffic RNG is not
  reseeded per shot.
- **World outside the scene**:
  - the terrain: tiles are rebuilt in place, the manifest has no hash or code version, and roads were
    rewritten on 2026-10-19;
  - the user's `settings.json` (rings, detail, traffic, occasion preferences) and `user://placed`;
  - the real date (calendar occasions, the farm month);
  - the Godot build and GPU (Metal fence timeouts gave garbage geometry: #881);
  - the songs, downloaded from incompetech.
- **Smaller issues**: the GPX cinema seed uses `string.GetHashCode()` (randomized per process), and
  `--shot` waits a fixed time instead of for the tiles.

## The options

### A. Intents as data (re-simulated)

The director's `Shot` moves from C# into a data file. Places, hour, style, actors, camera keys, bubbles
and props are already plain records. Only the ~30 lambdas need a data form:

- **Input curves**: a list of segments per channel (constant, linear ramp, sine, step pattern), e.g.
  `"throttle": [[0, 1.9, 0.25], [1.9, null, 0]]`.
- **Named behaviours with parameters**: `road`, `follow`, `walk-path`, `fly-to`, `fight-pose-list`,
  `ufo-switches`.

- **Size:** tiny (a 4-min film ≈ 60–100 KB of JSON, ~15 KB gzipped).
- **Editing:** by a person, a tool or an LLM, without a rebuild.
- **Reproducibility:** no better than today. The same file gives a slightly different film each run;
  a car can miss a hairpin one time out of ten.
- **Coverage:** the C# lambdas that do not fit a curve (DormSet's `Func<Node3D>`) stay as named code
  hooks.

### B. Recorded state (the movie studio's way)

Every scene is recorded, as `.usmovie` does, and rendered from the recording.

- **Reproducibility:** exact, and the camera can be changed freely afterwards without re-simulating.
- **Editing:** nothing can be changed except by hand-editing tracks. Retiming a car or moving a start
  point means recording again.
- **Size:** big without work. Raw it is ~274 KB/min/actor, plus world objects and sound. Quantized
  positions (mm deltas), half floats and zstd would bring it to roughly 20–40 KB/min/actor (estimate).
- **Unsolved:** it knows nothing of intents or the terrain, so it cannot be re-taken on a newer map.

### C. Hybrid: intents plus baked "takes" (recommended)

The SFM model: a scene file says what should happen (A). The first render **bakes a take**, recording
the state the simulation actually produced for every actor and staged object (B's tracks, compressed).

- **Rendering:** later renders replay the take: same frames every time, only the camera, overlays,
  style and lighting are recomputed.
- **Changing an actor:** change its intents and re-simulate only that actor ("re-take shot 12, actor 2").
  The others keep their takes, and actors that interact (a beam lifting a car) are re-taken together.
- **New terrain or code:** a take records the terrain fingerprint and commit it was made on. On a newer
  map the director warns, and can re-take.
- **Size:** the scene stays small (KBs) and the takes are a sidecar of a few hundred KB to a few MB per
  film. Per-shot MP4s are ~80 MB/min.
- **Reuse of the movie studio:** the take format is `ActorIo` state (shared with the network and the
  movie studio). The studio could open a film's takes as a project and the director could render a
  studio project: one format, two front ends.

### D. Keep C#, add takes only

C without the data file: the film stays a C# script, and takes are recorded beside it. This is cheapest
and gives exact re-renders, but scenes still need a rebuild to change and tools cannot author them.

## Comparison

| | A intents | B state | C hybrid | D C# + takes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exact re-render | no | yes | yes | yes |
| Edit an actor's action | yes | no | yes (re-take one actor) | rebuild |
| Edit camera, overlays, style | yes | yes | yes | rebuild |
| Size, 4-min film | ~15 KB | ~2–5 MB* | ~15 KB + ~1–3 MB* | ~1–3 MB* |
| Authorable by tools/LLM | yes | no | yes | no |
| Survives a terrain rebuild | re-sim (moves) | stale | warns, re-take | warns, re-take |
| Unifies trailer + studio + playtest + GPX | partly | partly | yes | partly |

\* estimates from the code layouts, compressed; no `.usmovie` exists on disk to measure.

## What any option needs (the reproducibility pins)

1. **A scene header** with:
   - the game commit and the Godot build;
   - the **terrain fingerprint**: a per-tile content hash in `manifest.json`, which needs adding, and
     the hashes of the tiles the scene's coverage (`FilmCoverage`) touches;
   - date and occasion set explicitly, never "today";
   - the style;
   - the song's checksum and URL.
2. **An isolated profile for renders**: a fresh `user://` (settings, placed objects, chunk cache) from
   the scene file, not the user's own.
3. **Seeds**: every RNG a scene touches is seeded from (scene, shot, actor). Birds, occasion creatures,
   impact effects, traffic and vehicle ids; staged counters become per-shot. Fix the GPX
   `string.GetHashCode` seed.
4. **Settle on the tiles, not time**: `--shot` and the exporter use the same predicate
   (`CompleteNear`) as the director.

## Proposed format sketch (option C)

```jsonc
// fondue.scene.json — one file per film; takes in fondue.takes/ (binary, one per shot)
{
  "format": 1,
  "pins": { "commit": "78b67697", "godot": "4.7.2", "terrain": "sha256:…", "date": "2026-11-14", "style": "cartoon" },
  "song": { "title": "Hep Cats", "url": "https://incompetech.com/…", "sha256": "…", "bpm": 90, "firstBeat": 1.621 },
  "cast": { "lea": { "looks": "…packed…", "wears": [ "…" ] }, "zorp": { "preset": "alien:zorp" } },
  "shots": [
    {
      "n": 4, "bars": [5, 6], "hour": 17.5, "occasion": "none",
      "actors": [
        { "who": "ufo1", "ride": "ufo", "at": [2626790, 1092478, 40, "agl"], "heading": 210,
          "drive": "fly", "stick": [[0, 2, [0, -0.5]], [2, null, [0, 0]]],
          "ufo": { "beam": [[0.8, 3.5]], "lights": true } }
      ],
      "props": [ { "item": "caquelon", "at": [2626795, 1092470], "beamable": true } ],
      "camera": [ { "t": 0, "eye": { "on": "ufo1", "back": 14, "up": 3 }, "look": { "on": "ufo1" }, "lens": 35 } ],
      "bubbles": [ { "t": 0.6, "who": "nina", "id": "f04", "text": "Hey! That's our fondue!", "shape": "shout" } ],
      "take": "fondue.takes/shot04.take"
    }
  ]
}
```

A take holds, per actor and staged object, `ActorIo` state at 30 Hz with change-only properties as
events. It is quantized (positions as mm deltas from an LV95 origin, angles and the pose in 16 bits),
then zstd. Its header carries the pins it was made with.

## Phases (if C is chosen)

1. **Pins and seeds**:
   - the manifest's per-tile hash;
   - the render profile;
   - seeded RNGs and per-shot counters;
   - the header written to `version.txt` now.

   Useful on its own: it makes today's renders far more repeatable.
2. **Takes for today's C# films (D)**: record and replay actor state in the director, reusing `ActorIo`
   and the movie studio's tracks. Measure real sizes here.
3. **The data file (A on top)**:
   - the scene schema, curves and named behaviours;
   - port one film (showcase, 34 shots) and check it re-renders the same from its takes;
   - the film kit's C# records become the loader's target.
4. **Unify**:
   - the movie studio opens a scene and its takes and saves back to the scene format;
   - the playtest recorder and GPX replay write takes;
   - `--shot-queue` becomes a one-shot scene.

## Measurement (phase 1, #924, 2026-10-20)

Every shot below was rendered twice on this machine (Apple M1 Pro, macOS 15.8.1, Godot 4.7.1, the
2,698 real tiles on NicoDrive) as stills runs at `--fixed-fps 30`, with the track dump on
(`TRACKS=`), and compared with `tools/trailer-drift.py` (`trailer/render-pins`). Numbers are the
largest position difference of an object over the roll (what the film shows); "identical" is every
frame equal to the millimetre and the hundredth of a degree. **Unpinned** is `PINS=0` (the user's
profile, the real date, no seeds), which already had the macOS clock fix below; **before fixes** is
the first pinned build, before the measurement found the go, beam and clock problems.

- **Scripted motion re-simulates exactly once pinned**: flight on a scripted stick, walkers,
  craft, beams, herds, a crash and its ragdoll (sightings were not in the shots measured). The camera follows exactly (its keys on
  roads and world points are identical; its yaw follows a drifting car by up to 1°).
- **What the pins fixed, found by the dump** (each a commit of #924): the actors' go landed on
  either of a frame's two physics steps (the saucer's 36 cm); the tractor beam's bob ran on a
  run-long clock (the pot's 37 cm); a staged herd's seed was the run's staging count and its walk read
  the absolute clock; on macOS `GameClock.Fixed` was always false, so `ClockSync` (herds, sightings,
  signals) ran on the wall clock in every `--fixed-fps` run; the crash's stay-or-go coin and the
  Africa Twin egg (15-68 m apart) were unseeded.
- **What still drifts: ground vehicles under a controller** (autopilot, follow), by 8 cm to 2 m,
  from 0.2-0.5 s after the go (the dump's pre-roll frames show the first difference within a
  dozen frames). Their wheels' contacts with the terrain's and roads' collision differ run to run:
  tiles stream in on threads, so the collision bodies reach Jolt in another order and after another
  history of origin shifts. Building one tile at a time (`--builds 1`) did not remove it (pack up to
  2 m, Harvest 1.6 m). It is bimodal for the field machines (identical, 13 cm or 1.6 m): a small
  difference sometimes tips a discrete event.
- **A second OS** was not available to this run (one Mac); the cross-platform numbers are still to
  be taken. Jolt is not cross-platform deterministic in Godot's build, so expect at least the
  vehicle drift there, likely more.
- **Size**: the track dump of the six-car pack (7 objects, 297 frames, position and yaw only) is
  156 KB of CSV, 37 KB gzipped: ~32 KB per object-minute, inside the 20-40 KB estimated above for a
  quantized take.

### Recommendation: where takes live

Takes are worth having for every shot with a car, truck, bike or race in it: re-simulation on the
same machine, same commit and same terrain already moves them by a car's width. Scripted shots
(flight, walkers, craft, beams, herds, crashes) need none: pinned, they re-render exactly.

- **Takes on NicoDrive, an index in the repo.** A take is exact only on the terrain fingerprint and
  commit it was made with, and the terrain is not in the repo (10 GB), so a take in Git LFS is of
  no use to anyone without the drive. Keep the takes beside the film versions on the drive
  (`<trailers>/<film>/takes/shotNN.take`), and commit a small index per film
  (`docs/trailer/<film>.takes.json`: shot, take hash, size, the pins it was made with: commit,
  terrain fingerprint, date, Godot, GPU), so anyone can see which take a cut used and whether their
  map matches. Not NicoDrive alone: without the index nobody knows a take exists or what it was made on.
- **Take only what drifts**: the actors driven by a controller (autopilot, follow, scripted ride
  input), plus anything they touch; scripted actors, craft, herds and props re-simulate. That keeps
  a 4-min film's takes well under 1 MB.
- **Phase 2** records a take from the director's actor stream (the track dump is the prototype:
  add the pose and ride state, `ActorIo`), replays it, and re-runs this measurement expecting
  "identical" on every row.
