## What Swiss sources say

- **SSV Art. 71** (SR 741.21): the main head stands on the **right kerb**; it may be repeated **over its lane**, on the
  left or past the junction; on several lanes one way the left lane's head may stand on its left only; heads only over
  the road in special cases. Lower edge: kerb 2.35-3.50 m, **over the road 4.50-5.50 m** (more under tram/trolleybus
  wires). Art. 70 Abs. 6: heads over the road may be horizontal (red left, green right): not done, rare.
- **Stadt Bern, Handbuch Lichtsignalanlagen V2.1 (Dec 2025)**, ch. 12.2, Abb. 11-19 (the layouts this plan copies):
  - two or more lanes turning the same way: the turn's head **repeated over the road** (main and side road);
  - an arm **without a refuge** whose left lane has its own head: the left head **over the road**, apart from the
    through head (Abb. 14: both lanes then have a head overhead; the left kerb keeps its head);
  - a side road of two lanes without a refuge: repeaters over both lanes (Abb. 18);
  - three lanes, even with a refuge: the left and the through heads overhead (Abb. 19: the through head serves the
    middle and the kerb lane; the kerb pole keeps its head);
  - with a refuge, two lanes: no overhead head (Abb. 12, 13: the refuge's pole serves the left lane);
  - heads overhead are **300 mm** (kerb 200 mm); lower edge 4.50 / 4.80 / 5.20 m by route; under tram wires the
    head keeps 1.5 m sideways from the rail axis (wire 5.50-6.00 m); suspended carriers hang from span points on
    tram masts or house fronts.
- **Basel-Landschaft, ATS LSA Aussenanlagen V1.2 and drawings V1.4**: overhead heads 300 mm, plate lower edge **5.40 m**;
  mast arms and portals of one-piece square hollow sections (post RRW 300, arm 300 x 200), no splices, long portals as
  trusses; mast arm drawings TZ-1205 "one lane" (arm 3.0-4.5 m) and TZ-1206 "two lanes" (5.5-7.5 m), post about 6.1 m;
  arms up to 10 m, **from 7 m with a tie rod**; optional street lamp on top (10 m light point); the lane-arrow plate
  **above the red** on overhead heads, on a **hinge** (tall loads); colour anthracite DB703 (RAL 6013 reed green on
  older ones); Bern repaints in RAL 7042.
- No data: OSM has no usable tag for overhead heads. Rule only.

## The rule (per approach, `Layout/Exterior`)

After the approach's lanes and its plan's groups are known. A lane's **groups** are the moves it shows (its arrows).

1. **Trigger** (any one): (a) a lane whose nearest head showing its group (the right kerb's `Main`, the left kerb's or
   the refuge's `Second`) is more than `OverheadReach` 6 m sideways from the lane's middle (a left pocket on a 2+2 road:
   the left kerb is 7.5 m off; on a 1+1 road 4.5 m, no trigger); (b) two or more lanes show the same turn; (c) an
   approach of two or more lanes without a refuge on a road that yields when the lights are off (Bern's side road);
   (d) three or more lanes.
2. **Heads**: once triggered, one 300 mm head per distinct group set among the lanes, centred over its lanes; a
   right-turn-only kerb lane keeps only its kerb head. The kerb poles keep theirs (main, left repeater).
3. **Structure**, first that fits:
   - **span wire** where the arm carries a tram (OSM overlay `tram`, or rail street running on the arm): a wire across
     the arm between two anchor poles at the kerbs (8 m, by the main pole and the left kerb's pole), heads hung on
     hangers, lower edge 5.50 m, never within 1.5 m sideways of a rail;
   - **mast arm** from the right kerb's main pole (it becomes the post: kerb heads stay at 2.35 m on it) when the
     furthest head's middle is within 7.5 m of the post (10 m with a tie rod);
   - **mast arm from the refuge** reaching right where the approach has a refuge at least 1.5 m wide;
   - else a **portal** over the approach, posts at the right kerb and at the left kerb (or the refuge).
4. Lower edge of the overhead plates: `OverheadLowerEdge` 5.0 m (inside SSV's 4.50-5.50), 5.5 m on a span wire.

Tier 0 cases: Bern's Abb. 12-19 as approaches (lanes, refuge, groups) with the heads the figures show.

## Record

`RoadSignal` SGNL **version 3** (2 still read): per junction a list of `SignalOverhead` records: kind (arm, refuge arm,
portal, span), the arm index, foot A (tile-local), foot B (portal, span) or the arm's reach, top height, the car heading,
then per head its offset along the structure and its moves (the group as `SignalBuilder.ArrowMoves` reads it). The plan
byte gains the canton's **pole colour** (index into a small table: RAL 7001 default, DB703 Basel-Landschaft, RAL 7042
Bern; Geneva and Vaud from the user's photos, like `ArrowPlates`), applied to every signal pole and structure.

## Steps

1. `SignalPlan`: the record, version 3, the colour index; RoadGen's `PoleColour(canton)` beside `ArrowPlates`. Tier 0
   round trip.
2. RoadGen: `Exterior` decides (`OverheadPlan`), `TileRewriter.ExteriorWrite` places the feet (clear of carriageways,
   buildings, paths, as `PlacePole`) and writes; `--signal-check` counts structures by kind and fails a head over no lane,
   an arm past its reach, a head within 1.5 m of a rail. Tier 0: the Bern cases.
3. Runtime: `SignalBuilder` builds posts, arms (tie rod past 7 m), portal beams, span wires with a little sag, hangers,
   300 mm heads with plate and hinged arrow plate above; `SignalLamps` a lens size per instance (300 / 200 / 100 mm);
   collision on posts and anchor poles only (the lowest edge clears the 4 m vehicle limit). Model viewer: "Traffic light -
   mast arm", "- portal", "- span wire" (`TerrainShowcase`).
4. Test region: J6 and J7 (2+2 roads, double left) should get mast arms; a new tram arm for the span wire; a portal
   case; shots per structure; numbers on real tiles (Geneva, Zurich copies): approaches with overhead heads by kind.
5. Notes: `tools/traffic-signals`, `tools/signal-test-region`.

No new player action; bots: nothing (traffic obeys the plan, not the heads).
