# Parking areas as real lots

Status: **planned, agreed** (#499). Today a car park is a shader pattern and nothing else. This
document turns it into a laid-out place — rows of marked bays at the lot's own angle, aisles with
arrows, one findable entrance, cars in the bays, and the kerbside detail that makes it read as
designed rather than painted on.

Target: a player who drives into a Swiss car park should be able to tell where to go, which way
round the aisle runs, and which bay is free, without being told.

## Where we start from

These facts decide most of the trade-offs below.

1. **A car park is three lines of code.** `CoverFormat.PatternFor` maps `ParkingPublic`,
   `ParkingPrivate` and `RestArea` to `SurfacePattern.ParkingBays`; `CoverPalette` packs the
   pattern code into **vertex-colour alpha in quarter steps**; `shaders/body/terrain.gdshaderinc`
   dispatches on `int(COLOR.a * 4.0 + 0.5)` and paints a `bay_width` x `bay_depth` (2.5 x 5.0 m)
   grid of divider lines. No geometry, nothing standing in it.
2. **The grid runs on world axes, and cannot do otherwise.** TLM records no orientation for any
   surface pattern, and `never-derive-surface-tangent-from` forbids recovering one from the
   screen-space normal. `land-cover` already states the consequence: "the bay grid is
   world-aligned, so bays in a row that is not N-S/E-W do not follow the row". **This is the bug.**
3. **Alpha interpolates across a class boundary**, so the pattern bleeds one 2 m cell past the
   lot edge (`surface-patterns`). Anything that replaces it has to stop cleanly at a real edge.
4. **`.road` v3 takes new sections with no version bump.** A reader skips an unknown FourCC by
   length, so a `PARK` section loads in old game builds as absent. `APRP` area props, `PNT2`
   paint, `PPRP` point props and `LPRP` linear props all already exist, with runtime builders.
5. **A flush paved area is already a solved problem.** `AreaPropType.Pavement` (#123) is drawn by
   `src/Terrain/PavementBuilder.cs` in the road's asphalt, and the road blend holds the ground
   under it at road height (`HoldUnderPavement`) so the heightfield collision carries it like a
   ribbon. A parking pad is the same shape of thing, two orders of magnitude bigger.
6. **Kerbed areas are solved too.** `AreaPropType.Sidewalk` patches with `Height` = kerb and
   `PropFlags.Solid` are drawn and collided by `RoadStreetBuilder`/`StreetAreas.IsSolid` (#119),
   and `AreaPropType.Island` gives a grassed, kerbed island (#122). End islands and walkways need
   no new area machinery.
7. **Lane arrows exist, traced from the real SSV 6.06 drawing.** `ArrowShapes` and `Arrow()` in
   `tools/RoadGen/Rewrite/TileRewriter.TurnLanes.cs` build `PaintType.Arrow` triangles from
   outlines taken off the Commons SVG — the turn-lane note records that two attempts from memory
   were wrong first. They are `private static` and must be **shared, not copied**
   (`dead-code-and-shared-helpers`).
8. **The network stage has everything a planner needs.** `TileRewriter` works in 6 km blocks with
   a halo, and already holds the final segments, the junction polygons, `Facades` (wall triangles
   of the `.bldg`, loaded per tile on demand), `UrbanField` density, and the block's full-res
   terrain through `LoadGrids`/`SampleGround`. `StreetPlanner` and `CornerPlanner` are the
   precedent for a planner that consumes all of it.
9. **What the stage does *not* have is the parking polygon.** Cover is rasterised by `CoverStage`,
   which runs *after* the network stage, and the raster is 2 m and world-aligned anyway — using it
   would reintroduce fact 2. The polygon has to be dumped by a stage that holds the GeoPackage.
10. **The stage must stay reproducible.** `--rewrite` always works from the raw input in
    `<chunks>_temp/roads_raw/`, and a rebuild is checked byte-identical over 513 tiles. Anything
    new the stage reads has to live beside it, written before it, or that property is lost.
11. **Only an admin conjures vehicles online.** `VehicleManager.MayPark` gates *client* parks; a
    server-initiated `Place` is not gated, which is what `AfricaTwinEgg` relies on.
12. **`AfricaTwinEgg` already wants this.** It hunts for "a parking cell near the building if the
    cover raster has one, else a 2.5 m bay on the far side of the lane" entirely by hand, because
    no real bay exists to ask for.
13. **#496 phase 3 specifies the parked-vehicle mechanism**, and it is not built. Agreed with the
    owner: **#499 builds the shared layer, #496 phase 3 consumes it** (comment on #496).

## Where the layout is computed

A **pure-geometry `ParkingPlanner`** in the RoadGen network stage, emitting `APRP`/`PNT2`/`PPRP`/
`LPRP`/`PARK` into `.road` v3 — the mould of #123, #122 and #119. Baked, not runtime, because:

- **Seams.** A lot crossing a tile edge must get *one* layout. The stage sees 6 km blocks with a
  halo; a per-tile runtime pass would lay out each half independently and the rows would not meet.
- **Orientation.** The planner reads the real polygon and the roads beside it. A runtime pass from
  the cover raster is back to fact 2, which is the whole complaint.
- **Cost and agreement.** Nothing per tile build (the #221 rules), and every peer derives the same
  lot from the same bytes, so no part of a layout is ever sent.

The planner is **pure** (`Vec2` in, records out, no Godot, no I/O), which buys three callers:

| Caller | Gives the planner | For |
|---|---|---|
| `TileRewriter.Parking` | TLM rings, final segments, facades, ground | the real map |
| `ProceduralWorld.Settlements` | a generated village's lot rectangle | the generated fill, **no data needed** |
| `FixtureCourse` (`parking`) | a hand-built polygon | tier-1 checks, **no data needed** |

and a tier-0 unit test that pins a layout without any of them. The drive being unmounted does not
block a single step of this.

### Getting the polygon to the stage

Fact 9 says the stage cannot see cover, fact 10 says it cannot read anything written later. So the
**road stage** — which already holds the GeoPackage open — dumps the parking rings next to the raw
roads it already writes:

```
<chunks>_temp/parking_raw/<tile>.park     rings of tlm_areale_verkehrsareal (LV95, class, TLM uuid)
```

written by a `RawParking` helper modelled exactly on `RawRoads`, keyed by tile, `.part`-then-move
like the rest. `--rewrite` alone then still rebuilds byte-identically, and a region built before
#499 simply has no file and gets no lots. The hand-traced `cover_overrides.json` rings (#84) are
dumped through the same path, so Riddes house 15 gets real bays rather than a painted grid.

## The layout

All distances are Swiss practice (VSS 640 291a, Bern Normalien); every one is a named constant.

### 1. Orientation

The lot's own axis, in this order:

1. the bearing of the **bordering road** with the highest `priority`, if one runs within 30 deg of
   a principal axis of the polygon;
2. else the long axis of the polygon's **minimum-area rectangle** (rotating-calipers over the
   convex hull);
3. ties broken by the lower bearing, so the result never depends on ring winding or vertex order.

This is the fix for fact 2, and it is why the polygon has to survive to the stage.

### 2. Rows

Bays 2.5 x 5.0 m. Aisle and row depth by what fits, longest first:

| Layout | Module (two rows + aisle) | Used when |
|---|---|---|
| 90 deg, two-sided | 5.0 + 6.0 + 5.0 = 16.0 m | depth >= 16 m |
| 90 deg, one-sided | 5.0 + 6.0 = 11.0 m | depth >= 11 m |
| 45 deg, one-way aisle | 4.2 + 3.5 = 7.7 m | depth >= 7.7 m |
| parallel along the edge | 2.0 + 3.5 = 5.5 m | a strip, or a rest area |

Rows are laid from the axis through the polygon's centroid outward, so adding a vertex on the far
side does not shift every bay. A bay is kept only if its whole rectangle is inside the polygon,
inset 0.3 m, clear of every building footprint (`Facades`), clear of any road corridor, and its
ground is within 0.35 m of the row's plane (a lot on a slope terraces per row rather than
stretching one plane over it).

### 3. Aisles and arrows

The aisle network is one flush `AreaPropType.ParkingPad` triangulation covering aisles *and* bays
(one surface, so no seam between the two), with `PaintType.WhiteSolid` bay dividers 0.12 m wide
laid as geometry on it, open at the aisle end the way real bays are. One-way aisles (45 deg rows,
and any aisle narrower than 5.5 m) get `PaintType.Arrow` arrows every 15 m; two-way aisles get
none, which is also correct Swiss practice.

`Arrow()` and `ArrowShapes` **move from `TileRewriter.TurnLanes.cs` to `PaintEmitter`** and both
callers use the one copy (fact 7). No outline is retraced.

### 4. The entrance

On the bordering road with the highest `priority`, at the point of the lot edge nearest that
road's middle, 6.0 m wide (two-way) or 3.5 m (one-way lot):

- a throat joining the road edge to the first aisle, as pad;
- `PaintType.GiveWayLine` across the throat at the road, and a `StopLine` bar inside it;
- an `Arrow` pair, in and out;
- **barriered or not**, hashed from the lot key: a public lot in a town, or one at a station or a
  hospital, is paid and gets `PointPropType.TicketBarrier` (a housing and a boom, the boom's
  `Variant` saying up or down) plus a `TicketKiosk` within 3 m of the driver's window; a private
  or rural lot is open. A paid lot also gets a `ParkingSign` (the blue P) at the entrance.

Exit and entry are the same throat on a small lot, separate throats 12 m apart where the frontage
allows and the lot is over 60 bays.

### 5. If space allows

Only placed where the room test passes, and in this order of priority:

- **Sidewalks**: `AreaPropType.Sidewalk`, 1.5 m, kerb 0.12 m, along the row ends and from the
  nearest row end to the building door when one is within 40 m. Drawn and collided by the #119
  machinery unchanged.
- **End islands**: `AreaPropType.ParkingIsland`, kerbed, at a row end longer than 8 bays, 2.5 m
  wide, with **trees** in them. The trees are *not* `PPRP`: `CoverStage` runs after the stage,
  reads the islands out of the finished `.road`, and plants `KindSolitary` into the `.trees` file,
  so lot trees get the existing LOD, thinning and `TreeColliders` pooling for free.
- **Cart shelters**: `PointPropType.CartShelter` at a big-box store — a `Commercial` footprint over
  600 m² whose door faces the lot — one per 40 bays, within 25 m of a row end, never in an aisle.
- The bay pattern under a drawn lot is **suppressed**, or there would be two sets of bays (§Risks).

### 6. What the lot publishes

A new `PARK` section, so anything that wants a bay can ask for one instead of guessing (fact 12):

```
count u32, version u8 (1), then per bay 16 B:
  x, z f32 (tile-local centre), heading f32 (rad about +Y, 0 = -Z),
  flags u8 (Disabled, Loading, Shaded), kind u8 (Car, Motorcycle, Truck, Bus), pad u16
```

Bays are in a stable order (row index, then along the row), because the dormant-vehicle slot
ordinal is an index into them and must not move when a neighbouring tile is rebuilt.

## Dormant vehicles: the shared layer

The cars are **real drivable vehicles**. Spawning forty replicated `VehicleBody` nodes per lot is
not affordable, and scenery you cannot drive away is not what was asked for. So, exactly as
`docs/plans/industrial-sites.md` §"Phase 3" specifies — this builds it, that consumes it:

- **`DormantVehicles`, generalised over slot providers.** A provider is a pure function of a tile
  yielding `(owner, slot ordinal, VehicleSlot)`, where `VehicleSlot` carries LV95 position, yaw,
  `RideKind`, catalogue index and colour, all hashed from the owner key and the ordinal. Server
  and every client compute the same fleet from the same bytes; nothing is sent. **Parking bays are
  the first provider** (owner = the tile, ordinal = the `PARK` index); `SiteVehicles.For(tile)` is
  the second and needs no new machinery.
- **Fill fraction** hashed from the lot key, with a floor and a ceiling — a station lot is nearly
  full, a rural one nearly empty — and never the bay nearest the entrance, because a player
  arriving wants somewhere to put their own car.
- **Dormant draw**: the shared cached meshes `Traffic` already uses, one MultiMesh per tile per
  kind, with static collision from a pool, the way `TreeColliders` pools tree collision rather
  than building it per tile.
- **Waking**: anything that would move one — `VehicleReach` aiming at it, a collision, a shot —
  makes the client ask the server, which promotes the slot **exactly once** through
  `VehicleManager.Place` under the deterministic node name `veh_bay_<E>_<N>_<slot>`; every peer
  that sees that name stops drawing the dormant copy, the check `AfricaTwinEgg` already does. A
  server-initiated `Place` is not subject to `MayPark` (fact 11).
- **Re-sleeping is deliberately out of scope** until it is measured, as the industrial plan says.

## Phases

One issue, one PR (#499), in this order — each step is testable before the next:

1. **Format**: `AreaPropType.ParkingPad`/`ParkingIsland`, `PointPropType.TicketBarrier`/
   `TicketKiosk`/`CartShelter`/`ParkingSign`, `PaintType.DisabledBay`, the `PARK` section, codec
   round-trip, `--format-check`. Append-only, never reordered.
2. **`ParkingPlanner`** (pure) + `ParkingPlannerTests` + `RoadGen --parking-check` and
   `--parking-svg`.
3. **`RawParking`** dump from the road stage, `TileRewriter.Parking`, region stats.
4. **Runtime**: `ParkingBuilder` (barrier, kiosk, shelter, signs), the pad through
   `PavementBuilder`, kerbs through `RoadStreetBuilder`, paint through `RoadPaintBuilder`, the
   blend holding the pad, pattern suppression.
5. **Fixture course + generated village lot**, so it can be looked at with no data.
6. **`DormantVehicles`** + the wake path + the tier-2 check.
7. **Notes** (`docs/notes/tools/parking-lots.md`, `docs/notes/terrain/parking-runtime.md`, index
   lines), this plan updated, `industrial-sites.md` §"Phase 3" pointed at the shared layer.

## Verification

- **Tier 0** (`tools/test.sh unit`): bays inside the polygon; no bay overlapping another or an
  aisle; rows parallel to the fitted axis; the axis independent of ring winding and vertex order;
  a **pinned layout** for a fixed polygon; a **pinned slot list** for a fixed tile, because the
  industrial plan's own risk list calls a client/server fleet disagreement "a new consistency
  surface".
- **Tier 1** (`tools/test.sh quick`): `--chunks fixture:parking`; `--parking-check` synthetic
  self-check beside `--street-check`; `--parking-svg E,N` plan views.
- **Tier 2** (`tools/test.sh net`): waking is authority and replicated state, so it is checked on
  the **remote** peer — the car one client wakes is a real vehicle on the other, in the same bay,
  with the dormant copy gone on both. Required by the root `CLAUDE.md`.
- Screenshots from above of a laid-out lot (fixture, and a real one when the drive is back), and
  `--perflog` on a tile with a big lot before merging.

## Risks

- **Two sets of bays.** The shader pattern is baked from the cover raster at runtime and knows
  nothing about a drawn lot. Unsuppressed, every lot gets painted geometry *and* a world-aligned
  grid bleeding one cell past its edge (fact 3). The suppression has to key on the pad, not on the
  cover class, because a lot too small or too steep to lay out should keep the old pattern — it is
  still better than bare asphalt.
- **Bay order is a wire contract.** The dormant slot ordinal indexes `PARK`. If a rebuild reorders
  bays, every parked car in the region moves. Hence the stable order and the pinned test.
- **Lots on a slope.** A 200-bay lot on 4 % ground is not a plane. Terracing per row is the plan;
  if the row planes fight the blend, the fallback is to drop to one-sided rows and let the aisle
  take the grade.
- **Cost.** A big retail lot is a lot of paint primitives (two dividers per bay). The budget is the
  `road-format-v3` one, +10 % raw per tile; dividers are the thing to compact first if it blows,
  and `PNT2` already stores a referenced line as a reference rather than vertices.
- **A lot with no road.** TLM maps car parks with no mapped access. Those get rows and no entrance
  rather than being skipped — the ground is still better than a grid.
- **Overlap with #496.** The dormant layer lands here; if phase 3 starts concurrently it is built
  twice. Notified on #496 and to the session working on it.
