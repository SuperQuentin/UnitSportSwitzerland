# Overhead contact lines: trams and electrified rail (#1052)

## Decisions with the user (Oct 21 2026)

- **Scope**: all electrified rail, trams and main lines (standard and metre gauge, rack lines). The tiles carry no
  electrification (TLM has none): every rail at grade or on a bridge is electrified except funiculars (`Funicular`) and disused
  lines (`Disused`); none in tunnels (the wire ends at the portal). OSM `electrified=*` is a later refinement.
- **Street supports** (tram track in a carriageway or town paving): a span wire across the street, anchored on facades where
  buildings stand close on both sides, else on a catenary mast at the kerb (mixed where one side has a facade).
- **Spacing**: about 30 m on straight tram track (Bern: spans at most 32 m), tighter on curves.
- **Traffic lights** stay separate (#1037's facade span wires stay as they are).

## Reference values

- Tram: contact wire 5.80 m over the rail, zigzag (stagger) ±0.30 m, spans at most 32 m (Stadt Bern, Gleisersatz
  Brunnhof-Fischermätteli, technical report on the electrical installations, BERNMOBIL); a single contact wire (trolley-style,
  600 V), no messenger.
- Main line: contact wire 5.30-5.75 m over the rail (BLS, Lötschberg base line, EBV S3); taken 5.50 m. A messenger wire above
  it, droppers between (a catenary), system height taken 1.2 m; spans taken 60 m on straight track; cantilever masts beside the
  track.
- Curves: the wire runs straight between supports, so the chord's deviation from the track (L²/8R) must stay within the zigzag
  both ways: L ≤ √(8·R·0.6) = √(4.8·R) (12 m on a 30 m tram curve, 69 m on a 1000 m main-line curve).
