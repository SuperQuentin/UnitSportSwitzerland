# A round world: Earth curvature drawn round the camera

Status: **re-planned Nov 2026, in progress** (#187). The floating origin (`docs/plans/floating-origin.md`,
#185) is done; a regression net comes first (#1429: `docs/notes/general/regression-net.md`). The true
ellipsoid at altitude is the follow-up, `docs/plans/ellipsoid-handover.md` (#1430).

**Nov 3, 2026**: the flat CPU world below is a stage, not the end state. With the neighbours being
imported and the whole Earth to follow, tiles move to true Earth positions on a cube sphere
(`docs/plans/global-tiling.md`, #1443); this drop then shrinks to refraction only.

## Why

The world is flat: LV95 east/north become Godot X/-Z, and altitude becomes Y. The surface really drops
away from a viewer by about d² / 2R; with normal atmospheric refraction (k ≈ 0.13) it looks like
d² / 2R × 0.87:

| Distance | Drop (geometric) | Apparent drop (with refraction) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 km | 8 cm | 7 cm |
| 10 km | 7.8 m | 6.8 m |
| 50 km | 196 m | 171 m |
| 60 km (default `HorizonKm`) | 283 m | 246 m |
| 100 km | 785 m | 683 m |

So the snow peaks 50 km down the Rhône stand ~170 m too tall, and the server's line of sight over
`horizon.bin` (`Net/Interest.Clear`) sees through the Earth.

## What was planned first, and why it changed

The first plan (Oct 2026) followed Microsoft Flight Simulator 2020 (Asobo, GDC 2022: tiles meshed from
true positions on the ellipsoid, an "anchor space" following the camera with Y = up, collision read
back as a height lookup): rigid floating-origin shifts with a rotation (phase B), every tile in its own
tangent frame on WGS84 (phase C), a global tiling later (phase D).

Measured while re-planning: **rigid tile frames crack at our altitudes.** Tiles rotate against their
neighbours by θ = 1 km / R; a shared edge vertex at altitude h moves by h·θ, which is **~30 cm at
2,000 m** (verticals converge, so a 1 km LV95 tile is 1000·(1 + h/R) m wide at altitude h). The fix is
exact per-vertex geometry, which `HeightMapShape3D`'s regular grid cannot hold: every tile collider
would become a trimesh, every builder (roads, buildings, trees, interiors) would move to tile frames,
and every `Position.Y` read as altitude would need an audit. MSFS can do it because it owns its
physics; we run Jolt with heightmaps, cars, ragdolls and buildings.

What the rigid frames would add inside Switzerland is below what anyone can see: converging verticals
and a turning "up" within the 3 km origin radius are under 0.04°, the LV95 scale error ~0.02 %. The
one large effect is the drop, and a drop applied round the camera reproduces it exactly.

## Decisions

| Question | Decision |
|---|---|
| How the world is round near the ground | **A render-space drop**: every world shader lowers its vertices by d² / (2·R_eff) round the camera. CPU positions, physics, `GlobalPos`, the wire, tiles and builders stay flat LV95. |
| Refraction | **Apparent curvature**, k = 0.13 (the surveying standard, what photos and swisstopo panoramas show): R_eff = R / 0.87. R = 6,378,816 m (LV95's projection sphere), R_eff ≈ 7,331,970 m. |
| Measured from | `world_cam_pos` (`Styles/CameraGlobal`, pushed at `FramePreDraw` after any shift, through a doorway indoors), never `CAMERA_POSITION_WORLD`: shadow passes, mirrors, portals and both VR eyes get the same drop. |
| The server | Its line-of-sight rules (`Interest.Clear`, `PhotoJudge`) apply the same drop measured from the eye. |
| A true ellipsoid | At altitude only: `docs/plans/ellipsoid-handover.md` (#1430). The global tiling (old phase D) is that plan's question too. |

## Design

**The rule: CPU world positions stay flat; every draw curves.** The drop goes only into clip-space
`POSITION` and into screen projections of far places. `VERTEX` and `world_pos` stay flat, so facet
normals, `pattern_xz`, portal clipping, the sightline cut, the horizon cover and the far-tree ray
tracing do not change.

- `src/Core/Curvature.cs` (plain C#, unit-tested): R, k, R_eff, `Drop(d)`, `Apparent(p, eye)`, the
  line-of-sight helper; `Enabled` from `--curvature 0|1` and the debug menu (the client's picture
  only).
- `world_curvature` global (1/(2·R_eff), 0 = flat), pushed on change; `shaders/common/curvature.gdshaderinc`
  with `curved_view(view, world, VIEW_MATRIX)` and `uncurve(world)`, included from
  `common/world.gdshaderinc` and the avatar body.
- One line in each body's `POSITION = retro_snap(...)` (terrain, road before its far lift, building,
  tree incl. the far billboards, water, prop, path, interior, avatar), the bonfires, signal lamps and
  event flames; `uncurve()` where water reads the depth buffer.
- Culling: `ExtraCullMargin` = the drop at the farthest distance on horizon blocks and tile geometry.
- `TargetMarker` projects `Curvature.Apparent`.
- Accepted: StandardMaterial3D / Label3D far objects (race gates, POI markers, thermal clouds,
  particles, name tags) do not drop; their error is d / (2·R_eff), 0.04° at 10 km. Sun, north and the
  compass are unchanged (the follow-up's).

## Test

Through the regression net (#1429): `--curvature 0` on the branch matches `main`'s reference
screenshots within their noise floor in every shot; with curvature on, `near` shots and the model
viewer stay within it and `far`/`edge`/`night` change as intended; `tools/test.sh coords` compared
with the baseline, the pinned long-range rules (`--interestcheck` at 90 km, the PhotoJudge case at
20 km) flipped on purpose. Two players far apart on a level plain stop seeing each other where the
bulge hides them, on the server as on screen.
