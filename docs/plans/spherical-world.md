# A round world: placing tiles on the ellipsoid

Status: **planned, after the floating origin** (`docs/plans/floating-origin.md`, #185). Issue: #187.

## Why

The world is flat: LV95 east/north become Godot X/Z, and altitude becomes Y. This is fine for one
country, but it breaks in three ways as the world grows.

**1. Curvature is missing, and that is already visible today.** The surface drops away from a
viewer by about d² / 2R. With normal atmospheric refraction (k ≈ 0.13) it looks like
d² / 2R × 0.87:

| Distance | Drop (geometric) | Apparent drop (with refraction) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 km | 8 cm | 7 cm |
| 10 km | 7.8 m | 6.8 m |
| 50 km | 196 m | 171 m |
| 60 km (default `HorizonKm`) | 283 m | 246 m |
| 100 km | 785 m | 683 m |

So the snow peaks 50 km down the Rhône stand about 170 m too tall today. The line of sight over
`horizon.bin` in `InterestService` ignores curvature too, so it calls players visible when the
Earth is in the way.

**2. A single projection distorts more the further it reaches.** The scale error of a conformal
projection like LV95 grows roughly as d² / 2R² from its centre: about 0.3 % at 500 km, 1.2 % at
1,000 km and 5 % at 2,000 km. Grid north also drifts away from true north (meridian convergence).
That affects the sun, the stars and the compass. A flat map can never wrap around the planet, and
it fails at the poles.

**3. "Down" is not the same everywhere.** Over 1,000 km the vertical turns by 9°. A single flat
frame cannot have correct gravity for a plane flying from Geneva to Berlin.

## How Microsoft Flight Simulator (2020) does it

From Asobo's GDC 2022 talk, *Designing the Terrain System of Flight Simulator: Representing the
Earth* (Lionel Fuentes):

- **Data layout: a quadtree in the Mercator projection** (the Bing Maps tile system). The talk
  calls it "not ideal but the vast majority of available data uses it" and accepts that it
  "stretches at the poles". A tile's level of detail is just its subdivision depth.
- **The projection only addresses data. It does not shape the world.** Tiles are rendered on the
  real, curved Earth.
- **Large coordinates**: node positions on the CPU are **64-bit doubles**. On the GPU they use an
  inverted depth buffer and an **"anchor space"**: its origin is the camera position, its Y axis
  is the Earth's surface normal, and its X and Z are "computed from previous frame" (so they
  "drift over time"). The transform chain changes from local → world → view → projection to
  local → anchor → view → projection, so a vertex never passes through full-size world
  coordinates on the GPU.
- Carrying X and Z over from the previous frame, instead of deriving east and north fresh each
  time, means the frame never has to spin at the poles. "North" is computed, not assumed.
- **Collision** comes from the terrain at one fixed LOD (15), read back to the CPU.
- **Legacy flat geometry** (FSX airports) still exists, as small flat patches on the curved world.

What we take from it:

- The tile lattice is an **addressing scheme**: keep it.
- Geometry is placed on the **true ellipsoid**.
- The local frame is an **anchor that follows the camera** with Y = local up.

Godot (single precision, Jolt) cannot re-anchor physics every frame like MSFS. Our anchor moves
in steps instead: the floating origin from #185, with a rotation added to each shift.

## Decisions (proposed)

| Question | Proposal |
|---|---|
| Earth model | **WGS84 ellipsoid**. A sphere would cost the same and be wrong by up to 21 km in radius. |
| Global position | **ECEF doubles** (earth-centred, earth-fixed) inside `GlobalPos`, with conversions to geodetic (lat/lon/h) and LV95. LV95 → ETRS89 uses the existing `SwissProjection` (swisstopo approximate formulas, about 1 m, which is enough for placement). |
| Local frame (anchor) | The **tangent plane at the floating origin**: Y = ellipsoid normal there. Each shift is a **rigid transform** (a translation plus a small rotation), not a plain translation. |
| Anchor X/Z | Carried over from the previous anchor by the **minimal rotation** that takes the old up to the new one (the MSFS approach). No pole singularity. North is computed from the frame wherever it is needed. |
| Tile lattice | **Kept** (LV95 km tiles) as addressing for Switzerland and its neighbours. A global tiling is a later, separate phase that only a planet-scale world needs. |
| Tile geometry | Each tile is meshed in **its own tangent frame** at its centre, with curvature baked into the vertices. Its node transform is anchor⁻¹ · tile frame, computed in doubles and then cast to float. |
| Gravity | `-Y` of the current anchor. The error is at most the shift threshold / R: 3 km / 6,371 km = 0.03°, which no one can see or feel. |

## Design

### 1. Frames in the floating origin (lands with #185, or right after)

- `WorldOrigin.Shifted` carries a **`Transform3D`** (rotation plus translation) instead of a
  `Vector3`. On a flat world the rotation is the identity, so #185 can ship that way first.
- `GlobalPos` holds ECEF. The conversion `WorldOrigin.ToWorld/ToGlobal` becomes a rigid transform
  in doubles (anchor frame ↔ ECEF).
- Altitude is no longer simply Y: it is `geodetic(ToGlobal(p)).h`. Code that reads `Position.Y` as
  altitude above sea level is a new audit item (there is a lot of it). Within one tile the
  difference is the sag (up to 4 cm in a 1 km tile, about 70 cm at the 3 km shift threshold).
- A shift rotates the world as well as moving it: velocities and the angular state of physics
  bodies, cached directions and camera smoothing rotate by the same small angle.

### 2. Tiles on the ellipsoid

- **Tile frame**: tangent plane at the tile centre. Mesh vertices are built in that frame, either
  exactly (LV95 → geodetic → ECEF → tile frame) or with a quadratic sag (dx² + dz²) / 2R, which
  is accurate to well under 1 mm within 1 km. Builders run on workers, so the exact path is
  affordable, but it is measured first.
- **Collision**: `HeightMapShape3D` stays usable. In the tile frame, the sag goes into the heights,
  and the horizontal error is about 10⁻⁸ relative.
- **Horizon blocks** (10 km): same method. The sag at a block corner is about 4 m, so it must be
  baked in.
- **Seams**: neighbouring tiles share edge vertices computed from the same geodetic points, but in
  different frames and through float transforms. Measure the crack size (it should be well under
  1 mm) and add skirts if it shows.
- Roads, buildings, trees (MultiMesh), cover, interiors and door portals: all built in their tile
  frame. Within a tile, "up" differs from the tile frame's up by at most 0.006°, so objects are
  simply placed upright in the tile frame.

### 3. Everything that assumes a flat world

- **Sun, moon and stars** (`World/DayNight`): from the real latitude and longitude of the camera
  and the time, expressed in the anchor frame.
- **North**: compass, map UI, minimap and GPX overlays take north from the frame, not from `-Z`.
- **Distances**: the server uses ECEF chord distances (within 10 ppm of the geodesic, 1 m at
  100 km). `InterestService` line of sight over `horizon.bin` subtracts the curvature drop
  (with refraction).
- **Network**: positions on the wire are `GlobalPos` from #185. Whether that means ECEF or
  tile + offset is settled there.
- **Aircraft**: long flights work because the anchor turns with the ground under them. Check that
  flight instruments (attitude and heading) read the anchor frame.

### 4. Global tiling (only for a planet-scale world)

The LV95 lattice is fine as addressing from Switzerland out to roughly the neighbouring countries.
Far from Bern the tiles still look right (geometry comes from true positions), but their real size
drifts and the lattice cannot wrap. For a world beyond Europe, replace the addressing with a global
quadtree:

- **Web Mercator quadtree** (the MSFS and Bing/OSM choice): matches most available data, but its
  density is uneven and it stretches toward the poles.
- **Cube-sphere or S2 cells**: even density and no poles, but almost no data arrives in it.

This touches the preprocessor, the formats (`TileId`, `manifest.json`, `horizon.bin`) and the
generated fill. Decide when a region outside Europe is actually planned. Do not do it before.

## Phases

**Phase A: visual curvature, now (small, independent).**
- In the world shaders, drop vertices by d² / 2R × 0.87 relative to the camera, through one shared
  include. Apply the same drop in the `InterestService` line-of-sight test.
- Fixes the Swiss panorama straight away. Phase C replaces it with real geometry and then deletes
  it, and the image does not change.
- Limitation: materials outside our shaders (particles, standard materials) do not drop. That is
  invisible near the player (8 cm at 1 km) and acceptable for an interim.

**Phase B: rigid-frame floating origin (with or after #185).**
- `Shifted(Transform3D)`, `GlobalPos` in ECEF, an anchor frame on the tangent plane, and the
  minimal-rotation carry-over. The altitude audit (`Position.Y` as altitude).
- Done when: `--originstress 50` passes with rotations switched on, including physics bodies at
  speed and a long flight.

**Phase C: tiles on the ellipsoid.**
- Tile frames, baked sag, collision, horizon blocks, seams, roads, buildings and trees. Sun and
  north from the real position. Delete Phase A's shader drop.
- Done when: a screenshot of a known panorama (for example the Alps from the Jura) matches a
  photo, seams show no cracks under `--originstress`, and a flight from Geneva to St. Gallen has
  correct gravity and heading all the way.

**Phase D: global tiling (only if the world goes planet-scale).** See above.

## Multiplayer test

Dedicated server plus two clients on loopback, with `--originstress 50`:

- A flies 300 km east while B stays put. On B, A's aircraft stays at the right height above the
  terrain on the horizon, with no tilt jump at A's shifts.
- Two players 80 km apart on two peaks: each sees the other only if the line of sight clears the
  curved ground, matching what the server decided.

## Risks

- **Phase B's altitude audit** is large: any code that reads `Position.Y` as height above sea
  level or above the tile.
- **Physics under rotation**: rotating bodies by a few hundredths of a degree at each shift should
  be harmless, but a car on a slope at speed is the case to measure.
- **Mixed frames** inside one tile (props from data in LV95, procedural ones in tile space): every
  builder has to agree on the tile frame. Put this in a note and make one helper the only way
  to do it.
- **Phase D** is a data and format project of its own. Do not let it block A to C.

## Out of scope

- Ocean and tides, or geoid versus ellipsoid heights. swissALTI3D heights are orthometric
  (LHN95). At this scale the difference from ellipsoid heights is a near-constant ~50 m offset in
  Switzerland and does not change what is rendered. It needs a decision when data from another
  height system merges in.
