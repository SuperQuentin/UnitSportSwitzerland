# Home trainer rides: a GPX route on the roads, turn at junctions, a BLE smart trainer (#901)

Ride like Zwift or MyWhoosh. Import a GPX, ride it on the mapped Swiss roads, and the trainer's
watts move the bike. Nobody steers: the bike follows the road. At each junction the rider can
take another branch. Decided with the owner (Oct 19 2026):

- **Power**: a Bluetooth **FTMS** smart trainer on Windows (watts and cadence in, grade sent back
  as resistance). Keyboard/pad watts stay as the fallback, which is also how checks ride.
- **Off the GPX**: keep riding the road network. The straight-most branch is the default and
  arrows show at each junction. A **Rejoin** option leads back to the GPX.
- **Solo first.** Group rides (other trainer riders, ghosts on the same course) come later.

## What is there

- `Bicycle` runs the power equation on `RiderWatts`, with the road's grade
  (`m·a = P/v − ½ρ·CdA·v² − Crr·m·g·cosθ − m·g·sinθ`). Its doc comment already expects a trainer
  ("RideLink"), but none was ever written. `GameSettings`' Sim profile keeps watts meaningful.
- `FootPlayer.RideControls` replaces the keyboard while mounted (bots, probes and `AutoPilot` use
  it). The lean-steered `AutoPilot` can already hold a bike on a `RaceRoute`.
- `src/Gpx/`: `GpxParser` → `GpxTrack` (LV95). `TrackMatcher` map-matches it onto `RoadNetwork`
  (HMM + Viterbi, 98% of fixes on a road on a real 16 km ride). `RoadNetwork` is a graph: edges in
  LV95 with surveyed **heights** (bridges, tunnels), node adjacency, `Near`, `Path` (Dijkstra).
  `TrackRibbon` draws a course draped on the terrain.
- `World/LaneGraph`: lanes, widths, one-way, approaches at junctions (traffic and `RaceRoute`).

What is missing:
- `TrackMatcher` returns matched **points**, not the **edge sequence** a route needs.
- There is no "on rails" ride mode, no junction choice and no trainer link.

## Design

### 1. The course (`src/Trainer/RideCourse.cs`)

- **Built from** `RoadNetwork` plus the GPX's matched edge sequence. `TrackMatcher.Match` also
  returns `Edges: List<(int Edge, bool Forward)>` from the Viterbi path, with consecutive repeats
  collapsed and gaps bridged with `RoadNetwork.Path`.
- **The rider's place** is `(edge, arc, forward)` plus a lateral offset: keep right, half a lane
  in from the centreline, the lane width taken from `LaneGraph` when the edge has one.
  Position, tangent, curvature and **grade** all come from the edge. Height comes from
  `RoadEdge.Height`, so bridges and tunnels hold.
- **The plan** is the GPX edge list with an index. `OnRoute` is true while the current edge is the
  planned one.
- **At each node ahead**, list the travellable outgoing edges (`IsTravellable`, one-way
  respected, the incoming edge excluded). Sort them by signed turn angle measured 15 m in. Then
  pick the **default**:
  - on the route: the planned next edge;
  - off the route with Rejoin on: the next edge of the rejoin path;
  - otherwise: the smallest turn angle (straight-most);
  - at a dead end: turn around.
- **The choice** is an intent: `RideInput` gets a `Turn` field (−1 left, 0 default, +1 right) and
  a `UTurn` bit. A choice made before the junction is held until the junction is passed. "Left"
  takes the next branch to the left of the default.
- **Rejoin**: `RoadNetwork.Path` runs from the rider's node to the nearest planned node that is
  still ahead (within 3 km along the plan), which avoids routing back to a node already passed.
  The result is a temporary plan. Riding back onto the GPX edges resumes `OnRoute`.
- **Tile streaming**: the course loads `RoadNetwork` around the GPX's bounding box plus 2 km. Off
  the route, it loads further tiles as the rider nears the edge of the loaded area, off the main
  thread, like the matcher.

### 2. Riding on rails (`FootPlayer`)

- New seam: `FootPlayer.RideRail` (a `RideCourse`). When it is set, the mount's `Step` still
  computes the scalar speed (power, drag, grade, brake). The heading and position then come from
  the course and steering is ignored. Lean stays derived (`tan φ = v²κ/g`), so the bike banks
  into bends without anyone steering. The camera is the normal third-person chase, with
  first person available.
- **Collisions**: the ride ghosts through traffic and props, as in Zwift. A road-locked bike that
  stops dead on a parked car is worse than passing through it. Gravity and the ground are not
  used while on rails: the height is the road's.
- **The trainer forces the Sim profile** (watts only mean something there), as `--ride` already
  does.
- **Entry**: a menu item "Trainer ride…" (main menu and pause menu) opens a GPX file dialog,
  reusing `GpxSession`'s dialog, then a pairing screen (skippable) and the start at the GPX's
  first point. Headless: `--trainer <gpx>`.

### 3. RideLink: the trainer (`src/Trainer/`)

- `IRideLink { bool Connected; float Watts; float? Cadence; float? Speed; float? HeartRate;
  void SetGrade(float grade); }`.
  - `KeysLink`: the throttle holds a target power, and +/− steps it by 10 W (starts at 150 W).
    Without a trainer, the keys drive the ride.
  - `FtmsLink`: a Bluetooth smart trainer.
- **FTMS** (Fitness Machine Service `0x1826`):
  - **Indoor Bike Data** `0x2AD2` (notify) gives instantaneous speed, cadence and power. A flags
    word says which fields are present.
  - **Control Point** `0x2AD9`: Request Control `0x00`, Start `0x07`, then
    **Set Indoor Bike Simulation Parameters** `0x11` (wind m/s ×1000, grade % ×100, Crr ×10000,
    Cw kg/m ×100). Every write is acknowledged with `0x80` indications.
  - Fallbacks for trainers or power meters without FTMS: Cycling Power `0x1818`/`0x2A63`
    (watts) and CSC `0x1816` (cadence), read-only. Heart rate `0x180D` comes from a second device
    if one is paired.
- **Grade sent**: the road's grade averaged over the next ~10 m, scaled by a **trainer difficulty**
  setting (default 50%, as in Zwift), with descents halved again. It is sent at most once a
  second, and only when it changes by ≥ 0.2%. The rider's real mass comes from settings.
- **Windows BLE**: WinRT `Windows.Devices.Bluetooth` needs the TFM
  `net9.0-windows10.0.19041.0`. It is set **conditionally** in the csproj (Windows only), and the
  link code sits under `#if WINDOWS`. The Linux server and the Android client keep `net9.0` and
  see only `KeysLink`. **This is a spike first** (phase 2a): confirm that the editor build, the
  Windows export and `tools/release.sh` accept the windows TFM. If they do not, the fallback is a
  small bridge exe that talks to the game over localhost UDP.
- **Pairing UI**: scan → list (name, signal strength, FTMS / power / HR) → connect. The device
  is remembered in settings and reconnects on start. The connection state shows in the HUD.

### 4. HUD

- Watts, cadence, speed, heart rate when paired, grade, distance done / left, and the
  **elevation profile** of the plan ahead with the rider's dot on it.
- **Junction arrows** from 150 m before each junction (or less at walking pace). One arrow per
  branch, the default highlighted, the GPX branch in the route colour. They are drawn on the road
  and repeated in the HUD.
- "Off route, Rejoin (key)" banner. The course ribbon (`TrackRibbon`) draws the plan ahead.

### Controls (three devices, `new-action-three-devices`)

| Action | Keyboard | Gamepad | VR |
|---|---|---|---|
| Take left / right branch | `move_left` / `move_right` (A/D; steering already means this) | left stick or D-pad ←/→ | right stick ←/→ (`XrPad` D-pad when mounted) |
| Turn around | new `ride_uturn` (U) | D-pad ↓ | right stick ↓ |
| Rejoin GPX | new `ride_rejoin` (J) | D-pad ↑ | right stick ↑ |
| Fallback power ±10 W | new `ride_power_up/down` (+/−) | LB / RB | grip + right stick? decide in phase 1 |
| Pedal (fallback) | `throttle` | RT | trigger |

Each control gets a row in `xr/vr-action-map`, appears through `InputHints`, and is listed in
`ControlsHelp`.

### Bots (`new-feature-bots`)

- The turn choice is an intent (`RideInput.Turn` / `UTurn`), never a raw `Input` read. A
  server-run bot can ride a `RideCourse` through `RideControls` with fixed watts.
- Row in `bots/bot-capability-map`: "trainer course ride: gap until phase 4", because the solo
  ride is offline. The PR says so.

## Phases

1. **Course and rails, key power** (offline):
   - `TrackMatcher` edge sequence, `RideCourse`, `FootPlayer.RideRail`, junction choice,
     straight-most default, U-turn, Rejoin;
   - `KeysLink`, a minimal HUD with arrows, `--trainer <gpx>`.
2. **Trainer**:
   - 2a: spike on the Windows TFM;
   - 2b: `FtmsLink` (data in, grade out), CP/CSC/HR fallbacks, pairing UI, difficulty setting.
3. **Ride polish**:
   - elevation profile, ride summary (time, average and normalised power, ascent);
   - save the ridden track as GPX (Strava upload later, through its connector or a file).
4. **Group rides** (multiplayer): replicate the course position, other riders seen on the same
   roads, drafting (the `slipstream` note), bots on courses.

## Tests

- **Unit** (`tools/test.sh unit`):
  - `RideCourse` branch ordering and defaults (route, straight-most, dead end), Turn held across
    the junction, Rejoin target ahead and never behind;
  - FTMS Indoor Bike Data parsing over every flags combination, and Control Point encoding,
    from captured byte frames.
- **Quick** (`--trainercheck`, `--world fixture`): ride a fixture GPX with fixed watts. Assert
  that the rider stays within 1 m of its lane, the distance matches the power equation, a forced
  left at the fixture junction leaves the route, and Rejoin brings it back.
- **On the owner's trainer** (by hand): pairing, watts and cadence in the HUD, resistance felt
  changing on a climb. A playtest scenario in `tests/playtests/`.
