## 2. The ground under a site: stamps

A lander must not hang over a fresh 20 m bowl the crater field put there; a colony needs level ground; a pad and a
runway must be flat to the centimetre. `CraterField` is generated from the body's seed, so this is fixed in the height
function itself, the one every peer and every square asks:

- `BodyHeights` takes an optional `SurfaceStamps` (plain C#): a list of discs and strips on the body, each with a
  centre normal, a radius, a falloff and a mode. **Calm**: the generated craters fade out inside it (the data's slope
  kept: a historic site stays on its real ground). **Level**: the ground goes to one height (the data's height at the
  centre plus an offset) inside the radius and eases back to the natural ground across the falloff (colonies, pads).
  **Strip**: the same along a segment (the runway, the mass driver).
- The stamps are looked up through a coarse grid of cells on the sphere (as `CraterField`'s are), so a point far from
  every site pays one hash.
- The near square (2 m cells) holds a pad's edge; the medium square and the globe get the stamps too, so a colony's
  plateau does not float over the medium ground from afar.
- Tests (`MoonSitesTests`): the height inside a Level stamp is constant, a Calm stamp leaves the data's height, outside
  every falloff the height is unchanged to the bit.
