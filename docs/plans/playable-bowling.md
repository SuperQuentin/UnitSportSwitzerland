# Playable bowling (#1323, epic #1322)

Status: **planned, started.** Branch `feat/1323-bowling`, worktree `../UnitSportSwitzerland-1323`.
Pinball (#1325) is a separate plan and PR.

## Decisions (owner, 2026-10-28)

- **Controls, Wii Sports style**: zoom on the pins, then choose your spot on the approach, then aim,
  then the force (hold to set it). The **hook** comes from moving the mouse or stick sideways during
  the swing, like twisting the remote, and a curve preview shows it.
- **VR**: a real throw. The controller's release velocity and wrist roll become the ball's speed,
  line and spin. The Wii-style steps stay available in VR as an alternative.
- **Physics**: the server owns the result and the roller sees no lag (refined in the Design below).
- **Pinsetter**: a realistic deck cycle seen from the approach:
  - the table drops to pick up the standing pins after the first ball;
  - the sweep clears the deadwood;
  - the table re-spots the pins;
  - the ball drops into the pit, runs through the **subway under the capping** and is lifted into
    the hood.

  There is **one ball return per lane pair**, with a thunk. The machine behind the masking is not
  animated.
- **Joining**: you sign in at the **shoe counter**, which gives you a lane pair and a turn order
  (up to six players). Your figure **wears bowling shoes** while you are signed in.
- **Ball**: you pick a house ball at the rack (weight and colour) and carry it to the lane. It stays
  yours: after each roll it comes back to the pair's hood, and you lift it from there. In VR you
  grip it.
- **Bots**: you can add bot partners at the shoe counter. Server bots also go to a centre and bowl a
  game on their own. They aim at the pocket with a skill spread.

## Findings that shape the design

- **The server has no interior nodes and no interior collision** (`InteriorManager._Ready` returns
  early when headless). Nothing simulates a RigidBody on the server today: `DroppedItem` is
  simulated by its owner's client.
- **The interest pattern to copy** is inner doors and lifts:
  - state is keyed `(plan key, piece index)`;
  - the server checks `SpaceOf(peer)` and `LocalOf`;
  - `RpcId` goes only to peers inside;
  - a client sends `Request…(key)` once when it enters.

  It is not the cinema's broadcast to everyone.
- **The shared-clock replay pattern** is the crane's (`CraneService`): the server stamps the start
  time (`ClockSync.SimNow`) and every peer plays the same motion.

## Design

1. **The ball on the lane is a pure function** (`BowlingBall.Path(release, oil)`). It goes through
   three phases:
   - **skid** on the oiled first ~12 m;
   - **hook**: the spin turns into lateral speed on the dry backend;
   - **roll** into the pins.

   The gutters catch it past the lane's edges. Server and roller compute the same path from the
   release, so the roller's ball rolls at once and is never corrected on the lane.
2. **Pins in Jolt, on the server only, in a space of its own per active lane**
   (`PhysicsServer3D.SpaceCreate`), built from `LaneSpec` (pure plan data):
   - the pin deck, the kickbacks, the pit and its back cushion;
   - ten pins (0.381 m, 1.5 kg, mass low);
   - the ball arriving at the deck with the path's velocity and spin.

   The server simulates the roll **faster than real time** right after the release (~4 s of
   motion). It records ball and pin poses at 30 Hz into a compact clip (quantised position and
   rotation, a few KB) and sends it with the start time to the peers inside. Every peer replays the
   clip at the same `SimNow`. The ball needs ~2 s to reach the pins, so the clip arrives before it
   matters. No physics runs on clients, and nothing runs while a lane is idle.
3. **The lane rig on clients** (`LaneRig`, one per `LanePair` in a built interior):
   - the pins, the balls on the hood and the pinsetter's table and sweep are cut out of the merged
     mesh (`IsCarvedOut`) and become nodes;
   - the deck cycle is a timeline driven by the lane's phase and its start time;
   - the ball's subway run is shown where it shows (the pit, then the hood's lift).

   Nothing rebuilds a mesh.
4. **Game state on the server** (`BowlingLanes`, node `World/Bowling`), per `(plan key, pair)`:
   - the bowlers in turn order, their frames and the current frame and ball;
   - the phase (idle, waiting for a ball, rolling, cycle) and the standing pins.

   Ten-frame scoring is a pure, unit-tested function (strikes, spares, the 10th frame, splits
   marked). Joining is `ServeSignIn` at the shoe counter, which puts you on the pair with the fewest
   bowlers. Each action has a `Serve*`: `ServeSignIn`, `ServeTakeBall`, `ServeRelease`,
   `ServeLeave`, `ServeAddBot`. Bots call the same methods.
5. **Ball and shoes**:
   - the house ball is a held item that carries its weight and colour (`HeldItemVisual` at the hand);
   - the shoes override the `Feet` slot while you are signed in, in the hook where
     `OccasionHats` writes `OutfitBits`.
6. **Controls**:
   - a bowling mode at the approach (`BowlingAim`) takes the camera (a zoom on the pins, then
     behind the bowler) and steps through spot → aim → swing;
   - pad: stick and A;
   - VR: grip the ball, swing, let go (velocity from the hand's track), or the same steps through
     `XrPad`.

   `InputHints` show each step. New rows go in `vr-action-map` and `ControlsHelp`.
7. **Score screens**: the two screens over each pair show the frame grid, drawn into a small
   texture on change (`perf-no-per-frame-allocations`), with a strike and spare flash.
8. **Sound** (`[SoundShowcase]`): the ball's roll (its pitch from its speed), the pin crash (from
   the clip's impacts), the gutter, the sweep, and the thunk at the hood.
9. **Bots**:
   - **partners**: a bot body that stands at the approach. Its throw is the pocket line plus a
     skill spread, through `ServeRelease`.
   - **on their own**: a director picks a centre near players and plays a game there. This needs
     the bot inside the interior: a server floor for that one venue, built from its plan. That is
     the general gap in `bot-capability-map` (interiors), scoped here to bowling halls. It is done
     last, and it goes to its own issue if it grows.

## Phases (each pushed, each with a screenshot)

1. The lane rig: carved-out pins, hood balls, table and sweep, the deck cycle playing on a timer
   (model viewer, `--bowlingcheck`).
2. The ball's path and the pin simulation: a headless check that rolls known lines (strikes,
   pocket hits, gutters, 7-10 splits) and asserts the pin counts.
3. Game state, scoring, sign-in, shoes, ball pick, net sync. A `bowlingnetcheck.sh`: a remote peer
   sees the same pins fall and the same score. Late joiners get the standing pins.
4. Controls on keyboard, pad and VR, the camera and the score screens.
5. Sound.
6. Bots: partners first, then bots bowling on their own.
