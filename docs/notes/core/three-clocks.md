# Three clocks: environment time, simulation speed, real time (#579)

Every timed system reads exactly one of three clocks. Plan and phase status:
`docs/plans/three-clocks.md`.

| Clock | Read it as | What belongs in it |
|---|---|---|
| **Environment** | `World.WorldClock` (`Hour`, and `EnvNow` from phase 3) | time of day, traffic and railway signals, plant and build growth, campfire burn, craft stations, resource regrowth, fishing odds, the dawn chorus, clocks on walls |
| **Simulation** | `Core.GameClock.Now` locally; the server-owned `SimNow` for anything compared across peers (phase 2) | physics, vehicles, player movement, projectiles, weapon rate of fire, AI and NPCs, ragdolls, damage windows |
| **Real** | `Core.RealClock.Now` | UI, audio presentation, network plumbing, persistence stamps, force feedback, polling worker threads |

Environment time rides simulation speed, so at 0.25x the sun and the traffic lights crawl along
with the cars and the world stays internally consistent. Both world clocks are owned by the
server; real time is per-process and never synced.

## `RealClock` (built, phase 1)

`Core.RealClock.Now` is this process's monotonic wall clock in seconds.

- **Read it as a deadline, never as a delta**: `if (RealClock.Now < _nextAt) return;` then
  `_nextAt = RealClock.Now + Period;`. A deadline is right however often it is polled; an
  accumulator (`_since += delta`) silently under-counts the frames on which nobody looked, which is
  why there is deliberately no `RealClock.Delta`.
- **Why it exists**: `Engine.TimeScale` scales the `delta` the engine hands to every `_Process` and
  `_PhysicsProcess` in the process, and Godot offers no unscaled per-frame delta. A cadence
  accumulated from `delta` therefore stretches by `1 / TimeScale` — at 0.1x a 2 s period becomes
  20 s. That was worst in `Net.ClockSync`, whose pings are what both world clocks are derived from:
  a time scale would have degraded the sync precisely when the rest of the design depends on it.
- It does not speed up under `--fixed-fps` either, unlike `GameClock.Now`. A check waiting on a
  worker thread wants exactly this clock (`general/fast-checks`).

Moved onto it in phase 1: `ClockSync` ping cadence, `Handshake` client protocol timeout,
`QueryResponder` LAN snapshot, `InterestService` evaluation period, `MapScreen` download-job poll,
`MultiplayerScreen` LAN broadcast and probe, `LoadingScreen` joke rotation, `WebRadio` stream scan.

Deliberately left on the engine's `delta`: `Net.Swarm` (a load tool that measures frame times),
`Ui.TitleDiorama` (menu cosmetic, never scaled), `Audio.Ambience` (presentation, and moving it has
audible consequences — decide it with the rest of the audio in phase 2), and every probe and check,
which run at 1x.
