# LV95 to the Earth and back (#1430, phase H2)

- **Two ways from LV95 to latitude and longitude.** `SwissProjection` (tools/TerrainFormat) is
  swisstopo's approximate polynomials: under a metre in Switzerland, worse further out, and not
  an exact inverse. Fine for GPX and for placing French data near the border. Anything that must
  **round-trip** or leaves the country (the ellipsoid handover, `docs/plans/ellipsoid-handover.md`)
  uses `SwissGeodesy`: the rigorous oblique conformal cylinder on Bessel 1841, then CH1903+ to
  CHTRS95/ETRS89, which is a pure translation by definition (674.374 / 15.056 / 405.346 m).
  LV95 → ECEF → LV95 comes back within 0.1 mm, even 1,400 km out and 400 km up.
- **The way in from latitude and longitude** (GPS, OSM, IGN): `SwissGeodesy.ToLv95(lat, lon[, hEll])`
  and back `ToLatLon(e, n[, hLhn95])`, rigorous however far out. The game uses only these (#1443,
  G0a: GPX import and export, photo spots, landmarks, berths, the 1 August quays); the preprocessor's
  stages too since #1507, through `GeoInput` on the built tiles' ground (`tools/cube-compare`). A point without a height is taken at 500 m
  ellipsoidal; the height moves it by 2.6-2.8 cm per km (the datums' normals are not parallel), so
  pass it when it is known.
- **Types**: `EcefPos` (earth-centred doubles), `Geodetic` (degrees, ellipsoidal height),
  `Ellipsoid` (`Wgs84`, `Bessel1841`; geodetic ↔ ECEF), all in TerrainFormat so the preprocessor
  has them too; `Core/Planet` (`Earth`: GM, rotation, `Gravity(r)`, `CircularSpeed(r)`);
  `GlobalPos.ToEcef()` / `GlobalPos.FromEcef`. CHTRS95/ETRS89 is taken as WGS84 (~0.5 m apart today).
- **Heights.** `GlobalPos.Alt` and swissALTI3D are LHN95, roughly above sea level. The ellipsoidal
  height on WGS84 is that plus ~44-58 m: the separation of Bessel from WGS84 (~47-53 m) plus the
  geoid above Bessel, `SwissGeodesy.BesselGeoid` (−0.63 m in Bern, −6 to +5.7 m over the grid).
  It is a 20 km grid (273 values, cm) of swisstopo's CHGeo2004, bilinear, held at its edge
  outside 2,460,000-2,860,000 / 1,060,000-1,300,000. Both directions use the same model, so
  continuity never depends on its accuracy; only absolute heights against foreign data do.
- **The reference is swisstopo's REFRAME** service, `https://geodesy.geo.admin.ch/reframe/<op>?easting=&northing=&altitude=&format=json`:
  - `lv95towgs84` takes the altitude as a height **above Bessel** and applies no geoid (a fitted
    geoid came out as 0.0001 m, which is how this was found). It checks the projection and datum
    shift: they match to < 2 mm, inside and outside Switzerland.
  - `lhn95tobessel` gives the geoid (altitude 0 in, the geoid out). It answers over the whole grid
    but not at 2,900,000 / 1,300,000. To resample the grid, loop over the nodes with `xargs -P 8`
    (273 calls in a few seconds) and paste the centimetres into `GeoidCm`.
- **Grid north against true north**: `SwissGeodesy.GridConvergence(e, n)`, the meridian convergence (0 on Bern's
  meridian, ~+0.93° at Geneva); what the game turns every grid heading by (`core/true-north`, #1504).
- **The projection sphere's radius is 6,378,815.904 m** (`SwissGeodesy.R`): the same R the
  render-space curvature of #187 uses near Bern.
- **The valid domain** (#1480): LV95 round-trips to a tenth of a millimetre over the whole Earth **but a
  sliver within ~0.13° of Bern's antimeridian** (172.56° W; ~15 km wide at the equator, narrower toward the
  poles). The Gauss conformal sphere's longitude is α ≈ 1.00072 times the ellipsoid's, so it covers 2πα and
  overlaps there; points in the sliver come back up to 31 km off. `SwissGeodesy.InDomain(ecef)`,
  `MaxFromCentre` (π/α). `FromEcef` calls `SwissGeodesy.OutsideDomain` for a point in it, and the game logs a
  `[geodesy]` warning with the stack (`Core/Main`, at most every 10 s): never quietly. Heights alone
  (`Lhn95Height`, `OriginFrame.AltitudeOf` in an anchor frame) need no projection and are fine there.
  - Until #1480 the longitude's angle from Bern was not wrapped (`FromCentre`): from 172.6° W to 180° it
    came out as −187° to −180° and that whole 7.4° band was up to 31 km off (a polar orbit from Bern flies
    down its edge). Inside ±π the wrap changes nothing, to the bit.
  - Anything that must hold everywhere goes through ECEF, never `GlobalPos`: the Space anchors
    (`FrameAxes.Tangent`), the orbit. The wire and the server still carry LV95 (G2 of #1443 moves them).
- Tests: `tests/UnitSportSwitzerland.Tests/SwissGeodesyTests.cs` (REFRAME points, geoid between
  nodes, round trips, the equator and poles, out to the moon's distance) and `PlanetTests`.
