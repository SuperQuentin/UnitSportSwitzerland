# GlobalPos is an Earth position: ECEF inside, ENU maths, LV95 as notation

Since #1502 (G2 A1's last step, `docs/plans/global-tiling-g2.md`). Before it `GlobalPos` held LV95 `E`/`N` and an altitude.

- **Inside**: three ECEF doubles (WGS84). No `E`/`N`; the constructor is private. In: `GlobalPos.FromLv95(e, n, alt)` (a
  Swiss source, a literal, a typed coordinate), `FromEcef`, `FromWire`. Out: `Lv95` (`Lv95Coord(E, N, H)`, the notation:
  a person's text, a Swiss file, the LV95 tile key until D), `Ecef`, `Geodetic`, `Enu` (an `EnuFrame` there), `Alt` (LHN95).
- **Its maths are the Earth's**, in the local east-up-south at the place (x east, y up, z south), **true north, true
  metres**: `OffsetTo` / `Moved` (metres east, north, up; the horizontal kept level: east and north where the other
  place's plumb line meets this place's height, up the ellipsoidal height difference; exact inverses), `+ Vector3` /
  `a - b` (a straight vector in the ENU at the place; `b + (a - b)` is `a`), `BearingTo` (true north),
  `DistanceTo` (the chord), `HorizontalDistanceTo` (the level distance: the chord less its part along the mean plumb
  line, the geodesic at the mean height to 0.13 mm at 5 km, symmetric, no trigonometry), `Midpoint`, `WithAlt`.
- **A flat world's vectors are not ENU.** Every frame in play today is the flat LV95 one, whose world axes are the
  grid's: grid north turns from true north by the meridian convergence (0 at Bern, +0.95° at Geneva, −2° at Scuol) and a
  world metre is a grid metre (80-190 ppm). A world vector or yaw goes through the frame: `frame.EnuOf` /
  `WorldOfEnu` (also with a `GlobalPos` place), `TrueYaw` / `WorldYaw`, `EnuTurn` / `WorldTurn`; or take both ends to
  world (`origin.ToWorld(a) - origin.ToWorld(b)`) or to places (`origin.ToGlobal(w)`). Mixing them is a bug of up to 2°
  (35 cm on 10 m at Scuol).
- **Records keep LV95 grid yaws and vectors** (what A2 called GlobalPos's offset axes: `VehicleState`, `RadioState`,
  `DropState`, `Structure.Yaw`, `Sleeper.Yaw`, pallets, herds, `FootPlayer.GlobalYaw` / `GlobalVelocity`, the server's
  flat-world maths): they are the flat world's, so nothing changed for them. They convert with `Lv95Grid` (`TrueYaw`,
  `GridYaw`, `ToEnu`, `FromEnu`, `Axes`) and the frame's `WorldYawOfGrid` / `GridYawOf` / `WorldOfGrid` / `GridOfWorld`
  / `WorldTurnOfGrid` / `GridTurnOf` (free in a flat frame). Never feed one to `Moved` / `+` without `Lv95Grid.ToEnu`.
  They move to true north with G2 D.
- **Cost** (a loaded Windows machine, per call): `+` / `-` 0.12-0.15 µs (the ellipsoid's gradient as up, no iteration),
  `HorizontalDistanceTo` 0.1 µs, `DistanceTo` 15 ns; `Geodetic` 0.5 µs; `Alt`, `Lv95`, a flat `ToWorld(GlobalPos)`
  ~1 µs; `FromLv95`, a flat `ToGlobal`, `OffsetTo` ~1.5 µs; `Moved` ~2 µs; a flat `EnuAt` / `WorldYaw(GlobalPos)` /
  `Lv95Grid.*` ~4-5 µs. Per frame: `FootPlayer.Global` is kept while the body and the frame stay put, a remote's drawn
  heading turns through an `EnuNear` (one geodesy call per 100 m); keep a `Lv95` you read twice.
- **Equality** is the exact doubles: a place that went LV95 → ECEF → LV95 is not `==` to itself; compare with
  `DistanceTo(x) < tolerance`. `default(GlobalPos)` is the Earth's centre (an unpublished place).
- Tests: `GlobalPosTests` (ENU against `EnuFrame`, Vincenty within 1 mm at 5 km, the 172.56° W sliver), `WireFrameTests`
  (the grid helpers), `FlatLv95RatchetTests` (the LV95 member count only shrinks).
