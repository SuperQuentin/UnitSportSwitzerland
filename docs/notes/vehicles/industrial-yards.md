# Industrial yards: the fleet outside a site (#516, #496 phase 3)

The second provider for the dormant layer #499 built (`dormant-vehicles`). #497 gave the industrial
sites their insides; this parks their fleets on the ground outside.

- **`DormantSlots.ForSite`** sits beside `ForParking` in `src/Vehicles/VehicleSlot.cs` — **no
  Godot**, tier 0. Rows across the building's own front, at the pitch the longest thing in the
  fleet needs (4.2 x 16.5 m where there are lorries, 3.2 x 6.0 m where there are not), so a 13.6 m
  artic and a hatchback are never laid out on one grid. Every roll comes from the slot's own place
  in its own yard, never from a running counter, so one site's fleet never shifts when a
  neighbour's changes — the rule that lets the ordinal name the vehicle.
- **The mix is the trade**: a depot holds trailers on their legs, bare tractors and whole coupled
  artics with a staff car among them; a warehouse is mostly trailers at the dock; a works and a
  body shop park cars only. A dealership's forecourt is packed (0.92 against a works' 0.40),
  brighter, and all facing one way, because a forecourt is arranged to be looked at and a working
  yard is not.
- **A coupled artic is ONE slot** with `Train` set on a truck `KindId`, never two side by side: the
  pin angles are parked state and reconstructing them from two poses drifts. **A lone trailer gets
  `Load = 0`** — `Load` is the *tractor's* payload and the trailer's lives in `Train`'s high byte,
  so loading a trailer through the wrong field fails silently and reads as a physics bug. Both
  pinned in `SiteYardTests`.
- **`SiteYards`** (`src/Vehicles/SiteYards.cs`) is the Godot half, which may use `src/Interiors`
  where the provider may not. It finds each site with `BuildingTypes.SiteFor`, takes the wall whose
  door `BuildingFootprint` has already aimed at the street — that is where an apron, a forecourt
  and a lorry park actually are — and lays the yard in front of it past a 7 m `Apron` so the doors
  stay usable. Depth by trade: a depot 34 m (an artic has to swing), a warehouse 26, a works 20, a
  forecourt 17, a body shop 13.
- **A slot in a building or on a road is dropped, not nudged.** `SiteYards.Blocked` is applied in
  `DormantVehicles.Yards` rather than in the provider, because deciding it needs the tile's
  geometry and tier 0 has none. Dropping keeps the ordinals, which name the vehicle: a gap in a
  yard is fine, a renumbered fleet is not, and a nudged car is a car in a hedge.
- **The yard frame**: yaw 0 faces −Z (`RoadSignBuilder.Frame`), so out of the wall is
  `(−sin, −cos)` and along it is `(cos, −sin)`; `+v` is further from the building, and the heading
  whose facing is the door's outward normal is `atan2(−x, −z)`.
- **A mirrored heading is invisible to the obvious test.** `Every_slot_stands_inside_its_own_yard`
  walks the frame back with the same formula that laid it out, so it passes either way; and an
  assertion on |angle| off a world axis cannot tell +14° from −14°. #499 shipped exactly that bug
  in `ParkingPlanner.ToGodotHeading` — right square to north, wrong by twice the axis otherwise —
  with both of its angle checks green, and it was caught by eye in a screenshot. So `--sitecheck`
  asserts a **physical** fact instead: a forecourt car's **nose is further from the building than
  its tail**. That holds whichever convention is right, and it fails the moment the sign flips
  (verified by injecting the mirror: "a slot faces its own building, nose 18.5 m, tail 23.5 m").

## The apron (#583 phase 3)

The 7 m `Apron` between the facade and the fleet is no longer empty: a warehouse and a works keep a
**forklift** there (`DormantSlots.ForkliftOf`, 1.5 m past one end of the facade, ordinal 1000 so
the grid's names did not move), and the stacks of pallets a forklift lifts stand in a row along the
facade (`Items/SitePallets`). Both read `SiteYards.Fronts`, the yard with its facade's real span:
**the yard is centred on the main door, not on the facade**, so anything laid out along the facade
from the yard's own width lands past one corner wherever the door is off-centre. Details:
`pallets`.

## A lorry is not a car with different paint

`DormantVehicles.Draw` once instanced **every** slot from `TrafficMeshBuilder.Car` with a car-sized
box, which would have drawn a 13.6 m artic as a hatchback. #516 gave lorries their own path, which
built `BuildVisual` per slot — the tractor alone, no trailer, a full cab and dashboard each time,
and again at every redraw (#552 measured what that cost in Geneva). Since #552 every slot, car or
lorry, is drawn from its look (`DormantLooks`, see `dormant-vehicles`): the merged
`BuildParkedVisual` of the very state `Promote` wakes it with, every section posed, built once per
kind, train and load and instanced. Collision is `ParkedBox` plus each section's `ExtraBoxes`,
exactly as `VehicleBody` does for a real parked train.

## Check

`--sitecheck --systems ui` (`SiteProbe`, extended): for each of the five sites it asserts the yard
exists, has a fleet, that nothing stands inside the building it belongs to, and that the trade's
mix is right (goods vehicles at a depot and a warehouse, none at a works, a body shop or a
forecourt). It then builds every slot's `Rideable` and its mesh — the part no unit test can reach —
and, windowed, writes `<shot>_yards.png` looking across the depot's yard, because the only way to
see that an artic is an artic is to look at one. 13 tier-0 tests in `SiteYardTests` pin the
placement itself.

The wake path is untouched: #499 owns it and `tools/parkingnetcheck.sh` already checks it on both
peers.
