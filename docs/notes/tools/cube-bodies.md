# Other bodies' pyramids: `--cube-body` and the colour bake (#1849, H7c of `docs/plans/planets.md`)

The Moon's (and later Mars') coarse levels, in the Earth's format (G1's tiles, bundles, a manifest), on the body's own
cube (`CubeShape.Moon`: the 1,737.4 km sphere, face 0 on the near side's centre, the poles the centres of faces 4 and 5;
`tools/cube-tiles` has the format, `CubeShape` the placing).

## Heights: `TerrainPreprocessor --cube-body`

```
dotnet <tp>/TerrainPreprocessor.dll --cube-body moon --dem <whole-body DEM .tif> --cube-out <dir> [--cube-levels 0-5] [--jobs 4] [--cube-check]
```

- **The DEM** (`CylRaster`): one band of int16, uint16 or float32, uncompressed strips, classic TIFF or BigTIFF, simple
  cylindrical over the whole body (2:1, 180° W to 180° E, 90° N to 90° S), GDAL's SCALE/OFFSET metadata and NoData
  applied. That is how USGS ships LRO LOLA's `Lunar_LRO_LOLA_Global_LDEM_118m_Mar2014.tif` (92,160 × 46,080, int16 in
  0.5 m over the 1,737.4 km sphere, 8.5 GB) and MGS MOLA's 463 m mosaic. A missing sample takes the mean of the valid
  ones round it.
- **A height is a function of its lattice point**, as `--cube-global`'s: the point's planetocentric latitude and
  longitude (what the DEMs are gridded in; on Mars' ellipsoid it differs from the normal's by up to 0.3°), the mean of
  bilinear samples over its footprint. Two faces holding one edge point compute the same bits, which `--cube-check`
  proves. The maths live in `CubeBodyHeights` (linked into the tests, `CubeBodyTests`).
- **One face at a time**: the DEM is read as a window of rows and longitudes round the face (`CubeBodyHeights.WindowOf`,
  margins for the widest footprint), each row once and in file order, kept as the file's 16-bit samples. LOLA's
  windows: ~1 GB for an equatorial face, ~2.6 GB for a polar one (every longitude).
- **Mars' heights over its ellipsoid** (#1853): MOLA's topography is measured from its areoid, which stands kilometres
  off the IAU ellipsoid the cube is on. `--areoid mega90n000eb.img` (MOLA MEGDR, 16 px/°, big-endian int16 over
  3,396,000 m, east longitudes 0-360; `Areoid`, `AreoidTests`) adds the areoid's radius less the ellipsoid's along the
  same ray: `--cube-body mars --dem Mars_MGS_MOLA_DEM_mosaic_global_463m.tif --areoid mega90n000eb.img --cube-levels 0-4`.
- The manifest names the body (`"body": "Moon"`) and its orientation; the Earth's manifests carry no body.
- Build it out of the worktree (`no-builds-inside-the-project`): `dotnet build tools/TerrainPreprocessor -c Release -o <scratch>`.

## Colour: the Godot bake with `--body`

```
python tools/body_colour.py <colour .tif> <colour.jpg>          # Godot's Image reads no TIFF
<godot> --headless --path . -- --globealbedo <dir> --body moon --colour <colour.jpg>
```

- The Moon's colour is NASA SVS's CGI Moon Kit `lroc_color_poles.tif` (27,360 × 13,680 RGBA, LZW, ~400 m a pixel):
  LROC WAC's colour, Hapke-normalised, so it carries no shadows of its own (the LROC WAC *morphology* mosaic does: its
  low sun's shadows would be drawn twice under the game's own light). One image a tile (`CubeAlbedo` with one "month"),
  WebP, the Earth's sizes (`GlobeAlbedoBake.SizeFor`).
- Mars' colour is USGS's `Mars_Viking_ClrMosaic_global_925m.tif` (23,059 × 11,530 RGB, 0.8 GB): the 232 m one (12.7 GB)
  is cut to 21,600 across anyway. `body_colour.py` takes a mosaic a pixel or two off 2:1.
- The globe's brightness (`BodyGlobe.Exposure`) comes from the level-0 colour's mean: Moon 0.458 → 1.05, Mars
  0.316 → 1.52, the Earth's Blue Marble 0.169 → 2.2.
- The bake refuses a pyramid of another body (the manifest's `body`).

## Mercury and Venus (#1854)

- **Mercury**: USGS's `Mercury_Messenger_USGS_DEM_Global_665m_v2_32bit_clon0.tif`. Despite the name it is int16 in
  metres, LZW in 256 px tiles; `CylRaster` reads strips only, so GDAL makes an uncompressed strip copy first
  (`gdal.Translate(..., creationOptions=['TILED=NO','COMPRESS=NONE','BLOCKYSIZE=16'])`). The heights are over a
  2,439.4 km sphere, which becomes Mercury's reference (`Ellipsoid.Mercury`, `CubeShape.Mercury`). Colour: the greyscale
  250 m mosaic of 2013 (61,324 px across), averaged to 21,600 by GDAL first. The "EnhancedColor" mosaics are false
  colour (blue and orange): not used.
- **Venus**: Magellan's `Venus_Magellan_Topography_Global_4641m_v02.tif` (8,192 × 4,096, int16 metres over 6,051.0 km,
  `Ellipsoid.Venus`), levels 0-2; colour: the colourised radar mosaic `Venus_Magellan_C3-MDIR_Colorized_Global_Mosaic_4641m.tif`.
- `--globealbedo --body NAME` takes any body of the registry but the Earth.

## Bodies with no global heights: `--dem flat` (#1854)

Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto and Titan have global mosaics (Voyager/Galileo SSI 0.5-1.4 km; Titan's Cassini ISS
4 km) but no global DEM. `--cube-body NAME --dem flat` writes the pyramid's tiles at the reference sphere (levels 0-3, 0-2
for Titan, a few seconds); the colour carries the body from orbit, and the near ground's relief is its profile's
(`CelestialBody.CraterDensity`: Callisto 1, Ganymede 0.5, Europa 0.03, Titan 0.02, Io none). Sources, all -180..180 and
2:1 within a pixel: `Io_GalileoSSI-Voyager_Global_Mosaic_ClrMerge_1km.tif`, `Europa_Voyager_GalileoSSI_global_mosaic_500m.tif`,
`Ganymede_Voyager_GalileoSSI_Global_ClrMosaic_1435m.tif`, `Callisto_Voyager_GalileoSSI_global_mosaic_1km.tif`,
`Titan_ISS_P19658_Mosaic_Global_4km.tif`; their colour bakes take 1-21 s.

## The giants (#1854)

Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune are globes too: `--cube-body NAME --dem flat --cube-levels 0-1` on their IAU 1-bar
ellipsoids (`CubeShape.Jupiter`...), coloured from the sky's own 2k maps (`--colour res://assets/space/NAME_2k.jpg`, ~220
km a pixel on Jupiter). A giant's globe always hangs from its centre under `BodyGlobe`'s root, shrunk past the far plane
(its sphere of influence is millions of km, so it is the anchor where its tiles would pass the far plane; no ground to
hold to the millimetre). Saturn's rings are a ring mesh under the same root (`BodyGlobe.AddRings`,
`shaders/body_rings.gdshader`: the sky's ring shader without the sky's depth). Finer maps (Cassini's, Juno's) are a later
swap of the colour alone.

## The craters below the data

`Terrain/Bodies/CraterField` (#1849, the cratered profile): every bowl from 2.5 m to 320 m the 118 m DEM cannot hold,
seeded in a 3D grid round the sphere (no face seams), counted by the maria's equilibrium N(>D) = 0.154·D⁻² a km² (D in
km), fresh and worn ones by a seeded age, a parabolic bowl, a rim and ejecta to two radii. A function of (seed, point):
every peer makes the same ground. `CraterFieldTests`. The near ground adds it (`world/body-ground`), at the body's
`CelestialBody.CraterDensity` (Moon, Mercury, Callisto 1; Mars 0.15; 0 elsewhere).
