# Buildings on cube leaves (G2 B3 PR 3, #1507)

The buildings stage (swissBUILDINGS3D with GWR, French buildings from BD TOPO) made cube-native on the pieces
of `cube-frames`; the LV95 build (`buildings`, `france`) runs the same code. Tests: `FeatureSpaceTests`.

## The interface (`tools/TerrainPreprocessor/FeatureSpace.cs`)

`IFeatureSpace<TTile>`: where a feature stage works. A stage keeps its maths; this gives it
- `Tiles` (what the run writes), `Ground` (`IGround`, heights above sea level on the plane);
- `SourceBox(source)`, `SourceRegion(source)`: what to read, in the source's own coordinates;
- `FromSource(source, a, b, h)` → plane (x, y, hSea); `FromLatLon(lat, lon)` → plane;
- `TryOwner(x, y, out tile)`: the tile under a feature's centre, if this run writes it;
- `ToLocal(tile, x, y, h)` → floats in the tile's frame (x east, y up, z south).

Instances:
- `Lv95FeatureSpace(tiles, ground, geo)`: plane = LV95, owner `TileId.FromLv95`, local (E − MinE, H, MaxN − N),
  latitudes and longitudes through `GeoInput` (at the built ground, #1526). Deleted with the LV95 build (D).
- `FrameFeatureSpace(plan, ground, marginM)`: the frame's `WorkPlane`, owner the **core** leaf under the point's
  geodetic normal, points plane → ECEF → leaf anchor **rigorously** (`WorkFrame.ToEcef`, `EnuFrame.ToLocal`; no
  bilinear `LeafTransform`), so a building hanging tens of metres over its leaf's edge is as exact as one inside.

Generic on purpose: the roads and other point/line stages may take it.

## The stage

- `BuildingExtractor.Extract(space)` → `Dictionary<TTile, List<Building>>`. **Source-side work stays in LV95**:
  the stray-face pass and the GWR join (footprint box ±1 m in GWR's own LV95 points) are a join of two LV95
  sources, not stage geometry, so EGIDs match the LV95 build exactly. Then the geometry goes through the space:
  every ring point projected once; the owner (the source box's centre) and the 3 × 3 re-seat grid are the
  **source box's** (LV95 axes), each point taken onto the plane, so a frame (turned ~1° from grid north)
  samples the ground where the LV95 build does (a grid turned with the plane moved hillside buildings by tens
  of centimetres: vertical p99 40 cm against 22 cm on the subset); vertices and `MinY`/`MaxY` (the ring
  points' local heights) by `ToLocal`.
  A solid whose LV95 centre is outside `SourceRegion` is dropped before its vertices are projected (a frame's
  query box is its whole cell).
- `FranceBuildings.Build(features, space, stats)`: rings from `LatLonRings` by `FromLatLon` (the LV95 instance
  does what `BdFeature.OntoGround` did), owner by centroid, ground, walls/roofs by `ToLocal`; `MinY`/`MaxY` the
  base and ridge at the centroid in the tile's frame.
- LV95: `BuildingStage.Run` keeps its own ground (`OwnTileGround`: only the tile `FromLv95` names, unlike
  `Lv95Ground`) so its bytes do not move; `FranceStage` passes `Lv95Ground` as before.
- A building straddling a leaf or frame edge: owned whole by the leaf under its centre, never split, exactly as
  an LV95 tile owns it; a frame keeps only what its core owns, so each building is written once.

## `--cube-buildings`

```
TerrainPreprocessor --cube-buildings --cube-out <region with heights> --buildings-gdb <zip> ... --gwr data.sqlite \
  [--france minLon,minLat,maxLon,maxLat] [--cube-box minE,minN,maxE,maxN] [--tiles-file F] [--temp DIR] [--cube-margin 250]
```

- Wanted leaves: the manifest's leaves with `Heights` (in the box, under the listed LV95 tiles). One
  `FrameDriver` over them, margin 250 m (sources only); per frame a `FrameGround` reading any leaf's heights
  lazily from its own frame's `.cubl`, the extractor, the French footprints (BD TOPO fetched once for the box),
  one `Buildings` section a leaf (`LeafBuildings`, Brotli), `LeafWriter.WriteFrame`, the manifest at the end.
- French buildings carry `YearBuilt = FranceStage.FrenchMarker` as in the LV95 tiles.
- Keep `--temp` on one directory across runs: the FileGDB reader reuses unzipped sheets (1.5 GB for the 82
  sheets on D:; the first unzip is most of a small run's time).
