# Cube tiles: the global format and its tiler (#1443, G1, #1452)

The format the whole Earth will be tiled in (`docs/plans/global-tiling.md`), built beside the LV95
path: nothing in the game reads it yet (G2 switches). Code in `tools/TerrainFormat/Cube*.cs`,
`EnuFrame.cs` and `tools/TerrainPreprocessor/Cube*.cs`; tests `CubeSphereTests`, `CubeTileTests`.

## The cube sphere (`CubeSphere`, `CubeKey`)

- Equal-angle gnomonic: face (u, v) → `axis + tan(uπ/4)·right + tan(vπ/4)·up`, turned into ECEF and
  normalised, is the **geodetic normal** of the point (so lat/lon need no iteration; a height moves
  the point along it). `OnEllipsoid(normal, h)` is the point.
- **Orientation, fixed forever** (part of every address): face 0 centred on 46.95108° N 7.43863° E
  (the LV95 origin), twisted 28.5° about it. All eight cube corners are then in oceans, the nearest
  151 km from a Natural Earth 1:110m coast (Laptev Sea); searched every 0.1° over the 90° that
  differ (`ne_110m_land.geojson`, public domain; the search script is not in the repo, its result
  is pinned by `The_orientation_puts_each_corner_where_it_was_chosen`). The North Pole is an ordinary
  point of face 0, the South Pole of face 2.
- Keys `F<face>/L<level>/<x>_<y>` (x along u, y along v, 0 at −1); `Describe()` adds the centre's
  lat/lon, as every tool prints them; `FileStem` `F0_L13_x_y`. `Neighbour(dx, dy)` folds across a
  face edge on the doubled integer lattice (null past a cube corner: 3 cells meet there, so a corner
  cell has 7 neighbours). `LatticeOn` maps a lattice vertex to another face.
- **Exact edges**: lattice coordinates are exact doubles, `Warp` is odd to the bit and exactly ±1 at
  ±1, and the turn runs on the cube point in a fixed order, so a vertex reached from two (or three)
  faces is the same ECEF point to the last bit (tested to level 23).
- Cell sides at level 13: 1.22 km at a face centre, 1.16 km at a corner (120° there), 0.87 km at the
  middle of a face edge (the shortest, 1/√2).

## The tile (`CubeTile`, `CubeTileCodec`, `CubeBundle`, `CubeManifest`)

- The anchor: `CubeKey.Anchor`, the ENU frame at the cell's centre on the ellipsoid (`EnuFrame`:
  ECEF origin and axes in doubles; local axes are the game's, x east, y up, z south).
- **Leaf level 14** (chosen with the user on G1's numbers, `docs/plans/global-tiling.md` "G1
  results"): ~0.6 km tiles. Heights: WGS84 **ellipsoidal**, on the cell's lattice at (2^bits + 1)²
  vertices, row-major from the south-west (i along u, j along v). bits = 23 − level (512 cells at
  level 14, 1024 at 13: ~1.2 m at a face centre either way). NaN = no data.
- Encoding: 1 cm quanta above the tile's own base, planar prediction (W + S − SW), zigzag varints,
  Brotli. Every height within 5 mm whatever the relief; the ushort band (7.55 cm) and a per-tile
  offset+scale over 16 bits (over 1 cm once a tile spans 1,310 m; the local region's worst 1 km
  tile spans 1,304 m) do not hold 1 cm.
- Features in the anchor frame, floats: road centrelines (`CubeRoad`: source, `RoadClass`, width,
  points) and building footprints (`CubeBuilding`: points on the ground, height above the lowest).
- Bundles (`CUBB`, `F0_L9_x_y.cubb`): the tiles under one ancestor (default level − 5: 32 × 32
  tiles, ~20 km at level 14), an index of key/offset/length, then the tiles; `cube_manifest.json` lists every tile with
  its height range, counts and bytes.
- Geometry (`CubeGeometry.Vertices`): ground point and normal rigorous at a 17 × 17 control grid,
  bilinear between, `S + h·U`: within 0.3 mm of the rigorous vertex. Mesh quads split along
  (i, j)–(i+1, j+1). `MeshHeightAt` finds the mesh height below a tangent-plane point;
  `CollisionField` samples a regular height field from it.

## Leaves with content (G2 B1, #1505)

Every tile kind of a level-14 leaf as a **section of one entry** (layout A, chosen by the user on
measurements over the D: region: 1 read and 24 ms median cold a leaf, against 5.3 reads and 92 ms for
a bundle per kind; equal from the OS cache). Code: `CubeLeaf.cs` (container), `CubeLeafSections.cs`
(the section codecs, `CubeLeafContent`); tests `CubeLeafTests`. `--cube` writes their heights since B3 PR 1 (`cube-frames`), the other kinds come with the later B3 stages,
and the game reads none (C1).

- **Frame**: every position is in the leaf's anchor frame (`CubeKey.Anchor`): x east, y ENU up, z
  south, metres from the cell centre on the ellipsoid, floats. **y is not an altitude**: it is
  measured from the ellipsoid (~50 m below sea level in Switzerland, the geoid) along the centre's
  up, so the ellipsoid itself drops ~1.5 cm below y = 0 at a leaf's corner. Heights are ellipsoidal,
  as G1's. Lattice kinds are on the
  leaf's lattice (`CubeKey.LatticeBits`: 513² at level 14, row-major from the south-west, i along u,
  j along v), as the heights.
- **Entry** (`CubeLeafCodec`, `CULF`): head 20 B (magic, version, section count, face, level, x, y),
  a table of 12 B a section (kind u16, the section codec's own version u16 and flags u16, length
  u32), the payloads back to back. A reader skips a kind it does not know and a codec version newer
  than it reads (`CubeLeafContent.Skipped`), so neither bumps the entry version. `Decode` keeps
  every section, unknown ones too; `CubeLeafCodec.Replace` and `CubeBundle.UpdateLeaves` (a stage
  rewrites its own kinds in a bundle) keep every other section bit for bit.
- **Bundles**: `CUBL`, `F0_L9_x_y.cubl`, one per level-9 ancestor, `CubeBundle`'s index layout; the
  coarse pyramid keeps `.cubb` (CUBT entries) and colour `.cuba`.

- **Compression**: Brotli quality 6, window 22 (`LeafPack`): roads kept 72 % at quality 4 (.NET's
  `Optimal`), 56 % at 6 (0.9 s for that km²), 45 % at 11 (24 s); decoding costs the same.
- **The body split**: the road, building, tree, piste and field codecs are a 16-byte head (magic,
  version, flags, `TileId`: `TileHeader.WriteHead`/`ReadHead`) and a key-free body
  (`EncodeBody`/`DecodeBody`), which the leaf sections reuse. The LV95 files are byte-identical
  (pinned SHA-256 from main in `CubeLeafTests`; all 2,103 road, building and tree files of the D:
  region re-encode to their own bytes with the split codecs, as with main's). The record types keep
  their `TileId Id` until C1; inside a leaf it is default.
- **Pistes**: `PisteTile.Slot`/`Aux` index the LV95 1 m grid (row 0 north), so a leaf carries them
  on its lattice and `PisteTile.IndexAt` does not apply there; `AltLow`/`AltHigh` stay altitudes
  above sea level (the snow model's input), not ENU heights.
- **Fields**: outlines, not cells: the game rasterises its 4 m cells from them at load
  (`FieldTile.Build` → `FieldFormat.Rasterise`) and clips the drawing to them (`FieldMeshBuilder`,
  Versteckis' `VsGround` through `FieldTile`), so the leaf form needs no lattice; cells keyed by leaf
  come with the farm store's re-keying (D).
- **Re-encoding**: every section codec re-encodes a decoded section to the same bytes except the
  heights: G1's header keeps the height range before quantising (a decoded tile writes a range a few
  mm off, the same heights).

## Coarse levels: the pyramid (#1430 H4, agreed on #1455)

The globe (H4) and G2's far view are one pyramid in this format, from the leaves up to the faces.

- **Lattice cap**: `CubeKey.LatticeBits(level)` = min(23 − level, 9), the one source of a level's
  lattice (the tiler's `--cube-bits` default too): the level-14 leaves keep 512 cells, every level
  above has 512 (level 0: 19.6 km a cell, level 4: 1.2 km, ETOPO 2022's spacing). A coarse lattice
  point is a leaf lattice point to the bit (`CubePyramidTests`). `--cube-level 13` now defaults to 512
  cells too (G1 measured it with 1024: pass `--cube-bits 10` to repeat that).
- **Control points by distance**: `CubeGeometry.ControlStep(level, bits)` keeps the leaves' 32 (a 39 m
  span) and halves the step until a span is under 100 m, so coarse tiles are rigorous at every vertex
  (the leaves' step would sag 30 m at level 4, 7.7 km at level 0). The resampler takes the same step.
- **A coarse height is a function of its lattice point**, never of its tile: an average of the leaves
  takes the leaf vertices within half a coarse step of the point, the same from either neighbour, or
  the seams break (`--cube-check`'s shared-edge test catches it; run it on coarse levels too).
- **Quantum by level**: `CubeKey.QuantumFor(level)`, a thousandth of the lattice step, 1 cm to 1 m
  (1 cm at the leaves, 1 m from level 4 up), carried in each tile's header (`CubeTile.Quantum`): one
  value a level, so shared edges quantise alike. At 1 cm the global levels 0-4 were 882 MB, at 1 m 359.
- **Manifest version 2**: `levels`, one `CubeManifestLevel` each (bits, bundle level, colour size and
  colour bundle level); a version-1 manifest is its leaf level alone (`LevelsOrLeaf`).
- **Colour** (`CubeAlbedo`, `CubeAlbedoCodec`, entries `CUBC`): 12 monthly images (or one) per tile,
  WebP, row-major from the south-west like the heights, pixel centres a half pixel in. Bundled by their
  own level in `.cuba` files (`CubeBundle.AlbedoMagic` "CUBA", the same index layout as `.cubb`);
  leaves carry none. Encoded and decoded by Godot's `Image`: the tools have no image library.

## The tiler (`--cube`)

```
dotnet run --project tools/TerrainPreprocessor -c Release -- --cube --cube-out D:/.../cube13 \
  --in <swissALTI3D zips> [--rgealti <RGE ALTI dir> | --dem lambert93,<dir>,-0.28] [--cube-level 14] \
  [--cube-box minE,minN,maxE,maxN] [--tlm <swissTLM3D.gpkg>] [--buildings <swissBUILDINGS3D gpkg>] \
  [--cube-bdtopo] [--cube-cache DIR] [--cube-check | --cube-check-only] [--cube-terr <LV95 chunks>]
```

- **Since #1507 (G2 B3) it writes leaves**: one level-9 work frame at a time through the leaf writer,
  each leaf's `Heights` section into the frame's `.cubl` (G1's `.cubb` leaves are still read by the
  check and `--cube-swiss`); the driver, transforms, ground and writer are in `cube-frames`.
- Sources: swissALTI3D parsed once to float32 (`<cache>/alti/E_N.f32`, 16 MB a km², not the
  build's 7 cm ushorts; the 2 m corridor `E_N_2m.f32`, 1 MB, read where 0.5 m has nothing), an LRU
  of 300 in memory; then `ForeignDem` (RGE ALTI etc.) with its dz.
- Heights: the point on each vertex's normal whose LHN95 height (`SwissGeodesy.FromEcef`) equals the
  source height at its own LV95 position (`CubeHeightSource.ExactHeight`, a fixed point). The
  LV95 position, LHN95 height and each DEM's grid position are rigorous at 17 × 17 control points
  (at h 0 and 1,000 m: linear along the normal) and bilinear between; the **outer rows are
  rigorous**, a pure function of the shared point, so neighbours' edges are bit-identical even
  across face edges. French heights (IGN69) + dz are taken as LHN95, as the LV95 build does: the
  CH/FR seam stays continuous; absolute ellipsoidal heights far from Switzerland need a French geoid
  (RAF20) or EGM2008 later (the Swiss geoid grid is held at its edge outside it).
- Tile choice: a 100 m probe lattice under every swissALTI3D km² and every LV95 km² with foreign
  data (`ForeignDem.TilesIn`); `ForeignDem.BoundsLv95Km` retries on a 1 km lattice for samples
  smaller than its 5 km one.
- Features: swissTLM3D roads (`tlm_strassen_strasse`) and footprints (`tlm_bauten_gebaeude_footprint`,
  height from swissBUILDINGS3D's `DACH_MAX`), each point at the DEM height through
  `SwissGeodesy.ToEcef`; BD TOPO (`--cube-bdtopo`, the WFS per bundle) from its own lat/lon
  (`BdFeature.LatLonRings`) on the vertex's normal. Never `SwissProjection`.
- `--cube-check`: shared edges of every built neighbour pair (exact), vertices against the rigorous
  ground point, the resampling against exact, a tangent-plane collision field against the mesh, and
  the features against the mesh below them. `--cube-dump F0/L13/x_y` writes one tile as JSON (its
  surface every 4th vertex, features, and where the LV95 path put the French points) for pictures.
- Cost: ~0.75 CPU-s a level-14 tile for heights (1.4 km² of 1 m data a CPU-s), ~1 s more for
  features (GeoPackage and WFS reads); features are placed in kilometre order (the TLM's R-tree
  order thrashed the 16 MB source tiles); each tile is encoded the moment its heights exist; peak
  ~11 GB with `--cube-cache-tiles 300` (16 MB each).

## The harness (`--cubeharness`, `src/Terrain/CubeHarness.cs`)

`<godot> --path . -- --cubeharness <cube region dir> [--cubespeed 100] [--cuberadius 4000]` (a
window: rendering is measured; muted, no focus, vsync off): the region in one flat Y-up ENU anchor
frame (the tile nearest its middle, or `--cubeat lat,lon`), each tile placed by one rigid transform
composed in doubles (`TileTransform`). First collision on the steepest full tile and a middling
one: a tangent-plane `HeightMapShape3D` (heights divided by the spacing, one uniform scale; an
alignment probe checks rays at its own samples), a trimesh of the whole mesh and a 128-cell trimesh
patch, each against the mesh with vertical rays. Then a flight along the region's long axis with an
LOD step by distance (1, 2, 4, 8 within 0.8, 1.6, 3.2 km and beyond), one commit a frame. Writes
`test_output/cubeharness/<region>.md`. `--cubeshot <png>` takes one still instead (1.6 km up,
3 km south of the anchor, looking north). Godot collides with front faces only: trimesh triangles
are wound clockwise from above, as the render mesh.

- Found with it: the height field misses 2 cm against the mesh (hence trimesh patches, decided);
  a whole level-13 tile committed at once stalls the main thread ~120 ms (level 14: ~30 ms).
