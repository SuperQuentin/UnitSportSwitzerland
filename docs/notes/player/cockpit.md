# Cockpit: first-person driving (#69; trucks and buses #157)

- **View cycle** (`FootPlayer.ToggleView`, V / R3): at the wheel of a car, a truck or a bus it is three states,
  chase → cockpit with your own arms and legs → cockpit without them → chase. Saved as
  `GameSettings.ThirdPerson` + `CockpitBody` (Settings → Cockpit has the same toggle). On foot and
  on every other mount V is still first/third. `FootPlayer.InCockpit` = first person, `_ride is
  Car or Truck`, not the garage's orbit. `--view body|bare` starts a run in either cockpit state.
- **The camera** (`UpdateCockpitCamera`) sits at the driver figure's own eye, `CarRig.EyeFrame` (`HeavyRig.EyeFrame`),
  which is on the rig's pitching body: the dash does not bob in the lens when the nose dips. Half
  the body roll is taken off the horizon. Seat height and fore/aft (±10 / ±15 cm) and the FOV
  (50–100°, default 70, +6° at speed) are settings. **Head motion** (setting, on): the eye sways on
  a soft spring, back under acceleration and forward braking (`Car.AccelX`, 5 mm per m/s²) and
  out of a bend (`AccelY`, 6 mm), max 5–6 cm, rolling a little with it. **Free look**: mouse or
  right stick, which only spring back (with the pitch, to −0.1 rad) 0.6 s after the input stops,
  so a look over the shoulder can be held; looking sideways moves the head 7 cm that way and 5 cm
  forward, past the pillar.
- **What the local rig hides** (`CarRig.View`, set every frame from the player): `Body` hides the
  driver's head (a separate mesh), `Bare` the whole figure; every remote copy stays `Outside` and
  draws all of it. The glass's tint drops to 35% from the seat (`GlassFromSeat`): a windscreen is
  near clear from inside, while from outside the same pane only reads as glass with a tint.
- **HUD**: in the cockpit the speed/gear/rpm readout and the rev bar go (the dials show them)
  unless `CockpitHud` is on; tyre and brake wear, which the dash has no gauge for, stay.
- **Mirrors** (`CabMirrors`, shared by `CarRig` and `HeavyRig`, setting on by default, `--mirrors on|off`): rear-view and both
  door mirrors, each a `SubViewport` 48 px tall (PS1 look, nearest filtering) rendering from the
  **eye's reflection** in the mirror plane, looking square through it (along its normal) with an
  off-axis frustum (`SetFrustum`) framing exactly the glass, so the near plane IS the glass; the
  quad flips U. A camera aimed at the mirror's middle had its near plane square to the line of
  sight, and at a door mirror's slant its own housing showed as a black box in the middle. Only the local driver in the cockpit has them, built
  on first use. One mirror re-renders a frame, in turn (each at a third of the frame rate), with
  a 400 m far plane and without the viewmodel and door-portal layers. **Cost**, measured with
  `--ride car:0,75,x.png --brake-at 0 --view body --time 13 --vsync off --mirrorperf` (mirrors
  switched on and off every 3 s, parked): about **+0.5 ms a frame** (+0.49, +1.07, +0.50, +0.61,
  −0.70 on paired spans at ~100 fps, ~5%) and ~20–25 more draw calls. Frame time rose over the run
  from streaming, so only the pairs compare.
- **What others see**: the driver at the wheel through the glass, hands turning with it. The
  steering wheel's turn is `SteerAngle × CarSpec.SteerRatio` (15:1, ~1.4 turns each way at
  0.6 rad of lock) on every peer, from the pose's X that was already replicated; the throttle rides
  in the pose's W in eighths (bits 3–5, next to the brake/lights/roof flags), the brake foot from the
  brake-light bit. Nothing new is synced. Parked cars (`BuildParkedVisual`) have nobody in them.
- **Checks**: `--cockpitcheck` (headless: every car's seat fits, `car-cabin`),
  `--synccheck` (the car stage compares the mirror copy's wheel turn, pedals and driver on every
  fresh frame: 0.000° off at up to 406°), `tools/switchcheck.sh` (dedicated server + two clients
  on loopback: the watcher must see the other player's driver whole and the wheel at the front
  wheels' angle × ratio, 516° seen; writes `switchcheck_watch_cockpit.png`; `GODOT=<exe>`,
  `EXTRA="--time 13"` for daylight), `--avatars 2 out.png --cockpit [--car N] [--turn deg]
  [--throttle t] [--outside] [--bare] [--lights]` (one car, from the eye or outside).
- **Trucks and buses** (#157, `heavy-cabin`): the same cycle, camera, settings and mirrors (four on a
  truck, two on a bus's stalks); the look rests at −0.24 rad, not −0.1 (`HeavyCockpitPitch`), as you
  sit high over a flat wheel and the dials are well down. The HUD keeps what the dash has no gauge
  for: the retarder stage, hold, clutch and the train's weight. The wheel turns at 20:1
  (`HeavyCockpit.SteerRatio`, ~2.5 turns to full lock); remote copies get it from the replicated
  steer, the throttle from 3 bits in `Truck`'s pose W (shift 16) and the brake from its bit;
  `--heavynet` b logs the remote cab (wheel against steer × ratio, feet, driver) and shoots it.
- **Limits**: the hands follow a car's rim to ±100° (a heavy's to ±46°) and slide past that rather than shuffle; a remote driver's head does not turn with
  their free look (only yaw is replicated); the dials of a remote car read its rpm and wheel speed
  and its lights lamp follows the headlights, but the gear digit and the handbrake and engine lamps
  stay at their defaults (not replicated, only seen up close); a
  roadster's side glass belongs to the body, so it stays put when its door opens.
