# Hidden FTP test (#902)

- **What it does**: there is no test protocol and no screen. Every road-bike ride on measured
  watts is a test once it ends, as in Zwift:
  - **≥ 30 min**: FTP = 95% of the best 20-minute average;
  - **≥ 60 min**: the best 60-minute average too, and the higher of the two wins;
  - shorter rides, or a result under 50 W (a dead sensor), say nothing.
- **It only ever raises.** An easy ride's best 20 minutes are what the rider chose to do, not
  what they could, so they say nothing about the ceiling. Lowering an FTP is the rider's call; it
  can only be done in `user://rider.json` until a settings field exists.
- **Code**:
  - `Trainer/FtpDetector.cs` (pure, unit tested): `PowerLog`, `FtpDetector`, `RiderProfile`.
    `PowerLog` splits physics steps across whole seconds, so a second holds the time-weighted
    average whatever the frame rate, and it clamps spikes to 2500 W.
  - `Trainer/FtpWatch.cs`: `FootPlayer` calls `Sample` after each `Bicycle.Step`, for the local
    human only (`FtpRider`: no NPC, bot, puppet or proxy). `RideEnded` runs when `ApplyRide`
    leaves the bike and in `_ExitTree` (quitting on the bike). It prints an `[ftp]` line every
    ride, saves `user://rider.json` (FTP + the last 20 results), and raises `Said`, which
    `ClientWorld` toasts.
- **Measured watts only**: `Bicycle.PowerMeasured` is false until the trainer link (#901) sets
  it. Keyboard watts are whatever the key gives (180 W held reads 171 W), so they never count,
  unless `--ftp-keys` is given (to try it without a trainer).
- **Try it**: `--ride bike,1860 --world flat --ftp-keys --fixed-fps 60` (31 min of game time).
  Use a scratch `APPDATA`, or it writes your real `rider.json`.
