# Roof overhangs: Cartoon's High building detail (#1067)

> This file was generated by Claude.

- **`MeshDetail.High` only** (Cartoon, and the Realistic styles through the fallback chain):
  `BuildingMeshBuilder.Build(tile, detail)` appends overhang slabs after the solids. Low (PS1)
  builds exactly what it did before (the High branch is skipped whole). **Collision is untouched**:
  `BuildCollisionFaces` reads the surveyed solid, so gameplay does not depend on the style.
- **Which edges** (`RoofOverhangs.Plan`, pure, `RoofOverhangsTests`): the boundary edges of each
  pitched roof plane (`RoofOverhangs.PitchedRoof`, the same plane key the tile courses use) that
  no other roof triangle of the solid shares (ridges, hips and valleys are shared), facing down the
  slope (eave) or across it (verge), never up it (a lean-to's head against a wall). Flat roofs
  (|n.y| > 0.97) get none. A castle, a church tower's spire and an IKEA box keep their outline.
- **How far**: `RoofOverhangs.For(kind)`, in the roof's plane: house 0.7 m eave / 0.4 m verge, barn
  1.1 / 0.6, block 0.5 / 0.3, church, works, shop 0.4 / 0.25, garage and annex 0.35 / 0.25; at
  most half the plane's eave-to-ridge length (sheds).
- **Neighbours** (`RoofCover`: the tile's roof triangles in 4 m cells): an edge whose strip would
  pass within 0.3 m of another roof, or under it (three probes along the strip's middle line), gets
  none: a terraced row's party walls, a lower roof against a taller wing of the same solid. Without
  it a verge lay coplanar on the neighbour's roof and fought it.
- **Corners**: a strip ends where its offset edge crosses the plane's other boundary edge at that
  vertex (that edge's own offset, or the edge carried on when it has none), so an eave and a verge
  meet at one point, both verges of a gable meet at the ridge, and an eave next to a hip stops on
  the hip's line. Nearly parallel neighbours, or a vertex with more than two edges, end square.
- **The slab** (`BuildingMeshBuilder.Overhangs.cs`): the top in the roof's plane with the roof
  frame (CUSTOM0.w = 1), the soffit 18 cm straight below (vertical, not along the normal: offset
  along it, a steep gable's verge soffit poked through the other side's roof), the fascia on the
  outer edge, a closing end where a strip stops short of a neighbour. Faces are wound to face out
  (Godot's front is clockwise), never both ways. Timber soffit on houses, barns, annexes, garages;
  the wall's colour under the others.
- **Tile courses**: each plane's eave in `roofSpans` moves to the strips' lowest point before the
  roof triangles are written, so the shader's gutter runs along the overhang's edge and the courses
  carry across the wall line without a seam.
- Checks: model viewer `Terrain / Roof` (hipped, terraced row, lean-to) and `Terrain / Building`
  in `--style cartoon` and `ps1`; `--zfightcheck` (none of them fights); PS1 town shots are
  pixel-identical before and after (birds aside).
