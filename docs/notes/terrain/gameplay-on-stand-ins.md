# Gameplay on an earth leaf's stand-in tiles (#1704)

Under `--frame earth` a leaf's content is served as a stand-in tile (`LeafStandIn`, `terrain/earth-world`): its records
(roads, buildings, site plans, parked slots, yards) are named by the stand-in's `TileId` and lie in its own frame, the
leaf's anchor shifted 500 m. Everything keyed by a tile name and placed by E/N (sites, yards, cranes, pallets, the
dormant fleets) runs on it as on an LV95 tile, by these rules.

- **Places.** `GlobalPos.OfTile(tile, x, y, z)` / `OfTileEn(tile, e, n, h)` place a tile record by its tile's frame:
  LV95 with the height above sea level on an LV95 tile, the leaf's anchor frame on a stand-in. `InTile` / `InTileEn` are
  the inverses: compare a body with a record in the record's own tile frame (`LootService.EdgeDistance`, `YardIndex`).
  `GlobalPos.FromLv95` and `OriginFrame.ToWorld(e, n, h)` recognise a stand-in's E/N (past
  `LeafStandIn.FirstPlaceE`, 100,000 km E: never an LV95 place) and place it so, so flat-world code that places records
  by E/N lands right without a change. D removes both.
- **Headings and directions** (#1799) are the tile's too: a stand-in is turned in the world by its leaf's frame, and
  far from Switzerland (the world's axes carried along a long flight, `FrameAxes.Tangent`) by a lot, so a tile-frame
  direction is never a world one. A direction goes through `TileTransform(tile).Basis` (`DoorLink.Create`'s outward and
  up, `DoorIndex.SetTile`); a record's yaw through `WorldOrigin.WorldYawAt(e, n, yaw)` (a parked slot, a yard pallet),
  and into a `VehicleState` (grid axes) through `RecordYawAt`. LV95 tiles keep the yaw as it is. Near Geneva the turn
  is ~1°, so only a far place shows it: `--doorframecheck` (`door-portals`).
- **The tile under a body** is the ground's name for it: `ground.ContentName(ground.TileAt(world))`, never
  `origin.TileAt` (always an LV95 tile) or `GlobalPos.Tile`. A stand-in's `Neighbour(dx, dz)` is the next leaf's on the
  same cube face; leaves are ~610 m, so rings cover less ground than LV95 ones.
- **Files** come from `IWorldGround.Content` (`ClientWorld.TileFiles`, `ServerWorld`'s `content`), never the flat
  streamer's source: under earth the server's flat world holds no tile. The Battle Royale reads the leaves over its
  square itself (`BrLoot.LeafTiles`, `RoadNetwork.LoadLeavesAsync`).
- **The stand-in grid** (`LeafStand.Grid`) is rasterised from the lattice blended to the roads, the ground as walked
  on: what is planned on it (buildings, doors, the sites' offices and yards, parked vehicles) stands on the collision.
  From the bare lattice, a site office's sill was 1.3 m over its doorstep beside a road.
- **Building sites are graded** (#1731, `SitePads`, `LeafStand.Pads`): a fill leaf follows the real relief (#1678), so
  each site's area is levelled to the mean of the ground in it, with an 8 m skirt, after the road blend (a site keeps
  off the carriageway; graded before it, a raised road blended back into the pad as a step). The collision, the grid
  and the drawn mesh all carry it. No apron past the site's area: it reached the roads beside it.
- **A site's dressing rolls from its building's Earth identity**, so a leaf's sites carry other materials than the
  flat world's same village: a check finds a site that has what it needs (`SiteTiles.CraneWithPalletNear` walks
  `ProceduralWorld.SitesNear`), never names one.
- **Collision.** A leaf patch's buildings (shells, footings, office walls, garage links, stoops) are a child body
  `Buildings` of the patch's ground body (`LeafCollision.Buildings`, `EarthWorld.AddBody`); an open doorway lets a
  walker or a car through the bodies of the patches round it (`EarthWorld.BuildingBodiesNear`,
  `InteriorManager.BuildingBodies`). The sites' slabs, stairs and walkways (`LeafStand.SiteFaces`) stay with the
  ground: a raised door's deck is underfoot.
- **Interiors** of a leaf's building go down to -3000 m above sea level along the leaf's own up
  (`InteriorManager.PlacementFor`): the same place on the Earth for every peer. A world Y is a height over a frame's own
  tangent plane, and two frames 2 km apart put "world Y -3000" ~1 m apart (the server saw a lift rider out of the cabin).
- **Seeds.** A stand-in building's seeds hash from its Earth identity (`Fnv.Alias`, #1658); `Fnv.Resolve` (set by
  `EarthWorld`) registers a leaf's aliases the first time a seed asks for one of its keys, so a seed is the same
  whenever it is asked (a late joiner seeded a cinema from the server's table before it had the leaf).
- **Heights.** A body's world Y is no altitude in an earth frame: `GlobalPos.Alt` for an altitude, and a "ground"
  world height is set on a placed point, never passed to `ToWorld` as one (`BrCrates.WorldOf`).
