# Industrial sites: warehouses, works, depots, body shops, dealerships (#497)

Phase 1 of #496. Before this, every `BuildingKind.Industrial` solid — a 4,000 m² distribution shed
and a one-man workshop alike — got a single `RoomType.Workshop` with a workbench, two racks and
three crates.

- **Which industry** (`BuildingTypes.SiteFor`, `BuildingType.Warehouse/Factory/Depot/Mechanic/Dealership`):
  the cadastre only says "industrial", so the industry is **invented**, the way `IsBank` and
  `ShopTables.TypeFor` invent a bank and a shop — a pure function of key, kind, footprint and wall
  height, agreed by server and every client with nothing sent. Geometry decides what it *can* be,
  `Fnv.Unit(key + "|site")` decides between what is left:
  | Building | Becomes |
  |---|---|
  | tall and slender (long side ≤ 14 m, wall ≥ max(10, 1.6·long)) | nothing — a silo or a tank has no floor to walk |
  | `Industrial` under 60 m² | nothing — the old single `Workshop` |
  | `Industrial`, wall ≥ 9 m | `Factory` always: that height is a crane or a press, not racking |
  | `Industrial` ≥ 600 m², low | `Warehouse` 55% / `Factory` |
  | `Industrial` ≥ 200 m², low | `Warehouse` 40% / `Factory` 35% / `Depot` |
  | `Industrial` 60–200 m² | `Depot` 50% / `Mechanic` |
  | `Garage` ≥ 200 m² | `Dealership` 35% / `Depot` 30% / `Mechanic` |
  | `Garage` 120–200 m² | `Mechanic` 75% / `Depot` |
  | `Garage` under 120 m² | nothing — somebody's garage (the generated world's are 3.4 x 6.2 m) |
  `BuildingTypes.Detect` is **untouched**: a site needs no `BuildingGroup`, so churches cannot
  regress. Grouping a hall with its office annex and silos is phase 2, with the multi-door work.
- **The plan** (`InteriorGenerator.Industrial.cs`, `TryIndustrial`, taken before the
  single-room/cored paths — as a branch, not an early return, so `Generate` keeps one exit and one
  `Furnish` call and anything that must run over every finished plan cannot miss a site): one **hall** the full height of the building — an industrial solid always
  plans as one tall storey — plus a low **service block** across the back wall where the hall keeps
  7 m of its own depth. The block's rooms are real rooms with a 2.6 m ceiling standing inside a 9 m
  hall: `RoomPlan.Clear` overrides the `Span × StoreyHeight` headroom, and `InteriorLayout.ClearOf`
  is the one place that reads it. Each has a doorway into the hall (`Other = 0`, so the validator's
  reachability walk from room 0 reaches them all).
  Programs: warehouse/depot `Dispatch, PartsStore, BreakRoom, WC`; works `ControlRoom, LockerRoom,
  BreakRoom, WC` (+ a `PaintBooth` 35% of the time); body shop `Office, PartsStore, WC`; dealership
  `Office, BreakRoom, WC`.
- **The hall lays itself out** (`LaysItselfOut`, `HallLayout`), because a warehouse is *aisles* and a
  works is *lines*, and both read as broken if the generic wall-hugging placer scatters them:
  - `Aisles` — pairs of pallet-racking runs back to back, 2.9 m aisles between, bays at 2.7 m, plus a
    painted aisle line and the odd pallet or drum stack left out in the lane.
  - `Line` — one to three conveyor runs down the long axis, machines alternating either side, a
    gantry beam overhead, raw stock and finished pallets at the ends, a yellow walkway beside.
  - `Bays` — deep bays off the entry wall, each marked out, with a drive-on lift and a tool chest,
    and one vehicle **up on its lift** (a lorry in a depot, a car in a body shop).
  - `ShowroomFloor` — cars on low plinths in a grid.
  Then the generic placer adds `HallDressing`: the safety kit (signs, extinguisher, hard-hat rack),
  benches, racks, tyres, drums.
- **A site hall is not a stair core.** `Furnish` treats room 0 of a multi-room floor as the stairwell
  and keeps a 1.4 m strip inside the door clear; on a site hall that blocked the whole front bay, so
  `isCore` now also requires `!LaysItselfOut`.
- **Continuous runs need `Free(..., gap: 0)`.** Racking bays touch end to end and stand back to back
  — that *is* pallet racking — and a 0.2 m gap made the placer reject every second bay and the whole
  second run (155 bays became 50). Conveyor sections lie end to end for the same reason.
- **Pieces that are stood on or hung** (`InteriorValidator`): `ShowroomPlinth`, `CarLift` and
  `FloorMarking` join `Rug` and `Dais` in the list exempt from the furniture-overlap rule, which is
  what lets a car stand on a plinth or a lorry on a ramp. `InteriorGenerator.Put` keeps
  `FloorMarking` and `Gantry` out of `placed` for the same reason: paint and a roof beam take no
  floor space.
- **Cost.** `PieceBudget` = area / 9, capped at `MaxHallPieces` = 110. A racking bay is the most
  expensive piece in the game (~1,200 vertices), and a 120 x 120 m clamped warehouse would otherwise
  plan 1,500 of them. Measured interiors: warehouse 108 bays / 125k vertices, works 99 pieces / 33k,
  depot 15.9k, body shop 9k, dealership 13.8k. Thinning racking by distance from the door is the
  lever if that ever bites.
- **High-bay lighting** (`RoomLights`): a room over ~11 m a side gets a grid of lamps (up to 6 x 6 at
  11 m centres) instead of one lamp with a 30 m reach, which left a shed's corners black.
- **Loot**: 14 new containers (`PalletRack`, `Pallet`, `BarrelStack`, `SackStack`, `OilDrum`,
  `JerryCan`, `TyreStack`, `ToolChest`, `Machine`, `Compressor`, `Locker`, `TruckProp`, `DeskCounter`)
  and room overrides for the new `(piece, room)` pairs. **`Abundance` is flat per container for a
  site** (`SiteAbundance`: warehouse 0.22, works 0.18, depot 0.20, body shop 0.26, dealership 0.12)
  rather than one building's budget over however many bays its hall fits — a hall with 108 racking
  bays would otherwise have stocked 8% of them. Houses are untouched.
- **The PAUSA machine keeps its dice.** A `BreakRoom` is now a `VendingRoom`, so a site's mess room
  is where a works' machine stands — but it is still the per-building roll of #273
  (`VendingChance`, 15% for `Industrial`), not one in every mess room. Hard-coding it in the mess
  room's own pieces put one in 100% of works and failed `--shopcheck`. `Garage` joins the table at
  15%, which only reaches a trade garage big enough to be a site with a mess room.
- **Plan version 13**: stored interiors regenerate. #498 (door identity) holds 14 on its own branch;
  there is only one `CurrentVersion`, so whichever of the two rebases onto the other takes the
  **next** number, never a lower one.
- **Not in phase 1** (#496): loading-bay doors as real portals (needs a door id per door, not per
  building), the exterior — yards, docks, parked fleets, facade dressing — the drivable forklift,
  grouping a hall with its office annex, and an industrial hum in `BuildingSounds`.

## Meeting #498 (door identity, PR #500)

- **Plan version**: there is one `CurrentVersion`. #497 holds 13, #498 holds 14 on its own branch;
  whichever rebases onto the other takes the **next** number, never a lower one.
- **Doorway heights**: #498 sizes an extra door's opening from `StoreyHeight − Slab`, the storey's
  headroom. A service-block room has a ceiling of its own, so a side door landing on its outer wall
  could be cut taller than the room. `InteriorValidator` now rejects **any opening taller than its
  room** (`o.Top > l.ClearOf(r)`), so the merge cannot land that quietly — it fails `--sitecheck`.
  The fix is one clamp to `l.ClearOf(room)` on whichever side merges second.
- **Bay doors are not in #497.** #498 made the leaf a property of the door (`DoorHang`, `Vehicle`),
  so a bay is `Hang = RollUp, Vehicle = true` on an Industrial door and nothing gates on the kind.
  What is left for this epic's phase 2 is the *placement*: `DoorBudget.AlongRun` spaces doors 22 m
  apart, and bays want a per-site-type run at 4.5 m centres.
- **One exit**: `InteriorGenerator.Generate` branches rather than returning early for a site, so
  #498's per-plan door-cutting pass (called once before `Furnish`) covers industrial sites too.
