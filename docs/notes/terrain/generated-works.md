# A works in the generated villages, and the first drive through a loading bay (#531)

#497, #516 and #528 built industrial sites, their yards and their loading bays, and **nothing had
ever driven into one**. This put a works in the generated world so a check could, and then drove
one — which found three bugs that every existing check was blind to.

## The premise was half wrong, and worth recording

The issue said "no industrial building exists, so nothing can drive into one". The **real tiles
have 62 industrial buildings** around the default spawn, carrying 21 works, 7 haulier depots,
4 body shops, 3 dealerships and a warehouse (`--lootstats 2`, `0 of 788 plans fail validation` —
the first run of the epic against real data rather than synthetic boxes). What was missing was
narrower: the **generated world and the fixtures** had none, so no check that runs without the
tile download could reach a site. A worktree runs on the generated fill unless pointed at the
tiles, which is how the wrong conclusion was reached — see `general/worktrees-have-no-terrain-tiles`.

## The works

`ProceduralWorld.Settlements` gives a village longer than `WorksMinVillage` (200 m) one works,
`WorksBeyondEnd` (70 m) past its last house along the valley road and `WorksSetback` (52 m) off
the centre line, its long wall to the road. That wall is the one `BuildingFootprint` puts the door
on, so it is also the wall the bays (#528) and the yard (#516) go on. Two sizes by a hash of where
it stands — not the village rng, which would move every house — so `BuildingTypes.SiteFor` makes
some of them warehouses or works and the smaller ones depots.

**It is yielded last in `PlansNear`, after the garages.** A building's index in its tile is its
name — the interior's plan key, its loot records, a check's hard-coded `2585_1114_52` — so a kind
added later must come last or it renames everything after it. Garages learnt this in #139.

## Three bugs the drive found

None of them was visible to `--sitecheck`, `--doorcheck` or any unit test, because all of those
look at plans and none of them drives.

1. **Furniture stood in every loading bay.** `Furnish` kept a lane clear only for
   `BuildingFootprint.VehicleDoor(kind)` — Garage and Agricultural. A works is neither, so its
   bays had car lifts and racking in them and the car was shoved back into the yard. The lane is
   now chosen by the **opening**, not the building's kind: any ground-floor entry at least
   `VehicleEntryWidth` (2.5 m) wide gets one, which separates a 1.0–1.8 m pedestrian entrance from
   a 2.8 m-and-up vehicle one with room to spare.
2. **A vehicle door arrived at a 1.8 m doorway.** `InteriorGenerator.Entrances` clamped every
   extra door's doorway to `Math.Min(d.Width, 1.8f)` — right for the pedestrian side doors #498
   added, wrong the moment an extra door is a 4.5 m bay. The car crossed the portal and wedged
   3 cm in. A door with `DoorSpot.Vehicle` now keeps its full width. **Plan version 16.**
3. **The drive aimed at the office door.** `DoorIndex.NearestOfKind` prefers slot 0, which is right
   when the main door is the vehicle door (a garage, a barn) and wrong on a works, where slot 0 is
   the office door and the bays are the extras. It takes `vehicleOnly` now, and with it every
   candidate is already a door to drive through so the nearest wins rather than the main one.

**A workshop's ramp belongs IN the lane.** The first fix for (1) also threw out the body shop's
car lifts, which stand in front of its doors because driving onto one is the point. `HallLayout`
takes the lanes as a separate list: `Bays` lays its drive-on ramps, its floor markings and the
vehicle on one, and only then closes the lanes against everything else; the other layouts close
them first.

## Check

**`tools/baycheck.sh`** (tier 2, net): a dedicated server on the **generated world** — so it needs
no terrain download and runs on a fresh clone. Client A finds the nearest Industrial door a vehicle
fits through, drives in through its portal, parks, gets out, gets back in and reverses out; client
B watches from the street and must see **a door whose slot is not 0** standing open and the driver
**inside the works**, neither of which it could know from its own guess. That is the remote-peer
rule in the root `CLAUDE.md`, and it is the first time a vehicle has gone through a loading bay.

Measured: A drives 15.5 m in at 21 km/h, parks inside, reverses out; B sees
`door 2583_1113_259_1 open True swing 1.00 leaf on the facade` and the driver inside.
