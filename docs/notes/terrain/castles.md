# Castles (#859)

> This file was generated by Claude.

- **No dataset says "castle".** swissBUILDINGS3D draws Château de Chillon as **one solid** (2316
  triangles, 72 x 105 m, 44 m) and the GWR files it as GKLAS 1274 ("other minor structure"), so it
  was dressed as an `Annex`: taupe walls, no detail. Many famous castles have no GWR name at all
  (Lenzburg, Castelgrande, Thun).
- **Detection is offline, matching is runtime.** `TerrainPreprocessor --castles src/Interiors/Castles.List.cs
  --out <chunks> --gwr <data.sqlite> --osm <switzerland .osm.pbf>` (~40 s) resolves the candidates
  against the built `.bldg` tiles and writes a committed C# list: per castle its name and one LV95
  point inside each of its solids. The game (`Castles.Match`) marks a solid as a castle when its
  real footprint (`Building.Covers`: roof/floor triangles from above, not the box) holds a point:
  exact, unique, and a pure function of the tile, like `ikea`. No tile rebuild, nothing sent.
- **Candidates**: OSM `historic=castle` closed ways (every solid whose centre is inside) and nodes;
  then GWR names whose first word is Schloss/Burg/Burgruine/Ruine/Château/Castello/Castel/Festung/Fort,
  not matching the exclusion regex (addresses with digits, Schlosserei, -strasse, hotel, Château d'Eau,
  outbuildings...), built before 1850 or of unknown year, at least 120 m², a home or farm only if
  before 1600. A point takes the solid covering it (or the nearest old one within 25 m: a courtyard),
  then the old solids touching it within 110 m. OSM goes first and its names win.
- **Numbers (Oct 2026, national tiles):** 897 OSM + 86 GWR candidates -> 708 castles, 1226 solids;
  235 candidates had no solid (ruins without walls, tiles not built). Most solids: Festung Aarburg 43,
  Schloss Lenzburg 32.
- **Regenerate** after a building rebuild (solid indices do not matter, points do: a re-seated or
  re-cut solid still covers its point unless its footprint changed).
- **Kind** (`CastleKind`, in the list): OSM `ruins=yes`, a `castle_type` or a name with
  ruine/rovine/diroccato -> Ruin; `castle_type` stately/manor/palace/schloss -> Manor;
  defensive/fortress/castrum/burg -> Fortress; else the solids' median year (from 1650 a manor),
  else a fortress. Swiss mappers tag nearly every "Schloss" `stately` (Thun's keep too) and the GWR
  year is often a rebuild's (Tarasp 1628), so the game dresses a **keep-like** solid of a manor (16 m+
  and taller than 1.1 x its plan's short side) as a fortress anyway. Oct 2026: 142 fortresses, 371
  manors, 195 ruins.
- **Region** (`CastleRegion`, from the canton the stage writes: the canton most GWR buildings of the
  castle's km cell are in). Researched Oct 2026, sources below:
  - **Savoy** (VD, GE, FR, and across the border): the Savoyard castles, the "carré savoyard" with
    round corner towers (Yverdon 1258-65, Grandson, Morges, Champvent), grey-beige limestone rubble,
    brown tiles. Chillon is the reference (user photos).
  - **Jura** (NE, JU): the yellow Hauterive limestone of the Jura lakes (Neuchâtel's château).
  - **Plateau** (BE, SO, AG, LU, ZH, ZG, BS, BL): whitewashed render with dressed grey-green molasse
    sandstone at the corners and windows (Bern's Ostermundigen/Gurten stone), red tiles; Thun's white
    towers, Lenzburg, the Bernese bailiffs' castles (Aarberg 1608-10, Trachselwald 1624-30).
  - **East** (TG, SG, SH, AR, AI, GL): rendered, a half-timbered top storey, red tiles.
  - **Alpine** (VS and central Switzerland): dark gneiss rubble under stone-slab roofs (lauzes:
    silvery-grey, bluish or greenish cleft stone; Valère, Tourbillon).
  - **Raetia** (GR): whitewashed rubble (Tarasp) under grey slabs.
  - **Ticino**: grey granite rubble, gneiss slab roofs (piode), swallowtail (Ghibelline) merlons
    (Bellinzona; the merlon form is reported, not verified against the UNESCO dossier).
- **Dress** (`BuildingMeshBuilder.Castle.cs`, `DressOf`, only solids of no church group): the
  region's stone and roof palettes (two or three each, picked by castle name; Chillon draws Savoyard
  stone 1 and roof 0, its limestone and brown-grey tiles), weathered at 40% of `ApplyAge`, then the
  kind: a ruin's stone 14% darker; a manor's render (cream, white, light ochre, pale grey; the Jura's
  in its stone), a slate mansard on a quarter of Savoyard, Jura and plateau manors. No solar panels.
- **Walls** carry UV2.y = 4 + level head + 2 x foot on the ground (within 1.5 m of the solid's base)
  + 4 x the variant (4 to 23), v in metres above their **wall run's** foot, UV2.x the run's height,
  CUSTOM0.z its length (`WallRun.Bottom/Top/Level/Length`), and the castle's shutter paint in the
  wall colour's alpha. Shader `castle_wall` variants:
  - 0 rubble (Savoy, Jura, Alpine, Ticino fortresses): uneven rubble courses, patches, rain streaks,
    up to two rows of two-light Gothic windows and small square ones, slits below, an **arched corbel
    table** under a level head.
  - 1 rendered (Plateau, Raetia): lime render, long-and-short sandstone quoins, a dressed plinth,
    cross-mullioned (Kreuzstock) windows, slits.
  - 2 manor: render, quoins, a cornice, storeys of 3.5 m with sash windows 2.9 m apart symmetric about
    the wall's middle (none by the quoins, none in a gable's top 30%), painted shutters (green,
    grey-blue, ox-blood: one paint a castle).
  - 3 ruin: rubble with moss patches, darker toward the head, empty openings, never lit.
  - 4 timbered (East): render under a half-timbered top storey (posts 1.3 m apart, sill and head
    rails, St Andrew's crosses, small windows).
  - 5 brick (`Castles.Brick`, by name: OSM records almost no castle material, six in Switzerland and
    none brick): Vufflens, c. 1415, brick and Piedmontese, "unique to this region". Tuned on a photo
    of it in the sun (user, Oct 2026): a warm tan-orange brick, not red; stretcher bond 0.30 x 0.085 m
    with lime joints, some bricks burnt dark; cross windows framed in pale stone; a deep machicolated
    corbel table (1.3 m, against 0.8 m on rubble) under every level head; orange-brown tiles. Every
    solid of the castle, palace and keep alike. Its slim turrets' slate spires are vertical
    cylinders in the data, so they are drawn as brick.
  All get a damp foot (green on stone, grey on render) 2.6-3.8 m up: the run starts under the ground
  or the water. The per-wall hash seed is rounded (the interpolated tangent jitters into speckle).
- **Ruins** (`BuildingMeshBuilder.Ruins.cs`): a ruin's solid is a closed box with a lid in the data,
  so it is not drawn; each of its wall runs (1 m+ long, 1.5 m+ tall) becomes a slab a castle wall
  thick (22% of the plan's short side, 0.6-1.4 m) in 0.8 m columns, broken at a height that a smooth
  noise along the wall and a column jitter set (up to 85% gone, half as much at the ends: the corners
  stand), each column with its outer and inner face dressed as the ruin variant (the openings break
  off with it), a moss-green cap, bare darker steps; the run ends 2 cm in so a corner hides them. Open
  to the sky: the ground shows inside. It **collides** as it is drawn (`BuildCollisionFaces`, both
  windings), so nobody stands on the missing lid; server and clients build the same. Nor is it an
  occluder (`BuildingOcclusion`, like a building site's open shell). A ruin with no
  wall run to rebuild keeps its solid and its broken merlons.
- **Fallen stones** (`FallenStones`): under a wall standing on the ground, each column drops one block
  per 2.5 m it lost (a chance for the rest, 4 at most), seven in ten outward, the rest into the
  courtyard, rolled up to 2.5 m (further the higher the fall), 0.25-0.8 m, turned and tipped at
  random, a third of them mossy. The ground is the solid's foot + 0.8 m (`FoundationDepth`, the
  preprocessor's: the median ground of the footprint), and they sink 0.15 m more, so on a slope some
  bury and some sit proud. Part of `RuinWalls`, so they collide as drawn: a player climbs the rubble.
- **Footings** (every building, `BuildingMeshBuilder.Footings.cs`): a solid is seated on the median
  ground of its footprint, so on a slope its downhill base stands in the air. At runtime, from the
  tile's terrain (`ChunkGrid.SampleMeshHeight`, the drawn surface), each wall's bottom edge in pieces
  of 4 m gets a footing down to the ground and 0.5 m into it (past the road blend's 0.35 m), in the
  wall's colour darkened (UV2.y -1: plain, plaster in the realistic style), colliding like the wall.
  A building whose base is anywhere more than 3 m up gets none (in the air: left as it is). Local
  world, Oct 2026: of 308,759 buildings 57% sit in the ground, 28% stand up to 1 m above it, 14%
  1-3 m (all now footed), 1% 3-10 m and 65 over 10 m (left). The 3-10 m ones are mostly real
  buildings on steep slopes with a tall downhill facade (a block of flats on a cliff above Riddes).
- **Geometry** (appended after the doors): a 0.9 m parapet on every open edge of a flat roof (an
  edge no other flat face shares, with nothing of the solid as high 0.4 m past it; each corner owned
  by one edge) with square merlons, Ticino's notched swallowtails, a ruin's broken ones (35% gone, the
  rest worn), a manor's none; **iron finials** on roof peaks (a vertex topping 2+ sloping faces with no
  roof vertex as high within 6 m), not on ruins; **wooden oriels** (Chillon's latrines) on Savoyard,
  Jura and Alpine rubble fortresses' long (14 m+), tall (12 m+) rectangular walls on the ground; the
  Swiss flag on each castle's tallest solid (12 m+) and its canton's, simplified to colour fields on
  a 6 x 6 grid (`FlagCell`), on the next; none on ruins. Visual only: no collision.
