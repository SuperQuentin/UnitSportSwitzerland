# Building sites: the planner (#606, part of #605)

Every `BuildingKind.UnderConstruction` building (swissBUILDINGS3D `Im Bau`) becomes a building site.
This is the planner: what phase the site is at and where everything on it stands. Nothing is drawn
yet; the shell (#608), the dressing (#609), the cranes (#610) and the machines (#616) read it.

## The data

Measured over the 626 local real tiles: **569 sites** (0.6 % of buildings), mostly around Geneva
and Lausanne. 550 have an EGID and 524 a GWR floor count. Median height 9.1 m, plan box quartiles
260 / 530 / 1,025 m², the biggest 100-145 m long. Most are flat prisms of about 94 triangles.

The `Im Bau` solid is the volume **as surveyed**, so it records how far the site had got: a 1.3 m
solid whose GWR record says 6 floors is a base slab. That is where the phase comes from.

## The phase (`ConstructionSites.Progress`)

`h` is the plan box's eave above the solid's base, measured against GWR's floors:

| `h` | Phase | Storeys |
|---|---|---|
| under `FoundationsBelow` (2.6 m) | `Foundations` | 0 built |
| up to floors x 3 m − 1.5 m | `Shell` | `h / 3 m` built (rounding up from 85 %), the GWR count to come |
| within 1.5 m of floors x 3 m, or more | `ToppedOut` | as many as GWR says, or more where each would be over 4.5 m: one GWR floor 20.8 m high is 5 storeys of 4.16 m, a hall's storeys, not one |

With no floor count (45 sites), the target is hashed from the footprint (2-3, 3-5, 4-7 storeys) and
35 % of those are topped out. The issue said `Foundations` below 1.5 m; 2.6 m is "the first storey's
walls are not up", which is what the phase means.

Real mix: **74 foundations, 262 shells, 233 topped out.**

## The layout (`ConstructionSites.Plan`)

- **The front** is the plan box side facing `BuildingFootprint.StreetNear` (the street a front door
  would face), so the gate and the yard are on the street.
- **The site is a rectangle round the plan box**, aligned with it: 11-20 m deep at the front for the
  yard (8-10 m on a house plot), 3.5-8.5 m at the sides, 3-4 m behind. The cadastre's plots are not
  loaded, so every hoarding panel (3.5 m, a Bauzaun panel) on a road, a footpath or in another
  building is **dropped, never nudged**, as `SiteYards` drops yard slots. The notch of an L-shaped
  building is inside the rectangle, so it is yard. The shell should follow the real shape through
  `PlanOutline.Wings` (#577); the planner does not derive a second outline.
- **Kept clear**: the plan box plus `Scaffold` (1.5 m), and the lane from the gate to the building.
- **Order**: cranes, office containers (by the gate), toilets, soil heap (foundations: big; shell:
  small; topped out: none), materials (by the first crane's base), skips, then the machines. A
  zone that does not fit turns 90°, then shrinks to 70 %; a machine that does not fit turns 90°,
  then is **dropped, keeping its ordinal** (`MachineSlot.Ordinal` is its place in `Roles`), because
  the dormant provider names the vehicle by it.
- **Cranes**: none on 60 % of topped-out sites (taken down), and 40 % of house plots at their
  foundations. A house plot (under 350 m², at most 3 storeys) gets one self-erecting crane, anything
  else 1-3 tower cranes by the long side (55 m, 100 m). Each takes **its share of the long side**,
  first outside the scaffolding on the street side, then the other side; where no 4.6 m base fits
  there, or a jib from there could not reach the far corners of its share, it stands **inside**
  (`CraneSpot.Inside`) and #608 leaves an opening in the slabs round the mast. Jib = reach to its
  share's corners + 4 m, within 25-75 m. Hook = target height + 8-12 m.
- **Machines by phase** (`Roles`): foundations excavator, loader, tipper, a second tipper (50 %),
  van; shell mixer, telehandler, mini dumper, van, excavator (30 %); topped out telehandler, roller
  (40 %), vans. House plots: mini excavator, mini dumper, a van. Excavators at the dig face the
  building, everything else faces the gate.

## Three things the plan views caught that the unit tests did not

A throwaway scan over the 569 real sites drew plan views (roads, neighbours, hoarding, zones, crane
circles, machines). Each of these passed every unit test:

1. **Zones were turned 90°.** `Place(width, depth, axis)` runs `depth` along `axis`; passing the
   wall's direction instead of the front put the office's long side into the yard.
2. **Cranes bunched on the short side.** Spreading them along the street wall, which for a deep
   building is its short side, put three cranes on 40 m and maxed out every jib (75 m). They spread
   along the long side now.
3. **No room for a base** on a long thin block between neighbours, and no reach from outside on a
   73 x 89 m block: the inside fallback.

## Cost

`Placer` lays a grid over the site (0.5 m on a house plot, 1 m, 1.5 m past 6,000 m²), sorts it by
score once per item and takes the first point that fits, so a site costs a few obstacle tests per
item. `SiteObstacles` gathers the other buildings and road segments within the site's reach once
per site. Measured: all 569 real sites in 27.7 s, **49 ms a site on average, 470 ms worst** (a
145 x 29 m block). A first version that tested every grid point against every road of the tile did
not finish in 10 minutes. It runs off the main thread with the tile; if a busy tile ever shows,
the grid sort is the lever.

## Checks

`ConstructionSiteTests` (tier 0, 21 tests): determinism, the phase on the measured cases, the site
facing its street, the hoarding closed but for the gate, nothing on a road, in a neighbour, the
scaffolding or the way in, cranes reaching every corner and clearing the finished building, the
inside fallback, house plots, machines by phase and their headings, ordinals, and the role numbers.
The Godot half (`SitePlans.For`) only adds `StreetNear` and the tile's boxes; #607 adds
`--constructioncheck` on hand-made solids.
