# How big a door is, outside and inside (#509)

A door is **one hole**. The footprint settles its size against the wall it stands on, and
everything else — the baked facade door, the interior opening, the portal, the leaf — takes that
number rather than deciding again.

Before #509 two things decided separately, and on some buildings they disagreed:

- A main door's height came from `BuildingKind` alone. Everything the kind table did not name got
  2.8 m, which includes `Annex` (sheds) and `Industrial`. A 4x3 m shed 2.6 m tall was handed a
  2.8 m door: `BuildingMeshBuilder.AppendDoor` pushed it through the roof, and the interior
  opening planned from `DoorSpot.Height` was taller than the storey. Only a barn's door was kept
  under the eave (#135).
- The interior entry openings were their own constants and never looked at the facade door. Only
  `VehicleDoor` kinds took `DoorSpot.Height` through. So a shed wore a 2.8 m door outside and a
  2.1 m one inside.

## The rules now

- **`BuildingFootprint.FitUnderEave(want, eave, barn)`** is the one place a door's height is
  settled: the kind's own height, capped at `eave - DoorUnderEave` (0.35 m, the lintel's wall),
  floored at `MinDoorHeight` (2.0 m; `MinBarnDoorHeight`, 2.5 m, for a pair). `box.Eave` is the
  wall plate on a long side and where the slopes start on a gable end, so the door's top corners
  stay on wall. The silo fallback (no flat run is door-wide) goes through it too.
- **A wall too low for the door its kind wants is demoted**, not squashed: `LowWallPenalty` (1.5)
  off its score, so the uphill end of a hillside house loses to the end that has wall to spare.
  It can still take the door if nothing better does — a building always gets a way in.
- **An extra door (#498) is a fixed pedestrian size**, so a wall with no room for one simply does
  not get one, rather than being cut down.
- **The plan takes the facade door's height.** `InteriorLayout.DoorHeight` carries it for
  single-door plans and `EntrancePlan.DoorHeight` for the rest, so `DoorLink` no longer falls back
  to the kind constant. `InteriorGenerator.SingleRoom`'s entry opening is
  `min(doorHeight, clear - 0.15)` for **every** kind, not just the vehicle ones.

Two interior openings are deliberately left on their own constants, because their gap is a hand's
width and reads as one hole: a cored plan's lobby (2.2 m) and a church's (2.5 m).

## Checking it

`--doorcheck` (`src/Interiors/DoorCheck.cs`) asserts both halves per building: the main door is no
taller than the wall it stands on, and its head is under the eave wherever the wall can take a
door at all.

The size match is asserted in two directions, because they fail for different reasons:

- **Never a taller hole behind a shorter door** (`inside <= d.Height + SameHole`, 0.15 m). This is
  the half a player sees, and it is what caught #497's loading bay — a 3.2 m opening standing
  behind a 2.8 m facade door, and 3.0 m behind the low works' 2.25 m.
- **And not needlessly shorter**: the doorway is the door's own height unless the room it opens
  into has a lower ceiling than the storey. The want is `min(d.Height, ClearOf(room) - 0.15)`, not
  the storey's clear height — #498-with-#497 cut extra doors under `ClearOf(room)` precisely
  because a works hall's 2.6 m service block stands inside a 9 m hall. `DoorCheck.RoomClearAt`
  finds that room the same way `InteriorLayout.OpeningOf` finds the opening, so the two agree on
  which room is meant.

The *"a low works"*
box (12x8x3.4 m) is the case where the clamp has to bite: a works wants 2.8 m and gets 2.25 m,
inside and out. The *"a shed"* box has so little wall above the no-grid ground guess
(`b.MinY + 0.8 m`, what `Compute` assumes when there is no `ChunkGrid` — in the real game there
always is) that the `MinDoorHeight` floor wins instead, which is why the check asserts the
contract rather than a flat "under the eave".

Changing door geometry on existing buildings means `InteriorLayout.CurrentVersion` goes up: 15.
