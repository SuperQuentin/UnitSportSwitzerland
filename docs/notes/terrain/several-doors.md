# Several doors on one building (#498)

A 100 m city block with one front door reads as a prop, and a barn with nothing but its
wagon-sized pair has no way in for a person. A building now carries as many doors as its size
deserves, and each of them is a real door: it opens, it has a portal, you walk through it.

- **A door is named by its building plus its slot.** `DoorKey(TileE, TileN, Index, Slot)`
  (`src/Interiors/DoorKey.cs`, no Godot in it, unit-tested). Slot 0's text is **byte-identical** to
  `BuildingKey.ToString()` — `"E_N_I"` — so every stored loot record, stored plan and door RPC for
  the millions of one-door buildings is unchanged; the extras are `"E_N_I_<slot>"`, and `TryParse`
  takes 3 or 4 parts. `BuildingKey` stays the **building**: the interior plan key, the space a
  player is in, the loot file. `DoorKey.Building` strips the slot and `InteriorManager.PlanKey`
  normalises through it, so a slot can never reach a plan key and a building's doors all share one
  interior.
- **How many, and where** (`src/Interiors/DoorBudget.cs`, pure, tier 0: `DoorBudgetTests`).
  `Total(kind, width, depth)` = one door per two `Spacing` (22 m) of plan-box perimeter, one per
  three for a house or a shed (so every home in the country keeps its single front door), never
  under 2 for a barn or a garage, never over `MaxPerBuilding` (8).
  `AlongRun(runLength, doorWidth, limit)` gives offsets from a wall run's middle, `Spacing` apart,
  **middle first** — so a wall that takes one door keeps exactly the door it had before any of this
  existed, and a budget that runs out drops the outermost.
- **Which walls.** `BuildingFootprint.Compute` ranks facade facets as it always did and the winner
  is still slot 0. The extras then fill that same wall run first (a street front gets an entrance
  every 22 m), then the next-best facets one run at a time (the back and side doors), until the
  budget is spent. Each candidate must be `DoorOnWall`, not `Covered` by a fellow solid of a group,
  not on stilts or on the buried side of a hillside house, and at least `MinGap` (3 m) of wall from
  every door already placed, edge to edge, which also keeps two doors off a shared corner.
  `ComputeDoors` returns a flat `DoorSpot[]` — several entries per building, main door first — and
  `Footprint.Extra` carries slots 1 and up.
- **An extra door is a plain pedestrian door**, `ServiceDoorFor(kind)`: the kind's own door where
  that is already pedestrian (a long shop front gets more 1.8 m shop doors), else
  `DoorBudget.ServiceWidth` x `ServiceHeight` (1.0 x 2.1 m) — a barn's side door, a works' fire exit.
  It is guaranteed under the eave; a **main** door is not (see the `--doorcheck` note about low sheds).
- **The leaf is a fact about the door, not about its kind.** `DoorHang { Inward, OutwardPair, RollUp }`
  plus `bool Vehicle` on `DoorSpot`, `EntrancePlan`, `DoorLink` and `DoorIndex.Entry`;
  `DoorBudget.HangFor(kind)` / `VehicleFor(kind)` are the defaults a door starts from.
  `DoorLeaf.SwingsOut/RollsUp/OnFacade/LeafWidth/OpenReach` take the hang, and `CreateOnFacade`
  takes the hang (how it moves) and the kind (how it is painted). So a barn's pair stays a pair and
  its side door swings into the hall; `DoorIndex.VehicleDoorAhead` and the server's reach check ask
  the door, not the building; and driving out of an interior no longer gates on the kind at all.
  **A loading bay is then one line** — `Hang = RollUp, Vehicle = true` on an Industrial `DoorSpot` —
  which is what #496/#497 phase 2 needs.
- **Inside, a doorway each** (`InteriorGenerator.Entrances`, run just before `Furnish`). Every
  facade door gets an `EntrancePlan`, the main one first, and an `OpeningKind.Entry` cut in the
  ground-floor room nearest it, on the wall facing the same way, clamped into the plan box.
  `FitEntry` picks the room and the spot: a room whose wall is on that side of the box, with wall left
  over beside its other cuts, no stair shaft or flight to step into. It tries the facing wall
  first, then round the building. The opening is cut under the ceiling of the room it opens into
  (`ClearOf`), not of the storey: a works hall's service block is a 2.6 m room standing inside a
  9 m hall (#497). **Inside and outside are not the same building** — the plan box is
  a clamped rectangle over a TIN solid, and a 300 m warehouse is 120 m of plan — so the two line up
  plausibly, not exactly, which is all a player can tell through a doorway.
  Furniture needs nothing new: `Furnish` already blocks every non-window opening (`Clearance`).
- **A door the plan could fit no doorway for reads as locked.** `InteriorLayout.EntranceOf(door)`
  returns null for it and `ServeDoor` refuses — better than a portal onto another door's doorway.
  `EntranceFor` keeps its old "or the main one" fallback for everything that only wants somewhere
  to measure from. `InteriorValidator` fails a plan with two entrances for one door.
- **One sign per building.** `DoorSpot.Bank` and `.Shop` are set on slot 0 only, so a long shop
  front does not grow a sign per entrance (`BankSigns`). Occasions hash a door as
  `Index + Slot * 1_000_000` (`TileContext.DoorKey`), well past any tile's building count, so a main
  door keeps the identity it had and the Halloween lantern or Christmas gift at it stays put.
- **Churches** already had one entrance per member solid; they now get one per door per member, and
  the main entrance is still one of the solids' own front doors, never a side door.
- **Plan version 14**: stored plans regenerate (#497's industrial sites took 13). One number for
  both, so whichever rebases onto the other takes the next — never a lower one, which would
  regenerate the plans saved under the higher and then collide when that number is reissued.
- **Still gated on the kind, not the door**: the main entry's width and height
  (`InteriorGenerator` line ~62 and `SingleRoom`), and the clear lane a vehicle door keeps through
  the room. Per-door versions of those are what an industrial bay will want next.
- **Picking a building of a kind to walk to** is `DoorIndex.NearestOfKind`, not `Nearest`: plain
  distance, that building's main door for choice, and none of `Nearest`'s reach rules (outside
  only, within 2.5 m of the player's own level), which for a target hundreds of metres off over
  sloping ground made "the nearest barn" a matter of luck — and, once barns had two doors, handed
  `--doorkind Agricultural` a side door, leaving the pair's own behaviour untested.
- **Check**: `--doorcheck` (quick tier, no world): synthetic box solids — house, shed, barn, garage,
  block of flats, 100 m shop front, works hall (which goes through #497's industrial generator) —
  through the real `ComputeDoors` and `InteriorGenerator`. Every door named once, on a wall, `MinGap` from the others, no more than
  `Spacing` of its own wall between two of them, a barn and a garage with a pedestrian door beside
  their vehicle one, every plan valid, and every facade door arriving at a doorway inside that
  agrees with it on how the door hangs. The spacing arithmetic itself is tier 0
  (`DoorBudgetTests`, `DoorKeyTests`). One solid is **turned 31 deg** and the check asserts each
  doorway's inward direction against its door's outward (#524): distance and containment survive a
  mirrored plan frame, a direction does not, and on a box square to the world a mirror and the
  truth agree — so without a turned solid the check could not see the class of bug #499 shipped.
  Demonstrated both ways: mirroring `Entrances`' `faces` fails 7 assertions, and giving the check
  itself a wrong-handed frame fails **only** the turned block.
- **A mirror is only visible where the construction is asymmetric**, and that is the condition for
  the trick above being worth anything. A doorway is asymmetric — a door on the north facade must
  arrive on the north wall, one wall out of four — so turning the solid exposes a flipped frame.
  A row of loading bays spaced evenly about a wall's middle is **symmetric**, so a mirrored wall
  tangent maps the row onto itself and no assertion, on a turned solid or not, can see it (#528
  injected exactly that fault and left both `--sitecheck` and `--doorcheck` green). Where such a
  row is asymmetric — a wall with room on one side only — the mirror puts doors off the wall and
  `DoorOnWall` drops them, which the count does see. So: before adding a turned case, ask whether
  the thing under test is asymmetric; if it is not, say so rather than implying it is covered. Live: `--interiorcheck` walks in and out of a house with a
  cellar, and `--interiorcheck --doorkind Agricultural` into a barn by its pair, with its open
  leaves' edges in reach. Measured on the generated world: 100 m shop front 6 doors, works hall 5,
  block of flats 3, barn and garage 2, house and shed 1.
