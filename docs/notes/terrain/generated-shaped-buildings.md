# Generated world: shaped buildings (#598)

The generated villages (`generated-relief`) used to be all rectangles. Now each village also has
**two shaped buildings**, so interiors that follow a building's real outline (#577,
`apartment-blocks`) are met in every world the net checks run on, not only in `--flatcheck`.

- **Shapes** (`ProceduralWorld.Shapes.cs`, `MakeShape`): right-angled ones (an L, a U, a T, a ring
  round a courtyard) and ones with walls at other angles (a trapezoid narrowing to its back, a
  block with its corners cut, a bar bent in the middle like a wide V, a skewed parallelogram).
  Wings are 11–13 m deep, as a stairwell and two flats need; sizes roll per building.
- **A plan with an outline**: `Plan.Outline` is a `Shape` in the plan's own frame (a along u, b
  to its left): rings (the outer one counter-clockwise, a courtyard's clockwise, so every edge's
  outside is on its right) and convex roof parts. `Plan.Rect` is then only its bounding box, which
  every other test (overlap, trees, tile membership) keeps reading. `Solid` walls every ring and
  fans each roof part flat at the eave; no pitch.
- **Placement** (`AddShaped`, after the town): the shapes go round by the village's id (two per
  village), so neighbouring villages show different ones. Each tries spots along the main street
  out from the middle, either side, 30 or 44 m behind it, and takes the first `Clear` one: its
  outline, walked every 4 m, 8 m (10 by a `Road`) off every street, clear of the valley road and
  railway, 8 m past the river's bank, off lakes, on ground within 4 m corner to corner (its solid
  drops anything over 4.5 m once blended), overlapping no planned building by 4 m. Mostly
  apartment blocks; three in ten angled ones are commercial (shops under flats by the usual roll).
  Its own seed: **no other building moves**.
- **Tile order**: `PlansNear` yields shaped buildings last, after garages, so no other building's
  index in its tile (interior keys, saves) changes.
- `ShapedNear(e, n, radius)`: the planned ones with their shape name, kind and outline in LV95,
  for checks and probes (planned, not built: a solid can still turn one down).

## Interiors

Right-angled shapes peel into wings (`PlanOutline`). An angled outline peels into **one** wing,
its largest rectangle, planned alone (`TryWings` takes a lone wing; every door goes to the wing
wall facing its way); a box over 92 % covered (small cut corners) is planned as the box. So a
bent bar fills a little over half its outline, a trapezoid or a skewed block three quarters.
Shaped commercial buildings that do not roll shops under flats keep the box plan (`MixedUse` is
the only commercial plan that knows wings).
