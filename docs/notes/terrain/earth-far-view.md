# The earth world's far view: the globe all the way down (#1629, G2 C6)

Under `--frame earth` there is no `HorizonLayer`: `World/Globe/Globe` (`Earth = true`) is the far view at every
altitude, from the street to orbit, in one quadtree and one size-on-screen rule (`Styles.LodScale`).

- **The horizon** (`GlobeGround.HorizonAngle`): the camera's horizon over the WGS84 ellipsoid under it, plus the reach
  of a 9 km summit past it (`PeakM`): 340 km of tiles from the street, ~530 km from 3 km. It was taken on a 6,371 km
  sphere: at Geneva the ellipsoid is 6,367 km, so below ~4 km the camera was "under ground", the horizon 0, and only
  the tiles over the nadir drew (the grey void of #1629). The split distance runs from each tile's anchor on the
  ellipsoid (the sphere's centre stood ~20 km off at Swiss latitudes), never under `NearestM` (200 m).
- **Stepping aside for the leaves**: `EarthWorld.TileEntered`/`TileUnloaded` reach `Globe.SetLeafDrawn` (wired in
  `ClientWorld`). A drawn leaf's globe tile is left out, and every ancestor of a drawn leaf always splits, so the
  globe's coarser ground (every 8th vertex) never shows through a leaf; the leaf's neighbours stay the globe's. Its
  skirts close the seam. While a split's children load, the parent still draws (a frame or two of overlap).
- **Near the ground in the style's terrain material** (below `TerrainBelowM`, 20 km): every tile takes
  `StyleKit.Material(Terrain)` (one shared instance, restyled by the kit), so it is lit, hazed, banded (PS1) and
  lifted by refraction as the flat terrain is. Above 20 km, `globe.gdshader` (the true sun's terminator over the whole
  Earth); refraction's lift is gone by then, so the two meet. `world_fill_hide` is 0 in this world (it is the flat
  world's fill dissolve: at 1 `flat_hide` discards every terrain fragment) and `globe.gdshader`'s fill push is off by
  its own `fill_push` uniform.
- **Colour per vertex, in the palette** (`GlobeGround`): each texel of a tile's colour image is read back through the
  bake's encoding (`Unstore`, the exact inverse of `OurColours.ForCartoonShader`) and classified as cover weights
  (water, forest, rock, snow; the rest open ground). A vertex is painted `CoverPalette.ColorFor` of those classes at
  its own altitude, as the flat horizon blocks are: open ground in altitude bands, the snow line. The photo's own
  colours (Blue Marble olive; over the region the palette averaged with roofs) drew a pale beige plain.
  A tile with no colour (the leaves' level) takes its ancestor's weights for its part (`AncestorPalette`).
- **Water**: a leaf-level tile's heights are the lake's bed (#298); `LeafStore.WaterOf` reads the leaf's water section
  alone and lifts the bed to the surface, the leaf's water deciding wet and dry. Coarser levels already hold the
  surface; there a vertex whose eight neighbours are level within 5 cm is water (`GlobeGround.Level`: Blue Marble draws
  inland lakes black).
- **Holes**: NaN heights (past the data a level was built from) are filled from their neighbours
  (`GlobeGround.FillHoles`); they drew at 0 m. A tile with no height at all is not built (its parent stays).
- Checks: `GlobeGroundTests`; side-by-sides at Geneva against flat (`--origin 2500300,1117900 --at …`, a `--shot-queue`).
