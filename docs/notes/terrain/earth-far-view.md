# The earth world's far view: the globe all the way down (#1629, G2 C6)

Under `--frame earth` there is no `HorizonLayer`: `World/Globe/Globe` (`Earth = true`) is the far view at every
altitude, from the street to orbit, in one quadtree and one size-on-screen rule (`Styles.LodScale`).

- **The horizon** (`GlobeGround.HorizonAngle`): the camera's horizon over the WGS84 ellipsoid under it, plus the reach
  of a 9 km summit past it (`PeakM`): 340 km of tiles from the street, ~530 km from 3 km. It was taken on a 6,371 km
  sphere: at Geneva the ellipsoid is 6,367 km, so below ~4 km the camera was "under ground", the horizon 0, and only
  the tiles over the nadir drew (the grey void of #1629). The split distance runs from each tile's anchor on the
  ellipsoid (the sphere's centre stood ~20 km off at Swiss latitudes), never under `NearestM` (200 m).
- **Stepping aside for the leaves**: `EarthWorld.TileEntered`/`TileUnloaded` reach `Globe.SetLeafDrawn` (wired in
  `ClientWorld`). A drawn leaf's globe tile is left out, and every ancestor of a drawn leaf always splits, so the
  globe's coarser ground (every 8th vertex) never shows through a leaf; the leaf's neighbours stay the globe's. Its
  skirts close the seam. While a split's children load, the parent still draws (a frame or two of overlap).
- **Near the ground in the style's terrain material** (below `TerrainBelowM`, 20 km): every tile takes
  `StyleKit.Material(Terrain)` (one shared instance, restyled by the kit), so it is lit, hazed, banded (PS1) and
  lifted by refraction as the flat terrain is. Above 20 km, `globe.gdshader` (the true sun's terminator over the whole
  Earth); refraction's lift is gone by then, so the two meet. `world_fill_hide` is 0 in this world (it is the flat
  world's fill dissolve: at 1 `flat_hide` discards every terrain fragment) and `globe.gdshader`'s fill push is off by
  its own `fill_push` uniform.
- **The terminator holds still** (#1798): `world_true_sun` (`Atmosphere.TrueSun`) is the ephemeris' sun as an
  Earth-fixed direction, read every frame and turned into `Globe.Axes`, the axes the tiles are placed in (the
  atmosphere runs after the globe, which places its tiles again at each re-anchor). Read twice a second in world
  axes it lagged twice over: behind each re-anchor while the globe turned under it (20 km a turn in Space) and
  13 km a read at the game's day speed. Check: `--fly 0,500000,0,30,100000,6 --flyterminator [file.csv]` under
  `--frame earth --sun real` (the globe's sun taken back to the Earth each frame: steps past the sun's own motion,
  and the offset off the ephemeris; 53 km before, 0.00 after).
- **Colour per vertex, in the palette** (`GlobeGround`): each texel of a tile's colour image is read back through the
  bake's encoding (`Unstore`, the exact inverse of `OurColours.ForCartoonShader`) and classified as cover weights
  (water, forest, rock, snow; the rest open ground). A vertex is painted `CoverPalette.ColorFor` of those classes at
  its own altitude, as the flat horizon blocks are: open ground in altitude bands, the snow line. The photo's own
  colours (Blue Marble olive; over the region the palette averaged with roofs) drew a pale beige plain.
  A tile with no colour (the leaves' level) takes its ancestor's weights for its part (`AncestorPalette`).
- **Far leaves** (`Globe.FarLeaves.cs`), the flat world's real-tile rings: within `FarLeafM` (the flat streamer's ring
  radius, 15.5 km at Medium) every ancestor of a real leaf splits down to the leaves, and a real leaf is built from its
  own heights, water, cover and trees (`LeafStore.Sections`: those sections alone, never cached, so `EarthWorld`'s 96
  near leaves are not churned) at `LeafStride` 16 (32 cells of 19 m): painted by its cover as flat tiles are, its trees
  as far billboards (`FarTreeShare`, the flat far rings' 25 %, an even share by index). Within `FarBuildingsM` (the
  flat building rings, 3.5 km) its buildings come as boxes (`BuildingMeshBuilder.BuildProxies`, what flat draws past
  1 km), asked after each plan and dropped 500 m past it. Built in the leaf's anchor frame under the tile's node; the
  materials are the flat streamer's (`FurnishMaterials`), the terrain one is the world's (`FogUniforms`, restyled).
  Fill leaves (the generator's, past the region) stay bare: producing ~2,400 of them would take minutes. Only leaves on
  this copy's disk (`LeafStore.IsLocal`): a leaf the server streams (#1655) is never fetched for the far band, and its
  spot keeps the coarser level.
- **Water**: a leaf-level tile's heights are the lake's bed (#298, Lavaux's 310 m down). It is lifted to the leaf's own
  water, and to the coarser level's surface sampled under it (`FillFromAncestors`) where that is level or the leaf lies
  more than `BedM` (20 m) under it (shore vertices outside the leaf's water mask drew a wall that deep). Coarser levels
  hold the surface; there a vertex whose eight neighbours are level within 5 cm, and whose cover reads at least a little
  water (`WaterHint`), is water (`GlobeGround.Level`: Blue Marble draws inland lakes black, near-black reads as water).
  Past the near leaves a lake is the world's water too (#1669, `Globe.Lakes`): a surface over every drawn cell of water
  (a far leaf's own, or a coarser level's level vertex with a plain water weight, `LakeWeight`, or standing at one of
  the pyramid's lake levels, `CubeManifest.Lakes` / `GlobeGround.OnLake`: Blue Marble's dark shore read as forest and
  left 2 km of Léman ground), the world's water
  material with no waves (as flat's far tiles), shown below 20 km; the ground under it sinks 3 m by the shore and 100 m
  inside (the water shaders shade by depth). Only a real leaf's heights are taken for a bed: a generated fill leaf
  under a mountain the generator lacks (the Salève) drew a lake on its slope.
- **The pyramid's Swiss levels** are built from B4's leaves at their surface, ETOPO's lakes at the leaves' level and a
  1.5 km blend past the region's edge (#1654, `tools/cube-tiles`): the trench east of Lavaux and the lake at two levels
  are gone. A far leaf takes the same surface (`CubeSurface.SurfaceAt`: a bed left dry by the leaf's edge is water).
- **Holes**: NaN heights (past the data a level was built from) come from the coarser levels first
  (`GlobeGround.FillFromAncestors`, bilinear in each ancestor's lattice), then from their neighbours
  (`GlobeGround.FillHoles`, never taken for water); they drew at 0 m. A tile with no height at all is not built.
- Checks: `GlobeGroundTests`; side-by-sides against flat at Geneva (`--origin 2500300,1117900 --at …`) and Lavaux
  (2545000,1149400), street and 600 m, every style, through a `--shot-queue`. A worktree without the LFS assets cannot
  run Realistic (it waits on its textures): copy the main checkout's `.godot/imported` first.
- Shots wait for the far leaves too (#1679): under `--frame earth` `--shot`/`--shot-queue` (and so the refshots) take
  the picture once `EarthWorld.Idle` and `Globe.Idle` hold together (`ClientWorld.EarthIdle`): nothing on a globe worker
  or waiting to be added, and a plan since the last commit asked for nothing. Bounded by `ShotRunner.CompleteTimeout`;
  a shot that waited logs `[shot] … waited N s for the ground (at first: …)` with both worlds' status.
