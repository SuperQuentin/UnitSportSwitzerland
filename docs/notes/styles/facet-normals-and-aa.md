# Facet normals from view space; the main view's anti-aliasing (#768)

## Fuzzy faces: world-space derivative normals

- **Symptom**: in the lit styles (Cartoon most), faces facing one cardinal direction and its
  reverse flickered light/shade per 2×2 pixel quad where the sun grazed them; the other pair was
  clean. At J2 of the signal test region the west faces were fuzzy at 13:00 only (shade at 9:00,
  full sun at 17:00).
- **Not shadow acne**: sun shadows off left it unchanged.
- **Cause**: the bodies took a flat face's normal as `cross(dFdy(world_pos), dFdx(world_pos))`.
  World x and z reach ±3 km before the floating origin shifts (`OriginShifter.ThresholdM`) and y
  (altitude) never shifts. A float there steps in 0.1–0.25 mm, against about 5 mm between pixels
  on a face a few metres away, so the derivative along a big axis is rounding noise. That tilts a
  face whose normal lies along that axis by 1–2° per quad (here x ≈ −1500, z ≈ 13: east and west
  faces only). Cartoon's terminator is 0.08 wide in N·L, so only grazing faces show it.
- **Fix**: `facet_normal(VERTEX, INV_VIEW_MATRIX)` in `shaders/common/style.gdshaderinc` (view
  space is centred on the camera; a rotation keeps the cross product's sense, so every body's own
  orientation step is unchanged). It is used by road, prop, building, terrain, path and tree.
  `interior.gdshaderinc` had already done the same (interiors sit tens of km from the origin).
  Any new body that derives a facet normal uses it.

## Anti-aliasing

- **Setting**: `GameSettings.AntiAliasing` (Off / FXAA / MSAA 2x / 4x / 8x), a Video row under
  "3D resolution", FXAA by default. `Core/DisplaySettings` applies it to the main viewport on
  every settings change and style switch.
- **PS1**: it is always off there, since the jagged low-resolution edges are its look (as the 3D
  resolution keeps its canvas size). The row is greyed out while PS1 is the style and follows a
  live switch.
- **Elsewhere**: door portals and photos copy the main viewport's MSAA and FXAA; the headset has
  its own `VrMsaa`.
- **Why not MSAA by default**: MSAA shades a pixel for any triangle covering one of its samples,
  with the vertex colours extrapolated past the triangle. Godot has no `centroid` qualifier. The
  distant ground is sub-pixel triangles with different cover colours, so it sparkles in a band
  along the horizon. Clamping the colour does not help; a constant colour removes it. FXAA is clean.
- **`--aa off|fxaa|msaa2x|msaa4x|msaa8x`**: this run's anti-aliasing, not saved, for comparison shots.
- **Checks**: J2 close-ups at 13:00 (`-1512.5,g3.0,9,12,-135`) in Cartoon with `--aa off` and
  `--aa msaa4x`, in Realistic- and PS1, and a Geneva street and aerial view at 13:00 and 17:00.
  `--settings` with `--shot` shows the row in Cartoon (enabled) and PS1 (greyed out).
