# Zoom-aware detail: LOD by size on screen (#1433)

Every level of detail used to be picked by distance only, so a narrow field of view drew far
things at the detail of their true distance: at the camera item's 200 mm a house 2 km off is as
big on screen as one 180 m off walking, and was drawn for 2 km. Phase C0 of #1430
(`docs/plans/ellipsoid-handover.md`, "Detail by size on screen").

- **One zoom factor.** `Styles/ZoomRule.Of(fov)` = tan(34°) / tan(fov / 2), never below 1: 1 at
  the walking view (`FootPlayer.BaseFov`, 68°; the user's choice, not the 35 mm lens), x2 at
  35 mm, x11.2 at 200 mm, x8.6 for the binoculars (9°). A wider view (fast car, plane) keeps its
  detail. `Styles/LodScale` reads it from the active camera every frame (`CameraGlobal`, on
  `FramePreDraw`) and rounds it **down** to a step, a power of 1.25 with 5 % hysteresis
  (`ZoomRule.StepFor`): 200 mm is step 10, x9.31. Everything uses the step, the shader global
  included, so they agree on which tree is 3D and nothing rebuilds per frame of a zoom ease.
  XR and orthographic cameras are 1. `--zoom-lod off` turns it all off (the before/after check).
- **The view cone.** `LodScale.InCone`: the horizontal half FOV plus a margin of half of it
  (2° to 15°). Only the terrain rings and `NearTrees` test it; the rest relies on frustum culling.
- **Tree shaders** (`shaders/body/tree.gdshaderinc`): the handover distance is
  `distance(.., world_cam_pos) / world_lod_scale`, for the 3D trees and the billboards alike.
- **`Terrain/NearTrees`** (Cartoon's heavy 3D trees) gathers the trees within range, plus those in
  the cone within range x (step x 1.25): the extra step covers the frame between the shader taking
  a new step and the refill. A step change or a 5° turn while zoomed refills at once. A zoomed cone
  over a forest overflows 16 000 trees a kind: the buffers double, up to 65 536.
- **Sun shadows** (`CameraGlobal.ScaleShadows`): the sun's `DirectionalShadowMaxDistance` is
  multiplied by the step (the user's choice over capping the trees at the shadow range). Without
  it, the 3D trees a zoomed view reaches past the 400 m cascades neither cast nor received a
  shadow. The cascades keep their split fractions, so at x9.31 the last one runs 1.3-3.7 km:
  coarser texels, which the lens magnifies.
- **Visibility ranges** (`Styles/ZoomRanges`): Godot's ranges have no FOV term. Every node with a
  range is tracked (`ZoomRanges.Track(node)` once its range is set, `TrackTree(root)` for a built
  sign), and on a step change its begin, end and margins are multiplied by the step straight on
  the rendering server (`InstanceGeometrySetVisibilityRange`), 4 000 a frame. The node keeps its
  base range, so the movie recorder still reads it. Freed nodes are pruned on the pass and when
  the list doubles. **A new `VisibilityRangeEnd` must be tracked**, and re-tracked whenever code
  sets it again (a node's own setter pushes its base range back).
  Not tracked on purpose: `VehicleBody.FadeAtRange`, the fade at the edge of the server's interest
  range (#689), which must end before the vehicle leaves this peer.
- **Terrain rings** (`ChunkManager.RecomputeDesired`, `ZoomedDist`): a tile inside the cone picks
  its stride, roads, buildings and tree density by `ZoomRule.Ring(ring, step)` = round(ring /
  step), never under 1 (ring 0's stride for a ring-1 tile is four times the vertices for nothing
  a lens shows). Collision and unloading stay by the ring. The desired set is keyed on the step
  and on the camera's tile and heading in 2.5° steps (`ZoomView`), so a pan re-evaluates.
  The horizon blocks (100 m lattice, one resolution) are unchanged.

## Measured

Lausanne from 120 m above Sauvabelin (`--origin 2538500,1154500`, shot line
`0,g120,0,-8,195,8,...`), default settings (15 rings, Medium, Cartoon), 1152x648, RTX 4070 Ti,
`--perflog`, a `fov 6.87` line for 200 mm (`core/commands`):

| 200 mm, steady (27-29 s each) | `--zoom-lod off` | zoom LOD on |
|---|---|---|
| GPU ms p50 / p95 | 6.28 / 6.52 | 6.33 / 7.70 |
| render CPU ms p50 | 2.30 | 2.74 |
| primitives p50 | 13.4M | 29.9M |
| VRAM | 4.25 GB | 5.40 GB |
| frames over 33 ms | 4 | 27 |

- **Where the cost is**: the hitches all fall in the ~7 s after zooming in, while the cone's tiles
  refine (main-thread commits of 16-125 ms for stride-2 tiles with buildings,
  `terrain/stutter-main-thread-commit-problem`); after that the zoomed frames are as smooth as
  without. The isolated ~100 ms frames in both runs are the screenshot readback.
- **The first cone** was the load order's: 15° margin plus 16 heading sectors (±11.25° of
  rounding), so a 200 mm view (±6°) refined a ±32° wedge, and VRAM went 4.25 -> 7.5 GB. The
  proportional margin and 2.5° headings brought it to 5.4 GB.
- **`ZoomRanges`**: ~5,200 ranges tracked in a city; a step change rescales ~4,200 of them in
  2.2 ms in all (one frame at 4,000 a frame).
- **Looks**: at 200 mm the trees 2 km off are Cartoon's 3D trees with shadows instead of
  billboards. Under snow the 3D trees read paler than the billboards, as they do walking at the
  220 m handover: the two shade differently (smooth mesh normals against traced facets), which
  the zoom only moves further out.

## How to check

- `--shot-queue` with `fov 68` / `fov 6.87` lines, once with `--zoom-lod off` and once without;
  each zoomed shot logs `zoomlod=x<step> ranges=<tracked> pass=<ms>/<set>`.
- `ZoomRuleTests` (tier 0) pins the factor, the steps and the ring rule.
- **Zoom cap** (#1813): `ZoomRule.MaxZoom` = 1100 (the telescope at 20,000 mm, a 0.07° view): past it the detail stops following the zoom. The telescope reaches a 9.9″ view for the planets, and a mountain at x10,000 would otherwise ask for every tile's finest detail to the horizon.
