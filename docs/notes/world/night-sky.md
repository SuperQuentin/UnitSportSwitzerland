# The night sky: stars, planets, moons, satellites (#1444, C1b of #1430)

Everything here is drawn only under the real sun (`SunPath.Model == Real`: not in checks,
screenshots or the fun world unless `--sun real`), placed just inside the camera's far plane at its
true angular size, and read from `DayNight.Sky` (the date, hour and camera place, the celestial
sphere's axes `Stars`, the sun and the moon). The moon itself: `items/telescope`.

- **Stars** (`World/StarField`, `shaders/sky_point.gdshader`): the Yale Bright Star Catalogue's 9,096
  stars (`assets/space/stars_bsc5.json`, J2000 RA/Dec, V, B-V), one mesh of quads built once, each
  vertex its catalogue direction, turned each frame by `Sky.Stars` (`SunPath.SkyFrame`:
  `Ephemeris.Celestial`, precession and sidereal time). A fixed size in pixels by magnitude
  (`StarField.Look`, 1.2-4.6 px), tinted by B-V, fading in with `world_night` and out near the horizon.
  Proper motion and nutation are left out.
- **Planets** (`Ephemeris.Planets.cs`): JPL's approximate Keplerian elements (table 1, 1800-2050), the
  Earth as the barycentre less the moon's share, light time, annual aberration, precession to the date.
  Against Horizons from Bern: Mercury 0.006°, Venus and Mars 0.010°, Jupiter 0.028°, **Saturn 0.080°**
  (the table's own limit: more than the telescope's narrowest view, but the lock follows our own place,
  so nobody sees it unless they hold a sky app up against it), Uranus 0.003°, Neptune 0.012°.
  Their axes: the IAU WGCCRE models (`Ephemeris.Axes`, at light time; Mars' pole from the 2009 report);
  Horizons' sub-observer latitudes are **planetodetic**, so the tests flatten ours by the radii first.
  Magnitudes: Mallama and Hilton's absolute magnitudes and phase terms (`Ephemeris.Magnitude`).
- **Big moons**: Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto and Titan as circles in their planet's equator, the
  phase fitted to Horizons on 2026-01-01 (`Satellites` table in `Ephemeris.Planets.cs`): within 3.3 %
  of their orbit at the ten test dates.
- **Their depth** (#1518, `shaders/common/sky_depth.gdshaderinc`): stars, planets, rings, the moon and satellites
  keep a depth of at least 1.5e-7 (reversed: 0 is the far plane). High up the camera sees 1 : 3 million from near
  to far (0.33 m to 1,117 km at 66 km), and a star straight ahead at 95 % of the far plane fell under a step of the
  24-bit depth buffer: it read as the far plane and failed the test, so the stars went in a disc round whatever was
  looked at (the shuttle's film, and a planet looked at from space). Ridges within ~2 steps of the far plane
  (past ~62 km of a 70 km view) no longer hide them; the horizon's fade covers most of those.
- **Drawing them** (`World/SkyBodies`, `shaders/planet.gdshader`, `planet_rings.gdshader`): by eye a
  point sized by magnitude (Venus alone shows by day: Jupiter and Mercury dotted a noon sky, #1497); once more than about a pixel wide (zoomed), the body:
  a unit sphere whose basis is the body's own axes, squashed along the pole (Jupiter, Saturn), its map
  read from the vertex, lit from **its own direction to the sun** (not the Earth's), limb-darkened for
  the giants. Saturn's rings are an annulus in its equator; the rings' far side and a moon behind its
  planet are hidden by an `occluder` test. A planetary system is drawn at one scale, so a moon passes
  in front of or behind its planet. Places are kept on the celestial sphere and only turned each frame;
  the ephemeris runs twice a second, every frame for what is zoomed on (it is ~50 µs a body). Updated in
  `_Process` (a headless run never draws) and again at `FramePreDraw` from where the camera ended up.
- **Satellites** (`Core/Sgp4`, `World/Satellites`): TLEs propagated with SGP4 (near-Earth branch, WGS-72;
  Vallado's verification case to a metre; the ISS against Horizons within 0.15° a day after its epoch).
  The shipped snapshot is CelesTrak's `stations` and `visual` groups (169 satellites,
  `assets/space/tle_snapshot.json`); in normal play the `stations`, `visual` and `starlink` groups are
  fetched from CelesTrak once a day into `user://tle/` (never in checks, screenshot runs or with
  `--date`). A worker propagates all of them to the sky's moment and a second later; each frame carries
  them on along that line (about every 30 s of sky time a new run). Shown only while the sun is 4° down
  here and the satellite is out of the Earth's shadow (a cylinder), as a point by its standard magnitude
  at 1,000 km (the ISS -1.8, Starlink 5.5, others 3). Deep-space orbits (over 225 min) are left out.
  Satellites have periods, but drag and the Earth's bulge move them minutes off within a week or two:
  hence the fresh elements; with old ones their planes stay right and only their timing drifts.
- **The telescope** locks on whatever is nearest the middle (`TelescopeView.ToggleLock`: the moon,
  `SkyBodies.Nearest`, `Satellites.Nearest`), up to 20,000 mm (Jupiter a fifth of the view).
- **Checks**: `--skycheck --systems ui,physics,sky --sun real --date 2026-12-11 --occasion none` (the
  catalogue, every body where the ephemeris has it, Jupiter at 04:00 and Saturn at 20:00 held in the
  middle to 0.00001°, satellites before dawn and the lock on one; windowed `test_output/sky_*.png`).
  Unit: `EphemerisTests` (planets, faces, big moons), `Sgp4Tests`. Screenshots of the sky with the
  shot queue: `--sun real --date ... --occasion none` (December's snowfall hides everything).
- Not yet: Saturn's shadow on its rings, the ISS as a model in the telescope, a cloudy sky when it
  snows, proper motion, Pluto and the asteroids.
