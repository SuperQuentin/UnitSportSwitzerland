# One look per body, from space (#1998, H7b of `docs/plans/planets.md` section 6)

A body is drawn by its globe (`World/Bodies/BodyGlobe`, its pyramid's tiles in scaled space) or by its sphere in the
sky (`World/MoonDisc` for the Moon, `World/SkyBodies` for the planets and big moons, a point below a pixel or two).
`World/Bodies/BodyLook` makes the two one picture from space:

- **Which draws it** (`BodyLook.WantsGlobe`, read by `BodyViews._Process`): the globe while the anchor is on the body,
  or in space (`BodyLook.InSpace`: `Atmosphere.Space`, or an airless anchor) while the body's radius is at least
  `GlobeShare` (1 %) of the view's height (kept down to 0.8 of it). Below that, and always from inside the air (the
  Earth's sky by day, the telescope from the ground), the sky's sphere. Before: the globe when the anchor was on the body
  or within ten of its radii, so the handover between the Moon and the Earth swapped the crisp globe for the sky disc in
  one frame (a glow, #1988's clip).
- **How it is lit** (`BodyLook.Of`, `Write`): in space the sky's spheres take the globe's formula through their shaders'
  `globe_look` (moon.gdshader, planet.gdshader: `globe_lit`, the photo lifted for Cartoon, Lambert, the night floor 0.06
  or a giant's 0.015 with its 0.3 terminator wrap) at the globe's own exposure (`BodyGlobe.Expose` →
  `BodyLook.SetExposure`). From the ground they keep the sky's look (added onto the sky, the full Moon a flat disc).
  The Moon's halo (a star's brightness for a disc too small to read) fades out in space once the disc is a few pixels.
- **Where it is** (`BodyLook.Seen`): in space the sky's spheres are placed by the frame tree from the camera (direction,
  distance, the body's own axes, the Sun's direction at it), as the globes are. The sky's own reading (`SunPath`, an
  observer counted from the Earth) is right on and near the Earth but ~90° off for the Moon from 140,000 km out
  sunward: the sphere was drawn off-screen while the globe stood in the middle.
- **Check**: `--bodyviewcheck` (windowed, `--frame earth --bodies DIR`): from the Moon's sunward side, a pair at the
  handover (68,000 km, the Moon's anchor then the Earth's) and a pair at the globe's size edge (142,000 km, the globe
  forced on then off, `BodyLook.ForceGlobe`), each compared inside the Moon's disc. Pictures in
  `test_output/bodies/bodyview_*.png`.
- **Placed just before drawing** (#2036): whatever is put about the camera (another body's globe shrunk under its root,
  Saturn's rings' slab and the camera's height over it, the sky's spheres, the Moon's disc, the stars, the sky floor) is
  placed again on `RenderingServer.FramePreDraw`, after every node moved. In `_Process` alone the globe and its rings
  were a frame of the fly camera's own step behind while the air (world axes) was not: at full speed by Saturn the
  parts came apart by a third of the view and snapped back on a stop. `--bodyplacecheck` (windowed) strafes past
  Saturn (2.3 million and 250,000 km), Jupiter and the Moon (from the Earth's side) at full speed and holds every
  part within 1 px of the true centre; pictures `test_output/bodies/bodyplace_*.png`.
- **Not yet**: a cross-fade between the two (they match, so the hard swap does not show); the stars thin while the
  anchor is the Moon (the Moon sky session's `StarField`).
