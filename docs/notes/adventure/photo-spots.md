# Photo spots: the 30 curated views, for the team to review (#1227)

`src/Adventure/Rewards/PhotoSpots.cs` (`PhotoSpots.Curated`, each `Review = true`). Decided Oct 2026: Claude drafts
them, the team reviews. Plus one spot per viewpoint POI, made from the catalogue (`OfViewpoint`: the highest tier-1/2
peak 3-25 km off that rises over it).

## Rules

- A spot is a standing disc (150 m) and a target (LV95, a height). After a shot the client reports every spot whose disc
  holds the player and not yet taken (`RewardPhotos.Report`, its bearing, pitch and the frame's angle; the print is a
  square, so one angle). The server judges from the body's own position (`PhotoJudge`): in the disc, the target inside
  80 % of the half-angle both ways, a line of sight over the coarse terrain sampled every 50 m (past the first 30 m and
  short of the target's last 150 m; unloaded terrain counts as clear). Buildings are not in the line of sight.
- The first time: the `photos` section, `PhotoTaken(peer, key, spot, poiId?)`, a Fine roll; the print's sidecar names
  the spot (`PhotoMeta.Spot`). The Collection tab lists the 30 (taken ✓). The plan's Atlas tick is not built.

## How they were drafted and checked

Positions are WGS84 from memory, converted with `SwissProjection`, then checked against the main checkout's terrain
tiles with a throwaway tool (stand height, the target's ground, the line of sight from the disc's centre with
`PhotoJudge`): every one is clear from its centre and from at least a third of its disc. These were moved after that
check: the Chillon stand down to the lakeside path (378 m), the Riffelsee stand onto the lake (2757 m), the Eggishorn
and Monte Brè stands onto their summits, the Pilatus target onto the Tomlishorn (2128 m), the Niesen target onto its
summit, the Moléson, Rochers de Naye, Stockhorn, Gornergrat, Oeschinensee and Rigi stands 15-34 m to a clear point; the
Eggishorn's target became the Jungfrau (the Konkordiaplatz is hidden behind the glacier's bend from the summit).

**To review on the map**: that each stand is where people really take the picture (the path, the platform, the bridge),
that the name reads right, and that the target is the thing in the picture (a castle's keep, not its hill).
