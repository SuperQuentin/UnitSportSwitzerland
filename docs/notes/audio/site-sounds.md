# The sound of a building site (#617, part of #605)

A building site at work is heard before it is seen: hammering on the shell, a vibrator in a pour, an
angle grinder, a reversing beeper in the yard, a radio in the office, the crane's slewing motor. It
falls silent when the crane stops.

## Three files

- **`SiteSfx`** (`src/Audio/`, tier 0): the synthesis, `SfxSynth`'s method with no engine call
  (mono floats at `Dsp.Rate` = 22,050 Hz, peak 0.9, deterministic per variant). Hammer blows (even
  variants steel: a click over four inharmonic partials; odd timber: a knock and a thud), a grinder
  cut (2.2-3.8 s: band-limited noise, wobble, a whine, sparks), a vibrator (3-6 s, ~150 Hz with
  harmonics, ~7 Hz swell), a **beeper**, a radio (8 s of pentatonic tune, bass, kick and hat, a murmur
  of speech-like noise, squeezed into 280 Hz-3 kHz) and a crane motor (4 s whine swelling and rising).
  `SiteSfx.Clips(kind)` bakes a kind once, lazily, from any thread.
- **`SiteSoundPlan`** (`src/Terrain/Construction/`, tier 0): which sound, where on the site, after how
  long. A pure function of the site, a question number and the environment clock.
- **`SiteSounds`** (`src/Terrain/Construction/`, client only): the node. `ClientWorld` creates it with
  the ambience, behind `Systems.Audio`, so `--systems` runs without audio do not get it.

## The two clocks

(`core/three-clocks`.) *Whether* a site is noisy is **environment time**, `CraneMotion.Working`
(07-12 and 13-17, not Sundays), the same call `SiteCranes` makes, so the crane, the hammering and the
radio start and stop together. *When the next sound comes* is audio presentation, the **real clock**
(`SiteSounds` accumulates `_Process` delta): the gaps are for the ear, and a 24-minute day should not
turn a hammer into a machine gun. Outside hours a site is asked again every 5 s.

## What plays where

- **Sites.** `SiteSounds` plans the 3 x 3 tiles round the listener that come within 210 m
  (`LoadBuildingsAsync`, `LoadRoadsAsync`, `SitePlans.For`, on a worker, cached per tile, dropped two
  tiles away). Only the 2 nearest sites within 150 m of their *fence* (`SiteSoundPlan.Distance` to
  `Area`) make sound; the first sound comes up to 4 s after arriving.
- **Kinds by phase.** Foundations: vibrator, beeper, formwork hammering. Shell: mostly hammering, then
  the grinder, crane, beeper, vibrator. Topped out: grinder, hammer, radio. A radio needs an office
  zone, a crane motor a crane; a kind the site has no place for is not offered.
- **Where.** Hammer (a burst of 3-9 blows, 0.45-0.9 s apart, +-15 %), grinder and vibrator on the plan
  box at a built level (the hammer towards the top one); the beeper on the lane from the gate to the
  building; the radio in the office zone; the crane motor at a crane's base, 4 m up.
- **Cost.** A tick every 0.25 s over a handful of cached sites; `_Process` only pops due blows off a
  24-slot struct queue; 8 `AudioStreamPlayer3D` on the `Sfx` bus, a full pool drops a sound. The clips
  (~1.2 M samples) are baked on a worker the first time a site is near, then encoded on the main thread.
- **The origin.** Sites and queued blows are tile-local; a position becomes world space the moment a
  sound plays, so nothing is cached across a shift. The pool's players are `TopLevel`, which
  `OriginShifter` moves itself, so the node is not `IOriginShiftAware`.

## For the machines (#611-#614)

`SiteSfx.Beeper(beeps, variant)` is public: a reversing alarm, ~1 kHz, 0.5 s on and 0.5 s off. A
reversing dumper or telehandler can play it, or loop it, from its own node. The site's own beeper in the
yard stands for them until the machines exist.

## Checks

`SiteSoundTests` (tier 0, 64 cases): every clip made, finite, inside [-1, 1], loud enough (RMS > 0.02),
identical on a second run, variants that differ, clip lengths, the vibrator's peak near 150 Hz, the
beeper's rhythm and 1 kHz, the radio band-limited; and for the plan: **no sound outside working hours or
on a Sunday** (nine hour/day cases against every phase), one in hours, the hours being the crane's,
every position on the site, each kind where it is made, no radio without an office or crane motor
without a crane, determinism, variety by phase, waits that outlast a burst.

## Not done

- **Nobody has heard it.** Judged by numbers and by reading, as the water sounds were (`audio`): the
  tune, the grinder and the beeper want an ear on a real site (a Geneva tile).
- **No machine sounds.** Engines, tipping, the telehandler's hydraulics come with #611-#614.
- Sound through the hoarding or a roof is not filtered: a listener indoors hears a site at full level.
- The crane's motor does not follow the real slew: it is a one-shot while the crane works, not tied to
  `CraneMotion.Pose`'s yaw rate.
